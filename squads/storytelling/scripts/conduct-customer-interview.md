# Conduct Customer Interview

## Purpose

Provide a repeatable, step-by-step script for conducting customer story interviews. The goal is to elicit authentic narratives — with specific details, emotions, and transformation arcs — that can be shaped into marketing stories.

## Prerequisites

- Interview subject identified and confirmed (customer, partner, or internal champion)
- Signed release or consent form permitting use of quotes and story details
- Background research completed: account history, product usage, support tickets, prior testimonials
- Recording tool tested (audio at minimum; video preferred)
- Interview scheduled for 45-60 minutes with a 15-minute buffer
- Interviewer has reviewed `docs/framework-selection-guide.md` to identify a likely narrative structure

## Steps

1. **Prepare the Interview Guide (1 day before)**
   - Review the customer's background and note 3-5 specific moments to probe.
   - Draft 10-12 open-ended questions organized into three acts: Before, During, After.
   - Customize questions to the customer's context; avoid generic prompts.
   - Share the general topic areas (not exact questions) with the interviewee in advance.

2. **Set Up the Environment (15 minutes before)**
   - Test recording equipment and confirm backup recording method.
   - Open your note-taking document with the question guide.
   - Have the consent form ready for verbal or written confirmation.
   - Minimize distractions: close unrelated tabs, silence notifications.

3. **Open the Interview (5 minutes)**
   - Thank the customer for their time and explain how the story will be used.
   - Confirm consent to record: "Is it okay if I record this so I can focus on our conversation?"
   - Set expectations: "This will feel more like a conversation than a formal interview."
   - Start recording.

4. **Act 1 — The Before State (10-15 minutes)**
   - "Take me back to before you started using [product/service]. What was your day-to-day like?"
   - "What was the biggest challenge you were facing at that time?"
   - "How did that challenge affect you personally or your team?"
   - "What had you already tried to solve this problem?"
   - Listen for emotional language, specific numbers, and vivid details. Probe with "Tell me more about that."

5. **Act 2 — The Turning Point (10-15 minutes)**
   - "How did you first come across [product/service]?"
   - "What made you decide to give it a try?"
   - "Walk me through the first time you used it. What happened?"
   - "Was there a specific moment when you thought, 'This is different'?"
   - "Were there any doubts or setbacks along the way?"
   - Capture the pivotal moment — the scene the audience will remember.

6. **Act 3 — The After State (10-15 minutes)**
   - "What does your day-to-day look like now?"
   - "Can you share a specific result or metric that changed?"
   - "How do your colleagues or customers react to the change?"
   - "What would you say to someone in your old situation?"
   - Push for concrete before-and-after comparisons with numbers when possible.

7. **Bonus Round — The Unexpected Detail (5 minutes)**
   - "Is there anything I haven't asked about that you think is part of this story?"
   - "What's one thing that would surprise people about your experience?"
   - Often the best quote or anecdote comes from this unprompted space.

8. **Close the Interview (5 minutes)**
   - Thank the customer sincerely.
   - Explain next steps: "I'll draft the story and share it with you for review before anything goes live."
   - Confirm the best way to follow up with clarifying questions.
   - Stop recording.

9. **Post-Interview Processing (within 48 hours)**
   - Upload the recording to the shared drive with standardized naming: `interview_[customer]_[date]`.
   - Transcribe the interview (automated tool or manual).
   - Highlight the top 5-7 quotes and the core transformation arc.
   - Create a one-paragraph story summary.

10. **Enter into Story Bank**
    - Create a story bank entry with status "interviewed."
    - Tag with customer name, industry, product, framework, and emotional theme.
    - Attach the transcript and summary.
    - Follow `scripts/build-story-bank.md` for formatting.

11. **Draft Review Cycle**
    - Share the draft story with the customer for factual accuracy and tone approval.
    - Incorporate feedback within 3 business days.
    - Run the final draft through `scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md`.

12. **Archive and Index**
    - Store the approved story, raw transcript, consent form, and recording together.
    - Update the story bank entry status to "approved" or "published."
    - Notify the cross-squad contacts per `scripts/cross-squad-handoff.md`.

## Expected Output

- Recorded interview (45-60 minutes) stored in shared drive.
- Transcribed interview with highlighted quotes.
- One-paragraph story summary.
- Story bank entry in "interviewed" status with all metadata.
- Draft story ready for customer review.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Customer gives short, generic answers | Redirect with: "Can you walk me through a specific example?" |
| Customer goes off-topic for extended periods | Gently steer: "That's great context. Let me bring us back to [topic]." |
| Recording fails mid-interview | Switch to backup immediately; take detailed notes as a safety net |
| Customer is reluctant to share metrics | Offer ranges ("roughly 2x or 3x?") or qualitative comparisons instead |
| Customer requests to retract a statement | Note the retraction; omit from the transcript and all derivatives |
| Legal or compliance concerns arise | Pause and consult with the legal team before proceeding |
