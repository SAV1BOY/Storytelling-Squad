# Prepare Pitch Rehearsal

## Purpose

Structure and run pitch rehearsal sessions where squad members practice delivering narrative-driven pitches. Rehearsals build confidence, refine delivery, and pressure-test story structure before high-stakes presentations to clients, executives, or cross-functional teams.

## Prerequisites

- A completed story draft or pitch deck that needs rehearsal
- The presenter has identified the target audience and presentation context
- A rehearsal room or video call with recording capability
- 2-4 reviewers who can simulate the target audience
- Timer for managing run-throughs
- Feedback form prepared (see Step 3)
- Recommended: run after at least one improv workshop for team familiarity with feedback norms

## Steps

1. **Schedule the Rehearsal (3-5 days before the real pitch)**
   - Book a 60-minute session; allow for 2-3 full run-throughs plus feedback.
   - Invite reviewers who represent different perspectives: a storytelling peer, a subject-matter expert, and someone unfamiliar with the topic (fresh-ears perspective).
   - Share the pitch draft or deck with reviewers 24 hours in advance.
   - Confirm the presentation format: live in-room, video call, or recorded async.

2. **Prepare the Presenter**
   - Presenter completes a Pitch Prep Worksheet:
     - What is the one sentence this audience should remember?
     - What is the emotional journey: where do they start and where should they end?
     - What is the ask or call to action?
     - What are the 3 likely objections and how will you address them?
   - Presenter practices alone at least once before the rehearsal.

3. **Create the Feedback Form**
   - Prepare a structured form for reviewers with these dimensions:
     - Opening hook: Did the first 30 seconds grab attention? (1-5)
     - Story structure: Was there a clear arc — setup, tension, resolution? (1-5)
     - Emotional connection: Did you feel something? What? (1-5 + open text)
     - Clarity: Could you repeat the main message back? (1-5)
     - Pacing: Too fast, too slow, or just right? (1-5)
     - Delivery: Confidence, eye contact, vocal variety (1-5)
     - Call to action: Clear and compelling? (1-5)
     - Top strength: one thing to keep.
     - Top improvement: one thing to change.

4. **Set Up the Room**
   - Arrange seating to simulate the real presentation environment.
   - Test projector, screen sharing, or any visual aids.
   - Start the recording.
   - Place the timer where the presenter can see it.

5. **Run-Through 1: Full Delivery (15 minutes)**
   - Presenter delivers the complete pitch without interruption.
   - Reviewers take notes on the feedback form silently.
   - Time the presentation; note if it ran over or under the target duration.

6. **Feedback Round 1 (10 minutes)**
   - Each reviewer shares their "top strength" first (positive framing).
   - Then each shares their "top improvement" with a specific suggestion.
   - Presenter listens without defending; takes notes.
   - Facilitator synthesizes: "The common theme I'm hearing is..."

7. **Adjustment Break (5 minutes)**
   - Presenter revises based on feedback: adjust one section, cut one slide, or change one transition.
   - Reviewers discuss among themselves what they want to watch for in the next run.

8. **Run-Through 2: Focused Delivery (12 minutes)**
   - Presenter delivers again, focusing on the adjustments.
   - Reviewers score only the dimensions that were flagged for improvement.
   - Pay special attention to the opening hook and the closing call to action.

9. **Feedback Round 2 (8 minutes)**
   - Focus on whether the adjustments landed.
   - Offer one more refinement suggestion per reviewer.
   - Discuss pacing: "You had 2 minutes left — where could you add a pause or a story beat?"

10. **Objection Simulation (5 minutes)**
    - Reviewers role-play as the target audience and ask the 3 anticipated objections.
    - Presenter practices responding in real time.
    - Facilitator coaches on bridging techniques: acknowledge, reframe, redirect.

11. **Final Notes and Action Items (5 minutes)**
    - Presenter states their top 3 changes for the final version.
    - Facilitator confirms deadline for final revisions.
    - If another rehearsal is needed, schedule it immediately.
    - Stop recording; share the recording with the presenter.

12. **Post-Rehearsal Follow-Up**
    - Presenter submits the final version to the squad lead for review.
    - Run the final version through `scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md` if it is a formal deliverable.
    - After the real pitch, presenter shares the outcome with the squad for collective learning.
    - Archive the rehearsal recording and feedback forms in the shared drive.

## Expected Output

- Presenter has completed 2 full run-throughs with structured feedback.
- Documented feedback forms from all reviewers.
- Clear action items for final revisions.
- Recording available for self-review.
- Presenter confidence measurably improved for the live delivery.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Presenter freezes or becomes anxious | Pause; run a quick improv warm-up exercise; restart with a supportive framing |
| Feedback becomes vague ("It was good") | Redirect reviewers to the specific dimensions on the feedback form |
| Reviewers give conflicting advice | Facilitator identifies the core tension and helps the presenter decide which direction aligns with the audience |
| Pitch runs significantly over time | Identify the section with the lowest impact and cut it; tighten transitions |
| Presenter resists feedback | Remind them: "The goal is to make the story land, not to judge the storyteller" |
| No time for a second run-through | Prioritize the opening and closing sections for a partial run-through |
