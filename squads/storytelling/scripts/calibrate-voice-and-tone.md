# Calibrate Voice and Tone

## Purpose

Establish a repeatable process for calibrating brand voice and tone specifically for storytelling outputs. This ensures that every narrative produced by the squad sounds like it comes from the same brand, while allowing tonal variation based on audience, channel, and emotional context.

## Prerequisites

- Existing brand guidelines (if available) with voice attributes defined
- Access to 10-15 published narrative assets representing the current voice
- Participation from the Brand squad or brand owner for alignment
- A target audience profile or persona document
- Whiteboard or collaboration tool for workshop exercises
- Squad members who will be writing stories should attend the calibration session

## Steps

1. **Gather Voice Inputs (Day 1)**
   - Collect the current brand guidelines, style guide, and any voice documentation.
   - Pull 10-15 published stories, case studies, or narrative assets as voice samples.
   - Identify 3-5 assets that stakeholders consider "ideal voice" and 3-5 that feel off-brand.
   - Note any feedback from the narrative audit on voice consistency.

2. **Define Voice Attributes (Workshop — 60 minutes)**
   - Assemble the calibration team: storytelling squad, Brand squad representative, and one stakeholder.
   - Brainstorm voice attributes as adjective pairs on a spectrum:
     - Formal ---- Casual
     - Authoritative ---- Approachable
     - Serious ---- Playful
     - Technical ---- Plain-spoken
     - Reserved ---- Enthusiastic
   - Place a marker on each spectrum where the brand should sit.
   - Select the top 4 attributes that define the voice (e.g., "Confident but not arrogant, warm but not saccharine").

3. **Create the Voice Card**
   - Document the 4 voice attributes in a one-page Voice Card.
   - For each attribute, provide:
     - A definition in one sentence.
     - A "this, not that" example (e.g., "We say 'Let's figure this out together,' not 'Per our policy, the resolution process is as follows'").
     - A sample paragraph demonstrating the attribute in a storytelling context.
   - Store the Voice Card in the docs directory.

4. **Map Tone Variations (Workshop — 30 minutes)**
   - Tone shifts based on context while voice stays constant.
   - Create a tone matrix with rows for common scenarios:
     - Customer success story
     - Product launch narrative
     - Crisis or recovery story
     - Thought leadership piece
     - Social media micro-story
     - Internal culture story
   - For each scenario, note how the tone adjusts (e.g., customer success = celebratory and specific; crisis = measured and empathetic).
   - Document which voice attributes amplify and which dial back per scenario.

5. **Build a Before/After Gallery**
   - Take 5 off-brand samples from the audit and rewrite them in the calibrated voice.
   - Place the original and rewritten versions side by side.
   - Annotate the changes: "Changed passive voice to active," "Replaced jargon with plain language," "Added a specific detail to create warmth."
   - Use this gallery as a training reference.

6. **Develop a Word Bank**
   - Create two lists: Preferred Words and Avoided Words.
   - Preferred Words: terms that reinforce voice attributes (e.g., "build," "partner," "unlock").
   - Avoided Words: terms that contradict the voice (e.g., "leverage," "synergize," "circle back").
   - Include brand-specific terminology with correct usage and capitalization.
   - Keep the word bank to a single page for quick reference.

7. **Test with a Live Brief**
   - Select an active story brief from the backlog.
   - Have two squad members independently draft the opening paragraph using the new voice calibration.
   - Compare the drafts: do they sound like the same brand?
   - Adjust the Voice Card or Word Bank if there are significant divergences.

8. **Calibrate for Agents**
   - Translate the Voice Card into agent-ready prompts.
   - For each storytelling agent, create a voice preamble that sets the voice attributes and tone context.
   - Test each agent with a standard prompt and evaluate the output against the Voice Card.
   - Iterate until agent outputs consistently match the calibrated voice.

9. **Get Stakeholder Sign-Off**
   - Present the Voice Card, Tone Matrix, Word Bank, and Before/After Gallery to the brand owner.
   - Incorporate feedback and finalize all documents.
   - Obtain written or recorded approval.

10. **Distribute and Train**
    - Share the finalized voice materials with the full storytelling squad.
    - Conduct a 30-minute training session walking through real examples.
    - Add voice calibration to the onboarding checklist (`scripts/onboard-new-storyteller.md`).
    - Post the Voice Card in the squad Slack channel for easy access.

11. **Schedule Recalibration**
    - Voice calibration should be reviewed every two quarters or after a major brand refresh.
    - Add a recalibration check to `scripts/quarterly-narrative-review.md`.
    - Track voice consistency scores in the quality gate process.

## Expected Output

- Voice Card: one-page document with 4 attributes, definitions, examples, and sample paragraphs.
- Tone Matrix: scenario-by-attribute grid showing tonal adjustments.
- Before/After Gallery: 5 rewritten assets with annotations.
- Word Bank: preferred and avoided word lists.
- Agent voice preambles for all 12 storytelling agents.
- Stakeholder approval documented.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Stakeholders disagree on voice attributes | Use the spectrum exercise to find compromise; escalate to brand owner for final call |
| Voice feels too generic after calibration | Add more "this, not that" examples with brand-specific nuance |
| Agents produce inconsistent voice despite preambles | Increase specificity in the preamble; include 2-3 example outputs for each agent |
| Writers struggle to adopt the new voice | Pair them with a buddy for the first 3 stories; use the Before/After Gallery in reviews |
| Voice needs to differ significantly across product lines | Create sub-Voice Cards that inherit the parent voice but adjust 1-2 attributes |
