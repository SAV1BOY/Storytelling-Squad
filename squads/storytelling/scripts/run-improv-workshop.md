# Run Improv Workshop

## Purpose

Facilitate an improvisation training session that sharpens storytelling instincts, builds creative confidence, and strengthens collaborative narrative-building skills within the squad. Improv exercises develop the ability to think on your feet, embrace unexpected directions, and construct stories in real time.

## Prerequisites

- 4-10 participants; mixed experience levels are ideal
- A physical room with space to stand and move, or a video call with cameras on
- 90-minute time block with no hard stop (allow 15 minutes of overflow)
- Facilitator has practiced each exercise at least once beforehand
- No specialized equipment needed; a timer and a whiteboard are helpful
- Psychological safety established: remind the group this is a judgment-free zone

## Steps

1. **Prepare the Session Plan (30 minutes before)**
   - Select 5-6 exercises from the menu below based on the team's experience level.
   - Order them from low-risk warm-ups to higher-challenge narrative exercises.
   - Prepare any prompts, word lists, or scenario cards needed.
   - Set up the room: clear the center, arrange chairs in a circle.

2. **Open the Session (5 minutes)**
   - Welcome participants and explain the purpose: "We're here to build storytelling muscle."
   - State the three rules of improv for storytelling:
     - "Yes, and..." — Accept and build on what others offer.
     - Specifics are interesting — Details make stories memorable.
     - Let yourself fail — Bad ideas lead to good ones.
   - Emphasize: there are no wrong answers.

3. **Warm-Up: Word Association Chain (5 minutes)**
   - Stand in a circle. One person says a word. The next person says the first word that comes to mind.
   - Go around the circle 3 times, increasing speed.
   - Debrief: "Notice how your brain made connections you didn't plan. That's the creative muscle we're warming up."

4. **Exercise 1: One-Word Story (10 minutes)**
   - The group tells a story one word at a time, going around the circle.
   - Facilitator provides the opening: "Once upon a time, in a..."
   - Run 2-3 rounds with different openings: a customer scenario, a product launch, a brand origin.
   - Debrief: "What made the story flow? What caused it to stumble?"

5. **Exercise 2: Yes, And Scene Building (10 minutes)**
   - Pair up. Person A makes a statement about a customer situation. Person B responds with "Yes, and..." adding a detail.
   - Alternate for 2 minutes per pair.
   - Then switch: Person B leads, Person A responds.
   - Debrief: "How did it feel to accept instead of redirect? What happened to the story?"

6. **Exercise 3: Emotional Replay (15 minutes)**
   - One person tells a short (60-second) story about a customer experience in a neutral tone.
   - The same person retells it three times with different emotions: excitement, empathy, urgency.
   - Audience observes how the same facts feel different depending on emotional framing.
   - Rotate: 3-4 people take turns.
   - Debrief: "Which version would resonate most with the target audience? Why?"

7. **Break (5 minutes)**
   - Stretch, hydrate, reset.

8. **Exercise 4: The Expert Game (15 minutes)**
   - One person is the "expert" on a made-up topic (e.g., "underwater brand strategy").
   - The audience asks questions. The expert must answer confidently and consistently.
   - After 3 minutes, a new expert takes over with a new topic.
   - Run 3 rounds.
   - Debrief: "How did commitment to the premise make the story believable? How does this apply to brand storytelling?"

9. **Exercise 5: Story Spine (15 minutes)**
   - Use the Kenn Adams Story Spine structure:
     - "Once upon a time..."
     - "Every day..."
     - "But one day..."
     - "Because of that..."
     - "Because of that..."
     - "Until finally..."
     - "And ever since then..."
   - Go around the circle, each person completing the next line.
   - Run with a marketing scenario: the "character" is a customer persona.
   - Run a second round with a brand-as-character scenario.
   - Debrief: "This is the backbone of every story framework we use."

10. **Exercise 6: Pitch Improv (10 minutes)**
    - Give each person a random product (e.g., "a pillow that tells bedtime stories") and 60 seconds to pitch it using a story.
    - Audience gives a thumbs up for the moment the pitch became compelling.
    - Run 4-5 pitches.
    - Debrief: "What made the compelling moment work? How quickly did the story hook land?"

11. **Close and Reflect (10 minutes)**
    - Gather the group back into the circle.
    - Each person shares one takeaway they will apply to their next story assignment.
    - Facilitator summarizes the common themes.
    - Announce the next workshop date (recommended: monthly cadence).

12. **Post-Session Documentation**
    - Record the exercises used and any modifications made.
    - Note participant feedback and suggested improvements.
    - Update the workshop log in the shared drive.
    - Share key takeaways in the `#storytelling-squad` Slack channel.

## Expected Output

- Team has practiced 5-6 improv exercises connected to storytelling skills.
- Each participant has one documented takeaway for their work.
- Facilitator has notes for improving the next workshop.
- Increased creative confidence and collaborative energy within the squad.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Participants feel self-conscious or resistant | Start with lower-risk written exercises; let reluctant members observe the first round |
| One person dominates the exercises | Use structured turn-taking (circle format) and time boxes |
| Exercises feel disconnected from real work | Use actual customer scenarios and brand contexts as prompts |
| Remote participants have difficulty with physical exercises | Adapt all exercises for seated, camera-on participation; use chat for written rounds |
| Group energy drops mid-session | Move the break earlier; swap in a high-energy exercise like Pitch Improv |
| Facilitator is new to improv | Practice each exercise with one partner before the session; keep the debrief prompts on a card |
