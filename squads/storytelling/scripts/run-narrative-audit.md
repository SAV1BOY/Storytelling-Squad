# Run Narrative Audit

## Purpose

Audit all existing brand narrative assets to assess consistency, quality, coverage, and alignment with current business objectives. The audit produces a gap analysis and a prioritized action plan for narrative improvements.

## Prerequisites

- Access to all repositories where narrative assets live: website copy, blog posts, case studies, social media, sales decks, email sequences, ad copy
- Current brand guidelines and voice documentation
- List of active campaigns and their associated messaging
- The storytelling squad's framework library for benchmarking
- Spreadsheet or database tool for tracking audit findings
- Dedicated 2-4 hours of uninterrupted audit time per asset category

## Steps

1. **Define Audit Scope (Day 1)**
   - List every channel and format where narrative assets exist.
   - Agree with stakeholders on which asset categories are in scope (e.g., website, blog, case studies, social, sales collateral, email).
   - Set the audit timeframe: assets published in the last 12 months unless otherwise specified.
   - Document exclusions and rationale.

2. **Create the Audit Inventory (Day 1-2)**
   - Build a master spreadsheet with columns: Asset Title, URL/Location, Format, Channel, Date Published, Owner, Framework Used, Target Audience, Status.
   - Populate the inventory by crawling each channel systematically.
   - Aim for completeness; partial inventories lead to blind spots.

3. **Establish Scoring Criteria (Day 2)**
   - Define 5-7 scoring dimensions aligned with `docs/quality-standards.md`:
     - Narrative structure (clear beginning, middle, end)
     - Emotional resonance (does it evoke feeling?)
     - Brand voice consistency (matches current guidelines?)
     - Audience relevance (speaks to the intended persona?)
     - Call-to-action clarity (what should the reader do next?)
     - Factual accuracy (data points verified?)
     - Visual-narrative alignment (do visuals support the story?)
   - Use a 1-5 scale for each dimension.

4. **Score Each Asset (Day 3-5)**
   - Review each asset and assign scores across all dimensions.
   - Add qualitative notes: what works, what does not, specific examples.
   - Flag assets that are outdated, factually incorrect, or off-brand for immediate action.
   - Aim for 15-20 assets per audit session to maintain consistency.

5. **Identify Patterns (Day 6)**
   - Aggregate scores by channel, format, and audience segment.
   - Look for systemic strengths: "Blog posts consistently score high on structure."
   - Look for systemic weaknesses: "Social copy lacks emotional resonance across the board."
   - Note framework usage patterns: which frameworks are overused, underused, or misapplied.

6. **Conduct Gap Analysis (Day 6-7)**
   - Compare the inventory against the ideal narrative portfolio:
     - Are all key personas represented with stories?
     - Are all stages of the buyer journey covered?
     - Are there stories for each major product or service?
   - Document coverage gaps as specific opportunities.

7. **Benchmark Against Competitors (Day 7)**
   - Select 2-3 competitor narrative assets per channel for comparison.
   - Score them using the same criteria for relative positioning.
   - Note tactics or approaches worth adopting or differentiating from.

8. **Prioritize Findings (Day 8)**
   - Rank issues by impact (audience reach multiplied by severity of the gap).
   - Categorize into: Quick Wins (fix in 1 week), Medium Efforts (1-4 weeks), Strategic Initiatives (1-3 months).
   - Assign owners and tentative deadlines for the top 10 items.

9. **Draft the Audit Report**
   - Executive summary: one paragraph on overall narrative health.
   - Scorecard: average scores by dimension and channel.
   - Top 5 strengths to amplify.
   - Top 5 weaknesses to address.
   - Gap analysis with specific story opportunities.
   - Prioritized action plan with owners and deadlines.

10. **Present to Stakeholders**
    - Schedule a 30-minute readout with squad leads and marketing leadership.
    - Focus on the action plan rather than the raw scores.
    - Solicit input on priority adjustments.
    - Confirm resource allocation for the top initiatives.

11. **Integrate with Quarterly Review**
    - Feed the audit findings into `scripts/quarterly-narrative-review.md`.
    - Update the story bank with flagged assets that need revision.
    - Schedule follow-up audits for high-priority channels at the next quarter.

12. **Archive the Audit**
    - Store the master spreadsheet, report, and presentation in the shared drive.
    - Name the folder: `narrative-audit_[YYYY-Q#]`.
    - Update `docs/changelog.md` with the audit completion date and key findings.

## Expected Output

- Master inventory spreadsheet with scores for every in-scope asset.
- Narrative Audit Report with executive summary, scorecard, gap analysis, and action plan.
- Prioritized list of 10+ improvements with owners and deadlines.
- Stakeholder alignment on top priorities.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Cannot access certain asset repositories | Request access through the asset owner; document the gap and audit later |
| Scoring feels subjective across reviewers | Calibrate by having two reviewers score the same 5 assets and aligning on discrepancies |
| Too many assets to audit in the timeframe | Prioritize by channel reach; sample 20% of low-traffic channels |
| Stakeholders disagree on priorities | Use the impact-effort matrix to depersonalize the ranking |
| Audit reveals brand voice inconsistencies | Escalate to the Brand squad and schedule a voice calibration session |
