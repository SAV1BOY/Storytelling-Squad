# Crisis Narrative Project

## Purpose
Prepare and deploy narrative communication during organizational crises — PR incidents, product failures, leadership changes, market disruptions.

---

## Project Overview
- **Duration:** Immediate (crisis response) + 2-4 weeks (recovery narrative)
- **Agents:** Brand Narrator (lead), Clarity Editor, Story Architect

---

## Phase 1: Immediate Response (Hours 1-24)

### The Initial Statement
Structure: Acknowledge → Empathize → Act → Commit
1. **Acknowledge** the situation directly — no euphemisms, no minimizing
2. **Empathize** with those affected — demonstrate you understand the impact
3. **Act** — state what you are doing right now to address the situation
4. **Commit** — state what you will do next and when you will update

### Clarity Rules for Crisis
- No jargon. Use the simplest possible language.
- No passive voice. "We made a mistake" not "Mistakes were made."
- No speculation. Only state what you know to be true.
- No blame deflection. Accept responsibility where appropriate.
- Be brief. The initial statement should be under 200 words.

### Channel Protocol
- Internal communication goes first — employees hear from you before the public
- Post the official statement on your website (not just social media)
- Respond to direct inquiries with the approved statement — no improvisation

---

## Phase 2: Ongoing Communication (Days 2-14)

### Update Cadence
- Daily updates for active crises
- Every-other-day updates for stabilized situations
- Weekly updates for recovery phase

### Update Structure
- What has happened since the last update
- What actions have been taken
- What the next steps are
- When the next update will come

### Narrative Tone
- Humble but not helpless
- Transparent but not reckless
- Forward-looking but not dismissive of the current pain
- Human — speak as a person, not a press release

---

## Phase 3: Recovery Narrative (Weeks 2-8)

### The Turning-Point Story
Once the crisis is stabilized, craft a turning-point narrative:
1. What happened (honest account)
2. What we learned (genuine insight, not PR spin)
3. What we changed (concrete actions taken)
4. Where we are now (measurable improvement)
5. What we are building toward (forward vision)

### Rebuilding Trust Through Story
- Share specific stories of how affected people were helped
- Show the process changes, not just promise them
- Give the team credit — humanize the recovery
- Use callbacks to the organization's core values

---

## Pre-Crisis Preparation

Build these before a crisis hits:
- [ ] Crisis communication templates (initial statement, update, recovery)
- [ ] Spokesperson identification and media training
- [ ] Internal communication chain established
- [ ] Holding statement drafted for common crisis scenarios
- [ ] Approval process defined (who approves what, how fast)
