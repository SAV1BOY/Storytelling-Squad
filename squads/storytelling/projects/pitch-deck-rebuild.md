# Pitch Deck Rebuild

## Project Overview

Rebuild a sales or investor pitch deck using narrative structure, replacing feature-list presentations with story-driven decks that persuade through emotional engagement and logical progression. The new deck takes the audience on a journey from problem through transformation to vision, making the case irresistible.

Most pitch decks fail because they are organized around the company, not around the audience's journey. This project reverses that structure.

---

## Scope

### In Scope

- Audit of existing pitch deck(s) and sales/investor materials
- Audience analysis (who the deck is presented to and what they care about)
- Narrative architecture for the deck (story arc, key beats, emotional journey)
- Slide-by-slide narrative copy and speaker notes
- Two deck versions: investor pitch and sales pitch (if applicable)
- Presenter coaching guide with delivery notes
- Supporting one-pager leave-behind with narrative alignment

### Out of Scope

- Slide visual design (narrative copy and structure only; design by Design Squad)
- Financial model creation (narrative framing of financials only)
- Live presenter coaching sessions (written guide only; coaching by request)
- CRM or sales tool integration

---

## Deliverables

1. **Deck Audit Report** — Analysis of current deck strengths, weaknesses, and narrative gaps
2. **Audience Insight Brief** — Summary of audience priorities, objections, and decision-making patterns
3. **Deck Narrative Blueprint** — Story arc, slide sequence, key beats, and emotional journey map
4. **Full Deck Copy** — Slide-by-slide headlines, body copy, and speaker notes (25-35 slides)
5. **Presenter Guide** — Delivery notes, transition scripts, Q&A preparation, and storytelling tips
6. **Leave-Behind One-Pager** — Narrative-aligned summary document for post-meeting follow-up
7. **Objection Narrative Bank** — Story-driven responses to the top 10 expected objections
8. **Deck Customization Guide** — Instructions for adapting the deck for different audience segments

## Workflow

### Step 1: Current Deck Audit

Review the existing deck slide by slide. Assess narrative flow, clarity of argument, emotional engagement, visual-verbal alignment, and audience focus.

### Step 2: Audience Deep-Dive

Interview sales reps and leadership about the audience: who they present to, what questions arise, where deals stall, what objections appear, and what resonates most.

### Step 3: Competitive Deck Review

Analyze available competitor pitch materials to understand the narrative landscape. Identify where the client's story can differentiate.

### Step 4: Narrative Architecture

Design the deck's story arc using a proven structure:
- **Act 1 (Slides 1-8)**: The world as it is. Establish the problem, the stakes, and why the status quo is failing.
- **Act 2 (Slides 9-20)**: The solution journey. Introduce the approach, demonstrate how it works, prove it with evidence.
- **Act 3 (Slides 21-30+)**: The future. Paint the vision, present the ask, and close with confidence.

### Step 5: Slide-by-Slide Copy

Write headlines, body text, and speaker notes for every slide. Each slide serves one purpose and advances the narrative by one beat.

### Step 6: Supporting Materials

Produce the presenter guide, leave-behind one-pager, objection bank, and customization guide.

### Step 7: Review and Rehearsal

Present the deck to stakeholders. Gather feedback on narrative flow, clarity, and persuasiveness. Observe a live rehearsal and refine based on delivery dynamics.

### Step 8: Final Polish

Incorporate all feedback. Finalize copy, speaker notes, and supporting materials. Hand off to design for visual production.

## Success Metrics

### Presentation Metrics

- Audience engagement during presentation (qualitative feedback from presenters)
- Meeting-to-next-step conversion rate (target: >15% improvement)
- Average presentation length (shorter is often better; target: within planned time)
- Presenter confidence score (self-reported by sales team)

### Sales Impact Metrics

- Win rate change for deals using the new deck
- Sales cycle length change
- Average deal size change (narrative decks often support premium positioning)
- Proposal request rate after pitch

### Investor Impact Metrics (if applicable)

- Meeting-to-term-sheet conversion rate
- Valuation achieved vs. target
- Follow-up meeting request rate
- Investor feedback quality
