# Crisis Narrative Response

## Project Overview

Develop and execute a narrative-driven crisis communication plan that protects brand trust through transparency, empathy, and coherent storytelling. This project provides the strategic narrative framework, messaging architecture, and content for responding to a brand-threatening crisis.

Crisis communication fails when organizations default to legal defensiveness or corporate jargon. The narrative approach centers on honest storytelling: what happened, why, what we are doing about it, and what the audience can expect.

---

## Scope

### In Scope

- Crisis narrative assessment (understanding the situation, stakeholders, and risks)
- Core crisis narrative development (the honest story of what happened)
- Stakeholder-specific messaging (customers, employees, investors, media, partners)
- Public statement and communications sequence
- Internal communication narrative
- Media response framework and spokesperson preparation
- Social media monitoring and response narrative guidelines
- Recovery narrative plan (the story of how the organization responds and rebuilds)

### Out of Scope

- Legal strategy and liability assessment (coordinate with legal counsel)
- Operational crisis management (logistics, safety, compliance)
- Technical remediation (cybersecurity, product recalls, etc.)
- Regulatory filings and formal disclosures

---

## Deliverables

1. **Crisis Narrative Assessment** — Situation analysis, stakeholder map, risk evaluation, and narrative landscape
2. **Core Crisis Statement** — The foundational public statement anchored in truth and accountability
3. **Stakeholder Message Matrix** — Tailored messaging for each stakeholder group with key narratives and talking points
4. **Communication Sequence Plan** — Timeline and channel plan for all crisis communications
5. **Internal Communication Package** — Employee briefing, FAQ, and manager talking points
6. **Media Response Kit** — Press statement, media Q&A, spokesperson briefing, and interview preparation
7. **Social Media Response Playbook** — Monitoring triggers, response templates, escalation protocols
8. **Recovery Narrative Plan** — The 30/60/90-day story of remediation, learning, and rebuilding trust
9. **Post-Crisis Review Template** — Framework for evaluating narrative response effectiveness

## Workflow

### Step 1: Rapid Situation Assessment

Within the first 6 hours: understand exactly what happened, who is affected, what the organization knows and does not know, and what the legal and operational teams are doing. Separate facts from speculation.

### Step 2: Stakeholder Mapping

Identify all affected stakeholders and rank by urgency: who needs to hear from us first? Typically: directly affected parties, employees, customers, investors, media, public. Never let media be the first to know.

### Step 3: Core Narrative Development

Craft the honest story. This must include:
- **Acknowledgment**: What happened, stated plainly
- **Accountability**: What the organization's responsibility is
- **Empathy**: Recognition of the impact on those affected
- **Action**: What is being done right now
- **Commitment**: What the organization commits to going forward

### Step 4: Public Statement

Issue the core public statement. It should be human, not corporate. Use first person. Name the specific issue. Avoid weasel words, passive voice, and deflection.

### Step 5: Stakeholder-Specific Communications

Adapt the core narrative for each stakeholder group, emphasizing what matters most to them. Customers need to know how they are affected and what to do. Employees need transparency and direction. Investors need an assessment of impact and mitigation.

### Step 6: Media Engagement

Prepare the spokesperson with the narrative, anticipated questions, bridging techniques, and boundary statements (what can and cannot be shared). Be proactive with media rather than reactive.

### Step 7: Ongoing Narrative Management

Monitor public response. Update the narrative as new information emerges. Maintain communication cadence: even when there is no new information, communicate that you are still working on it.

### Step 8: Recovery Narrative

At the 2-week mark, transition from crisis response to recovery storytelling. Tell the story of what the organization has learned, what has changed, and what the path forward looks like. This narrative continues for 30-90 days.

## Success Metrics

### Response Metrics (0-72 hours)

- Time to first public statement (target: within 24 hours)
- Stakeholder communication coverage (% of stakeholder groups reached within 72 hours)
- Message consistency across channels (no contradictions)
- Spokesperson performance (media feedback, message discipline)

### Management Metrics (3-14 days)

- Sentiment trend (stabilizing, improving, or deteriorating)
- Media coverage tone (factual, critical, sympathetic)
- Social media conversation volume and sentiment
- Employee confidence (pulse survey or manager feedback)
- Customer inquiry volume and resolution rate

### Recovery Metrics (14-90 days)

- Brand trust score recovery (pre-crisis baseline vs. current)
- Customer retention rate during and after crisis
- Employee retention and engagement during and after crisis
- Media narrative shift (from crisis coverage to recovery/redemption coverage)
- Time to trust recovery vs. industry benchmarks
