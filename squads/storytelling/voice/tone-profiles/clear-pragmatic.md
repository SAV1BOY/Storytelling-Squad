# Clear-Pragmatic Tone Profile

## Lineage
Drawn from Donald Miller's *Building a StoryBrand*, direct-response copywriting tradition,
Hemingway's prose economy, and the plain-language movement. Every sentence earns its place
by advancing clarity or action.

## Core Attributes

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **No fluff** | If a sentence doesn't move the reader toward understanding or action, delete it. |
| **Direct address** | Speak to "you." Make the reader the hero, not the brand. |
| **Benefit-first** | Lead with what the reader gains, not what the product does. |
| **Simple vocabulary** | Prefer "use" over "utilize," "help" over "facilitate," "start" over "commence." |
| **Short sentences** | Average sentence length: 12–16 words. Max: 25. |

## Voice Signature Phrases
- "Here's what that means for you."
- "The problem is simple."
- "Most [people/companies] get this wrong."
- "You don't need more [complexity]. You need a clear [plan/path/message]."
- "Let's make this concrete."
- "Step one. Step two. Step three."
- "That's it. Nothing fancy."

## Sentence-Level Patterns
1. **Problem → agitation → solution (PAS):** "Your message is confusing. Confused customers don't buy. Let's fix your message."
2. **If-then clarity:** "If you can't explain your offer in one sentence, your customers can't either."
3. **Numbered steps:** "Do three things: name the problem, show the plan, call them to action."
4. **Contrast pair:** "You don't need a bigger budget. You need a clearer story."

## Paragraph Rhythm
- One idea per paragraph.
- Lead sentence carries the weight. Supporting sentences add evidence or example.
- Close with a forward-leaning line that points to the next action.
- Generous white space. Dense paragraphs signal unclear thinking.

## The StoryBrand One-Liner Structure
1. **The Problem** — Name the pain the audience recognizes.
2. **The Solution** — Name what you offer (in plain terms).
3. **The Result** — Name the successful outcome.

> Example: "Most small businesses lose customers because their website is confusing.
> We help you clarify your message so customers engage, and your revenue grows."

## Word Replacement Guide

| Replace This | With This |
|-------------|-----------|
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help |
| leverage | use / build on |
| synergy | teamwork / alignment |
| ideate | brainstorm / think through |
| deliverables | what you get |
| bandwidth | time / capacity |
| paradigm | model / approach |
| optimize | improve |
| robust | strong |

## When to Use
- Landing pages, product descriptions, onboarding flows.
- Email subject lines and preview text.
- Any content where the reader is scanning, not studying.
- Internal communications that need to drive action.
- Sales pages where conversion matters more than brand poetry.

## When to Avoid
- Long-form thought leadership where nuance is the point.
- Luxury or premium brand contexts that rely on mystique.
- Academic or regulatory writing that requires precise jargon.
- Audiences who interpret simplicity as superficiality (some expert segments).

## Calibration Dials

| Dial | Low (1) | Mid (5) | High (10) |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Sentence length | Ultra-short fragments | Mixed short and medium | Slightly longer, still clear |
| Warmth | Clinical efficiency | Friendly and direct | Encouraging, almost coaching |
| Jargon tolerance | Zero jargon | Industry-standard terms OK | Some technical depth |
| CTA directness | Implied next step | Clear suggestion | Explicit imperative ("Buy now") |

## Example Paragraph (Calibration 6/10)
> Your customers have a problem. You have a solution. But between the problem and
> the solution, there's a wall of confusing language, cluttered design, and too many
> choices. Tear down the wall. Say one thing clearly. Show them the next step. When
> you do, they'll move.

## Pitfalls
- **Oversimplification.** Clear is not the same as shallow. Respect the reader's intelligence.
- **Robotic tone.** Short sentences can feel cold. Add occasional warmth or humor.
- **Repetitive structure.** Vary syntax even within a clear framework.
- **Bossiness.** Direct is not the same as commanding. Respect autonomy.
