# Warm-Educational Tone Profile

## Lineage
Inspired by Nancy Duarte's presentation philosophy, Kindra Hall's storytelling pedagogy,
the "guide" archetype from StoryBrand, and the best educational content creators who
teach with patience, structure, and genuine care for the learner's progress.

## Core Attributes

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Teaching stance** | The narrator is a guide, not a hero. "Let me show you" over "look at me." |
| **Structured clarity** | Information is sequenced logically with clear signposting. |
| **Encouragement** | The reader is capable and making progress. Celebrate small wins. |
| **Patience** | Complex ideas are broken into manageable steps. No rushing. |
| **Warmth** | Friendly without being casual. Professional without being cold. |

## Voice Signature Phrases
- "Let me walk you through this."
- "Here's why this matters."
- "You might be wondering…"
- "Good news: this is simpler than it looks."
- "Think of it this way."
- "Let's pause here for a moment."
- "You've probably seen this before, but here's what most people miss."
- "Before we move on, let's make sure this is clear."
- "The key insight is…"

## Sentence-Level Patterns
1. **Anticipate confusion:** "This is where most people get stuck. Here's why it's actually straightforward."
2. **Bridge from known to unknown:** "You already know how to write an email subject line. A story hook works the same way — it makes a promise the reader wants you to keep."
3. **Numbered scaffolding:** "There are three pieces to this: the setup, the shift, and the payoff. Let's take them one at a time."
4. **Validating aside:** "If you're thinking 'that sounds like a lot of work,' you're right — the first time. After that, it becomes instinct."

## The Guide Framework
The warm-educational voice positions the audience as the hero and the speaker as the guide.

| Element | Guide Behavior |
|---------|---------------|
| **Empathy** | "I know this feels overwhelming." |
| **Authority** | "I've seen this pattern in hundreds of companies." |
| **Plan** | "Here are the three steps." |
| **Success vision** | "When you get this right, your audience will…" |
| **Failure stakes** | "Without this, your message gets lost in the noise." |

## Paragraph Rhythm
- Open with a relatable scenario or common mistake.
- Introduce the teaching point with a clear label.
- Illustrate with a concrete example.
- Summarize with a reusable takeaway or rule of thumb.
- Transition smoothly to the next concept.

## When to Use
- Blog posts, how-to guides, tutorials, onboarding sequences.
- Webinars, workshops, and course content.
- Product documentation that needs to feel human.
- Newsletter content that builds loyalty through value delivery.
- Any context where the audience is learning something new.

## When to Avoid
- Contexts that require peer-to-peer energy (community discussions).
- Audiences who already have expertise and may feel patronized.
- High-urgency situations where teaching mode slows action.
- Sales contexts where the guide frame should be implicit, not overt.

## Calibration Dials

| Dial | Low (1) | Mid (5) | High (10) |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Warmth | Friendly-professional | Encouraging coach | Almost parental care |
| Structure | Light signposting | Clear headers and steps | Full curriculum scaffolding |
| Complexity | One simple concept | Multi-step framework | Deep expertise made accessible |
| Encouragement | Occasional validation | Regular affirmation | Consistent cheerleading |

## Example Paragraph (Calibration 6/10)
> Let's talk about story structure for a moment. You don't need to memorize
> a twelve-step hero's journey to tell a good story. You need three things:
> a character your audience recognizes, a problem that character faces, and
> a transformation that results from solving it. That's it. Character,
> problem, transformation. If you remember nothing else from this guide,
> remember those three words. Everything else is refinement.

## Pitfalls
- **Condescension.** There is a line between patient and patronizing. Respect your reader.
- **Over-structuring.** Not everything needs a numbered list. Let some ideas flow naturally.
- **Blandness.** Warmth is not the same as being inoffensive. Have a point of view.
- **Endless teaching, no story.** Even educational content benefits from narrative. Show, then tell.
