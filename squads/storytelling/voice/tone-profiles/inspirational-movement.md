# Inspirational-Movement Tone Profile

## Lineage
Drawn from Marshall Ganz's public narrative framework (Story of Self, Story of Us,
Story of Now), civil rights oratory, social movement rhetoric, and the tradition
of mobilizing language that turns individuals into collectives.

## Core Attributes

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Mobilizing energy** | Language that moves people from passive agreement to active participation. |
| **Values-driven** | The narrative is anchored in shared values, not features or benefits. |
| **Urgency** | The moment demands action. Waiting is a choice with consequences. |
| **Collective identity** | "We" is the protagonist. The movement is bigger than any individual. |
| **Moral clarity** | Right and wrong are named plainly. Ambiguity serves the status quo. |

## Voice Signature Phrases
- "This is our moment."
- "We didn't choose this fight. But we will not walk away from it."
- "The question is not whether we can. The question is whether we will."
- "Every one of us has a story. Together, those stories become a movement."
- "Not someday. Today."
- "They told us it couldn't be done. They were describing their own limits, not ours."
- "When we look back at this moment, what will we say we did?"

## The Ganz Framework Applied

### Story of Self
Why *I* was called to this work. Personal experience that reveals values.
> "I became an educator because I watched my mother teach herself to read at
> forty-three. She sat at our kitchen table every night with a dictionary and
> a flashlight, and she never once complained. That's where I learned that
> learning is an act of courage."

### Story of Us
Why *we* share this calling. Shared experience that creates community identity.
> "Every person in this room has felt the frustration of watching talent go
> unrecognized because the system wasn't built for people like us. That
> frustration is not weakness. It is fuel."

### Story of Now
Why *now* is the moment to act. Urgency, choice, and a concrete ask.
> "The policy changes in sixty days. Sixty days. We can spend those days
> hoping someone else speaks up, or we can spend them making sure our
> voices are impossible to ignore. I'm asking you to choose the second."

## Sentence-Level Patterns
1. **Anaphora for momentum:** "We are the teachers. We are the builders. We are the ones they said would never make it."
2. **Call and response rhythm:** "They said no. We said watch."
3. **Values naming:** "This is about dignity. This is about fairness. This is about the future we are building for people who aren't in this room yet."
4. **Concrete ask after emotional peak:** "So here is what I'm asking: show up on Thursday. Bring one person. Tell your story."

## Paragraph Rhythm
- Open with shared experience or value.
- Escalate through repetition and intensification.
- Peak with emotional or moral clarity.
- Land on a specific, actionable ask.
- Silence after the ask. Let it sit.

## When to Use
- Nonprofit fundraising and volunteer recruitment.
- Internal alignment during organizational change.
- Brand campaigns that position customers as part of a movement.
- Community building, advocacy, and social impact storytelling.
- Kickoff speeches, rally points, campaign launches.

## When to Avoid
- Transactional contexts (e-commerce product pages, feature lists).
- Audiences who are skeptical of collective language (highly individualist segments).
- Situations where urgency feels manufactured or exploitative.
- Low-stakes contexts where movement language would feel disproportionate.

## Calibration Dials

| Dial | Low (1) | Mid (5) | High (10) |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Urgency | Gentle invitation | Clear time pressure | "Now or never" intensity |
| Collective scope | Small team | Community or industry | Global or generational |
| Emotional register | Hopeful and warm | Passionate and resolute | Fierce and unwavering |
| Action specificity | General aspiration | Named action | Exact date, place, commitment |

## Example Paragraph (Calibration 8/10)
> Twelve years ago, there were zero maternal health clinics in this district.
> Zero. Mothers traveled four hours on unpaved roads, in labor, hoping they
> would arrive in time. Today there are three clinics — not because the
> government built them, but because women in this community decided that
> hoping was not a strategy. They organized. They fundraised. They built.
> And now I'm standing in one of those clinics asking you: what will *your*
> community look like in twelve years if you start building today?

## Pitfalls
- **Performative urgency.** If the moment isn't genuinely urgent, the audience will feel manipulated.
- **Empty "we."** Collective language without genuine shared identity rings hollow.
- **All inspiration, no instruction.** Movement language without a clear ask produces applause, not action.
- **Savior framing.** The community is the hero, not the speaker.
