# Epic-Mythic Tone Profile

## Lineage
Rooted in Joseph Campbell's *Hero with a Thousand Faces*, classical mythology,
Tolkien's world-building, and the narrative voice of documentary epics. This tone
treats the subject as part of a larger, timeless pattern.

## Core Attributes

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Grandeur** | Elevated diction, sweeping scope, language that reaches upward. |
| **Universality** | The story is not about one person — it is about *every* person who has faced this threshold. |
| **Archetypal framing** | Characters map to the Hero, the Mentor, the Threshold Guardian, the Shadow. |
| **Timelessness** | Present events echo ancient ones. "This has always been true." |
| **Inevitability** | The narrative has weight, momentum, a sense of destiny. |

## Voice Signature Phrases
- "There comes a moment in every [role]'s journey when…"
- "This is the oldest story there is."
- "They stood at the threshold."
- "What they didn't know — what no one could have known —"
- "And so it began."
- "The world before, and the world after."
- "Not because it was easy. Because it was necessary."

## Sentence-Level Patterns
1. **Tricolon (rule of three):** "They came with nothing. They built with everything. They left behind a world."
2. **Anaphora (repeated opening):** "There was a time when the market didn't exist. There was a time when no one believed. There was a time before the story you're about to hear."
3. **Long periodic sentence:** "Through three failed prototypes, two near-bankruptcies, and one phone call at 4 a.m. that almost ended it all, the team held."
4. **Short declarative after buildup:** "Everything changed."

## Paragraph Rhythm
- Open with a wide-angle establishing shot (context, era, stakes).
- Narrow to a pivotal character or decision.
- Expand again to consequence and legacy.
- Close with a resonant, almost aphoristic line.

## Archetypal Story Beats
1. **The Ordinary World** — Establish the known, the comfortable, the "before."
2. **The Call** — An event disrupts equilibrium. A question demands an answer.
3. **The Threshold** — The protagonist commits, crossing from safety into the unknown.
4. **Trials and Allies** — Obstacles test resolve; mentors and companions appear.
5. **The Ordeal** — The darkest moment, the cave, the near-defeat.
6. **The Transformation** — Something is learned, earned, or sacrificed.
7. **The Return** — The hero brings the gift back to the ordinary world.

## When to Use
- Brand origin stories for companies with ambitious missions.
- Keynote openings and closings.
- Fundraising narratives for movements or nonprofits.
- Product launches framed as paradigm shifts.
- Video scripts for documentaries or brand films.

## When to Avoid
- Quick social media posts (the tone needs room to breathe).
- Technical documentation or support content.
- Audiences skeptical of grandiosity (developer communities, for instance).
- Low-stakes contexts where epic tone would feel disproportionate.

## Calibration Dials

| Dial | Low (1) | Mid (5) | High (10) |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Scope | One person's day | A company's arc | Civilization-level narrative |
| Diction | Slightly elevated | Formal and rhythmic | Near-poetic, incantatory |
| Archetype visibility | Subtle subtext | Named roles (mentor, challenge) | Explicit mythic framing |
| Emotional register | Inspirational warmth | Awe and reverence | Sublime, almost sacred |

## Example Paragraph (Calibration 8/10)
> For three hundred years, the problem had no name. Builders worked around it.
> Engineers compensated for it. Entire industries were shaped — deformed — by its
> invisible gravity. Then, in a basement lab in São Paulo, two people who had
> never published a paper asked a question no one with credentials would risk:
> *What if the constraint itself is wrong?* That question became a prototype.
> The prototype became a platform. And the platform became the ground beneath
> an industry that had forgotten it was standing on sand.

## Pitfalls
- **Pomposity.** Grandeur without substance is parody. Earn the epic with real stakes.
- **Vagueness masquerading as universality.** Specific details anchor the myth.
- **Hero worship.** Even in epic tone, the hero should be human, not a deity.
- **Pacing drag.** Epic does not mean slow. Vary sentence length relentlessly.
