# Data-Informed Storyteller Tone Profile

## Lineage
Drawn from the best business storytelling — Harvard Business Review case narratives,
McKinsey's data-driven insights, Hans Rosling's *Factfulness*, and the practice of
turning numbers into narratives without sacrificing rigor.

## Core Attributes

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Credibility first** | Claims are supported by evidence. Sources are cited or citable. |
| **Evidence-based reasoning** | Data precedes opinion. Insight emerges from patterns, not assumptions. |
| **Insight-driven narrative** | Numbers alone are not the story — the story is what the numbers reveal. |
| **Measured confidence** | Strong claims have strong evidence. Weak evidence gets appropriate hedging. |
| **Translation skill** | Complex data is made accessible without being dumbed down. |

## Voice Signature Phrases
- "The data tells an interesting story."
- "Here's what the numbers actually show."
- "When we looked at [N] companies over [time], a pattern emerged."
- "Conventional wisdom says [X]. The evidence says something different."
- "Let me put that in context."
- "That's not an opinion. That's what [N]% of respondents reported."
- "The outlier is where the story gets interesting."
- "Correlation, yes. But the causal mechanism is…"

## Sentence-Level Patterns
1. **Data anchor then narrative:** "Of the 2,400 product launches we tracked, only 14% used any form of customer story in their go-to-market. Those 14% had a 3.2x higher conversion rate at day 30. The story isn't that storytelling works. The story is that 86% of teams still don't use it."
2. **Scale translation:** "A 12% increase in retention sounds modest until you calculate what it means over five years: $4.2 million in recovered revenue for a mid-market SaaS company."
3. **Humanized data point:** "Behind that 34% churn number are real people — a VP of Marketing who championed the tool, fought for the budget, and couldn't show ROI by quarter three."
4. **Counter-intuitive finding:** "The highest-performing sales teams in our dataset spent 40% less time on product demos and 60% more time asking questions. Less telling, more listening."

## Data-to-Story Framework

### Step 1: Find the Tension
Every good data story has a gap between expectation and reality.
> Expected: Longer sales pages convert better. Found: Pages over 3,000 words
> had 22% lower conversion than pages at 1,200–1,800 words.

### Step 2: Name the Character
Who is affected by this data? Make the number a person.
> "The SaaS founder spending $40K/month on content that nobody reads past
> the second paragraph."

### Step 3: Show the Journey
How did we go from not knowing to knowing? What did we test?
> "We A/B tested 147 landing pages across 12 industries over 9 months."

### Step 4: Deliver the Insight
What should the reader do differently now?
> "Cut your sales page to under 2,000 words. Front-load the customer story.
> Move the pricing table up."

### Step 5: Acknowledge Limitations
Credibility requires honesty about what the data doesn't show.
> "This data is from B2B SaaS. Consumer e-commerce may behave differently."

## When to Use
- White papers, research reports, case studies.
- Board presentations and executive briefings.
- Content marketing for analytical audiences (finance, engineering, data science).
- Product marketing where proof is the primary persuasion lever.
- Investor updates and quarterly reviews.

## When to Avoid
- Emotional-first contexts (nonprofits appealing to compassion).
- Early awareness content where the audience doesn't yet care about proof.
- Social media posts where data density kills engagement.
- Creative brand campaigns prioritizing feeling over fact.

## Calibration Dials

| Dial | Low (1) | Mid (5) | High (10) |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Data density | One anchoring stat | Several supporting stats | Full dataset narrative |
| Narrative warmth | Cool and analytical | Balanced data + human story | Data as backdrop for character |
| Technical depth | Accessible to all | Requires domain familiarity | Specialist audience |
| Hedging level | Confident assertions | Appropriate caveats | Full academic caution |

## Example Paragraph (Calibration 6/10)
> Last year, we analyzed the onboarding emails of 300 SaaS companies. The
> average sequence was seven emails over fourteen days. But here's what
> surprised us: the companies with the highest activation rates (top decile)
> didn't send more emails. They sent fewer — four, on average — and every
> single one included a customer story. Not a testimonial. A story: a named
> person, a specific problem, a measurable outcome. The data suggests that
> narrative density matters more than send frequency.

## Pitfalls
- **Death by chart.** Too many numbers numb the reader. Choose the one stat that matters most.
- **False precision.** Saying "73.4% of customers" when the sample is 30 people erodes trust.
- **Data without story.** A report is not a narrative. Find the human thread.
- **Cherry-picking.** Present the full picture, including inconvenient findings.
