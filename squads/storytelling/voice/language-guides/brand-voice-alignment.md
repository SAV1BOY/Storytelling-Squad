# Brand Voice Alignment Guide

## Purpose
This guide helps align story voice with an existing brand voice. When the storytelling
squad creates content for a brand (internal or client), the narrative voice must feel
native to that brand while still leveraging storytelling best practices.

## The Alignment Process

### Step 1: Audit the Existing Brand Voice
Before writing a single word of story, answer these questions:

1. **What three adjectives define the brand voice?** (e.g., "bold, warm, precise")
2. **What is the brand's default person?** (first, second, third?)
3. **What reading level does the brand target?**
4. **What words does the brand always use?** (branded terms, tagline language)
5. **What words does the brand never use?** (competitor terms, banned language)
6. **What is the brand's relationship to humor?** (never, occasionally, frequently)
7. **What is the brand's relationship to vulnerability?** (never, carefully, openly)
8. **Who is the brand's "character" — if the brand were a person at a dinner party, how would they behave?**

### Step 2: Map Brand Voice to Tone Profiles
Use the squad's tone profiles as a starting point and blend as needed.

| Brand Archetype | Primary Tone Profile | Secondary Blend |
|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Innovator / Disruptor | Provocative-Challenger | Data-Informed |
| Caregiver / Educator | Warm-Educational | Intimate-Personal |
| Hero / Achiever | Epic-Mythic | Clear-Pragmatic |
| Sage / Expert | Data-Informed | Clear-Pragmatic |
| Jester / Creator | Playful-Spontaneous | Intimate-Personal |
| Activist / Rebel | Inspirational-Movement | Provocative-Challenger |
| Everyperson / Companion | Clear-Pragmatic | Warm-Educational |

### Step 3: Create a Story Voice Brief
For each project, produce a one-page brief that includes:

**Story Voice Brief Template:**
```
Brand: [Name]
Project: [Campaign / piece type]
Primary tone: [From tone profiles]
Secondary tone: [Blend]
Audience: [Segment]
Emotional target: [What should the reader feel after reading?]
Vocabulary guardrails: [Must-use terms / banned terms]
Example sentence in-voice: [Write one]
Anti-example: [Write a sentence that is NOT in-voice]
```

### Step 4: Write a Voice Sample
Before creating full content, write a 100-word voice sample and get stakeholder
approval. This prevents full-draft rewrites.

### Step 5: Calibrate with Feedback
After the first draft, score it against the brand voice adjectives:
- Does it sound [adjective 1]? Where specifically?
- Does it sound [adjective 2]? Where specifically?
- Does it sound [adjective 3]? Where specifically?
- Are there any sentences that break voice? Flag them.

## Common Alignment Challenges

### Challenge: Brand voice is corporate; story voice needs warmth
**Solution:** Keep vocabulary corporate but add narrative structure. Use third-person
stories about customers rather than first-person vulnerability. Data-informed tone
with warm transitions.

### Challenge: Brand voice is playful; story needs gravitas
**Solution:** Open with playful energy, then shift register for the serious beat. Signal
the shift explicitly: "Okay, real talk for a second." Return to playful for the close.

### Challenge: Brand is new and voice is undefined
**Solution:** Use this as an opportunity. Draft three voice samples in different tones.
Let the brand team react. Their reactions will reveal the voice faster than a workshop.

### Challenge: Multiple sub-brands with different voices
**Solution:** Create a shared "voice foundation" (values, banned words, reading level)
and allow each sub-brand to layer its own tone profile on top.

## Cross-Squad Coordination
When the storytelling squad produces content that the brand, content, or design
squads will also touch:
1. Share the Story Voice Brief before drafting.
2. Include voice notes in the content handoff document.
3. Flag sentences that are "voice-critical" — where changing wording changes meaning.
4. Offer a 15-minute voice walkthrough for editors unfamiliar with the project tone.

## Voice Consistency Across Formats

| Format | Voice Adaptation |
|--------|-----------------|
| Long-form blog | Full voice expression; room for all tone elements |
| Social post | Amplify one voice trait; strip everything else |
| Email subject | Most energetic version of the voice in 6–10 words |
| Video script | Spoken cadence; shorter sentences; more contractions |
| Sales deck | Voice in headlines; neutral in body; voice in speaker notes |
| Ad copy | Voice distilled to its single most distinctive trait |

## Quality Check Questions
Before delivering any story content, ask:
1. If I removed the logo, would the audience still recognize the brand?
2. Does this story sound like it belongs on this brand's website?
3. Would a brand team member read this aloud comfortably?
4. Is the story serving the brand, or is the brand serving the story?
