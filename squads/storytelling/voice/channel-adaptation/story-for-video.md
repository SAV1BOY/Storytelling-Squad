# Story Adaptation: Video

## Purpose
Adapt narrative storytelling for video content — from 15-second social clips to
30-minute brand documentaries. Video is the most immersive storytelling medium,
but also the least forgiving of weak structure.

## The Video Storytelling Equation
**Great video story = Strong hook + Clear structure + Emotional pacing + Visual proof + Timed CTA**

## The 3-Second Rule
Viewers decide to stay or leave in the first 3 seconds. Those 3 seconds must accomplish
at least one of:
- Show something visually unexpected
- State something surprising or counterintuitive
- Create an emotional reaction (laughter, shock, curiosity)
- Pose a question the viewer needs answered

**Strong 3-second hooks:**
- "This company was 48 hours from bankruptcy." [cut to: thriving office]
- "What if I told you this $10M product started as a joke?"
- [Visual: person crying at desk] "This is the moment Sarah knew she had to leave."
- "Everything in this video is wrong. Keep watching and I'll show you why."

## Structure by Video Length

### 15–30 Seconds (Social / Ad)
- **0–3s:** Hook (visual + verbal)
- **3–15s:** One story beat (the problem OR the transformation — not both)
- **15–25s:** Proof or emotional payoff
- **25–30s:** CTA (text overlay + verbal)

### 60–90 Seconds (Reel / Short)
- **0–3s:** Hook
- **3–20s:** Setup (character + problem)
- **20–50s:** Journey (attempt + turning point)
- **50–75s:** Resolution (outcome + proof)
- **75–90s:** Takeaway + CTA

### 3–5 Minutes (YouTube / Brand Film)
- **0–15s:** Cold open (start in the middle of the most dramatic moment)
- **15–45s:** Rewind to the beginning (context, character, ordinary world)
- **45s–2m:** Rising action (challenge, attempts, escalation)
- **2m–3m30s:** Climax (the turning point, the decision, the breakthrough)
- **3m30s–4m30s:** Resolution (outcomes, data, emotional payoff)
- **4m30s–5m:** Reflection + CTA

### 10–30 Minutes (Documentary / Webinar Story)
- **0–2m:** Cold open with the most compelling scene
- **2–5m:** Act I — The world before (character, context, stakes)
- **5–12m:** Act II — The struggle (obstacles, failures, allies, enemies)
- **12–18m:** Act II turning point — The discovery (the insight that changes everything)
- **18–25m:** Act III — The new world (results, proof, transformation)
- **25–30m:** Epilogue + CTA

## Pacing and Rhythm

### Visual Pacing Rules
- **Cut every 3–5 seconds** in social video. Never hold a static shot longer.
- **Cut every 5–10 seconds** in brand films.
- **Match cuts to narrative beats.** New idea = new shot.
- **Use B-roll to illustrate, not decorate.** Every visual should advance the story.

### Audio Pacing Rules
- **Speak at 150–170 words per minute** for clarity (natural speech is ~130, which feels slow on video).
- **Pause before key moments.** Silence is the most underused tool in video.
- **Music follows emotion, not the other way around.** Don't let the soundtrack tell the audience what to feel. Let the story do it, and let music amplify.

### The Emotional Pacing Curve
```
Engagement
^
|     /\        /\
|    /  \      /  \     /\
|   /    \    /    \   /  \
|  /      \  /      \ /    \___
| /        \/        \
|/
+---------------------------------> Time
Hook  Setup  Dip  Build  Climax  Resolution
```

Never let engagement flatline. Alternate between tension and release.

## Script Writing for Video

### Write for the Ear
- Use contractions. Always.
- Keep sentences to 10–15 words maximum.
- Read every line aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.
- Write as you speak, then tighten.

### Write for the Eye
- After writing dialogue/narration, add a visual column.
- For every line, ask: "What is the viewer SEEING while hearing this?"
- Visual and audio should complement, not duplicate.

| Audio | Visual |
|-------|--------|
| "We started with nothing." | Empty garage, single desk, one laptop. |
| "Within six months, everything changed." | Time-lapse: garage fills with people, equipment, whiteboards. |
| "But the real breakthrough wasn't the product." | Close-up: founder's face, quiet moment. |
| "It was the first customer who said, 'This saved my business.'" | Cut to: real customer, in their own space, speaking. |

## CTA Timing in Video

| Video Length | CTA Placement | CTA Type |
|-------------|---------------|----------|
| 15–30s | Last 5 seconds | Text overlay + voice |
| 60–90s | Last 10 seconds | Verbal + text overlay |
| 3–5m | At 75% mark AND end | Mid-roll soft CTA + end hard CTA |
| 10–30m | Multiple touchpoints | Subscribe prompt + end CTA |
