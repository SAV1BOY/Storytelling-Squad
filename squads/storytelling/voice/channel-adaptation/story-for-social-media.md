# Story Adaptation: Social Media

## Purpose
Adapt narrative storytelling for social media formats — threads, carousels, reels,
and single posts — without losing the story's core structure or emotional impact.

## Universal Social Storytelling Principles

1. **The first line is everything.** You have 1–2 seconds to earn the scroll-stop. Lead with tension, surprise, or a specific number.
2. **One story per post.** Social is not the place for multi-layered narratives. One character, one problem, one transformation.
3. **Write for thumbs, not eyes.** Short paragraphs. Line breaks between thoughts. Visual breathing room.
4. **End with forward energy.** Every post should leave the reader wanting to do something: comment, share, save, click.

## Format-Specific Guidance

### Twitter/X Thread (5–12 posts)

**Structure:**
- **Tweet 1 (Hook):** Surprising claim, specific outcome, or "here's what happened when…"
- **Tweets 2–3 (Setup):** The before state. The problem. The stakes.
- **Tweets 4–8 (Journey):** What was tried. What failed. What changed.
- **Tweet 9–10 (Transformation):** The result. The data. The outcome.
- **Tweet 11 (Lesson):** The reusable insight.
- **Tweet 12 (CTA):** Follow, retweet, reply, link.

**Example Hook Tweets:**
- "In 2019, I was $47,000 in debt and selling my furniture to make rent. Last month my company did $2.1M in revenue. Here's exactly what changed (thread):"
- "The worst pitch I ever gave led to our biggest deal. Let me explain."
- "3 things I'd do differently if I were starting a SaaS company today:"

**Thread-Specific Tips:**
- Number your tweets for readability ("1/12" or "1.").
- Each tweet should be self-contained AND advance the narrative.
- Add a "bookmark this" prompt midway through for engagement signal.
- Pin the thread or add it to a highlight.

### LinkedIn Post (Single Post, 1300 characters max for above-fold)

**Structure:**
- **Line 1:** Hook (pattern interrupt or bold claim).
- **Lines 2–3:** Setup (context, character, problem).
- **Lines 4–8:** Journey (what happened, in compressed form).
- **Lines 9–10:** Insight (the lesson or reframe).
- **Line 11:** CTA (question for comments).

**Example:**
> I got fired on a Tuesday.
>
> Not "let go." Not "restructured." Fired.
>
> My manager said, "It's not personal." It was absolutely personal — I had
> disagreed with the VP in a meeting that morning.
>
> For three months, I didn't tell anyone. I said I was "consulting."
>
> Then a former colleague called and said, "I heard. Same thing happened to me
> last year. Want to build something together?"
>
> That phone call became a company. That company now employs 34 people.
>
> The lesson isn't "getting fired is a blessing." That's too neat.
>
> The lesson is: the people who know your worst moments are often the ones who
> help you build your best ones.
>
> Has a professional setback ever led to something unexpected?

### Instagram Carousel (5–10 slides)

**Structure:**
- **Slide 1 (Cover):** Bold hook text + visual. Must communicate the topic in 3–5 words.
- **Slides 2–3 (Problem):** Name the pain. Show the "before."
- **Slides 4–7 (Framework/Journey):** Steps, insights, or narrative beats.
- **Slide 8–9 (Result):** The transformation or key takeaway.
- **Slide 10 (CTA):** Save, share, follow, comment, link in bio.

**Design Principles:**
- One idea per slide. Max 30 words per slide.
- Consistent color palette and typography.
- Use the "swipe to learn more" curiosity gap between slides.
- Narrative captions under the carousel add depth for engaged readers.

### Short-Form Video (Reels / TikTok / Shorts, 15–90 seconds)

**Structure:**
- **0–3 sec (Hook):** Visual or verbal pattern interrupt. "The worst advice I ever got…"
- **3–15 sec (Setup):** The context. Keep it tight. One sentence of backstory.
- **15–45 sec (Journey):** The conflict, attempt, or discovery. Show, don't just tell.
- **45–60 sec (Payoff):** The result, twist, or insight.
- **60–90 sec (CTA):** "Follow for more" or "Comment your experience."

**Video-Specific Tips:**
- Speak to the camera as if to one person, not an audience.
- Use captions — 80%+ of social video is watched without sound.
- Cut every pause. Social video should feel slightly faster than natural speech.
- Hook visually AND verbally in the first second.

## Platform-Specific Tone Adjustments

| Platform | Tone | Formality | Humor | Hashtags |
|----------|------|-----------|-------|----------|
| Twitter/X | Sharp, concise, opinionated | Casual to professional | Welcome | 0–2 |
| LinkedIn | Professional, reflective, value-adding | Professional | Occasional | 3–5 |
| Instagram | Visual, aspirational, warm | Casual | Welcome | 5–15 (in first comment) |
| TikTok | Raw, authentic, fast | Very casual | Expected | 3–5 |
| Facebook | Conversational, community-oriented | Casual to professional | Welcome | 0–2 |

## Metrics That Matter for Story Posts

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|--------|------------------|
| Save rate | "This was useful enough to return to" |
| Share rate | "This resonated enough to pass along" |
| Comment depth | "This sparked genuine engagement" |
| Completion rate (video) | "The story held attention" |
| Profile visits after post | "The story made them curious about the author" |
