# Story Adaptation: Advertising

## Purpose
Adapt narrative storytelling for advertising — where every second costs money, attention
is hostile, and the story must sell without feeling like a sales pitch.

## The Advertising Storytelling Paradox
Ads interrupt. Stories attract. The art of story-driven advertising is making the
interruption feel like a gift.

## The 3-Second Hook Rule
In paid media, you are paying for every second of attention. If the first 3 seconds
don't earn the next 3, the money is wasted.

### Hook Types That Work in Ads

**1. The Unexpected Visual**
Show something that doesn't belong. A fish in an office. A CEO mopping floors. A product
in an impossible context. The brain processes novelty before logic.

**2. The Bold Claim**
"This $12 product replaced my $200/month subscription."
"97% of marketers get this wrong."
"The ad you're about to skip will save you $10,000."

**3. The Conflict Opener**
"My co-founder and I haven't spoken in six months. Here's why."
"The doctor told her to stop. She didn't listen."
"They said we'd fail. They were right — at first."

**4. The Direct Address**
"Hey, [target audience]. Stop scrolling. This is for you."
"If you've ever [specific frustration], watch this."
"You're about to make a mistake. Let me help."

**5. The Mid-Scene Start**
Don't set up. Start in the middle.
[Video opens: Person staring at laptop, tears in eyes]
"It was 3 a.m. and I'd just lost everything."

## Ad Story Structures by Format

### 15-Second Ad (Instagram/TikTok)
- **0–2s:** Hook (visual + text overlay)
- **2–8s:** One story beat (problem or transformation)
- **8–13s:** Proof (result, testimonial snippet, data)
- **13–15s:** CTA + brand

**Example script:**
> [0s: Woman staring at empty inbox] "Zero replies."
> [3s: Cut to her rewriting the email] "So I rewrote it using [Product]."
> [7s: Inbox filling with responses] "47 replies in 24 hours."
> [11s: Her smiling at camera] "Try it free."
> [13s: Logo + CTA button]

### 30-Second Ad (YouTube Pre-Roll / TV)
- **0–3s:** Hook (earn the skip-proof)
- **3–10s:** Setup (character + problem)
- **10–20s:** Turn (discovery of solution + proof)
- **20–27s:** Outcome (transformation + data)
- **27–30s:** CTA + brand

### 60-Second Ad (YouTube / Facebook / CTV)
- **0–5s:** Hook (dramatic opening)
- **5–15s:** Character + stakes
- **15–30s:** Journey (attempt, failure, discovery)
- **30–45s:** Transformation (with proof)
- **45–55s:** Emotional payoff
- **55–60s:** CTA + brand

### Long-Form Ad (2–5 Minutes, YouTube / Branded Content)
Full story structure with the product woven into the narrative:
1. Cold open: the most dramatic moment.
2. Rewind: how did we get here?
3. The struggle: real obstacles, real emotion.
4. The discovery: the product enters naturally as part of the solution.
5. The transformation: measurable results + emotional resolution.
6. The invitation: CTA that feels like the natural next chapter.

## Platform-Specific Rules

### Facebook / Instagram
- Square (1:1) or vertical (4:5 / 9:16) formats.
- Sound-off by default — use captions and text overlays.
- Front-load the hook in the first 1–2 seconds.
- Carousel ads can tell a sequential story across slides.
- UGC-style (user-generated content) outperforms polished production.

### YouTube
- 5-second skip window: hook must be irresistible before second 5.
- Pre-roll: treat it as a micro-story, not a brand announcement.
- Longer formats (2–5 min) perform well when the story is genuinely engaging.
- End screens and cards can extend the narrative journey.

### TikTok
- Native-feeling content outperforms branded-looking content.
- Use trending sounds and formats but adapt them to your story.
- Creator partnerships for story ads feel more authentic.
- Comment section is part of the story — seed it with questions.

### LinkedIn
- Professional context = problem/solution stories win.
- Single-image ads with story-driven copy outperform generic brand ads.
- Video testimonials with narrative structure over talking-head endorsements.

## The Product Integration Spectrum

| Level | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Invisible** | Product is never shown; brand appears only at end | Emotional brand film |
| **Background** | Product is visible but not the focus | Product on desk in a workspace story |
| **Supporting** | Product helps the character but isn't the hero | Character uses tool to solve problem |
| **Central** | Product is the turning point of the story | "Then I found [Product]" |
| **Demo** | Product functionality is the story | Screen recording as narrative |

Best practice: Level 2–3 (Supporting to Central) for story ads. The character is the hero; the product is the mentor.

## Copy Formulas for Story Ads

**PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve):**
> Tired of [problem]? It gets worse: [agitation]. [Product] fixes it in [timeframe].

**BAB (Before-After-Bridge):**
> Before: [painful state]. After: [desired state]. Bridge: [Product] gets you there.

**Star-Story-Solution:**
> Meet [character]. [Their story of struggle]. [Product] was the solution.
