# Story Adaptation: Email

## Purpose
Adapt narrative storytelling for email — the most personal digital channel. Email
is where stories build trust, nurture relationships, and drive action over time.

## The Email Storytelling Advantage
Email is the only marketing channel where the audience has explicitly asked to hear
from you. This creates both opportunity and obligation: they expect value, and they
will unsubscribe if you waste their time.

## Anatomy of a Story Email

### Subject Line (40–60 characters)
The subject line is a micro-story: it opens a loop that the email body closes.

**Story-driven subject lines:**
- "The email that cost us $50,000"
- "She said no. Then everything changed."
- "I almost deleted this before sending it"
- "What happened after we fired our biggest client"
- "3 words that saved our product launch"
- "The mistake I keep making (and you probably do too)"
- "I lied in my last email. Here's the truth."

**Patterns that work:**
- Specific number + unexpected outcome
- Character + conflict in miniature
- Confession or vulnerability signal
- Curiosity gap (open loop)

### Preview Text (40–90 characters)
The preview text is the subject line's wingman. It adds context, not repetition.

**Subject:** "The email that cost us $50,000"
**Preview:** "And the one change that made it back in 48 hours."

**Subject:** "She said no."
**Preview:** "But not for the reason I expected."

### Opening Line (first 1–2 sentences)
Do NOT open with "Hi [Name], I hope this finds you well." Open in the middle of
the story.

**Strong story openers for email:**
- "Last Tuesday at 6:47 a.m., I got a text from our head of sales."
- "I want to tell you about the worst meeting I've had this year."
- "Three years ago, I made a promise to myself. Yesterday I broke it."
- "You know that feeling when you realize you've been solving the wrong problem?"
- "I wasn't going to send this email. Then I read your reply from last week."

### Body (300–600 words for story emails)
Structure the body like a micro-story:

1. **Scene** (2–3 sentences): Place the reader in a specific moment.
2. **Tension** (2–3 sentences): Introduce the problem or conflict.
3. **Turn** (1–2 sentences): The insight, change, or decision.
4. **Resolution** (2–3 sentences): The outcome and what it means.
5. **Bridge** (1 sentence): Connect the story to the reader's situation.
6. **CTA** (1 sentence): One clear next step.

### CTA (Call to Action)
One CTA per email. Not two. Not three. One.

**Story-driven CTAs:**
- "If this sounds like your situation, here's what I'd try first: [link]"
- "I wrote a longer version of this with all the details: [link]"
- "Hit reply and tell me: have you ever faced something like this?"
- "We built a tool that solves exactly this problem: [link]"

## Email Sequence as Story Arc

A nurture sequence is a serialized story. Each email is an episode.

### 5-Email Welcome Sequence (Story Arc)

| Email | Story Beat | Subject Line Example | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| 1 | Origin | "Why I started this (it's not what you think)" | Establish character and values |
| 2 | Problem | "The problem nobody was willing to name" | Build shared understanding |
| 3 | Failed attempt | "What I tried first (and why it didn't work)" | Build credibility through honesty |
| 4 | Breakthrough | "The moment everything changed" | Introduce the solution naturally |
| 5 | Invitation | "Your turn" | CTA to engage, buy, or join |

### 7-Email Launch Sequence (Story Arc)

| Email | Story Beat | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | The world before | Paint the problem landscape |
| 2 | The inciting incident | Why now? What changed? |
| 3 | The attempt (with proof) | Show the solution working |
| 4 | The obstacle | Address objections through narrative |
| 5 | The transformation | Customer story as social proof |
| 6 | The decision | Create urgency; name the choice |
| 7 | The door closing | Final CTA with deadline |

## Formatting for Email Readability

- **Line length:** 50–70 characters per line for desktop. Mobile wraps automatically.
- **Paragraph length:** 1–3 sentences max.
- **White space:** Double line breaks between paragraphs.
- **Bold:** Use for one key phrase per section, not entire sentences.
- **Links:** Inline text links outperform buttons for story emails. Buttons work for promotional emails.
- **Images:** Optional. Text-only emails often feel more personal and avoid spam filters.
- **P.S.:** The P.S. is the second-most-read part of any email. Use it for a secondary CTA or a personal note.

## Segmentation and Story Relevance

| Segment | Story Type | Emotional Register |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| New subscribers | Origin stories, founder stories | Warm, welcoming, trustworthy |
| Engaged but not buying | Objection-handling stories, customer transformations | Empathetic, proof-heavy |
| Lapsed subscribers | "We miss you" stories, new development narratives | Honest, low-pressure |
| Post-purchase | Success stories, advanced use cases | Supportive, community-building |
