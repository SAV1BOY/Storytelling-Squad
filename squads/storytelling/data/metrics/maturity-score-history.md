# Maturity Score History
> Tracking Storytelling Squad maturity across five dimensions over time

## Purpose
This document tracks the squad's maturity progression using a five-dimension model. Each
dimension is scored 1-10 quarterly. The maturity model helps the squad identify where to
invest in process, capability, and tooling improvements. Scores are based on evidence from
the quarterly narrative review.

## Maturity Model Dimensions

### 1. Process Maturity
How well-defined, documented, and consistently followed are storytelling workflows.
- **Level 1-3**: Ad hoc — stories created on request with no consistent process
- **Level 4-6**: Defined — workflows exist and are mostly followed; some inconsistency
- **Level 7-8**: Managed — workflows are consistently followed with quality gates enforced
- **Level 9-10**: Optimized — workflows are continuously improved based on performance data

### 2. Asset Quality
The average quality of narrative assets in the Story Bank and related registries.
- **Level 1-3**: Most stories are generic, lack proof, or miss emotional resonance
- **Level 4-6**: Stories have structure but inconsistent quality; some are strong
- **Level 7-8**: Consistently high quality with clear structure, proof, and emotional arc
- **Level 9-10**: Stories are exceptional — specific, emotionally powerful, and validated

### 3. Team Capability
The storytelling skills of squad members and contributing teams across the organization.
- **Level 1-3**: Storytelling is seen as a "nice to have"; few people trained
- **Level 4-6**: Core squad is skilled; contributing teams understand basic frameworks
- **Level 7-8**: Squad is highly skilled; contributing teams create quality stories independently
- **Level 9-10**: Storytelling capability is distributed; any team member can tell a good story

### 4. Measurement Rigor
How well storytelling performance is tracked, analyzed, and used to improve.
- **Level 1-3**: No performance tracking; stories are published and forgotten
- **Level 4-6**: Basic metrics tracked; some stories have performance data
- **Level 7-8**: Comprehensive tracking with attribution; data drives decisions
- **Level 9-10**: Predictive — performance data reliably predicts which stories will succeed

### 5. Cross-Squad Impact
How effectively narratives flow from the Storytelling Squad to other teams and back.
- **Level 1-3**: Stories stay within the squad; other teams create their own messaging
- **Level 4-6**: Handoffs exist but are inconsistent; some teams use shared stories
- **Level 7-8**: Structured handoffs with high adoption; other squads actively request stories
- **Level 9-10**: Narrative is the connective tissue of the organization; all squads are aligned

## Q2 2026 Priority Investments

Based on current scores and gap analysis:

1. **Cross-Squad Impact (4.5 → 6.0)**: Biggest gap relative to target. Actions:
   - Conduct handoff training sessions with Support, HR, and Partner teams
   - Create narrative asset packages tailored to each squad's specific needs
   - Establish monthly narrative office hours for all squads

2. **Team Capability (5.5 → 6.5)**: Critical for scale. Actions:
   - Run SB7 training for all customer-facing teams (Support, Sales, CS)
   - Continue improv training loop with expanded participant pool
   - Create self-service storytelling toolkit for non-squad contributors

3. **Measurement Rigor (5.0 → 6.5)**: Needed to prove value and optimize. Actions:
   - Implement multi-touch attribution for story-influenced pipeline
   - Increase sample sizes by tracking all content with narrative tags
   - Conduct first A/B test between narrative and informational approaches per channel

## Measurement Notes
- Scores assessed by Story Strategist with input from squad leads and stakeholders
- Each score requires minimum 3 supporting evidence points
- Overall score is unweighted average of 5 dimensions
- Targets set based on achievable improvement rate of 1-1.5 points per dimension per quarter
