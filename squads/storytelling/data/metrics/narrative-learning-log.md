# Narrative Learning Log
> Structured learnings from storytelling activities, experiments, and audience feedback

## Purpose
This log captures specific, actionable learnings from storytelling practice. Unlike the research
notes (which capture raw insights), this log documents validated findings from our own work
with enough structure to inform future decisions. Reviewed monthly; validated learnings are
promoted to lessons-learned-registry.yaml.

## Log Format
Each entry: Date | Activity | Learning | Evidence | Confidence | Action

---

### March 2026

**2026-03-07 | Sales pitch debrief**
Learning: When the prospect mentions a specific pain point, immediately pivoting to a relevant
customer story (even mid-script) dramatically increases engagement. Scripted responses feel
canned; narrative pivots feel authentic.
Evidence: 3 of 3 pitches where the presenter pivoted to a customer story after hearing the
prospect's pain resulted in deeper engagement and follow-up meetings.
Confidence: Medium (small sample)
Action: Add "story triggers" to the pitch cheat sheet — a menu of customer stories mapped to
common pain points for instant narrative pivots.

**2026-03-04 | Email sequence A/B test**
Learning: Email sequences that reference the previous email's story ("Remember the hospital I
mentioned yesterday?") have 18% higher open rates than sequences with independent stories.
Narrative continuity rewards serial readers.
Evidence: A/B test across 2,400 subscribers. Continuity variant: 34% avg open rate. Independent
variant: 28.8% avg open rate. Statistical significance confirmed.
Confidence: High
Action: Standardize callbacks and continuity references in all email sequence workflows. Update
wf-email-narrative-sequence transition design step.

**2026-03-01 | Story Bank Q1 review**
Learning: Stories tagged with "trust" as primary emotion have the longest shelf life. They
remain effective for 6+ months without feeling stale. Stories tagged with "urgency" decay
fastest, becoming irrelevant within 8-12 weeks as the urgency context changes.
Evidence: Engagement rate decay analysis across 11 stories tracked over 3+ months. Trust
stories: -5% decay per month. Urgency stories: -22% decay per month.
Confidence: Medium (limited sample, one quarter of data)
Action: Weight the Story Bank toward trust-building narratives for evergreen content. Reserve
urgency narratives for campaign-specific, time-bounded use only.

### February 2026

**2026-02-22 | Webinar post-mortem**
Learning: Polls placed immediately after a teaching moment have 3x the response rate of polls
placed at section transitions. Engagement is highest when the audience is actively processing
a new idea.
Evidence: Webinar analytics from Feb 20 webinar: mid-teaching polls averaged 67% response
rate; transition polls averaged 23% response rate.
Confidence: Medium (single webinar)
Action: Move all webinar polls to follow teaching moments directly. Update wf-webinar-narrative-build
engagement planning step.

**2026-02-15 | Improv session debrief**
Learning: Participants who went through the Emotional Replay exercise (IG-2026-002) showed
measurable improvement in pitch delivery vocal variety within the same session. The exercise
breaks the monotone habit faster than any feedback or coaching.
Evidence: Pre/post vocal variety scores: +2.1 points average improvement in single session.
Confidence: High (consistent across 6 participants)
Action: Make Emotional Replay mandatory in pre-pitch warm-ups. Already added to workflow.

**2026-02-08 | Case study customer feedback**
Learning: Customers prefer to approve case studies that position them as innovative decision-
makers, not just satisfied users. Framing the customer as the hero (who made a bold choice)
rather than a passive beneficiary doubles approval speed and enthusiasm.
Evidence: Two versions of Meridian Health draft sent to customer contact. "Bold decision-maker"
version approved in 3 days with enthusiasm. "Satisfied user" version returned with extensive
edits after 9 days.
Confidence: Medium (single case)
Action: Update case study interview guide to probe for decision-making courage, not just
satisfaction. Frame the customer's choice as brave and informed in all drafts.

### January 2026

**2026-01-28 | Hook A/B test results**
Learning: Hooks with specific numbers outperform hooks with vague quantifiers by 31%. "47
minutes" beats "nearly an hour." "3 people" beats "a small team." Specificity signals truth.
Evidence: Hook bank A/B tests across blog and social: specific-number hooks averaged 7.8%
CTR; vague-quantifier hooks averaged 5.9% CTR. n=14 hook pairs.
Confidence: High
Action: Updated all hook writing guidelines to require specific numbers. Added specificity
check to cl-hook-development checklist.

**2026-01-15 | First customer interview technique**
Learning: The question "What would you tell a skeptical colleague?" produces dramatically better
quotes than "How would you describe the results?" See LL-2026-001 for full details.
Evidence: Documented in lessons-learned-registry.yaml
Confidence: High
Action: Added to standard customer interview guide. Already producing results in subsequent interviews.
