# Glossary of Storytelling Terms

## Purpose

Provide clear definitions of all storytelling terms used throughout the Storytelling Squad's scripts, docs, frameworks, and workflows. This is a living document — new terms are added as the squad's vocabulary evolves.

## How to Use This Glossary

- Terms are listed alphabetically.
- Cross-references point to the documents where the term is most relevant.
- If a term has a specific meaning within the squad that differs from common usage, the squad-specific meaning is noted.

## Terms

### A

**Agitation** — The second stage of the PAS (Problem-Agitation-Solution) framework. Agitation deepens the emotional impact of the problem before presenting the solution. The goal is to make the reader feel the problem's urgency, not just understand it intellectually. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Archetype** — A universally recognizable character pattern (e.g., the Hero, the Mentor, the Rebel). Archetypes give characters instant resonance because audiences recognize the pattern. Based on Joseph Campbell's work. See `docs/agent-guide.md` (Character Designer).

**Approved (status)** — A story bank status indicating the narrative has passed the quality gate and is ready for cross-squad handoff. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

### B

**Beat sheet** — A document listing the key narrative moments (beats) in a story, in order. Produced by the Story Architect agent. Each beat describes what happens and what value shifts occur. See `docs/agent-guide.md`.

**Before-After-Bridge (BAB)** — A storytelling framework that shows the audience's current state (before), their desired state (after), and the solution that bridges the gap. Effective for case studies and testimonials. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**BrandScript** — A messaging framework from Donald Miller's StoryBrand methodology. It defines 7 elements: Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Failure, and Success. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Buyer journey stage** — The stage of the audience's decision process: awareness, consideration, decision, or retention. Stories are tagged by stage in the story bank. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

### C

**Calibration session** — A meeting where multiple reviewers score the same stories independently, then compare and align their scoring. Ensures consistency across reviewers. See `docs/quality-standards.md`.

**Call to action (CTA)** — The specific next step the audience is asked to take after consuming the story. A quality dimension in the scoring rubric. See `docs/quality-standards.md`.

**Conflict** — The central tension in a story that creates interest and drives the narrative forward. Without conflict, a story is a brochure. See `squad-overview.md`.

**Conditional Pass** — A quality gate result (score 25-29) indicating minor revisions are needed. Only the flagged dimensions are re-reviewed. See `scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md`.

### D

**Discovery (status)** — The first story bank status. A story lead has been identified but source material (interview, data) has not yet been gathered. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

**Dot voting** — A prioritization technique used in workshops. Each participant places dots (stickers or marks) on their preferred options. Used in story discovery sessions. See `scripts/run-story-discovery-session.md`.

### E

**Emotional arc** — The trajectory of emotional intensity across a story. Designed by the Emotional Arc Designer agent. Maps peaks, valleys, and transitions. See `docs/agent-guide.md`.

**Emotional theme** — A tag applied to story bank entries describing the primary emotion: triumph, transformation, resilience, innovation, urgency, empathy. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

**Empathy accelerator** — A narrative technique that quickly builds audience identification with a character. Examples: shared frustration, relatable flaw, specific detail. See `docs/agent-guide.md` (Character Designer).

### F

**Five Commandments** — Shawn Coyne's Story Grid framework for scene construction: inciting incident, progressive complication, crisis, climax, resolution. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Frame control** — Oren Klaff's concept from Pitch Anything. Every interaction involves competing frames; the person who controls the frame controls the outcome. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

### G

**Grunt test** — The StoryBrand test for clarity: can a caveman look at your content and immediately understand what you offer, how it helps them, and what to do next? See `docs/agent-guide.md` (Clarity Editor).

**Guide** — In the StoryBrand framework, the brand's role. The customer is the hero; the brand is the guide who helps the hero succeed. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

### H

**Handoff Brief** — A one-page document prepared when transferring a narrative asset to another squad. Contains: summary, framework, audience, key quotes, constraints, and attachments. See `scripts/cross-squad-handoff.md`.

**Hero's Journey** — Joseph Campbell's universal narrative structure: ordinary world, call to adventure, trials, transformation, return. Adapted by Christopher Vogler for modern storytelling. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Hook** — The opening element of a story designed to capture attention immediately. Evaluated as part of the Craft and Polish quality dimension. See `docs/quality-standards.md`.

### I

**Improv** — Improvisation exercises used to develop storytelling instincts, creative confidence, and collaborative skills. The squad runs monthly improv workshops. See `scripts/run-improv-workshop.md`.

### M

**Master index** — The central spreadsheet or database view that tracks all story bank entries with their metadata. The single source of truth for story status. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

**Micro-story** — A short-form narrative (50-150 words) designed for social media, email, or ads. Uses PAS or Pixar Story Spine framework. See `docs/template-usage-guide.md`.

### N

**Narrative audit** — A systematic review of all existing brand narrative assets, scoring them for quality, consistency, and coverage. See `scripts/run-narrative-audit.md`.

**Narrative health** — The overall quality, consistency, and coverage of a brand's narrative assets. Assessed during the quarterly narrative review. See `scripts/quarterly-narrative-review.md`.

### P

**PAS (Problem-Agitation-Solution)** — A short-form storytelling framework. State the problem, agitate its emotional impact, present the solution. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Pipeline velocity** — The average number of days a story takes to move from discovery to approved status. A production metric. See `docs/metrics-and-measurement.md`.

**Pixar Story Spine** — A narrative template: "Once upon a time... Every day... But one day... Because of that... Until finally... And ever since then..." See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Proof block** — A discrete unit of evidence inserted into a narrative: a statistic, a quote, a third-party validation, or a case reference. Produced by the Proof Curator agent. See `docs/agent-guide.md`.

### Q

**Quality gate** — A formal review process that scores narratives across 7 dimensions and determines whether they pass, need revision, or require rework. See `scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md`.

### R

**Retellability** — The likelihood that an audience member will retell the story in their own words. A key design principle of the squad: stories must be retellable. See `squad-overview.md`.

**Rework** — A quality gate result (score below 20) indicating fundamental issues. The story returns to the drafting stage with detailed guidance. See `scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md`.

### S

**Sparkline** — Nancy Duarte's contrast pattern for presentations. Alternates between "what is" (current reality) and "what could be" (future possibility) to build momentum. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**STAR** — A structured storytelling framework: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Effective for case studies and analytical audiences. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Status** — A story bank entry's current stage: discovery, interviewed, drafted, reviewed, approved, published, or archived. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

**Story bank** — The central repository of all narrative assets maintained by the Storytelling Squad. See `docs/story-bank-operations.md`.

**Story Spine** — See Pixar Story Spine.

**StoryBrand** — Donald Miller's 7-element messaging framework positioning the customer as hero and the brand as guide. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

**Swipe file** — A curated collection of excellent storytelling examples used for inspiration and technique study. See `docs/swipe-file-curation.md`.

### T

**Tone Matrix** — A grid mapping how tone adjusts across common storytelling scenarios while the brand voice stays constant. See `docs/voice-calibration-guide.md`.

**Transformation arc** — The change a character undergoes in a story, from a before state to an after state. The core of most storytelling frameworks. See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md`.

### V

**Voice Card** — A one-page document defining the brand's 4 core voice attributes with definitions, examples, and sample paragraphs. See `docs/voice-calibration-guide.md`.

**Voice preamble** — A text block included at the start of agent prompts that sets the voice attributes, tone context, and word bank for the output. See `docs/agent-guide.md`.

### W

**Word Bank** — A reference list of preferred words (reinforcing the brand voice) and avoided words (contradicting the brand voice). See `docs/voice-calibration-guide.md`.

### Y

**"Yes, and..."** — The foundational improv principle. Accept what your scene partner offers and build on it. Applied to collaborative storytelling and brainstorming. See `scripts/run-improv-workshop.md`.

## Maintaining This Glossary

- Add new terms as they emerge in squad work.
- Review quarterly during the narrative review for completeness.
- Remove terms that are no longer used.
- Ensure definitions remain aligned with actual squad practice.
