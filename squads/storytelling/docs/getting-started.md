# Getting Started with the Storytelling Squad

## Overview

The Storytelling Squad is the narrative engine of the Marketing Management Operating System (MMOS). We transform raw insights — customer interviews, market data, brand positioning — into compelling stories that drive engagement, conversion, and loyalty. This guide gets you oriented quickly so you can start contributing or requesting work from the squad.

## Who This Guide Is For

- **New squad members** joining the Storytelling Squad (see also `scripts/onboard-new-storyteller.md` for the full onboarding checklist).
- **Cross-squad partners** from Copy, Brand, or Traffic who need to understand how to work with us.
- **Stakeholders and leadership** who want to understand what the squad produces and how to request work.

## Core Philosophy

Before you start, understand the beliefs that drive every decision in this squad:

- The customer is always the hero; the brand is always the guide.
- Every story needs conflict — without it, you have a brochure.
- Proof converts; story persuades; both are required.
- A story that cannot be retold is a story that failed.
- Craft is systematic — good storytelling is learnable and repeatable.

For the full philosophy, see `squad-overview.md`.

## What the Squad Produces

| Output Type | Description | Typical Turnaround |
|---|---|---|
| Customer stories | Interview-based narratives featuring real customers | 2-3 weeks |
| Data stories | Narratives built from quantitative data and metrics | 1-2 weeks |
| Brand narratives | Origin stories, mission narratives, brand manifestos | 3-4 weeks |
| Campaign narratives | Story arcs that underpin multi-channel campaigns | 2-4 weeks |
| Pitch narratives | Presentation stories for sales, investors, or partners | 1-2 weeks |
| Micro-stories | Short-form narratives for social, email, or ads | 3-5 days |

## How the Squad Is Organized

### People
- **Squad Lead**: Owns the narrative pipeline, assigns work, runs the quarterly review.
- **Story Architects**: Senior storytellers who design narrative structures and mentor others.
- **Storytellers**: Core producers who research, interview, draft, and refine stories.
- **Story Researchers**: Focused on discovery sessions, customer interviews, and data analysis.

### Agents
The squad operates 12 AI storytelling agents, each specialized for a different task. See `docs/agent-guide.md` for the full agent roster, use cases, and prompting guidance.

### Frameworks
We use a library of storytelling frameworks (Hero's Journey, StoryBrand, Problem-Agitation-Solution, and more). See `docs/framework-selection-guide.md` to choose the right one for your project.

## How to Request Work from the Squad

1. **Submit a Story Brief** via the project management tool or the `#storytelling-requests` Slack channel.
2. Include: business objective, target audience, desired format, key messages, deadline, and any existing materials.
3. The squad lead triages the request within 1 business day.
4. A storyteller is assigned and reaches out for a kickoff conversation.
5. The story moves through the pipeline: Discovery, Draft, Review, Approval, Handoff.

## The Story Lifecycle

Every story follows this lifecycle:

```
Discovery --> Interviewed --> Drafted --> Reviewed --> Approved --> Published --> Archived
```

- **Discovery**: A story lead has been identified but not yet researched.
- **Interviewed**: Source material (interview, data) has been gathered.
- **Drafted**: A narrative draft exists.
- **Reviewed**: The draft has been through the quality gate.
- **Approved**: The story has passed the quality gate and is ready for handoff.
- **Published**: The story is live in its target channel.
- **Archived**: The story has been retired or superseded.

## Common First Mistakes

1. **Trying to do everything at once.** Pick one project. Complete it. Then expand.
2. **Skipping the audit.** Without knowing where you are, you cannot plan where to go.
3. **Writing without a framework.** Every piece of content should start with story architecture, not a blank page.
4. **Ignoring proof.** A beautiful story without evidence is creative writing, not business storytelling.
5. **Working in isolation.** Use the peer review sessions and improv workshops to sharpen your craft collaboratively.
