# Swipe File Guide

## Purpose
A swipe file is a collection of exemplary work — stories, hooks, emails, headlines, speeches, and campaigns that demonstrate exceptional narrative craft. This guide explains how to build, organize, and use swipe files for the Storytelling Squad.

---

## What Goes in a Swipe File

### Excellent Hooks
- Opening lines from articles, emails, or speeches that stopped you
- Social media posts that achieved exceptional engagement
- Video openings that made you stop scrolling
- Tag each with the hook type (contrarian, specificity, question, in medias res)

### Powerful Transformations
- Before/after narratives that made you feel the distance
- Customer stories that made you want to buy the product
- Founder stories that made you believe in the mission

### Memorable Proof Blocks
- Statistics presented in ways that created impact
- Testimonials that felt genuine, not manufactured
- Case study excerpts that combined story and data effectively

### Exceptional Structures
- Presentation outlines that demonstrated perfect rhythm
- Email sequences that built progressively to a conversion
- Blog posts that maintained attention from hook to CTA

---

## Tagging System

Every swipe file entry should include:
- **Source:** Where it came from (URL, brand, author, date)
- **Type:** Hook / transformation / proof / structure / campaign
- **Technique:** The specific narrative technique demonstrated
- **Industry:** The industry context
- **Channel:** Where it was deployed
- **Why it works:** 1-2 sentences explaining the craft

---

## How to Use Swipe Files

### For Inspiration (Not Copying)
Swipe files are for understanding technique, not for plagiarism. Study the structure, the rhythm, and the choices — then apply those principles to original content.

### For Pattern Recognition
When you collect 10+ examples of the same technique, patterns emerge. These patterns become squad knowledge — documented as components and patterns in the lib/.

### For Benchmarking
Score your own content using the squad rubrics, then compare against swipe file exemplars. Where do the exemplars score higher? That is where your craft needs development.

### For Training
Walk through swipe file entries with your team. Analyze what makes each one effective using squad frameworks.

---

## Building Your Swipe File

### Daily Habit (5 minutes)
When you encounter excellent narrative work — in your inbox, on social media, in a podcast — save it immediately with a quick tag.

### Weekly Review (15 minutes)
Review the week's additions. Add proper tags. Delete entries that seemed good in the moment but do not hold up on review.

### Monthly Curation (30 minutes)
Organize by technique. Identify which technique categories are thin. Actively seek examples to fill gaps.

## Swipe File Sources

| Source | What to look for |
|--------|-----------------|
| Email inbox | Subject lines, opening lines, narrative sequences |
| LinkedIn | Posts with high engagement — analyze why |
| TED Talks | Hooks, transformation arcs, memorable moments |
| Product Hunt | Launch narratives, brand positioning stories |
| Podcasts | Interview moments, storytelling episodes |
| Case study libraries | HubSpot, Salesforce, Stripe — analyze the best |
| Award shows | Cannes Lions, Webby Awards — winning narratives |
| Books | Opening paragraphs, memorable passages |
