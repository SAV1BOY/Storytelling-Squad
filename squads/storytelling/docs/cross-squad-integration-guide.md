# Cross-Squad Integration Guide

## Purpose
The Storytelling Squad does not operate in isolation. Stories feed into Copy, Brand, and Traffic squads — and their work feeds back into ours. This guide defines the integration points, handoff protocols, and shared standards between squads.

---

## Squad Integration Map

### Storytelling --> Copy Squad
**What we hand off:** Narrative frameworks, story arcs, key messages, BrandScripts
**What they do with it:** Transform narrative structure into channel-specific copy (ads, emails, landing pages)
**Integration point:** The narrative framework document serves as the brief for all copy work
**Shared standard:** Both squads use the clarity linter rules — content must pass the grunt test

### Storytelling --> Brand Squad
**What we hand off:** Brand narrative identity, archetype definitions, voice and tone guidelines embedded in story
**What they do with it:** Translate narrative identity into visual identity, brand guidelines, design systems
**Integration point:** Brand story document feeds directly into brand guide development
**Shared standard:** Character archetypes must be consistent between narrative and visual expression

### Storytelling --> Traffic Squad
**What we hand off:** Story-driven content assets (blogs, videos, case studies) and conversion narrative sequences
**What they do with it:** Distribute story content through paid and organic channels, optimize for reach
**Integration point:** Content calendar alignment — story production must match distribution windows
**Shared standard:** Hook testing — both squads test hooks before scaling distribution

---

## What We Need From Other Squads

### From Copy Squad
- Performance data on which narrative angles convert best
- Customer language from ad testing (actual phrases customers respond to)
- A/B test results that reveal audience preferences

### From Brand Squad
- Visual assets that match the narrative tone
- Brand guidelines that reflect the narrative archetypes
- Feedback on whether new stories align with brand evolution

### From Traffic Squad
- Channel performance data by content type
- Audience insights from distribution analytics
- Distribution timeline requirements for production planning

## Collaboration Workflows

### Workflow: Campaign Narrative Development
1. Storytelling Squad creates the campaign narrative arc
2. Brand Squad confirms visual alignment
3. Copy Squad adapts narrative to channel-specific copy
4. Traffic Squad distributes and reports performance
5. Storytelling Squad incorporates performance data into future narratives

### Workflow: Customer Story Production
1. Storytelling Squad conducts interview and drafts story
2. Copy Squad creates derivative copy (social posts, email excerpts, ad copy)
3. Brand Squad creates visual companion assets
4. Traffic Squad distributes across channels
5. All squads review performance data together

### Workflow: Brand Narrative Refresh
1. Storytelling Squad leads discovery and narrative architecture
2. Brand Squad translates narrative into updated visual identity
3. Copy Squad rewrites key touchpoints (website, emails) to match new narrative
4. Traffic Squad adjusts targeting and messaging based on new positioning

## Communication Protocols

### Shared Channels
- Weekly sync: 30-minute cross-squad alignment meeting
- Shared Slack channel for real-time coordination
- Shared project board with visibility across all squads

### Handoff Standards
- Every handoff includes a brief document explaining context, not just the asset
- Receiving squad confirms receipt and flags questions within 48 hours
- Original squad remains available for clarification during the receiving squad's production

### Conflict Resolution
When squads disagree on narrative direction:
1. Check the master narrative framework — does it resolve the disagreement?
2. If not, the Brand Squad lead makes the final call on brand alignment
3. If the disagreement is about effectiveness, run a test — data decides
