# Storytelling Squad Overview

## Vision
Every organization has stories worth telling. Most organizations tell them badly — or not at all. The Storytelling Squad exists to close that gap by providing a complete, systematic approach to business narrative that transforms how organizations communicate, persuade, and connect.

## Mission
Equip teams with the frameworks, components, and processes to create compelling narratives that drive measurable business outcomes across every channel and format.

---

## Core Beliefs

### 1. Story is infrastructure, not decoration
A narrative framework is not a nice-to-have layer on top of messaging. It is the foundation that every piece of content, every pitch, and every conversation is built on. Without story infrastructure, content is random and inconsistent.

### 2. The customer is the hero
In every story we help create, the customer is the protagonist. The brand is the guide — the Gandalf, not the Frodo. This is the most common mistake in corporate storytelling: making the brand the center of the narrative. The audience doesn't care about your brand; they care about themselves.

### 3. Conflict is mandatory
A story without conflict is a brochure. Conflict creates tension. Tension creates attention. Attention creates the opportunity to persuade. We never shy away from conflict in narrative — we design it deliberately.

### 4. Proof and story are partners
A story without proof is fiction. Proof without story is a spreadsheet. The best business narratives weave evidence into the emotional fabric of the story so that credibility and connection build simultaneously.

### 5. Stories must be retellable
A story that stays in one conversation has limited value. A story that gets retold by the audience — in their words, to their peers — has exponential value. We design every story for retellability.

### 6. Craft is systematic
Good storytelling is not a mystical talent. It is a set of learnable principles applied with discipline and tested with rigor. This squad makes the implicit explicit — turning narrative intuition into repeatable process.

## What the Squad Contains

### Library (`lib/`)
Reusable narrative building blocks, patterns, utilities, and taxonomies:
- **Components:** Individual narrative elements (hooks, characters, scenes, etc.)
- **Patterns:** Recurring narrative structures (arcs, contrast, tension, etc.)
- **Utilities:** Rules, rubrics, and scoring systems
- **Taxonomies:** Classification systems for story types, emotions, archetypes, etc.

### Scripts (`scripts/`)
Operational tools for analysis, validation, and reporting:
- Tagging, scoring, and gap detection for story banks
- Structural validation for narrative completeness
- Consistency checking across channels
- Quarterly reporting and analysis

### Documentation (`docs/`)
Guides for using the squad effectively:
- Getting started, workflows, framework selection
- Agent roles and when to use each
- Cross-squad integration and naming conventions

### Authority (`authority/`)
Strategies for building storytelling credibility:
- Thought leadership, speaking, publishing
- Workshops, community engagement, client retention

### Projects (`projects/`)
Structured project templates for common narrative initiatives:
- Brand stories, founder stories, pitch decks
- Case study pipelines, launch narratives, audits

### Archive (`archive/`)
Reference material — iconic stories analyzed, historical evolution, failures studied, and industry shifts tracked.

## How to Use the Squad

### For a specific deliverable:
1. Identify the deliverable type → go to `projects/`
2. Select the project template → follow the workflow
3. Pull components from `lib/` as needed
4. Validate using the relevant `scripts/`
5. Score using the appropriate rubric from `lib/utilities/`

### For strategic narrative work:
1. Start with a narrative audit (see `getting-started.md`)
2. Build a BrandScript (check with `brand-script-completeness-checker.md`)
3. Develop a story bank (use `story-bank-tagger.md` and `story-bank-gap-detector.md`)
4. Deploy narratives across channels (check with `narrative-consistency-checker.md`)
5. Report quarterly (use `quarterly-narrative-report-builder.md`)

## Foundational Frameworks Referenced

This squad draws on established narrative and communication frameworks:
- **StoryBrand** (Donald Miller) — BrandScript 7-element framework
- **Story Grid** (Shawn Coyne) — Scene commandments and genre theory
- **Resonate** (Nancy Duarte) — Sparkline contrast patterns
- **Pitch Anything** (Oren Klaff) — Frame control theory
- **Impro** (Keith Johnstone) — Status dynamics
- **Save the Cat** (Blake Snyder) — Genre classification
- **The Hero with a Thousand Faces** (Joseph Campbell) — Archetype system
- **Made to Stick** (Chip & Dan Heath) — Stickiness principles
- **Stories That Stick** (Kindra Hall) — Story type framework
- **Building a StoryBrand** (Donald Miller) — Customer-as-hero positioning
