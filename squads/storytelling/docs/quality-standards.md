# Quality Standards

## Purpose

Define the quality standards, scoring rubrics, and benchmarks for all narrative deliverables produced by the Storytelling Squad. These standards are applied during the quality gate process (`scripts/story-quality-gate-process.md`) and inform peer reviews, training, and quarterly assessments.

## Quality Philosophy

Quality in storytelling is not subjective opinion — it is measurable craft. Every narrative can be evaluated against structural, emotional, and technical dimensions. The rubrics below make implicit quality judgments explicit and repeatable.

Quality is not perfection. The goal is consistent excellence with clear thresholds that distinguish work ready for publication from work that needs revision.

## The Seven Quality Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Narrative Structure (Weight: High)

Does the story have a clear, purposeful architecture?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear beginning, middle, and end. Framework elements fully realized. Every section earns its place. |
| 4 | Structure is sound with one minor gap (e.g., weak transition between sections). |
| 3 | Structure is present but predictable or missing one element (e.g., conflict is stated but not developed). |
| 2 | Structure is partial. Major elements missing (e.g., no clear resolution). |
| 1 | No discernible structure. Reads as a collection of facts rather than a narrative. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Is there a defined character?
- Is there a meaningful conflict or tension?
- Is there a transformation or resolution?
- Does the chosen framework fit the story's purpose?
- Are the sections in a logical and emotionally compelling order?

### Dimension 2: Emotional Resonance (Weight: High)

Does the story make the reader feel something?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Evokes a strong, specific emotion. Vivid details create genuine empathy. The emotional arc builds and resolves. |
| 4 | Emotional content is present and mostly effective. One section could be more vivid. |
| 3 | Emotion is present but generic (e.g., "they were happy with the results"). Lacks specific, sensory detail. |
| 2 | Emotion is attempted but feels forced or manipulative. |
| 1 | No emotional content. Reads as a report or product description. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Are there specific, vivid details that create empathy?
- Is the emotion earned through the narrative, not just stated?
- Does the emotional arc build and resolve?
- Would the target audience connect emotionally?

### Dimension 3: Voice and Tone Consistency (Weight: Medium)

Does the story sound like the brand?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Perfectly matches the Voice Card across all sections. Tone is appropriate for the scenario. |
| 4 | Matches the Voice Card with one minor deviation. Tone is mostly appropriate. |
| 3 | Generally on-brand but with 2-3 sections that shift in voice or tone. |
| 2 | Significant voice inconsistency. Sections feel like different authors. |
| 1 | Does not match the Voice Card. Off-brand throughout. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Do the 4 voice attributes come through consistently?
- Is the tone appropriate for the content type per the Tone Matrix?
- Are preferred words used and avoided words absent?
- Would the audience recognize this as the brand's voice?

### Dimension 4: Audience Relevance (Weight: Medium)

Is the story written for the right people?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Directly addresses the target audience's concerns, motivations, and language. The reader thinks, "This is about me." |
| 4 | Audience-appropriate with one section that could be more targeted. |
| 3 | Generally relevant but could apply to a broader audience than intended. Lacks persona-specific details. |
| 2 | Partially misses the audience. Uses jargon they would not know or omits concerns they have. |
| 1 | Wrong audience. The story speaks to a different persona entirely. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Does the story address the audience's specific pain points?
- Is the language accessible to the intended reader?
- Is the level of detail appropriate (not too basic, not too advanced)?
- Would this audience find the story credible?

### Dimension 5: Factual Accuracy (Weight: High)

Are the facts right?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | All data points verified against sources. Quotes accurately attributed. Claims supported by evidence. |
| 4 | All data points verified; one minor attribution detail could be clearer. |
| 3 | Most facts are accurate; 1-2 data points need verification or clarification. |
| 2 | Multiple unverified claims. Some data points lack source attribution. |
| 1 | Contains factual errors, misattributed quotes, or unsupported claims. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Is every data point traceable to a source?
- Are quotes accurately transcribed and properly attributed?
- Are correlations presented as correlations, not causation?
- Are there any overstatements or misleading comparisons?

**Note:** A score of 1 on Factual Accuracy triggers an automatic "Rework" regardless of total score.

### Dimension 6: Call to Action (Weight: Medium)

Does the reader know what to do next?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear, singular CTA that flows naturally from the story's resolution. Compelling and specific. |
| 4 | CTA is present and clear but could be more tightly connected to the narrative. |
| 3 | CTA is present but generic (e.g., "Learn more"). Not connected to the story. |
| 2 | CTA is vague, buried, or there are multiple competing CTAs. |
| 1 | No CTA. The story ends without directing the reader. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Is there exactly one primary CTA?
- Does the CTA emerge naturally from the story's resolution?
- Is the CTA appropriate for the buyer journey stage?
- Is the CTA specific enough to act on?

### Dimension 7: Craft and Polish (Weight: Low)

Is the writing clean and professional?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Error-free. Strong pacing, smooth transitions, powerful opening hook, satisfying close. |
| 4 | Near error-free. One minor pacing issue or slightly weak transition. |
| 3 | A few grammatical errors or one section with pacing problems. Needs a final editing pass. |
| 2 | Multiple errors. Sections drag or rush. Transitions are abrupt. |
| 1 | Significant errors throughout. Draft-quality writing presented as finished work. |

**What reviewers look for:**
- Is the writing free of grammatical and spelling errors?
- Is the pacing effective? No sections that drag or rush?
- Are transitions between sections smooth?
- Is the opening hook strong? Is the close memorable?
- Is the length appropriate for the format?

## Scoring Thresholds

| Total Score (out of 35) | Gate Result | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 30-35 | Pass | Ready for "approved" status |
| 25-29 | Conditional Pass | Minor revisions; re-review flagged dimensions only |
| 20-24 | Revise | Significant revisions; full re-review required |
| Below 20 | Rework | Return to drafting with detailed guidance |

**Automatic overrides:**
- A score of 1 on any single dimension triggers "Revise" regardless of total.
- A score of 1 on Factual Accuracy triggers "Rework" regardless of total.

## Benchmarks by Output Type

| Output Type | Target Total Score | Critical Dimensions |
|---|---|---|
| Customer story | 28+ | Emotional Resonance, Factual Accuracy |
| Data story | 27+ | Factual Accuracy, Narrative Structure |
| Brand narrative | 29+ | Voice Consistency, Emotional Resonance |
| Pitch narrative | 28+ | Narrative Structure, Call to Action |
| Micro-story | 26+ | Craft and Polish, Audience Relevance |
| Campaign narrative | 29+ | Voice Consistency, Audience Relevance |

## Quality Score Tracking

### Individual Tracking
Each squad member's quality gate scores are tracked over time. Trends reveal:
- Consistent strengths to celebrate and leverage.
- Recurring weaknesses to address through training.
- Improvement trajectory after feedback and coaching.

### Squad-Level Tracking
Aggregate scores are reviewed quarterly (`scripts/quarterly-narrative-review.md`):
- Average score by dimension identifies systemic strengths and weaknesses.
- Average score by output type identifies which formats need more support.
- Score trends over time measure the impact of training and process improvements.

### Calibration Sessions
To ensure scoring consistency across reviewers, conduct calibration sessions quarterly:
1. Select 3 stories.
2. Have 2-3 reviewers score each independently.
3. Compare scores and discuss discrepancies.
4. Align on interpretation of each score level.
5. Document calibration decisions.
