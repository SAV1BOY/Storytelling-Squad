# Agent Roles Guide — When to Use Each of the 12 Agents

## Purpose
The Storytelling Squad has 12 specialized agents, each designed for a specific narrative function. Using the right agent for the right task ensures quality and efficiency. This guide maps each agent to its ideal use cases.

---

## The 12 Agents

### 1. Story Architect
**Role:** Designs the narrative structure from scratch — the blueprint before the building.
**Use when:** Starting a new narrative project, restructuring an existing one, or building a story framework.
**Inputs:** Business context, audience, objective, available story material
**Outputs:** Story arc, beat sheet, scene outline, structural framework
**Do not use for:** Copy editing, visual design, or proof gathering

### 2. Clarity Editor
**Role:** Sharpens language, cuts fat, and ensures the grunt test is passed.
**Use when:** A draft exists but is too long, unclear, or jargon-heavy.
**Inputs:** Draft content of any format
**Outputs:** Tighter, clearer version with specific edits tracked
**Tools used:** clarity-linter-rules.md, story-trim-rules.md, clarity-score-calculator.md

### 3. Proof Curator
**Role:** Gathers, formats, and places proof blocks within narratives.
**Use when:** A story lacks credibility, or claims need supporting evidence.
**Inputs:** Narrative draft, available data sources, customer contacts
**Outputs:** Proof blocks inserted at optimal positions

### 4. Hook Specialist
**Role:** Creates and optimizes opening hooks for maximum stopping power.
**Use when:** Content isn't getting engagement, or you need a strong opening for a high-stakes piece.
**Inputs:** Core story, audience, channel
**Outputs:** 3-5 hook alternatives with strength scores

### 5. Character Designer
**Role:** Builds dimensional characters using the want/need/lie/flaw framework.
**Use when:** A narrative feels flat or the protagonist is a cardboard cutout.
**Inputs:** Real person details (customer, founder, team member) or brand persona
**Outputs:** Full character architecture with empathy accelerators

### 6. Conflict Engineer
**Role:** Designs and escalates conflict within narratives.
**Use when:** A story lacks tension, feels too smooth, or the audience isn't invested.
**Inputs:** Story outline or draft
**Outputs:** Conflict map with escalation plan, stakes definition, and dilemma design

### 7. Scene Builder
**Role:** Constructs individual scenes using Coyne's five commandments.
**Use when:** You need a single powerful scene — a turning point, a climax, or a key moment.
**Inputs:** Scene context, characters, desired value shift
**Outputs:** Complete scene with all five commandments

### 8. Pitch Strategist
**Role:** Designs pitch narratives, timing, and delivery strategy.
**Use when:** Preparing for investor pitches, sales presentations, or partnership proposals.
**Inputs:** Business information, audience profile, time constraints
**Outputs:** Pitch structure, timing script, deck flow recommendations
**Tools used:** pitch-timing-calculator.md, pitch-scoring-rubric.md, deck-flow-analyzer.md

### 9. Brand Narrator
**Role:** Develops and maintains the brand's narrative identity.
**Use when:** Building or refreshing a brand story, creating a BrandScript, or ensuring narrative consistency.
**Inputs:** Brand history, values, customers, competitive landscape
**Outputs:** BrandScript, brand narrative framework, consistency guidelines
**Tools used:** brand-script-completeness-checker.md, narrative-consistency-checker.md

### 10. Story Bank Manager
**Role:** Organizes, tags, and maintains the story bank.
**Use when:** Building a story bank from scratch, auditing an existing one, or finding the right story for a specific situation.
**Inputs:** Raw stories, tags, usage data
**Outputs:** Tagged story bank, gap report, retrieval recommendations
**Tools used:** story-bank-tagger.md, story-bank-gap-detector.md

### 11. Emotional Arc Designer
**Role:** Maps and optimizes the emotional trajectory of narratives.
**Use when:** A story is structurally sound but emotionally flat, or you need to calibrate intensity.
**Inputs:** Story draft or outline
**Outputs:** Emotional arc map, intensity recommendations, peak/valley placement
**Tools used:** emotional-arc-mapper.md, emotion-mapping-rubric.md

### 12. Narrative Analyst
**Role:** Evaluates narrative quality, runs diagnostics, and generates reports.
**Use when:** Scoring deliverables, running audits, or building quarterly reports.
**Inputs:** Completed narratives, story bank data, cross-channel content
**Outputs:** Quality scores, consistency reports, gap analyses, quarterly reports
**Tools used:** All scoring rubrics and checker scripts

## Agent Selection Quick Reference

| I need to... | Agent |
|--------------|-------|
| Build a narrative from nothing | Story Architect |
| Make existing content clearer | Clarity Editor |
| Add credibility to a story | Proof Curator |
| Grab attention in the first 5 seconds | Hook Specialist |
| Make a character feel real | Character Designer |
| Add tension to a flat story | Conflict Engineer |
| Build one powerful scene | Scene Builder |
| Prepare a pitch presentation | Pitch Strategist |
| Define the brand's story | Brand Narrator |
| Organize stories for retrieval | Story Bank Manager |
| Calibrate emotional intensity | Emotional Arc Designer |
| Score and report on quality | Narrative Analyst |

## Multi-Agent Workflows

Most projects require 2-4 agents working in sequence:
- **Case study:** Story Architect → Character Designer → Proof Curator → Clarity Editor
- **Pitch deck:** Story Architect → Pitch Strategist → Hook Specialist → Conflict Engineer
- **Brand story:** Brand Narrator → Character Designer → Emotional Arc Designer → Clarity Editor
- **Narrative audit:** Narrative Analyst → Story Bank Manager → Story Architect (for gap-filling)

See `workflow-guide.md` for complete workflow definitions.
