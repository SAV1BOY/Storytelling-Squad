# Storytelling Glossary

## Purpose
A shared vocabulary is essential for effective collaboration. This glossary defines the key terms used across the Storytelling Squad. When in doubt about meaning, this document is the authority.

---

## A

**Archetype:** A universally recognized character pattern (Campbell's 12). See `archetype-taxonomy.md`.

**Arc:** The full trajectory of change a character undergoes. Positive arcs move from lie to truth; negative arcs move from truth to lie.

## B

**Beat:** The smallest unit of story change — a single shift in value, emotion, or information. The atomic unit of narrative.

**BrandScript:** Donald Miller's 7-element framework for brand messaging: Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, CTA, Failure, Success.

## C

**Callback:** A narrative device where an element from the opening returns in the closing, reframed by the journey between.

**Climax:** The moment of irreversible decision in a scene or story. Reveals character through action under pressure.

**Conflict:** The opposition between what a character wants and the forces preventing them from getting it. The engine of story.

**Crisis:** The impossible choice a character faces — best bad choice or irreconcilable goods.

## D

**Dramatic irony:** When the audience knows something the character does not.

**Duarte sparkline:** Nancy Duarte's pattern of alternating "what is" and "what could be" in presentations.

## E

**Emotional arc:** The sequence of emotional states an audience experiences across a narrative, mapped by intensity (0-10).

**Epiphany beat:** The moment of insight that changes a character's understanding.

## F

**Five Commandments:** Shawn Coyne's scene requirements: inciting incident, progressive complication, crisis, climax, resolution.

**Flat arc:** A character who does not change but tests the world around them against a truth they hold.

**Foreshadowing:** Planting hints about future events that gain meaning in retrospect.

**Frame:** Oren Klaff's concept — a mental structure through which people interpret reality. Frame collisions determine who controls the interaction.

## G

**Genre:** A classification of story that sets audience expectations for content and emotional experience.

**Grunt test:** Donald Miller's clarity test: Can a caveman grunt what you offer, how it helps, and what to do next?

**Guide:** The brand's role in StoryBrand — not the hero but the experienced mentor (Gandalf, Yoda).

## H

**Hero:** The protagonist — in business storytelling, always the customer, never the brand.

**Hook:** The opening device that creates an open loop the audience must close. Must include pattern interrupt, curiosity gap, and relevance signal.

## I

**In medias res:** Starting the narrative in the middle of the action.

**Inciting incident:** The event that upsets the balance and forces the character to act.

## L

**Lie (the character's lie):** The false belief a character holds that prevents them from fulfilling their need.

## M

**Micro-story:** A complete narrative told in under 100 words or 60 seconds.

**Montage:** A compressed sequence of events showing passage of time or accumulated change.

## N

**Narrative consistency:** The degree to which a brand's story remains coherent across all channels and touchpoints.

**Need (character need):** What a character requires for genuine growth — often invisible to them at the start.

## O

**Open loop:** An unresolved question or situation that creates psychological tension until closed.

**Origin wound:** The founder's personal experience of the problem the brand solves.

## P

**Pattern interrupt:** A narrative device that breaks the audience's current mental state.

**Proof block:** A pre-formatted unit of evidence (stat, quote, case study) insertable into any narrative.

**Progressive complication:** A series of escalating obstacles culminating in a turning point.

## R

**Retellability:** The degree to which a story survives being retold by the audience in their own words.

**Revelation:** A moment when hidden information is disclosed, changing the audience's understanding.

## S

**Scene:** The fundamental unit of story, containing five commandments (Coyne).

**Sparkline:** See Duarte sparkline.

**Stakes:** What the character loses if they fail. Without stakes, there is no tension.

**Status (Johnstone):** Behavioral signals of relative power in an interaction — not rank or authority.

**Story bank:** A curated, tagged collection of narrative assets ready for deployment.

**Subtext:** The meaning beneath the surface of dialogue or action — what is communicated but not said.

## T

**Transformation:** The measurable change between State A and State B — the core of every story.

**Turning point:** The moment in progressive complication where the character's old approach becomes impossible.

## V

**Value shift:** The change in a core value (trust to betrayal, ignorance to knowledge) that occurs in every scene.

**Vision story:** A narrative that paints a picture of a desirable future state.

## W

**Want (character want):** The concrete, external goal a character consciously pursues.
