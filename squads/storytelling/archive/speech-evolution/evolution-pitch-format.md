# Evolution of the Pitch Format

## Purpose
Trace how pitch presentations have evolved from static proposals to story-driven performances, revealing what works today and what is coming next.

---

## Era 1: The Written Proposal (Pre-1990)
- **Format:** Multi-page document, mailed or hand-delivered
- **Structure:** Executive summary, problem statement, proposed solution, budget, timeline
- **Narrative level:** Zero. Pure information delivery in logical sequence.
- **Limitation:** No emotional engagement. Decisions made on paper, not in person.

## Era 2: The Bullet Point Deck (1990-2005)
- **Format:** PowerPoint slides projected in conference rooms
- **Structure:** Title slide, agenda, company overview, features, pricing, Q&A
- **Narrative level:** Minimal. "About us" slides replaced personal storytelling.
- **Dominant pattern:** Feature dump — list everything the product does.
- **Limitation:** Every deck looked the same. No differentiation through presentation.

## Era 3: The Story-Driven Pitch (2005-2015)
- **Format:** Designed slides with minimal text, presented with narrative
- **Influenced by:** Steve Jobs's keynote style, TED talks, Duarte and Klaff
- **Structure:** Hook, problem dramatization, insight, solution narrative, proof, CTA
- **Key shift:** The presentation became a performance. The speaker, not the slides, carried the story.
- **Examples:** Airbnb's original pitch deck, Jobs's iPhone introduction

## Era 4: The Demo-Story Hybrid (2015-2022)
- **Format:** Live demonstration woven into narrative, with slides as support
- **Structure:** Problem story → "Let me show you" → Live demo → Results story → CTA
- **Key shift:** Showing replaced telling. Audiences expected to see the product in action.
- **Limitation:** Demos can fail. Technical issues become the story.

## Era 5: The Conversational Pitch (2022-present)
- **Format:** Less formal, more dialogue-driven, often remote
- **Structure:** Opening story → Collaborative discovery → Tailored narrative → Co-created next steps
- **Key shift:** The pitch is a conversation, not a presentation. The audience participates in real-time.
- **Influenced by:** Remote work culture, shortened attention spans, AI-generated decks

---

## What Has Changed
- Slide count has decreased (from 60+ to 10-15)
- Text per slide has decreased (from paragraphs to 6 words)
- Storytelling has increased (from zero to central)
- Audience expectation of engagement has increased
- Formal structure has decreased (from rigid to fluid)

## What Has Not Changed
- The first 90 seconds determine whether the audience stays
- Proof is required for credibility
- The ask must be specific and singular
- Preparation and rehearsal separate good from great
- Personal stories outperform product descriptions
