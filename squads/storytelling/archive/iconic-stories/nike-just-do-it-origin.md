# Nike "Just Do It" — Origin and Narrative Analysis

## Overview
Launched in 1988, "Just Do It" became one of the most recognized slogans in history. Created by Wieden+Kennedy, it transformed Nike from a running shoe company into a global symbol of athletic aspiration. The campaign's narrative strategy is a masterclass in identity storytelling.

---

## The Origin Story

### The Inspiration
Dan Wieden, co-founder of Wieden+Kennedy, has stated that the phrase was inspired by the last words of convicted murderer Gary Gilmore, who said "Let's do it" before his execution in 1977. Wieden modified it to "Just Do It" — stripping the phrase of its dark origin and reframing it as a universal call to action.

### The Problem Nike Faced
By 1988, Nike was losing market share to Reebok, which had captured the aerobics and lifestyle market. Nike was seen as a serious athletic brand — respected but not aspirational to the broader consumer market. They needed a narrative that transcended sports.

### The Insight
The campaign team realized that Nike's story was not about shoes — it was about the internal battle every person fights between action and inaction. "Just Do It" named that battle and resolved it in three words.

---

## Narrative Techniques

### The Flat Arc Brand
Nike operates as a flat arc character — it holds a truth ("greatness is achieved through action") and tests the world against it. Nike doesn't change; it challenges others to change. This is the Guide archetype at its most powerful.

### Universal Conflict: Person vs. Self
Every "Just Do It" campaign is fundamentally about the internal conflict between wanting to act and wanting to stay comfortable. This is the most universally relatable conflict type — every human being experiences it daily.

### Inclusive Hero Design
The genius of "Just Do It" is that anyone can be the hero. The campaigns feature:
- Elite athletes (Michael Jordan, Serena Williams) — aspiration
- Everyday people running, training, struggling — identification
- People overcoming disabilities, age, circumstance — inspiration
The audience always sees someone they can project themselves onto.

### Minimalist Narrative
"Just Do It" is a complete story compressed to three words:
- Implied conflict: You're hesitating
- Implied action: Stop hesitating
- Implied transformation: You'll become the person who acts

## Campaign Evolution

### Phase 1 (1988-1995): Athletic Achievement
Stories of elite athletes pushing limits. Michael Jordan, Bo Jackson, Charles Barkley.
**Narrative function:** Establish the brand as the uniform of champions.

### Phase 2 (1995-2010): Democratization of Sport
Stories of everyday people finding their inner athlete. "If you have a body, you are an athlete."
**Narrative function:** Expand the hero from elite to universal.

### Phase 3 (2010-present): Social and Cultural Narrative
Stories that connect athletic determination to social justice, equality, and cultural change. Colin Kaepernick campaign: "Believe in something. Even if it means sacrificing everything."
**Narrative function:** Elevate the brand from athletic to philosophical.

---

## Lessons for Business Storytelling

- The best brand narratives name a universal internal conflict
- Three words can contain an entire story if they capture the right tension
- Brands with flat arcs (holding a truth) can sustain decades of storytelling
- Inclusive hero design expands the addressable audience without diluting the message
- Narrative evolution across eras keeps a brand relevant without abandoning its core
