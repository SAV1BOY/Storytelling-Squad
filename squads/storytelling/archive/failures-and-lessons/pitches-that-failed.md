# Pitches That Failed — Analysis

## Purpose
Study pitch failures to identify common structural and narrative mistakes that cause otherwise viable ideas to fail in presentation. Most pitch failures are narrative failures, not product failures.

---

## Failure Pattern 1: The Feature Avalanche
**What happens:** The pitcher lists every feature, capability, and technical detail, believing that comprehensiveness equals persuasion.
**Why it fails:** The audience is overwhelmed. They cannot identify which feature matters most. The pitch becomes a product manual, not a story.
**Example:** A SaaS startup listed 47 features across 35 slides. The investor's one comment: "So what does it actually do?"
**Fix:** Lead with one transformative outcome. Features support; they do not lead.

## Failure Pattern 2: The Missing Problem
**What happens:** The pitcher assumes the audience already understands and cares about the problem. They jump straight to the solution.
**Why it fails:** Without feeling the problem, the audience has no motivation to care about the solution. The pitch is an answer to a question nobody asked.
**Example:** A health-tech founder opened with: "We've built an AI-powered diagnostic platform." The investor asked: "For what problem?"
**Fix:** Spend 20-25% of the pitch making the problem viscerally felt before introducing any solution.

## Failure Pattern 3: The Credibility Desert
**What happens:** Bold claims are made without any supporting evidence. The pitcher says "game-changing" and "industry-leading" without proof.
**Why it fails:** Sophisticated audiences (investors, executives, experienced buyers) mentally discount unsupported claims. Each unproven assertion reduces trust.
**Example:** "Our platform is the most advanced in the market." Investor: "Based on what?"
**Fix:** Every major claim needs a proof block within 60 seconds. Named customers, specific metrics, third-party validation.

## Failure Pattern 4: The Time Collapse
**What happens:** The pitcher runs out of time before reaching the ask. They spend too long on background, demo, or features, and the CTA is rushed or skipped entirely.
**Why it fails:** The audience is left without a clear next step. The pitch creates interest but no action.
**Example:** A 10-minute pitch spent 8 minutes on the product demo. The ask ("We're raising $5M") was crammed into the final 30 seconds with no context.
**Fix:** Use the pitch timing calculator. Allocate time backward from the CTA. The ask gets 15-20% of total time.

## Failure Pattern 5: The Wrong Hero
**What happens:** The pitch is entirely about the company, the team, and the technology. The customer never appears.
**Why it fails:** The audience cannot see themselves in the story. The pitch is self-referential — it tells the company's story, not the audience's story.
**Example:** "We have 15 PhDs, 3 patents, and $10M in funding." No mention of customer problems, outcomes, or stories.
**Fix:** Make the customer the hero from slide 1. The company is the guide. Show customer transformation, not company achievements.

## Failure Pattern 6: The Defensive Pitch
**What happens:** The pitcher anticipates objections so aggressively that the pitch becomes a preemptive argument. "You might think X, but actually Y."
**Why it fails:** The audience feels manipulated. Addressing objections before they arise suggests the pitcher lacks confidence.
**Example:** "I know what you're thinking — another CRM. But this is different. I know that sounds like what everyone says..."
**Fix:** Tell a confident forward story. Address objections through narrative (objection-to-scene mapper), not through preemptive defense.

---

## The Post-Mortem Process

After any failed pitch, conduct a narrative post-mortem:
1. Which of the six failure patterns were present?
2. Where did the audience disengage? (observe body language, questions, energy)
3. What was the audience's most critical question? (this reveals what the pitch missed)
4. Score the pitch retroactively using the pitch scoring rubric
5. Identify the 2-3 highest-impact changes for the next iteration
