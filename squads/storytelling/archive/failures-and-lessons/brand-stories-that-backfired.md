# Brand Stories That Backfired

## Purpose
Study brand storytelling failures to identify the patterns that cause narratives to backfire. Understanding failure is as valuable as studying success — the same techniques that create powerful stories can create powerful backlash when misapplied.

---

## Case 1: Pepsi x Kendall Jenner (2017)
**The story Pepsi tried to tell:** Pepsi brings people together and can bridge social divides.
**What happened:** A commercial showed Kendall Jenner leaving a photo shoot to join a protest, then handing a police officer a Pepsi, apparently resolving the tension. The ad was pulled within 24 hours after massive backlash.

**Why it backfired:**
- Trivialized real social justice movements (Black Lives Matter)
- The protagonist was a wealthy celebrity, not someone with genuine stakes
- The "resolution" (handing someone a soda) was absurdly inadequate for the conflict depicted
- No authenticity: Pepsi had no track record of social justice engagement

**Lesson:** Never appropriate real human struggle for brand positioning unless the brand has genuinely participated in the cause. The conflict must be real, and the resolution must be proportionate.

## Case 2: WeWork "Our Mission" Narrative (2019)
**The story WeWork tried to tell:** WeWork is not a real estate company — it is a movement to elevate the world's consciousness.
**What happened:** The S-1 filing used the word "community" 150+ times and "mission" repeatedly. Investors saw through the narrative to unsustainable economics. The IPO collapsed.

**Why it backfired:**
- The narrative exceeded what the business could support
- Purpose story without purpose proof — no evidence the "consciousness" claim was real
- The founder's personal narrative (lavish spending, self-dealing) contradicted the mission
- Investors require proof, not poetry

**Lesson:** A narrative that exceeds the reality it describes is not inspiring — it is fraudulent. Story must match substance.

## Case 3: Gillette "The Best Men Can Be" (2019)
**The story Gillette tried to tell:** Toxic masculinity is a problem, and Gillette (which had previously celebrated traditional masculinity) was taking a stand.
**What happened:** Polarizing response — some praised the message while others felt lectured and condescended to by a razor company.

**Why it backfired (partially):**
- Brand history contradicted the new message — decades of "The Best a Man Can Get" celebrated the very masculinity now being criticized
- The tone was preachy, not narrative — it told men what was wrong instead of showing transformation
- The audience felt scolded, not inspired

**Lesson:** Brand narrative pivots must acknowledge the brand's history. You cannot credibly tell a new story that contradicts every previous story without addressing the contradiction directly.

## Case 4: Chase Bank "Monday Motivation" Tweet (2019)
**The story Chase tried to tell:** Lighthearted financial advice about spending habits.
**What happened:** Chase tweeted a fictional conversation: "You: why is my balance so low? Bank account: make coffee at home, don't eat out, etc." The backlash was immediate — a bank that received $25 billion in bailout money was lecturing individuals about their coffee spending.

**Why it backfired:**
- Status mismatch: a wealthy institution condescending to struggling individuals
- Context blindness: posted during a period of wage stagnation and student debt crisis
- The "character" (Chase) was the wrong messenger for this message
- Humor requires shared status — you cannot punch down and be funny

**Lesson:** Consider who is telling the story. The narrator's identity determines whether the same message is received as helpful or insulting.

---

## Common Backfire Patterns

1. **Appropriation:** Using someone else's struggle for brand benefit
2. **Narrative-reality gap:** The story promises more than the brand delivers
3. **Status mismatch:** High-status brand lectures low-status audience
4. **History contradiction:** New narrative conflicts with established brand history
5. **Tone-deafness:** Failure to read the cultural moment
