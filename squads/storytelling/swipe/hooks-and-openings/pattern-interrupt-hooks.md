# Pattern Interrupt Hooks: Examples

## Purpose
Pattern interrupts break the reader's autopilot. When scrolling, scanning, or skimming,
the brain filters out the expected. These hooks violate expectations to earn attention.

---

## Category 1: Visual / Format Pattern Interrupts

### For Written Content
1. **Start with a single word.** "No." (Then explain what you're saying no to.)
2. **Open with dialogue, no context.** "She said, 'If you send that email, I'm quitting.' I sent it."
3. **Use a number as the entire first line.** "$47,000. That's what the mistake cost."
4. **Lead with a list of one.** "Things I'd do differently: 1. Everything."
5. **Use white space as a character.** A blank line. Then another. Then: "Still here? Good."
6. **Open with a strikethrough.** "~~We're excited to announce~~ Let me just tell you what happened."
7. **Start with a P.S.** "P.S. I should have led with this, but I buried it at the end of the last email."

### For Video
8. **Open on an extreme close-up.** An eye. A hand. A product detail. Then pull back.
9. **Start with the ending.** "Here's the result." Then: "Now let me show you how."
10. **Begin with silence.** Three seconds of no audio. Text on screen: "This is what your customers hear when your story is unclear."
11. **Start mid-conversation.** Camera turns on during a heated discussion.
12. **Open upside-down or sideways.** The frame itself is wrong. The audience needs to stay to find out why.

## Category 2: Intellectual Pattern Interrupts

### Counterintuitive Claims
13. "The best marketing strategy I've ever seen involves zero marketing spend."
14. "I'm going to argue that your best customers are your biggest problem."
15. "Doing less is a competitive advantage. Here's the data."
16. "The most profitable decision we made was raising our prices 40%."
17. "Our highest-converting page has no images, no testimonials, and no CTA button."
18. "I stopped setting goals. My output doubled."
19. "The worst advice in business is 'follow your passion.'"

### Paradoxes
20. "The more choices you give customers, the less they buy."
21. "The faster we tried to grow, the slower we grew."
22. "Our most successful product was the one we tried to kill."
23. "The team that works fewer hours outperforms the team that works more."
24. "We got more customers by making the product harder to buy."

---

## Category 3: Emotional Pattern Interrupts

### Unexpected Vulnerability
25. "I cried in the office bathroom last Thursday. Then I came out and closed the biggest deal of the year."
26. "My therapist told me something last week that changed how I run my company."
27. "I'm terrified to publish this."
28. "I've never told this story publicly. My board of advisors told me not to."
29. "The presentation was going perfectly. Then my voice cracked."

### Unexpected Humor
30. "My marketing budget is smaller than my coffee budget. And my coffee budget is embarrassing."
31. "I Googled 'how to write a hook' before writing this. The irony is not lost on me."
32. "According to my analytics, three people will read this far. Hi, you three."
33. "I asked ChatGPT to write this. It was terrible. So here's the human version."
34. "The last time I tried this, it failed so spectacularly that my team made it into a meme."

---

## Category 4: Structural Pattern Interrupts

### Breaking the Expected Format
35. **The anti-listicle.** "This is not a list of 10 tips. This is one idea that makes the other nine irrelevant."
36. **The reverse timeline.** Start with the end. Work backward to the beginning.
37. **The mid-article author note.** "[Author's note: I almost deleted this section. Read it anyway.]"
38. **The letter format.** "Dear [target audience], This isn't a blog post. It's a letter."
39. **The single-sentence email.** Subject: "One line." Body: "The customers who leave tell you more than the customers who stay. Here's how to listen: [link]"
40. **The blank slide.** In a presentation: one completely black slide. Silence. Then the most important line.

---

## Category 5: Meta / Self-Aware Pattern Interrupts

41. "You've seen this format before. Hook. Story. Lesson. I'm going to skip steps 1 and 2."
42. "Every article about [topic] starts the same way. This one doesn't."
43. "I know exactly what you're thinking right now. And you're wrong."
44. "This is the part of the email where I'm supposed to tell you about our product. Instead, I'm going to tell you about a funeral."
45. "The algorithm wants me to start with a question. So here's my question: when did we start letting algorithms tell us how to start?"

## The Pattern Interrupt Spectrum

| Subtlety | Example | Risk Level |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| **Gentle** | Unexpected statistic as opening | Low — universally effective |
| **Moderate** | Counterintuitive claim | Medium — requires supporting evidence |
| **Bold** | Personal vulnerability or confession | Medium-high — requires earned trust |
| **Aggressive** | Breaking format conventions | High — can feel gimmicky if content doesn't deliver |
| **Meta** | Self-aware commentary on the format | Variable — works best with sophisticated audiences |

## Rules for Pattern Interrupts
1. **The interrupt must be earned by the content.** A shocking hook followed by boring content is worse than no hook at all.
2. **One per piece.** Don't stack pattern interrupts. One at the opening is enough.
3. **Know your audience's expectations.** You can only interrupt a pattern if you know what the pattern is.
4. **The interrupt creates a promise.** If you say "this isn't a listicle," you must deliver something better than a listicle.
5. **Authenticity over cleverness.** A genuine confession interrupts more effectively than a manufactured surprise.
