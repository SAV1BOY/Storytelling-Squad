# Question-Format Hooks: Library

## Purpose
Questions are the most reliable hook format. They activate the brain's need for closure
— the reader can't NOT try to answer. This library organizes question hooks by function
and context.

---

## Category 1: "Have You Ever" Questions
Tap into shared experience. The reader nods before they know they're engaged.

1. "Have you ever poured hours into a project only to realize nobody asked for it?"
2. "Have you ever explained your product to someone and watched their eyes glaze over?"
3. "Have you ever sent an email and immediately wished you could unsend it?"
4. "Have you ever been in a meeting where everyone agreed but nobody actually believed?"
5. "Have you ever looked at a competitor's success and thought, 'Why not us?'"
6. "Have you ever lost a deal and never found out why?"
7. "Have you ever stared at a blank page knowing exactly what you want to say but not how to start?"
8. "Have you ever hit 'publish' and heard nothing but crickets?"

## Category 2: "What If" Questions
Open possibility. Pull the reader into a better future.

9. "What if your best customers could do your marketing for you?"
10. "What if the reason your content isn't working has nothing to do with the content?"
11. "What if you could cut your sales cycle in half by changing one thing?"
12. "What if the meeting you're about to attend didn't need to happen?"
13. "What if your About page was the highest-converting page on your site?"
14. "What if the data you're ignoring is the data that matters most?"
15. "What if simplicity was your competitive advantage?"
16. "What if the problem isn't your team's execution — it's the story you're telling them?"

## Category 3: "Why" Questions
Challenge the status quo. Create productive discomfort.

17. "Why do 73% of startups fail at marketing before they fail at product?"
18. "Why does everyone say 'storytelling matters' but nobody teaches you how to do it?"
19. "Why is your competitor's product worse but their brand stronger?"
20. "Why do the best ideas often come from the quietest person in the room?"
21. "Why do we spend 80% of our budget on acquisition and 20% on retention when retention is 5x more profitable?"
22. "Why do landing pages with fewer words often convert better?"
23. "Why does your team write great emails to each other but terrible emails to customers?"

## Category 4: Provocative Questions
Challenge directly. Not for the timid.

24. "When was the last time your marketing actually changed someone's mind?"
25. "If your brand disappeared tomorrow, would anyone notice?"
26. "Can you describe what your company does in one sentence without using jargon?"
27. "Do your customers actually understand your value proposition — or are they just used to you?"
28. "Is your content strategy a strategy, or is it just a calendar?"
29. "Are you building a brand or just producing content?"
30. "If you were your own customer, would you buy from you?"

## Category 5: "Which / Who" Questions
Force a choice or create identification.

31. "Which matters more: what you sell or how you talk about it?"
32. "Who on your team could tell your company's story in 60 seconds? Could you?"
33. "Which would you choose: 10,000 followers or 100 customers who can't stop talking about you?"
34. "Who is your customer before they find you? What are they searching for?"
35. "Which of your products has the best story? And why isn't it your best-seller?"

## Category 6: "How" Questions
Promise a method. Imply that the answer follows.

36. "How do the fastest-growing SaaS companies onboard new users?"
37. "How do you write a subject line that gets a 40% open rate?"
38. "How did a three-person team outmarket a company with 200 employees?"
39. "How do you price a product when the competition is free?"
40. "How do you tell a story in 15 seconds?"
41. "How do you make data feel like a story?"
42. "How do the best pitchers handle 'no'?"

## Category 7: Rhetorical Questions
Not expecting an answer — making a point.

43. "If not now, when?"
44. "What are we waiting for?"
45. "How long are we going to accept this as normal?"
46. "Isn't it time we tried something different?"
47. "What's the worst that could happen if you start today?"
48. "If the best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago, what are you planting today?"

## Category 8: Data-Driven Questions
The number creates the hook; the question frames it.

49. "Did you know that 80% of website visitors never scroll past the first screen?"
50. "What would you do with an extra $200K in revenue from the same traffic?"
51. "If 68% of your email list has never opened a message, are they really your list?"
52. "How is it possible that companies spending $50K/month on content see zero pipeline impact?"
53. "Why do the top 10% of salespeople close 80% of the deals — and what do they do differently?"

---

## Question Hook Formula
**[Emotional trigger] + [Specific detail] + [Implied promise of answer]**

Example: "Why do 73% [specific] of startups [relevant audience] fail at marketing [emotional trigger] before they fail at product [unexpected twist]?" → Implies: keep reading and you'll learn why — and how to avoid it.

## When Questions Fail
- **Too vague:** "Want to grow your business?" (Every human: "Yes, obviously." No curiosity gap.)
- **Too obvious:** "Is customer service important?" (The answer is yes. No reason to keep reading.)
- **Too negative:** "Are you failing at everything?" (Creates shame, not curiosity.)
- **Answerable with a simple "no":** "Have you tried our product?" ("No." Closed loop.)

## Fix: Make the question specific, surprising, or challenging enough that the reader's answer is "I don't know — tell me."
