# Duarte-Style Presentation Transformations

## Purpose
Before/after examples showing how to transform flat, data-heavy, or corporate
presentations into narrative-driven experiences. Based on Nancy Duarte's
principles from *Resonate* and *slide:ology*.

---

## Transformation 1: The Data Dump → The Insight Story

### Before (8 slides of charts)
- Slide 1: "Q3 Revenue by Region" [bar chart]
- Slide 2: "Q3 Expenses by Department" [pie chart]
- Slide 3: "YoY Growth Comparison" [line graph]
- Slide 4: "Customer Acquisition Metrics" [table]
- Slide 5–8: More charts, more tables, more data.

**Problem:** Audience sees numbers. Audience does not see meaning.

### After (8 slides with narrative)
- Slide 1: **"Q3 had a surprise."** [Black slide, white text]
- Slide 2: **"Revenue grew 18% — but not where we expected."** [Single bar chart: LATAM highlighted]
- Slide 3: **"LATAM outperformed every region. Why?"** [Photo of São Paulo team]
- Slide 4: **"Three things the São Paulo team did differently."** [Three icons]
- Slide 5: **"1. They localized the onboarding story."** [Before/after screenshots]
- Slide 6: **"2. They cut the sales cycle by telling customer stories instead of product features."** [Quote from sales lead]
- Slide 7: **"3. They partnered with local influencers who told OUR story better than we could."** [Social media screenshots]
- Slide 8: **"The question for Q4: How do we make every region perform like São Paulo?"** [Map with rollout timeline]

**Transformation principle:** Don't show all the data. Find the ONE insight in the data and build a story around it.

## Transformation 2: The Feature Walkthrough → The Customer Journey

### Before (12 slides)
- Slide 1: "Product Update: New Features"
- Slide 2: "Feature 1: Enhanced Dashboard"
- Slide 3: "Feature 2: Automated Reporting"
- Slide 4: "Feature 3: Team Collaboration"
- Slides 5–12: More features, screenshots, specs.

**Problem:** Nobody remembers features. They remember experiences.

### After (12 slides)
- Slide 1: **"Meet Sarah. She manages a team of 12."** [Photo]
- Slide 2: **"Every Monday, Sarah spends 3 hours pulling reports."** [Clock visual]
- Slide 3: **"She compiles data from four tools into one spreadsheet."** [Screenshot of messy spreadsheet]
- Slide 4: **"By the time the report is ready, the data is already old."** [Calendar showing delay]
- Slide 5: **"What if the report was ready before Sarah got to work?"** [Transition slide]
- Slide 6: **"Automated Reporting: the data is compiled overnight."** [Product screenshot]
- Slide 7: **"Sarah opens her laptop Monday morning to this."** [Clean dashboard]
- Slide 8: **"Instead of pulling data, she analyzes it."** [Sarah working, not frustrated]
- Slide 9: **"She shares it with her team in one click."** [Collaboration feature]
- Slide 10: **"Her team sees their own metrics in real time."** [Team view]
- Slide 11: **"Sarah's Monday went from 3 hours of data pulling to 30 minutes of decision-making."**
- Slide 12: **"Be like Sarah."** [CTA: start free trial]

**Transformation principle:** Don't present features. Present a character using the features to solve a real problem.

## Transformation 3: The All-Hands Update → The Movement Rally

### Before (15 slides)
- Company metrics (revenue, headcount, NPS)
- Department updates (sales, marketing, engineering, support)
- OKR review
- Next quarter priorities
- "Any questions?"

**Problem:** Nobody is inspired. Everyone checks their phone.

### After (15 slides)
- Slide 1: **Customer quote** — the most powerful thing a customer said this quarter
- Slide 2: **"Here's where we are."** — Three key metrics, contextualized
- Slide 3: **Customer story** — One real customer, what changed for them
- Slide 4: **"What we learned this quarter."** — Top 3 insights (not just numbers)
- Slide 5: **"What we're proud of."** — Named individuals and teams
- Slide 6: **"What's still hard."** — Honest about challenges
- Slide 7: **"What's coming."** — Vision for next quarter framed as opportunity
- Slide 8: **Employee spotlight** — Someone who exemplifies the values
- Slide 9–13: Department updates (one slide each, headline + one key story)
- Slide 14: **"Where we're headed."** — Vision slide
- Slide 15: **"Here's what I'm asking each of you."** — Specific, actionable ask

**Transformation principle:** An all-hands is not a report. It's a rally. Open with a customer, close with a vision, sandwich updates in between.

## The Five Duarte Transformation Principles

1. **Find the story inside the data.** Every dataset has an insight. Find the ONE insight and build the narrative around it.
2. **Create a character.** Even in B2B, give the audience a person to follow. "Sarah" is more memorable than "the user."
3. **Use contrast.** Every slide should feel like either "what is" or "what could be." The oscillation creates energy.
4. **Design for glance.** If the audience can't understand the slide in 3 seconds, it's too complex.
5. **End with the audience.** The final slide should be about what the AUDIENCE should do, think, or feel — not about the presenter or the company.

## Slide Design Rules for Story Presentations

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| One idea per slide | Clarity, pacing, and memory |
| Minimal text (max 30 words) | The speaker is the narrative; slides are the backdrop |
| Full-bleed images for emotional moments | Visuals create feeling faster than text |
| Black/blank slides for emphasis | Force the audience to look at the speaker |
| Data in single-number format | "34%" on a black background > a complex chart |
| Quotes in large type | Let the customer's voice fill the screen |
