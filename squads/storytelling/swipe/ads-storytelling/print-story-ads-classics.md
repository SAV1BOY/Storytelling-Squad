# Classic Print Story Ads: Analysis

## Purpose
Timeless print advertisements that used storytelling to sell. These ads prove that
narrative works in static, single-image formats — and the principles translate to
every medium.

---

## Ad 1: Volkswagen — "Think Small" (1959, DDB)

**Headline:** Think Small.
**Visual:** Small VW Beetle in a sea of white space.
**Body copy (opening):** "Our little car isn't so much of a novelty anymore. A couple of dozen college kids don't try to squeeze inside it. The guy at the gas station doesn't ask where the gas goes. Nobody even stares at our shape."

**Story elements:**
- **Character:** The car itself, positioned as the humble underdog.
- **Conflict:** Every other car ad screams "bigger, faster, more." VW whispers.
- **Voice:** Self-deprecating, honest, conversational. Anti-advertising.
- **Contrast:** In an era of chrome and excess, "small" was provocative.

**Why it's a storytelling masterpiece:**
- The ad tells a story about what the car ISN'T — and makes that the advantage.
- Vulnerability (admitting the car is small, odd-looking) builds trust.
- The white space IS the narrative: there's room to think, just like the car suggests.
- Broke every advertising rule of the 1950s and created a new category.

**Takeaway:** The bravest brand story is the one that admits what everyone already thinks — and reframes it.

## Ad 2: Apple — "Think Different" (1997, TBWA\Chiat\Day)

**Headline:** Think Different.
**Visual:** Black-and-white portraits of Einstein, Gandhi, Lennon, MLK, Picasso.
**Body copy:** "Here's to the crazy ones. The misfits. The rebels. The troublemakers. The round pegs in the square holes. The ones who see things differently..."

**Story elements:**
- **Characters:** Real historical figures positioned as Apple's spiritual ancestors.
- **Conflict:** Conformity vs. creativity. "The people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do."
- **Voice:** Epic-mythic, inspirational, movement-building.
- **Implied story:** You (the reader) are one of these people. Apple is your tool.

**Why it's a storytelling masterpiece:**
- No product is shown. No feature is named. The ad sells identity.
- Each historical figure is a mini-story that the viewer fills in mentally.
- The copy is a manifesto — it gives the reader a tribe to belong to.
- Launched during Apple's near-death moment. The brand was the underdog, just like its heroes.

**Takeaway:** When you can't win on features, win on identity. Tell the customer who they are.

## Ad 3: Ogilvy — Rolls-Royce "At 60 Miles an Hour" (1958)

**Headline:** "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock."
**Visual:** The car, parked in front of a grocer's, a woman stepping out.
**Body copy:** 13 numbered facts about the engineering, presented in a calm, factual tone.

**Story elements:**
- **Character:** The car as protagonist — quiet, confident, understated.
- **Conflict:** How does one communicate extreme quality without shouting?
- **Voice:** Data-informed storyteller. Factual, specific, unhurried.
- **Proof structure:** 13 facts, each a micro-story of engineering obsession.

**Why it's a storytelling masterpiece:**
- The headline is a one-sentence story: you're in the car, at speed, and the only sound is the clock. You feel the experience.
- The body copy doesn't sell — it informs. And information, delivered this well, IS selling.
- Ogilvy reportedly spent three weeks reading about the car before writing. Research is story fuel.
- The ordinary setting (a grocer's) contrasts with extraordinary quality.

**Takeaway:** One specific, vivid detail is worth more than twenty claims.

## Ad 4: De Beers — "A Diamond Is Forever" (1947, N.W. Ayer)

**Headline:** A Diamond Is Forever.
**Visual:** A couple in silhouette, a diamond ring catching light.

**Story elements:**
- **The meta-story:** Before this campaign, diamonds were not the standard engagement gift. De Beers didn't just advertise a product — they wrote a cultural narrative.
- **Conflict:** How do you make a compressed piece of carbon feel like the ultimate symbol of eternal love?
- **Voice:** Romantic, aspirational, timeless.
- **Cultural engineering:** The ad created a tradition that didn't previously exist at scale.

**Why it's a storytelling masterpiece:**
- Four words created a multi-billion-dollar cultural norm.
- The story isn't in the ad — it's in every proposal that follows.
- The phrase maps the product's physical property (durability) to an emotional desire (eternal love).
- The most successful piece of commercial storytelling in the 20th century.

**Takeaway:** The most powerful stories don't sell products. They create rituals.

## Ad 5: Nike — "Just Do It" (1988, Wieden+Kennedy)

**Headline:** Just Do It.
**Visual:** Various athletes and everyday people in motion.

**Story elements:**
- **Character:** You. The reader. Not an athlete — a person with potential.
- **Conflict:** Hesitation. The gap between wanting to and doing it.
- **Voice:** Three words. Imperative. No explanation needed.
- **Implied story:** Every time you don't run, don't try, don't start — you know. Just do it.

**Why it's a storytelling masterpiece:**
- The entire narrative is compressed into three words — and the audience completes the story.
- Inclusive: works for a marathoner and for someone who hasn't run in ten years.
- The phrase is the CTA, the brand voice, and the story in one.
- Created an identity: Nike people are people who do.

**Takeaway:** The shortest stories are the stickiest. If you can compress your narrative to a phrase that the audience completes themselves, you've won.

## Principles from Classic Print Ads

| Principle | Example | Modern Application |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Specificity beats generality** | "At 60 mph, the loudest noise..." | Use exact numbers in ads, not "amazing results" |
| **Vulnerability builds trust** | VW "Think Small" | Admit what the audience already thinks |
| **Identity > features** | Apple "Think Different" | Sell who the customer becomes, not what they buy |
| **Ritual creation** | De Beers "A Diamond Is Forever" | Attach your product to a life moment |
| **Compression** | Nike "Just Do It" | Three-word stories that the audience completes |
| **White space is narrative** | VW ad layout | Let design carry meaning; don't fill every pixel |
| **Research fuels story** | Ogilvy's Rolls-Royce facts | Know 100 details; use the one that sings |
