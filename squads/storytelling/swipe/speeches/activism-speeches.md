# Activism Speeches: Ganz Pattern Analysis

## Purpose
Analysis of activism speeches that follow or parallel Marshall Ganz's public narrative
framework: Story of Self, Story of Us, Story of Now. These speeches mobilize
communities to act.

---

## 1. Cesar Chavez — "The Mexican American and the Church" (1968)

**Story of Self:** Chavez described growing up as a migrant farmworker, seeing his family exploited, watching his parents' faith guide them through poverty.

**Story of Us:** "We are not combating greed and power for our own sake, but for the sake of all workers." He connected the farmworkers' cause to the broader labor movement and to the moral authority of the church.

**Story of Now:** "The time is now for the Church to show its commitment to the poor." Direct ask to religious leaders to join the boycott. Specific action, specific audience, specific timeline.

**Ganz pattern analysis:**
- Self: values learned through personal experience (faith, dignity, family).
- Us: shared identity as workers, as people of faith, as Mexicans and Americans.
- Now: the grape boycott requires support TODAY — moral urgency.

**Technique:** Chavez used religious language and moral framing to speak to his specific audience (the church). The story was adapted to the listener's values.

## 2. Dolores Huerta — Various Addresses (1960s–present)

**Signature phrase:** "Si, se puede." (Yes, we can / Yes, it is possible.)

**Story of Self:** Huerta's narrative centered on her identity as a teacher who watched her students come to school hungry because their farmworker parents were underpaid. "I thought, 'I can't teach hungry children.'" She left teaching to organize.

**Story of Us:** "This is not just about wages. This is about dignity. Every person who picks the food you eat deserves the respect of a fair wage."

**Story of Now:** "We don't need their permission. We need our own courage. Walk out. March. Vote. Today."

**Ganz pattern analysis:**
- Self: the teaching experience revealed values — justice, dignity, action.
- Us: expanded from farmworkers to all working people, then to all people of conscience.
- Now: always specific, always actionable, always today.

**Technique:** Huerta's genius was the bridge from Self to Us. Her personal moment (hungry students) was instantly universal.

## 3. Greta Thunberg — UN Climate Action Summit (2019)

**"How dare you."**

**Story of Self:** "I shouldn't be up here. I should be back in school on the other side of the ocean." Her story: a teenager with Asperger's who decided to sit outside the Swedish Parliament alone, every Friday.

**Story of Us:** "You have stolen my dreams and my childhood with your empty words. And yet I'm one of the lucky ones. People are suffering. People are dying."

**Story of Now:** "We are in the beginning of a mass extinction, and all you can talk about is money and fairy tales of eternal economic growth. How dare you."

**Ganz pattern analysis:**
- Self: a child who should be in school — moral authority through innocence.
- Us: her generation, and the global majority who will bear climate consequences.
- Now: visceral urgency. Not persuasion. Indictment. "How dare you" is not an invitation to think. It's a demand to act.

**Technique:** Status inversion. A teenager lectures world leaders. The power dynamic (low status speaking to high status) creates moral authority.

## 4. Bryan Stevenson — Multiple Advocacy Speeches

**Context:** Stevenson is a lawyer and founder of the Equal Justice Initiative, advocating for wrongly condemned prisoners.

**Story of Self:** Stevenson tells the story of meeting Henry, a man on death row in Alabama. Henry was 14 when he was convicted. "I was a young lawyer, terrified, unprepared. Henry looked at me and said, 'Don't give up on me.'"

**Story of Us:** "For every nine people we've executed, we've identified one innocent person on death row. We would not tolerate a 10% error rate in any other system."

**Story of Now:** "We are defined not by how we treat the rich and the privileged, but by how we treat the poor, the disfavored, the condemned."

**Ganz pattern analysis:**
- Self: the encounter with Henry — a specific moment where values crystallized.
- Us: "we" are a society that tolerates injustice. The audience is implicated AND empowered.
- Now: the ask is moral identity, not just donation. "What kind of society do we want to be?"

**Technique:** One individual's story (Henry) becomes the lens through which a systemic issue becomes personal.

## 5. Barack Obama — 2008 Iowa Caucus Victory Speech

**This speech is a near-perfect Ganz implementation.**

**Story of Self:** Brief — he acknowledged his improbable candidacy.
**Story of Us:** "They said this day would never come. They said our sights were set too high. They said this country was too divided, too disillusioned to ever come together around a common purpose."
**Story of Now:** "This was the moment when we finally beat back the politics of fear, and doubt, and cynicism; the politics where we tear each other down instead of lifting this country up. This was the moment."

**Ganz pattern analysis:**
- Self: minimized (the movement is the hero, not the candidate).
- Us: maximized (the speech is almost entirely about "we").
- Now: the Iowa result is the proof that Now is possible. "You did this."

**Technique:** Crediting the audience as the hero. The speaker facilitates; the community acts.

## Ganz Public Narrative Framework Summary

| Component | Purpose | Time Allocation | Key Question |
|-----------|---------|----------------|-------------|
| **Story of Self** | Establish personal values and credibility | 15–20% | "Why am I called to this?" |
| **Story of Us** | Create shared identity and community | 30–40% | "What values do we share?" |
| **Story of Now** | Create urgency and demand action | 40–50% | "Why must we act now?" |

## Activation Principles
1. **Values first, issues second.** Don't start with the policy. Start with the value the policy serves.
2. **Specific action beats general aspiration.** "Show up Thursday at 6 p.m." > "Get involved."
3. **The audience is the protagonist.** The speaker's job is to connect, not to be the hero.
4. **Emotion is not weakness.** It is the mechanism of mobilization.
5. **Story of Us requires genuine shared experience.** Don't claim a "we" that doesn't exist.
