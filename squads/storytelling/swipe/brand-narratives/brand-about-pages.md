# Best "About" Pages with Narrative: Swipe Analysis

## Purpose
Analysis of About pages that use storytelling instead of corporate boilerplate. The
About page is the most under-utilized storytelling asset on most websites.

---

## Why the About Page Matters
The About page is typically the second or third most visited page on any website.
Visitors who go to the About page are asking one question: "Can I trust these people?"
Story answers that question better than credentials, logos, or mission statements.

---

## Example 1: The Founder-Journey About Page

### Structure:
1. **Opening:** The specific moment the company was born.
2. **The problem:** What the founder experienced personally.
3. **The journey:** Failed attempts, pivots, and learning.
4. **The breakthrough:** What made the current approach work.
5. **The team:** Introduced as characters, not resumes.
6. **The invitation:** What the company does for the reader.

### Example copy:
> **How we got here**
>
> In 2017, our founder Daniela was a VP of Marketing at a SaaS company. She
> had six direct reports, a $2M budget, and no way to prove that any of it
> was working.
>
> "I could show impressions," she says. "I could show clicks. But when the
> CEO asked, 'Is marketing driving revenue?' I couldn't answer with confidence."
>
> She tried every analytics tool on the market. Built custom dashboards. Hired
> a data analyst. The data was there — it was just scattered across twelve
> platforms in twelve different formats.
>
> So she built a prototype. A single screen that connected the dots between
> marketing spend and closed revenue. She showed it to three CMO friends.
> All three asked, "Can I use this?"
>
> That prototype became [Company Name].
>
> Today, we help 800+ marketing teams answer the only question that matters:
> "Is this working?" Not with more data. With clarity.

## Example 2: The Values-Driven About Page

### Structure:
1. **The belief:** What the company stands for, stated as a declaration.
2. **The why:** Why this belief matters in the current market.
3. **The proof:** How the belief manifests in the work.
4. **The people:** Team members as embodiments of the values.
5. **The join:** Invitation to the reader.

### Example copy:
> **What we believe**
>
> We believe that every business has a story worth telling. And that most
> businesses are telling it wrong — not because they lack talent, but because
> nobody taught them the structure.
>
> We exist to close that gap. To take the frameworks that Hollywood screenwriters,
> TED speakers, and master storytellers use and make them accessible to any
> brand, in any industry, at any stage.
>
> We are a squad of strategists, writers, and story architects who believe
> that clear beats clever, that specific beats general, and that every audience
> deserves a story that respects their time.
>
> If your current About page starts with "Founded in [year], [Company Name] is
> a leading provider of…" — we should talk.

## Example 3: The Customer-Centric About Page

### Structure:
1. **The reader's problem:** Start with the visitor, not the company.
2. **The company's role:** Positioned as the guide, not the hero.
3. **The process:** How the company helps, in three steps.
4. **The results:** What success looks like for the customer.
5. **The team:** Brief, human, focused on relevance to the customer.

### Example copy:
> **You have a story. We help you tell it.**
>
> You're here because you know your product is good. Maybe great. But when
> you try to explain why it matters, something gets lost. The pitch doesn't
> land. The website doesn't convert. The deck puts people to sleep.
>
> That's where we come in.
>
> We're a storytelling studio that works with companies who know they have
> something worth saying but haven't found the words yet. Our process is simple:
>
> 1. We listen. (Deeply. Probably more than you expect.)
> 2. We find the story hiding inside your data, your customers, and your team.
> 3. We craft it into content that moves people — to click, to buy, to believe.
>
> Our clients have increased conversion by an average of 42%. But the metric
> we care about most is this: can your customer tell someone else what you do,
> in one sentence, without checking your website?

## About Page Storytelling Principles

1. **Start with a person, not a company.** "Daniela was a VP of Marketing" > "Founded in 2017, [Company] is…"
2. **Answer "why," not "what."** The reader doesn't care about your founding year. They care about why you exist.
3. **Include the messy middle.** The struggles, pivots, and failures make the story believable.
4. **Show the team as humans.** Photos of people doing things > professional headshots. Quirky bios > resume bullets.
5. **End with the reader.** The About page should transition from "our story" to "your story" by the final section.
6. **One CTA.** The About page should have a single, clear next step: contact, demo, newsletter, etc.

---

## About Page Checklist
1. Does the first sentence mention a person, not the company?
2. Is there a specific origin moment or founding story?
3. Are values shown through story, not listed as bullet points?
4. Does the team section feel human (not corporate headshot gallery)?
5. Is the reader addressed directly ("you") by the halfway point?
6. Is there one clear CTA?
7. If you removed the company name, would this sound different from every other About page in your industry?
