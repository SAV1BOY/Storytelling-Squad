# Product Story Pages: Swipe Analysis

## Purpose
Product pages that go beyond features and specs to tell origin stories, design
narratives, and purpose-driven content that makes the product feel meaningful.

---

## Example 1: The Artisan / Craft Product

### Structure: Origin Story Page

**Section 1: The Founding Moment**
> "In 2016, our founder was sitting in a coffee shop in Porto, watching the barista
> hand-pour a ceramic cup he'd been using for twenty years. The cup had a small chip
> on the rim. When asked about it, the barista smiled and said, 'That's where the
> story lives.' That conversation became a company."

**Section 2: The Problem We Saw**
> "The world is drowning in disposable products designed to be replaced. We make
> things designed to be kept."

**Section 3: The Process**
> "Each piece is hand-thrown by one of twelve ceramicists in our Porto workshop.
> The clay comes from the Alentejo region. The glazes are mixed on-site using
> recipes that predate the workshop itself. From clay to kiln to your table:
> 14 days, 23 steps, zero shortcuts."

**Section 4: The Product**
Specific product details — dimensions, materials, care instructions — but framed
within the story context.

**Section 5: The Invitation**
> "Buy a cup. Use it every day. Chip it. Stain it. Let the story live."

### Why it works:
- The product is positioned as a character with a biography.
- The manufacturing process is a narrative journey, not a spec sheet.
- The philosophy (keep, don't replace) gives the purchase emotional meaning.
- The CTA is a continuation of the story, not a break from it.

## Example 2: The Tech Product with a Mission

### Structure: Problem-to-Product Narrative

**Section 1: The Statistic That Started Everything**
> "40% of food produced globally is wasted before it reaches a plate. That's
> 1.3 billion tons per year. We started with one question: what if your fridge
> could help fix that?"

**Section 2: The Team Behind It**
> "Three food scientists, two engineers, and a chef who was tired of throwing
> out produce every Sunday. That's who built [Product]. Not in a Silicon Valley
> incubator — in a kitchen, surrounded by wilting lettuce."

**Section 3: How It Works (Story-Wrapped)**
> "The sensor reads ethylene levels — the gas produce emits as it ripens. When
> your avocados are two days from perfect, the app tells you. When your berries
> need to be eaten today, you'll know before breakfast. No more opening the
> crisper drawer and finding a science experiment."

**Section 4: Real Impact**
> "Beta users reduced their food waste by an average of 37% in the first month.
> That's roughly $64/month in groceries that made it to a plate instead of a
> trash can."

**Section 5: CTA**
> "Join the waitlist. Your fridge will thank you. Your wallet will too."

### Why it works:
- The mission comes first. The product is the solution to a human problem.
- Technical details (ethylene sensors) are wrapped in a relatable context (your avocados).
- Real data anchors the emotional appeal.
- Humor keeps it accessible.

## Example 3: The Premium / Luxury Product

### Structure: Design Philosophy Narrative

**Section 1: The Detail**
> "The clasp took seven months to design. Seven months for a piece of metal
> smaller than a thumbnail. Our lead designer made forty-three prototypes.
> She rejected forty-two. The one that survived? It opens with a single
> press and closes with a sound — a soft, definitive click — that tells you
> it's secure without looking."

**Section 2: The Why**
> "We believe that the details you don't consciously notice are the ones that
> make you love a product. The weight distribution that makes it feel balanced.
> The curve that follows the natural line of your wrist. The color that looks
> different in morning light than evening light — on purpose."

**Section 3: Materials**
> "Italian calfskin from a tannery in Tuscany that's been operating since 1823.
> Stainless steel from a foundry in Sheffield. Thread from a single mill in
> Kyoto that produces 200 meters per day — not because they can't produce more,
> but because more would compromise the twist."

**Section 4: The Guarantee**
> "We guarantee this product for life. Not because we're generous. Because we
> engineered it not to fail."

### Why it works:
- Obsessive detail communicates quality without saying "high-quality."
- The narrative of the clasp's development is more persuasive than any feature list.
- Sourcing stories (Tuscany, Sheffield, Kyoto) create a sense of provenance.
- The guarantee reframes as engineering confidence, not marketing promise.

## Product Story Page Patterns

| Pattern | Description | Best For |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Origin story** | How the product came to exist | Artisan, founder-led, DTC |
| **Problem-solution** | The world's problem → our answer | Tech, health, sustainability |
| **Design obsession** | The detail that took longest | Premium, luxury, craft |
| **Customer journey** | One customer's experience, start to finish | SaaS, services, subscriptions |
| **Ingredient/material story** | Where the components come from | Food, beauty, fashion |

## Product Story Page Checklist
1. Does the page answer "why does this exist?" before "what does this do?"
2. Is there at least one specific, vivid origin detail?
3. Are features framed as outcomes for the customer?
4. Is there a human character in the story (founder, designer, customer)?
5. Does the story justify the price without mentioning the price?
6. Is the CTA a natural continuation of the narrative?
