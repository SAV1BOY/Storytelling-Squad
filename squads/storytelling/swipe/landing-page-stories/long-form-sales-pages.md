# Long-Form Sales Pages with Narrative: Swipe Analysis

## Purpose
Analysis of long-form sales pages (1,500–5,000+ words) that use extended narrative
to build desire, handle objections, and close — primarily used in info products,
coaching, courses, and high-ticket services.

---

## The Long-Form Narrative Sales Page Structure

### 1. The Hook (Top 10%)
Opens with a story or bold claim that identifies the reader's pain.

**Example opening:**
> "At 2 a.m. on a Wednesday, I was sitting in my car in a Walmart parking lot,
> crying into a gas station burrito, wondering how I'd gone from 'employee of
> the year' to 'can't make rent.' That was eighteen months ago. Last month,
> my business crossed $50K in monthly recurring revenue. This page tells you
> exactly how — and more importantly, how you can do it too."

### 2. The Problem (10–25%)
Agitate the pain. Describe the reader's current reality with specificity.

**Techniques:**
- "If you're like most [role], you've probably tried…"
- List the failed solutions (courses that didn't work, tools that promised results)
- Describe the emotional toll: frustration, doubt, imposter syndrome
- Name the internal belief holding them back: "Maybe this works for other people, but not for me."

**Example:**
> "You've read the books. You've taken the courses. You've followed the gurus.
> And every Monday morning, you're still staring at the same pipeline, the same
> conversion rate, and the same nagging feeling that you're missing something
> everyone else figured out."

### 3. The Turn (25–35%)
Introduce the insight or framework that changes everything.

**Example:**
> "Here's what I discovered after failing for two years: the problem wasn't my
> effort. It wasn't my market. It wasn't even my offer. It was the story I was
> telling about my offer. I was listing features when I should have been sharing
> transformations."

### 4. The Solution (35–60%)
Present the product/service as the vehicle for transformation. This is where
modules, features, and deliverables are listed — but framed as narrative outcomes.

**Instead of:** "Module 3: Email Marketing Fundamentals"
**Write:** "Week 3: The Email Sequence That Turned a 2% Open Rate into 41% — And Made Subscribers Reply 'How Did You Know?'"

**Each module/feature should follow:**
- What you'll learn → What that enables you to do → What that means for your results

### 5. Social Proof Stack (60–70%)
Testimonials, case studies, and results — presented as mini-stories.

**Narrative testimonial format:**
> "[Name] came to us with [specific problem]. Within [timeframe], she [specific
> action using the product], which led to [specific result]. In her words:
> '[direct quote].'"

### 6. Objection Handling (70–80%)
Address the top 5 objections through story and reframe.

| Objection | Narrative Response |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "It's too expensive" | "One client told me she couldn't afford it. She calculated what her current approach was costing her: $3,200/month in wasted ad spend. The program paid for itself in week two." |
| "I don't have time" | "The average participant spends 4 hours per week. Compare that to the 20+ hours you're spending doing things that aren't working." |
| "Will this work for my industry?" | "We've had [industry A], [industry B], and [industry C] all see results. The framework adapts because it's based on human psychology, not platform tricks." |

### 7. The Close (80–100%)
Restate the transformation. Present the offer clearly. Create urgency.

**Example closing:**
> "Six months from now, you'll be in one of two places. You'll either be right
> where you are — same pipeline, same frustration, same Sunday-night dread.
> Or you'll be the person who decided that today was the day the story changed.
> The program starts [date]. There are [N] spots. This is the last time we're
> offering it at this price."

## Long-Form Page Pacing

| Section | % of Page | Emotional State |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| Hook | 0–10% | Curiosity, recognition |
| Problem | 10–25% | Frustration, "that's me" |
| Turn | 25–35% | Hope, intrigue |
| Solution | 35–60% | Excitement, desire |
| Proof | 60–70% | Trust, belief |
| Objections | 70–80% | Relief, confidence |
| Close | 80–100% | Urgency, commitment |

---

## Design Elements That Support Narrative

- **Pull quotes** from testimonials break up text and add visual proof.
- **Before/after screenshots** provide concrete evidence.
- **Video testimonials** embedded at the proof section add dimension.
- **Progress indicators** ("You're halfway through — the best part is next") maintain reading momentum.
- **Sticky CTA bar** ensures the action is always one click away.

---

## Common Mistakes in Long-Form Sales Pages
1. **All story, no proof.** Narrative without evidence is fiction.
2. **Burying the offer.** The reader should know what you're selling by the 35% mark.
3. **Feature dumps without transformation framing.** Nobody buys modules. They buy outcomes.
4. **Fake scarcity.** "Only 3 spots left" when the countdown resets every week destroys trust.
5. **Ignoring mobile.** 60%+ of readers are on phones. Long paragraphs = scrolling death.
