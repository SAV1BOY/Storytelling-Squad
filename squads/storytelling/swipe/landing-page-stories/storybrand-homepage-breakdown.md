# StoryBrand Homepage — Narrative Breakdown

## Overview

StoryBrand's own homepage is the canonical example of the SB7 (StoryBrand 7-Part) Framework in action. Donald Miller's company practices what it preaches — the homepage follows the exact story structure taught in the "Building a StoryBrand" methodology. The customer is the hero, StoryBrand is the guide, and every section maps to one of the seven story elements. This breakdown analyzes how the homepage translates narrative theory into conversion-focused web design.

## Structural Breakdown

### Beat 1: The Hero Wants Something (Above the Fold)
The headline immediately addresses the customer's desire — typically framed as "Grow your business" or "Get your message clear." The hero (the visitor) has a want: business growth through clear messaging. The language is second-person ("you"), placing the visitor in the protagonist role from the first word.

### Beat 2: The Problem is Named
The page quickly identifies the visitor's problem at three levels:
- **External problem**: Marketing messages are not working
- **Internal problem**: The visitor feels confused, frustrated, or uncertain about their messaging
- **Philosophical problem**: It is unjust that good products fail because of unclear communication

This three-layer problem articulation creates deep resonance — the visitor feels truly understood.

### Beat 3: The Guide Appears
StoryBrand positions itself as the guide through two signals:
- **Empathy**: "We understand how frustrating it is when your marketing does not work"
- **Authority**: Social proof (number of businesses helped, recognizable logos, Donald Miller's credentials)

The guide does not compete with the hero for attention. It demonstrates competence and compassion.

### Beat 4: The Plan
The homepage presents a simple three-step plan:
1. Schedule a call / attend a workshop
2. Learn the framework
3. Clarify your message

The plan reduces the perceived complexity of the transformation. Three steps signal "this is manageable."

### Beat 5: The Call to Action
Two types of CTAs appear:
- **Direct CTA**: "Schedule a Call" or "Buy the Book" — the primary conversion action
- **Transitional CTA**: "Download the Free PDF" or "Watch the Video" — for visitors not ready to commit

The direct CTA appears multiple times. The transitional CTA captures leads who need more nurturing.

### Beat 6: Success (Stakes — Positive)
The page paints a picture of what life looks like after working with StoryBrand: clear messaging, growing revenue, confident marketing, a team aligned around a single story. These are aspirational outcomes stated concretely.

### Beat 7: Failure (Stakes — Negative)
The page also implies what happens without clear messaging: wasted marketing spend, lost customers, continued confusion. This creates urgency by showing the cost of inaction. The failure stakes are handled subtly — hinted at rather than hammered.

## Framework Used

**StoryBrand SB7 Framework**

The seven elements, in order:
1. **A Character** (the customer/visitor)
2. **Has a Problem** (external, internal, philosophical)
3. **And Meets a Guide** (empathy + authority)
4. **Who Gives Them a Plan** (simple steps)
5. **And Calls Them to Action** (direct + transitional)
6. **That Helps Them Avoid Failure** (negative stakes)
7. **And Ends in a Success** (positive transformation)

The homepage executes all seven in a linear scroll pattern, making the page itself a story the visitor walks through.

## Why It Works

1. **The visitor is the hero from word one**: No "we are" language in the hero section. Everything is framed around the visitor's desire and problem. This immediately differentiates from competitor sites that lead with company history.

2. **Three-layer problem articulation**: Most homepages address only the external problem (you need better marketing). StoryBrand also names the internal feeling (frustration, self-doubt) and the philosophical injustice (good businesses deserve to thrive). This creates "they really get me" resonance.

3. **The plan reduces friction**: By making the engagement path a clear 3-step process, the homepage removes the ambiguity that causes decision paralysis. The visitor knows exactly what happens next.

4. **Dual CTAs capture the full funnel**: Direct CTAs serve ready buyers. Transitional CTAs serve researchers. No visitor leaves without being offered an appropriate next step.

5. **Scroll equals story progression**: The page is designed so that scrolling feels like narrative movement. The visitor is literally progressing through a story, creating psychological momentum toward conversion.

## Key Techniques

### Technique: Hero-First Headlines
Write headlines that name the customer's desire, not your company's capabilities. Replace "We help businesses with..." with "Grow your business by..."

### Technique: Three-Layer Problem Stack
For every problem you address, articulate it at three levels:
- External (the practical issue)
- Internal (the emotional experience)
- Philosophical (why it should not be this way)

### Technique: Empathy Before Authority
When establishing your guide credentials, lead with empathy ("we understand") before authority ("we have helped 10,000 businesses"). The sequence matters — empathy earns the right to assert competence.

### Technique: The 3-Step Plan
Reduce any complex offering to three clear steps. Three is cognitively manageable and creates a sense of progression (beginning, middle, end). Each step should start with a verb.

### Technique: Stakes Sandwich
Show success first (aspirational), then hint at failure (urgency), then return to success (hope). This creates emotional oscillation that drives action.

## Marketing Application

| Application | How to Adapt |
|---|---|
| Any homepage | Map every section to one of the SB7 elements in order |
| Landing pages | Compress the 7 elements into a shorter format |
| Sales pages | Expand each element with more detail and social proof |
| Email sequences | Dedicate one email to each story element |
| Pitch decks | Structure slides to follow the 7-part framework |
| Product pages | Frame features as part of "the plan" the guide offers |

### Adaptation Template
1. Hero section: "[Desired outcome] for [your audience]"
2. Problem section: External problem → Internal feeling → Philosophical injustice
3. Guide section: Empathy statement + authority proof
4. Plan section: 3 numbered steps with verb-first labels
5. CTA section: Primary action button + secondary lead capture
6. Success section: Concrete outcomes with emotional resonance
7. Failure section: Subtle reminder of what inaction costs
