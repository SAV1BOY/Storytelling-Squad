# Agency Case Studies: Swipe Analysis

## Purpose
Agency case studies that demonstrate how storytelling transformed client outcomes.
Structured as narratives that sell the agency's thinking, not just their output.

---

## Example 1: Branding Agency — The Rename That Saved a Company

### The Story
> **When "TechLogix Solutions" Became "Relay"**
>
> The client was bleeding. Market share had dropped 12% in two years. Customer
> surveys revealed the problem wasn't the product — it was the perception. "When
> we asked customers to describe the brand in three words," says the agency's
> strategy lead, "the top answers were 'generic,' 'forgettable,' and 'fine.'"
>
> "Fine" is a death sentence in a crowded market.
>
> The agency spent six weeks in discovery. They interviewed 40 customers, 15
> employees, and 3 industry analysts. The insight that emerged: the product
> was exceptional at connecting disparate systems. But the name — "TechLogix
> Solutions" — sounded like every other B2B software company. It communicated
> nothing.
>
> The rename process was grueling. 200 name candidates reduced to 20, then 5,
> then 1: **Relay**. The word communicated the product's core function
> (connecting, passing, enabling) without jargon.
>
> The rebrand included: new visual identity, new website, new messaging
> framework, and a launch campaign built around the tagline "Everything
> connects."
>
> **Results (12 months post-launch):**
> - Brand awareness in target segment: +47%
> - Inbound demo requests: +83%
> - Customer description words: "connected," "modern," "clear"
> - Employee Net Promoter Score: +31 (team pride in the new brand)
> - Revenue: +22% YoY
>
> "The product didn't change," the CEO told the agency. "But for the first
> time, people could see what it actually does. You gave us a name that
> tells our story."

## Example 2: Content Agency — The Blog That Built a Sales Pipeline

### The Story
> **Zero to $1.2M in Pipeline from 12 Blog Posts**
>
> The client — a mid-market HR tech company — had been publishing two blog posts
> per week for three years. Total pipeline attributed to content: effectively zero.
>
> "They had 312 blog posts," the agency's content director recalls. "And not one
> of them answered a question their buyers were actually asking."
>
> The agency proposed a radical reduction: stop publishing twice a week. Publish
> once per month. But make each piece a narrative-driven, deeply researched
> guide that addressed a specific buying-stage question.
>
> **The approach:**
> - Interviewed 25 of the client's sales team to identify the top 12 questions
>   prospects asked before buying
> - Each blog post was structured as a story: a real company facing the question,
>   the journey to the answer, data supporting the conclusion
> - Posts averaged 2,500 words (vs. the previous 600-word average)
> - Each post included a custom calculator, template, or tool as a lead magnet
>
> **The first post** — "How Much Is Employee Turnover Actually Costing You?"
> — included a turnover cost calculator. It ranked #1 for the target keyword
> within 8 weeks and generated 340 leads in the first quarter.
>
> **12-month results (12 posts total):**
> - Organic traffic: +215%
> - Leads from content: 2,100+ (vs. ~30 in the prior year)
> - Pipeline attributed to content: $1.2M
> - Sales team usage: 9 of 12 posts were actively used in sales conversations
> - Client's internal reaction: "We published less and achieved infinitely more."

## Example 3: Performance Marketing Agency — Storytelling in Paid Media

### The Story
> **When We Stopped Selling and Started Telling**
>
> The client — an online education platform — was spending $180K/month on paid
> social with a CAC of $127. Industry benchmark was $85. Something was wrong.
>
> The agency audited the ad creative. Every ad followed the same pattern:
> feature → benefit → discount → CTA. "It was a factory line of forgettable
> ads," the creative director says. "Every ad looked like every other ed-tech ad."
>
> The agency proposed replacing 50% of the ad creative with student stories.
> Not testimonials — stories. Real students, filmed with their phones, talking
> about the moment they decided to enroll and what changed after.
>
> **Sample ad script (30 seconds):**
> "I was a receptionist. Not because I wanted to be — because I didn't think
> I could be anything else. Then I saw an ad. Not this one — a different one.
> And I clicked. Six months later, I'm a junior developer. Same desk. Different
> person."
>
> **Results (90-day test):**
> - CAC: $127 → $71 (44% reduction)
> - Click-through rate on story ads: 3.2x higher than feature ads
> - Video completion rate: 78% (vs. 31% for feature ads)
> - Enrollment from story ads: 2.1x higher conversion rate
> - Cost per enrollment: $127 → $59
>
> The agency scaled story ads to 80% of the creative mix. The client reduced
> ad spend by $40K/month while increasing enrollments by 35%.

## Agency Case Study Best Practices

| Element | What It Does | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Named strategist/director** | Humanizes the agency | "says the agency's content director" |
| **Client's initial state** | Shows the problem was real | "312 blog posts and zero pipeline" |
| **The insight** | Proves the agency thinks, not just executes | "The ads looked like every other ed-tech ad" |
| **The approach** | Shows methodology and strategic thinking | "Interviewed 25 salespeople" |
| **Honest friction** | Builds credibility | "The process was grueling" |
| **Hard metrics** | Proves ROI | "$1.2M pipeline from 12 posts" |
| **Client quote** | Provides external validation | "You gave us a name that tells our story" |

## Common Mistakes in Agency Case Studies
1. **Making the agency the hero.** The client is the hero. The agency is the guide.
2. **All metrics, no story.** Results without context are just numbers.
3. **Hiding the process.** Clients buy thinking and methodology, not just output.
4. **Cherry-picking only perfect outcomes.** Include honest challenges for credibility.
5. **No transferable insight.** The reader should learn something applicable to their own situation.
