# Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling
> Emma Coats (former Pixar storyboard artist), 2011 Twitter Thread / Pixar Internal

## Summary
In 2011, Emma Coats, a storyboard artist at Pixar Animation Studios, published 22 rules of
storytelling that she had absorbed during her time at the studio. Originally shared as a series
of tweets, the rules distill decades of Pixar's storytelling philosophy into actionable
principles. They are not formal Pixar doctrine but reflect the studio's culture of iterative
story development — a culture where every film is, as Ed Catmull has said, "ugly" in its
early stages and must be refined through relentless revision.

The rules range from structural principles ("Once upon a time... Every day... One day...
Because of that... Until finally...") to creative process advice ("Putting it on paper lets
you start fixing it. If it stays in your head, a perfect idea, you'll never share it with
anyone.") to character development insights ("You admire a character for trying more than
for their successes."). Collectively, they represent a philosophy where story serves character,
character serves theme, and revision is not a sign of failure but the fundamental mechanism
through which good stories become great.

The "story spine" framework embedded in Rule #4 has become one of the most widely adopted
storytelling templates in business, education, and marketing. Its power lies in its insistence
on causation: each beat leads to the next through "because of that," not "and then." This
single structural requirement transforms flat sequences into compelling narratives.

## Central Thesis
Great stories are not born; they are revised into existence. Pixar's storytelling principles
prioritize character over plot, causation over sequence, emotional truth over cleverness, and
the discipline of revision over the romance of inspiration.

## Key Concepts

### The 22 Rules with Marketing Applications

- **Rule 1**: You admire a character for trying more than for their successes.
  *Marketing*: Show customers struggling, not just succeeding. The effort is the story.

- **Rule 2**: Keep in mind what's interesting to you as an audience, not what's fun for you
  as a writer. They can be very different.
  *Marketing*: Write for the customer's interest, not the brand's excitement about its product.

- **Rule 3**: Trying for theme is important, but you won't see what the story is actually
  about until you're at the end. Now rewrite.
  *Marketing*: First drafts of campaigns reveal the real message. Revise toward that truth.

- **Rule 4**: The Story Spine — "Once upon a time there was ___. Every day, ___. One day,
  ___. Because of that, ___. Because of that, ___. Until finally, ___."
  *Marketing*: Use this spine for case studies, brand origin stories, and product narratives.

- **Rule 5**: Simplify. Focus. Combine characters. Hop over detours. You'll feel like you're
  losing valuable stuff but it sets you free.
  *Marketing*: Cut the features list. One message per piece of content.

- **Rule 6**: What is your character good at, comfortable with? Throw the polar opposite at
  them. Challenge them. How do they deal?
  *Marketing*: The best customer stories feature unexpected challenges and surprising responses.

- **Rule 7**: Come up with your ending before you figure out your middle.
  *Marketing*: Know the CTA before writing the content. Work backward from the desired action.

- **Rule 8**: Finish your story. Let go even if it's not perfect. In an ideal world you have
  both, but move on. Do better next time.
  *Marketing*: Ship the campaign. Iteration beats perfection.

- **Rule 9**: When you're stuck, make a list of what wouldn't happen next. Often the material
  to get you unstuck will show up.
  *Marketing*: When a campaign stalls, list what the brand would never say. The edge is there.

- **Rule 10**: Pull apart the stories you like. What you like in them is a part of you; you've
  got to recognize it before you can use it.
  *Marketing*: Analyze competitor content you admire. The admiration reveals your own brand voice.

- **Rule 11**: Putting it on paper lets you start fixing it. If it stays in your head, a
  perfect idea, you'll never share it with anyone.
  *Marketing*: Write the brief. Write the first draft. Perfection in your head is stagnation.

- **Rule 12**: Discount the first thing that comes to mind. And the second, third, fourth,
  fifth — get the obvious out of the way. Surprise yourself.
  *Marketing*: The first headline idea is the one every competitor would write. Go deeper.

- **Rule 13**: Give your characters opinions. Passive/malleable might seem likable but it's
  poison to the audience.
  *Marketing*: Brands with strong points of view attract loyalty. Neutrality is forgettable.

- **Rule 14**: Why must you tell this story? What's the belief burning within you that your
  story feeds off of? That's the heart of it.
  *Marketing*: Every campaign needs a "Why now?" and a "Why us?" that the team genuinely feels.

- **Rule 15**: If you were your character, in this situation, how would you feel? Honesty
  lends credibility to unbelievable situations.
  *Marketing*: Write customer stories from inside the customer's emotional experience.

- **Rule 16**: What are the stakes? Give us a reason to root for the character.
  *Marketing*: Every landing page needs stakes — what the customer loses by not acting.

- **Rule 17**: No work is ever wasted. If it's not working, let go and move on. It'll come
  back around to be useful later.
  *Marketing*: Killed campaigns and rejected ideas often resurface as future breakthroughs.

- **Rule 18**: You have to know yourself: the difference between doing your best and fussing.
  Story is testing, not refining.
  *Marketing*: A/B test stories. Let the audience decide, not the committee.

- **Rule 19**: Coincidences to get characters into trouble are great; coincidences to get
  them out of it are cheating.
  *Marketing*: Let the product be the earned solution, not a deus ex machina.

- **Rule 20**: Exercise: take the building blocks of a movie you dislike. Rearrange them.
  *Marketing*: Rework a competitor's failed campaign with your brand voice. Learn from failure.

- **Rule 21**: You gotta identify with your situation/characters; you can't just write "cool."
  *Marketing*: Never write content you wouldn't want to read yourself.

- **Rule 22**: What's the essence of your story? The most economical telling of it? If you
  know that, you can build out from there.
  *Marketing*: Before any campaign, write the one-sentence version. That is the campaign.
