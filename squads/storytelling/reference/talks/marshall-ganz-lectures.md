# Public Narrative: Story of Self, Us, and Now
> Marshall Ganz, Harvard Kennedy School Lectures on Public Narrative

## Summary
Marshall Ganz, a Harvard Kennedy School lecturer and former United Farm Workers organizer
under Cesar Chavez, developed the framework of "public narrative" as a leadership practice
that links three nested stories: Story of Self, Story of Us, and Story of Now. This framework
was adopted by the 2008 Obama presidential campaign as the core organizing methodology and has
since been used by movements, nonprofits, and organizations worldwide. Ganz's central insight
is that narrative is not a communication tool — it is a leadership practice that translates
values into action through emotional engagement.

Story of Self communicates the speaker's personal values by narrating the experiences that
shaped them — the choice points where they decided who they would be. Story of Us creates
shared identity by articulating the values, experiences, and aspirations that bind a community
together. Story of Now articulates the urgent challenge that demands action, the choice the
community faces, and the hopeful outcome that action can achieve. The three stories are nested:
Self establishes the speaker's credibility and values, Us connects those values to the
community's identity, and Now channels shared identity into specific collective action.

Ganz argues that the dominant model of mobilization — rational self-interest — fails because
it misunderstands human motivation. People do not act on information alone; they act on
emotion. And emotions are accessed through stories, not data. The specific emotions that drive
action are urgency (the challenge is now), anger (injustice demands response), hope (change is
possible), solidarity (we are in this together), and self-efficacy (we have the capacity to
act). Public narrative is the vehicle for engaging all five.

## Central Thesis
Leadership requires the capacity to translate values into action through narrative. Public
narrative weaves three stories — Self (why I am called), Us (why we are called), and Now
(what we are called to do) — to move communities from passive agreement to active engagement.
Emotion, not information, is the bridge between values and action.

## Key Concepts
- **Story of Self**: The personal narrative that communicates the speaker's values through
  choice points — moments where the speaker chose a path that revealed who they are. Not
  a biography but a values statement told through experience.
- **Story of Us**: The narrative that defines shared identity. What experiences, values, and
  aspirations bind this community together? What do "we" believe, and what have "we"
  overcome? Creates the "us" that can be mobilized.
- **Story of Now**: The urgent challenge, the choice, and the hope. What is the threat or
  opportunity we face right now? What specific action must we take? What hopeful outcome
  is possible if we act? Creates the mandate for action.
- **Choice Points**: The moments in a story where the character faces a decision that reveals
  their values. Choice points are the engine of narrative meaning — without choices, there
  is no character.
- **Emotional Engagement**: The five action emotions: urgency, anger, hope, solidarity, and
  self-efficacy. Public narrative must engage at least several of these to move people from
  agreement to action.
- **Values Into Action**: Narrative is not a communication strategy bolted onto organizing.
  It is the practice through which values are articulated, tested, and translated into
  collective behavior.
- **The Difference Between Story and Report**: A report conveys information. A story engages
  the heart. Reports generate understanding; stories generate action.

## Application to Marketing
- **Brand narrative architecture**: Structure brand stories as Self/Us/Now. Self = founder or
  brand origin (why we exist). Us = community identity (what we share with our customers).
  Now = current mission or campaign (what we must do together).
- **Campaign launches**: Every campaign should include all three stories. Why we (the brand)
  care about this. Why this matters to our community. What we need to do now.
- **Community building**: Online communities and loyalty programs need a Story of Us — a
  shared identity narrative that makes members feel they belong to something larger.
- **Fundraising**: Nonprofit fundraising that follows Self/Us/Now dramatically outperforms
  rational appeals. The donor must feel personally connected (Self), part of a movement (Us),
  and compelled to act immediately (Now).
- **Employee engagement**: Internal communications that follow public narrative structure —
  leader's personal story, team identity narrative, urgent current mission — create stronger
  alignment than strategic memos.
- **Social movements and advocacy**: Brands taking public positions on social issues should
  structure their communication as Self/Us/Now to avoid appearing opportunistic.
- **Customer onboarding**: Welcome sequences that tell the founder's story (Self), define
  the community (Us), and present the first action (Now) create stronger activation.
