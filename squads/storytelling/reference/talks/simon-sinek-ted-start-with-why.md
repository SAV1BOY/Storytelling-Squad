# Start With Why: How Great Leaders Inspire Action
> Simon Sinek, TED Talk, 2009

## Summary
Simon Sinek introduces the Golden Circle — a simple model that explains why some organizations
and leaders inspire where others cannot. The model has three concentric rings: Why (the core
purpose or belief), How (the process or values that differentiate), and What (the products or
services). Most companies communicate from the outside in — they start with What they do, then
explain How, and rarely articulate Why. Inspired leaders and organizations, regardless of size
or industry, all communicate from the inside out. They start with Why.

Sinek uses Apple as his primary commercial example. Apple does not say "We make great computers.
They're beautifully designed and easy to use. Want to buy one?" Instead, Apple communicates:
"Everything we do, we believe in challenging the status quo. We believe in thinking differently.
The way we challenge the status quo is by making our products beautifully designed and easy to
use. We happen to make great computers." The factual information is identical; the order is
reversed. And the order is everything.

The biological basis, Sinek argues, maps to the brain. The neocortex (outer brain) corresponds
to the What level — it processes rational, analytical thought and language. The limbic system
(inner brain) corresponds to Why and How — it drives feelings, trust, loyalty, and decision-
making, but has no capacity for language. This is why people say "it just feels right" — the
decision is limbic, but the language center cannot articulate it. Starting with Why speaks
directly to the part of the brain that controls behavior.

## Central Thesis
People do not buy what you do; they buy why you do it. Organizations that communicate their
purpose before their product inspire loyalty, action, and movements — because purpose speaks
to the limbic brain where decisions are actually made.

## Key Concepts
- **The Golden Circle**: Three concentric rings — Why (belief/purpose), How (differentiating
  process), What (product/output). Most communicate outside-in; inspired leaders go inside-out.
- **Why Before What**: The sequence of communication matters more than the content. Leading
  with purpose creates fundamentally different audience response than leading with features.
- **The Biology of Decision-Making**: The limbic system controls feelings, trust, and behavior
  but has no language capacity. The neocortex processes facts and language but does not drive
  decisions. Why speaks to the limbic system.
- **The Law of Diffusion of Innovation**: To achieve mass-market success, you must first win
  the 2.5% innovators and 13.5% early adopters. These groups are driven by belief (Why),
  not features (What). They adopt because it aligns with their identity.
- **The Celery Test**: If your Why is clear, decision-making becomes simple. Everything you
  do should be consistent with your Why — if it does not align, do not do it. This creates
  authenticity and trust.
- **Manipulation vs. Inspiration**: Price drops, promotions, fear, aspiration, and peer
  pressure are manipulations — they drive transactions but not loyalty. Inspiration drives
  loyalty because it connects to identity and belief.

## Application to Marketing
- **Brand positioning**: Every brand should be able to articulate its Why in one sentence
  before any campaign begins. If marketing leads with features, it speaks to the neocortex
  and competes on rational comparison. Leading with purpose creates loyalty.
- **Mission-driven campaigns**: Campaigns built around "why we exist" outperform those built
  around "what we sell." TOMS Shoes, Patagonia, and Warby Parker all lead with purpose.
- **Hiring and culture**: Internal marketing (employer branding) should lead with Why to
  attract believers, not mercenaries. People who share your Why are more productive and loyal.
- **Sales enablement**: Train sales teams to open conversations with Why, not What. "We
  believe small businesses deserve enterprise-level tools" before "We sell CRM software."
- **Content marketing**: Thought leadership that explores your Why (your beliefs about the
  industry, the future, the customer) creates stronger engagement than product content.
- **Product launches**: Structure launch narratives as Why-How-What. The belief, the approach
  that embodies that belief, then the product that results from the approach.
- **Customer segmentation**: Identify your innovators and early adopters — the people who
  share your Why — and serve them first. They will evangelize to the early majority.
