# How to Interview Customers for Case Studies and Testimonials
> Customer Story Interview Methodology for Marketing Teams

## Summary
Interviewing customers for case studies and testimonials is one of the most valuable and most
poorly executed activities in marketing. Most customer interviews produce flat, generic praise
("We love the product, it saved us time") because they use flat, generic questions. A skilled
customer interview, by contrast, uncovers the genuine transformation story that lives inside
the customer's experience — the specific moment when something changed, the real emotions of
the before state, and the concrete evidence of the after state.

This guide combines techniques from StoryCorps (deep listening, open-ended questions, silence
as a tool), The Moth (start in the action, find the moment of change, radical reduction), and
Matthew Dicks (the five-second moment, stakes, "but/therefore" structure) into a practical
framework for marketing teams. The goal is not to script a testimonial but to discover the
real story and then shape it with the customer's permission. The best customer stories feel
like they were told by the customer, not written by the marketing department — because they
were.

The interview process has three phases: preparation (understanding the customer's context and
identifying likely story angles), the interview itself (60 minutes of structured but flexible
conversation), and post-processing (identifying the story arc, extracting proof points, and
getting customer approval). Each phase is essential. Skipping preparation produces generic
questions. Skipping post-processing produces unusable raw material. The craft is in the full
process.

## Central Thesis
Every customer who has been genuinely helped by a product or service has a transformation
story. The interviewer's job is not to create that story but to discover it through skilled
questioning, active listening, and the courage to go deeper than surface-level satisfaction.
Authentic customer stories are more persuasive than any copy a marketing team can write.

## Key Concepts
- **Preparation Is Half the Interview**: Research the customer before the interview. Review
  their usage data, support tickets, onboarding notes, and any previous communications.
  Identify likely story angles. Prepare specific, contextual questions.
- **The Before/During/After Framework**: Structure the interview around three phases of the
  customer journey — the problem state (Before), the adoption and early experience (During),
  and the transformed state (After). Spend the most time on Before.
- **Pain Before Praise**: Most interviewers rush to the positive. Resist this. The depth of
  the problem makes the solution compelling. Ask detailed questions about the before state:
  What was failing? How did it feel? What had they tried? What was at stake?
- **Find the Five-Second Moment**: Somewhere in the customer's experience is a single moment
  when they realized the product actually worked — when their understanding shifted. This is
  the story's emotional core.
- **Their Words, Not Yours**: The customer's natural language is more credible than polished
  marketing copy. Capture their exact phrases. "It was like someone turned the lights on"
  is better than any tagline you could write.
- **The Follow-Up Ladder**: First answer: surface. Follow-up: detail. Second follow-up:
  emotion. Third follow-up: meaning. Keep going deeper until you hit genuine emotional
  material.
- **Silence After the Question**: Ask the question, then wait. Do not rephrase, do not offer
  examples, do not fill the silence. The customer needs time to access real memories.
- **Permission and Safety**: Begin by explaining the purpose, getting consent for recording,
  and assuring the customer they will review and approve everything before publication.
  Safety enables honesty.

## Interview Flow (60 Minutes)

### Phase 1: Warm-Up (5 minutes)
- Build rapport, explain the process, get recording consent
- "Tell me a bit about yourself and your role."
- "How long have you been using [product]?"

### Phase 2: The Before State (20 minutes)
- "Take me back to before you found us. What was going on in your world?"
- "What was the biggest challenge you were facing?"
- "How did that challenge affect you personally? Your team? Your business?"
- "What had you tried before that did not work?"
- "Was there a specific moment when you knew something had to change?"
- "How did it feel to be stuck in that situation?"
- "What was at stake if you did not solve this problem?"

### Phase 3: The Discovery and Decision (10 minutes)
- "How did you first hear about [product]?"
- "What made you decide to try it? What almost stopped you?"
- "What were your biggest concerns or hesitations?"
- "What was the tipping point — the thing that made you say yes?"

### Phase 4: The Experience (15 minutes)
- "Walk me through the early experience. What happened first?"
- "Was there a specific moment when you thought 'this is actually working'?"
- "What surprised you most?"
- "What was harder than expected? What was easier?"
- "Can you describe a specific day or incident where the difference was obvious?"

### Phase 5: The After State (10 minutes)
- "What is different now compared to before?"
- "Can you give me specific numbers, timelines, or measurable results?"
- "How has this changed things beyond just the immediate problem?"
- "What would you say to someone who is where you were before?"
- "If you had to describe the transformation in one sentence, what would it be?"

## Post-Interview Processing
1. **Identify the five-second moment** — the single instant of realization or change
2. **Map the transformation** — FROM [specific state] TO [specific state]
3. **Extract proof points** — numbers, timelines, specific measurable outcomes
4. **Note authentic language** — phrases the customer used that are naturally compelling
5. **Draft the narrative arc** — using the material, not inventing anything new
6. **Send for approval** — the customer must review and approve before publication
7. **Tag and store** — categorize by industry, use case, objection overcome, and story type
   for the story bank

## Compliance and Approval
- Always get written consent before the interview begins
- Record the interview (with permission) to capture exact language
- Send the drafted story to the customer for review before any publication
- Allow the customer to remove or modify any details they are uncomfortable with
- Get final written approval before publishing in any channel
- Store consent records alongside the published story
- Respect the customer's right to withdraw their story at any time

## Application to Marketing
- **Case studies**: Full narrative case studies for website and sales collateral, structured
  as Before/During/After with the five-second moment as the emotional climax
- **Video testimonials**: Use the interview structure for on-camera interviews; the Before
  questions produce the most compelling footage
- **Social proof snippets**: Extract key quotes for social media, ads, and landing pages
- **Email content**: Use customer stories in nurture sequences, organized by objection
- **Sales enablement**: Arm the sales team with customer stories organized by industry,
  company size, objection overcome, and use case
- **Podcast episodes**: Invite customers to tell their full story on the company podcast
  using this interview framework

## Notable Quotes

- "Their exact words are worth more than your best copywriting."
- "Every customer has a five-second moment. Your job is to find it."
- "Silence after a question is not awkward. It is where the real story lives."
