# StoryCorps — Interview Methodology

> StoryCorps, founded 2003 — Preserving and sharing humanity's stories

## Summary

StoryCorps is an American nonprofit organization that records, preserves, and shares the stories of everyday people. Since 2003, it has collected over 600,000 interviews — the largest single collection of human voices ever gathered. Its methodology for extracting meaningful personal stories through structured conversation is directly applicable to marketing storytelling, particularly customer interviews, founder story extraction, and testimonial development.

The StoryCorps approach differs from journalistic interviewing in its emphasis on deep listening, emotional safety, and the relationship between interviewer and subject. The goal is not information extraction but story emergence.

## Central Thesis

Everyone has a story worth telling, and the right questions asked in the right environment can unlock stories that neither the teller nor the listener expected. The interview is not an interrogation but a gift of attention.

## Key Concepts

- **The Great Questions List** — curated questions designed to open narrative, not close it
- **Open-ended prompts** — "Tell me about a time when..." rather than "Did you enjoy...?"
- **The 40-minute format** — enough time for depth, not so long that energy fades
- **Warm-up questions** — start with easy, comfortable questions before going deep
- **The facilitator's role** — active listening, follow-up, emotional safety, not leading
- **Silence as tool** — allowing pauses for the storyteller to access deeper memories
- **Emotional turning points** — guiding the conversation toward moments of change
- **The "What do you want people to know?" closer** — giving the storyteller agency over their message
- **Recorded format** — recording creates a sense of significance and permanence
- **One meaningful story > many surface stories** — depth over breadth

## Application to Marketing

1. **Customer story interviews** — Use StoryCorps question framework to extract authentic customer transformation stories for case studies and testimonials
2. **Founder interviews** — Apply the methodology to document founder origin stories with emotional depth
3. **Employee stories** — Capture employee narratives for employer branding and culture content
4. **Community stories** — Gather stories from brand community members for UGC and social proof
5. **Research interviews** — Use the deep listening approach for audience research and belief mapping

## Key Interview Questions (Adapted for Marketing)

- "Can you describe the moment when you realized you needed to make a change?"
- "What was the hardest part of the journey?"
- "What surprised you most about the experience?"
- "How would you describe yourself before vs. after?"
- "What would you tell someone who is where you were?"
- "What's the one thing you want people to understand about this?"
