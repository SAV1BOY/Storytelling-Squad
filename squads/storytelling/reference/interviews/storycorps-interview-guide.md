# StoryCorps Interview Methodology
> StoryCorps, Founded 2003 by Dave Isay

## Summary
StoryCorps is an American nonprofit organization that records, preserves, and shares the
stories of everyday people. Since 2003, it has collected over 600,000 interviews — the largest
single collection of human voices ever gathered. Its methodology is built on a deceptively
simple premise: when two people sit across from each other and one asks questions with genuine
curiosity, stories emerge that neither participant expected. The interview is not an
interrogation or a journalism exercise — it is a facilitated act of deep listening.

Dave Isay founded StoryCorps on the belief that every life contains stories worth preserving,
and that the act of listening to someone's story is itself an act of love. The methodology
reflects this belief: the interviewer is not a journalist seeking facts but a listener seeking
meaning. Questions are designed to open emotional doors, not extract information. The
environment is designed for intimacy — two chairs, a microphone, forty minutes, no audience.
The absence of an audience is crucial: people tell different stories when they are speaking to
one person they trust than when they are performing for a crowd.

StoryCorps's methodology has proven that the quality of the story depends almost entirely on
the quality of the questions and the quality of the listening. A great question opens space.
A great follow-up question goes deeper into that space. And the willingness to sit in silence
— to not fill the pause — often produces the most meaningful material. People need time to
access their deeper stories, and that time is created by a listener who does not rush.

## Central Thesis
The most meaningful stories emerge not from skilled storytellers but from skilled listeners.
The art of the interview is the art of creating a safe, intimate space where questions open
emotional doors and silence gives permission for depth. Everyone has a story worth telling —
the interviewer's job is to help them find it.

## Key Concepts
- **The Listener as Creator**: The quality of the story depends on the quality of the
  listening. StoryCorps trains facilitators to listen with their full attention, to follow
  the emotional thread rather than the factual one, and to be comfortable with silence.
- **Questions That Open Doors**: StoryCorps questions are designed to access emotion and
  meaning, not facts. "What was the happiest moment of your life?" opens a different door
  than "Tell me about your career." The best questions are simple, open-ended, and personal.
- **The Follow-Up Is Everything**: The first answer is often surface-level. The real story
  lives in the follow-up: "What did that feel like?" "What happened next?" "Why do you
  think that mattered so much?" The follow-up signals that the listener wants to go deeper.
- **Comfortable Silence**: When the interviewer does not rush to fill silence, the
  interviewee accesses deeper material. Silence is not awkward — it is generative. StoryCorps
  facilitators are trained to wait.
- **Two People, Not a Performance**: StoryCorps interviews are between two people — usually
  people who know each other. The absence of an audience creates intimacy that public
  storytelling cannot achieve.
- **The Question List as Starting Point**: StoryCorps provides question lists, but
  facilitators are trained to abandon the list and follow the conversation. The list is a
  safety net, not a script.
- **Preservation as Validation**: The act of recording someone's story communicates that
  their life matters. This is not a side effect — it is a core purpose. The recording itself
  is an act of dignity.
- **The 40-Minute Format**: Long enough for depth, short enough to maintain energy. The
  first ten minutes are warm-up; the deepest material typically emerges between minutes
  twenty and thirty-five.
- **Warm-Up Before Depth**: Start with comfortable, easy questions before moving toward
  emotional territory. Trust must be established before vulnerability is possible.

## Key Question Categories

### Identity and Values
- "What are you most proud of in your life?"
- "What lessons has your life taught you?"
- "How would you like to be remembered?"
- "What do you know now that you wish you had known when you were younger?"

### Relationships
- "Can you tell me about a person who has been a significant influence on your life?"
- "What is your earliest memory of [person]?"
- "What is the most important thing [person] taught you?"

### Turning Points
- "Was there a moment in your life that changed the direction of everything?"
- "What was the hardest decision you ever had to make?"
- "Tell me about a time you were really afraid."
- "What is a moment you will never forget?"

### Work and Purpose
- "What did you want to be when you grew up?"
- "What does your work mean to you?"
- "When did you know what you wanted to do with your life?"

### Legacy
- "If you could give one piece of advice to your grandchildren, what would it be?"
- "What do you want people to remember about you?"
- "Is there something you have never told anyone that you would like to say now?"

## Application to Marketing
- **Customer interviews**: Use StoryCorps methodology for customer research interviews.
  Replace structured questionnaires with open-ended questions that access emotional
  experience. "Tell me about a moment when..." produces richer material than "Rate your
  satisfaction."
- **Founder interviews for brand content**: Interview founders using StoryCorps questions
  about turning points, fears, and values. The resulting material is orders of magnitude
  richer than standard founder Q&As.
- **Employee stories for employer branding**: Use StoryCorps techniques to capture employee
  stories for recruitment marketing. Two employees interviewing each other produces more
  authentic content than HR-scripted testimonials.
- **Video testimonials**: Train video production teams in StoryCorps listening techniques.
  The interviewer behind the camera should follow emotional threads, not a shot list.
- **Podcast interviews**: Apply StoryCorps follow-up technique: when the guest says something
  interesting, go deeper rather than moving to the next question. The best podcast moments
  come from follow-ups.
- **Community research**: When researching new markets or communities, use StoryCorps-style
  interviews to understand the emotional landscape, not just the market data.
- **User research**: Product teams conducting user research can apply StoryCorps silence
  technique. Let users describe their experience without interruption or leading questions.
