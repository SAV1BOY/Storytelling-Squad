# Sequoia Capital's Recommended Pitch Deck Format
> Sequoia Capital, "Writing a Business Plan" Guide

## Summary
Sequoia Capital, one of the most influential venture capital firms in history (early investors
in Apple, Google, YouTube, Airbnb, and Stripe), published a recommended format for startup
pitch decks that has become the de facto standard in Silicon Valley. The format specifies ten
elements that, taken together, form a complete narrative arc: Company Purpose, Problem,
Solution, Why Now, Market Size, Product, Business Model, Team, Financials, and Vision. What
makes this format narratively significant is not the individual elements but their sequence —
they tell a story of urgency, capability, and destiny.

The format begins with Company Purpose — a single declarative sentence that captures the
company's reason for existing. This is not a product description; it is a belief statement.
From there, the format moves through Problem (the pain), Solution (the relief), and Why Now
(the timing). These four elements constitute the narrative setup: there is a pressing problem,
we have the answer, and the moment is now. The middle section — Market Size, Product, Business
Model — provides the proof that the opportunity is real and the execution is sound. The closing
— Team, Financials, Vision — answers the question "Why us?" and paints the picture of the
future the company will create.

The brilliance of Sequoia's format is that it embeds a story structure within what appears to
be a business document. The narrative arc — purpose, conflict, resolution, proof, vision —
mirrors classic story structure. Founders who follow this format are not just presenting data;
they are telling a story about why the world needs their company to exist, why now is the
moment, and why they are the people to build it.

## Central Thesis
A pitch deck is not a business document — it is a narrative vehicle. The ten-element format
creates a story arc that moves from purpose through problem and solution to vision, embedding
urgency, credibility, and ambition in a structure that investors can evaluate rationally
while experiencing emotionally.

## Key Concepts
- **Company Purpose (Slide 1)**: A single sentence that defines why the company exists. Not
  what it does — why it matters. "Organize the world's information" (Google). "Belong
  anywhere" (Airbnb). This is the Golden Circle's "Why."
- **Problem (Slide 2)**: The pain point, quantified and personalized. Show that real people
  suffer from this problem today. Use specific customer quotes or scenarios, not abstract
  market descriptions.
- **Solution (Slide 3)**: How you solve the problem. Keep it concrete and demonstrable. If
  possible, show the product in action. The solution should feel inevitable given the problem.
- **Why Now (Slide 4)**: What has changed in the world that makes this company possible and
  necessary today? Technology shifts, regulatory changes, cultural movements, market timing.
  This is the urgency narrative.
- **Market Size (Slide 5)**: TAM, SAM, SOM — but presented as a story of expansion, not a
  static number. Show how the market grows as the product improves and reaches new segments.
- **Product (Slide 6)**: The product in detail. Screenshots, demos, architecture. This is
  where you prove the solution is real, not theoretical.
- **Business Model (Slide 7)**: How you make money. Pricing, unit economics, customer
  acquisition costs, lifetime value. The narrative here is sustainability and scalability.
- **Team (Slide 8)**: Why these specific people are uniquely qualified to solve this problem.
  Not resumes — relevant experience and insight that only this team possesses.
- **Financials (Slide 9)**: Revenue, projections, key metrics. The narrative is trajectory —
  show momentum and the path to profitability or the next milestone.
- **Vision (Slide 10)**: The world you are building. If you succeed fully, what does the
  future look like? End with the biggest, most ambitious version of your company's impact.

## Application to Marketing
- **Product launch narratives**: Structure product launches as Purpose-Problem-Solution-
  Why Now. This sequence creates urgency and positions the product as inevitable.
- **Website architecture**: The Sequoia format maps to landing page structure. Hero section
  (Purpose), pain point section (Problem), product section (Solution), social proof
  (credibility), CTA (call to action).
- **Sales presentations**: Structure client pitches as a modified Sequoia deck. Open with
  purpose, identify the client's problem, present the solution, demonstrate credibility,
  close with the vision of partnership.
- **Content marketing strategy**: Use the ten elements as a content planning framework. Each
  element can be a blog post, a video, a social post series — together they tell the complete
  brand story.
- **Internal strategy presentations**: When presenting strategy to leadership or board, follow
  the Sequoia format. It imposes narrative discipline on what is often formless strategic data.
- **Competitive positioning**: The "Why Now" slide is particularly applicable to marketing.
  Content that explains why the market moment demands your product creates urgency that
  feature lists cannot.
- **Team pages**: Apply the Team slide principle to website team pages. Not resumes but
  narrative explanations of why each person is uniquely qualified for the company's mission.

## Notable Quotes
- "The company purpose is one sentence. If you can't say it in one sentence, you don't
  have clarity." (Paraphrased Sequoia guidance)
- "Tell the story of a real person who has this problem." (Paraphrased problem guidance)
- "Why now? What has changed? If this is such a good idea, why hasn't someone done it
  already?" (Paraphrased timing guidance)
- "End with the world you want to create, not the product you want to sell." (Paraphrased
  vision guidance)
