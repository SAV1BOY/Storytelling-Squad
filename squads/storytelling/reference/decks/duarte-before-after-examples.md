# Nancy Duarte's Before/After Slide Transformation Examples
> Nancy Duarte, Duarte Inc., "slide:ology" and "Resonate"

## Summary
Nancy Duarte, founder of Duarte Inc. and author of "slide:ology" and "Resonate," has built
a practice around transforming mediocre presentations into compelling narratives. Her before/
after transformation examples demonstrate that the problem with most presentations is not bad
content but bad storytelling. The same information, restructured through narrative principles,
becomes dramatically more persuasive. The transformation is not about adding polish — it is
about rethinking the relationship between the presenter, the content, and the audience.

Duarte's transformations consistently reveal the same set of structural problems in the
"before" decks: too much text on slides, no narrative arc, data without context, no audience-
centric framing, and no emotional engagement. The "after" versions apply a set of principles:
one idea per slide, visual over textual, contrast between "what is" and "what could be,"
audience as hero, and a clear call to action. The transformation is often radical — a 60-slide
bullet-point deck becomes a 30-slide visual narrative that takes the same amount of time but
leaves a profoundly different impression.

The most important insight from Duarte's before/after work is that the problem is not the
slides — it is the underlying narrative structure. A slide deck is not a document projected
on a wall; it is a visual companion to a spoken narrative. When presenters treat slides as
documents, they create cognitive overload. When they treat slides as narrative support, they
create emotional resonance. The transformation from before to after is fundamentally a
transformation from document to story.

## Central Thesis
Every mediocre presentation contains a great one. The transformation requires not better
design but better narrative thinking — restructuring information through the lens of audience
need, emotional contrast, and a clear arc from problem to resolution.

## Key Concepts
- **One Idea Per Slide**: The single most impactful transformation. Dense slides with multiple
  ideas create cognitive overload. Breaking content into one-idea slides creates pacing,
  rhythm, and comprehension.
- **Slide as Billboard, Not Document**: A slide should communicate its idea in three seconds
  — like a billboard on a highway. If the audience has to read it, the presenter has lost
  them. Words belong in the spoken narrative, not on the slide.
- **Data in Context**: Raw numbers are meaningless without context. Duarte transforms data
  slides by providing comparison, contrast, or narrative framing. "$2M revenue" means nothing.
  "$2M revenue, 3x last year" tells a story.
- **Before/After Contrast Slides**: Show the current state and the desired state on sequential
  slides to create felt tension. This mirrors the sparkline at the individual slide level.
- **The Audience is the Hero**: Restructure every slide to answer "Why should the audience
  care?" If a slide does not serve the audience's interest, cut it or reframe it.
- **Visual Metaphor Over Text**: Replace textual explanations with visual metaphors. A slide
  showing a fork in the road communicates "decision point" faster and more memorably than
  a bullet point that says "We face a strategic choice."
- **White Space as Confidence**: Dense slides signal insecurity — the presenter fears leaving
  anything out. Sparse slides signal confidence — the presenter trusts the spoken narrative.
- **Emotional Arc Across Slides**: The slide deck should follow an emotional arc: tension,
  escalation, resolution. Each section should feel emotionally different from the last.

## Application to Marketing
- **Sales deck overhaul**: Apply Duarte's one-idea-per-slide rule to sales decks. Most sales
  decks fail because they are documents projected on walls. Transform them into visual
  narratives with spoken content carrying the detail.
- **Investor pitch improvement**: Before/after the pitch deck using Duarte's principles.
  Cut text by 70%, increase images by 300%, restructure around a narrative arc.
- **Webinar slides**: Webinar presentations suffer acutely from the document-on-wall problem.
  Apply billboard thinking — each slide supports one spoken point.
- **Conference presentations**: Marketing teams presenting at conferences should apply
  emotional arc across the deck. Start with tension (the industry problem), escalate
  (consequences of inaction), resolve (the vision or solution).
- **Internal presentations**: Strategy presentations to leadership are often the worst
  offenders. Apply Duarte's before/after thinking: what would this deck look like if the
  audience's engagement mattered as much as the content's completeness?
- **Social media carousels**: Instagram and LinkedIn carousels are mini-slide decks. Apply
  one-idea-per-slide, billboard readability, and emotional arc across the sequence.
