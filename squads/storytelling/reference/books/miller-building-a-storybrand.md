# Building a StoryBrand
> Donald Miller, 2017

## Summary

Donald Miller's *Building a StoryBrand* is the most widely adopted framework for applying narrative structure to brand messaging. The book introduces the SB7 Framework—seven elements drawn from classical story structure—and argues that most businesses fail at marketing not because their products are bad but because their messaging is confusing. Confused customers do not buy. The remedy is to clarify your message by running it through a story framework where the customer is the hero and the brand is the guide.

Miller's core insight is that brands make a fatal error when they position themselves as the hero of the story. Customers do not care about the brand's journey; they care about their own. When a brand talks about its founding story, its awards, its internal culture, it is casting itself as the hero and pushing the customer into the role of audience member. The fix is to reposition: the customer is the hero with a problem, and the brand is the guide who has the empathy and authority to help them solve it.

The SB7 Framework consists of seven elements: (1) A Character (the customer) who has (2) A Problem (external, internal, and philosophical) meets (3) A Guide (the brand) who gives them (4) A Plan (a simple, clear process) and calls them to (5) Action (direct and transitional CTAs) that helps them avoid (6) Failure (the negative outcome if they don't act) and ends in (7) Success (the positive transformation). Miller argues that every piece of marketing communication—website, email, sales pitch, keynote—should be filtered through this framework.

The book also introduces the concept of three levels of problem: external (the tangible, surface-level issue), internal (the emotional frustration the external problem causes), and philosophical (the larger question of justice or meaning—why this problem should not exist). Miller argues that most brands address only the external problem, missing the internal and philosophical levels where purchasing decisions are actually made. People buy solutions to internal problems; they justify purchases with external reasons.

## Central Thesis

Miller's thesis is that story is the most powerful tool for organizing and clarifying brand communication, and that the specific story structure that works for marketing is one where the customer is the hero and the brand is the guide. This is not a metaphor or a creative exercise—it is a concrete framework that determines what goes on your website, what you say in sales conversations, what your emails contain, and how you structure your entire marketing funnel. Brands that clarify their message using story structure outperform competitors because clarity beats cleverness in marketing communication.

The deeper argument is that human brains are wired to pay attention to stories because stories are survival tools—they help us navigate threats and pursue opportunities. Marketing that follows story structure hooks into this survival-oriented attention system, while marketing that presents information without narrative structure is filtered out as noise.

## Key Concepts

- **The SB7 Framework**: Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Failure, Success—the seven elements that every brand story must contain.
- **Customer as Hero**: The customer is always the hero. The brand is never the hero. When brands talk about themselves, they are competing with the customer for the hero role, and the customer always wins by leaving.
- **Brand as Guide**: The guide has two characteristics: empathy (understanding the hero's pain) and authority (demonstrating competence to solve it). Both are required—empathy without authority is a peer, authority without empathy is a bully.
- **Three Levels of Problem**: External (the tangible problem), Internal (the emotional frustration), Philosophical (the "ought" statement—why this problem should not exist). Companies that address all three levels create the most compelling messaging.
- **The Villain**: Every story needs a villain. In brand storytelling, the villain is the root cause of the customer's problem—not a competitor, but a force (complexity, wasted time, uncertainty, inefficiency) that the customer is struggling against.
- **The Plan**: Guides give heroes a plan. In marketing, this is a simple 3-4 step process that removes confusion and gives the customer confidence to take action. Plans reduce the perceived risk of engagement.
- **Direct and Transitional CTAs**: Direct CTAs ask for the sale. Transitional CTAs offer value in exchange for a relationship (free guides, webinars, assessments). Both must be present and clearly visible.
- **Stakes: Failure and Success**: The brand must articulate both what the customer stands to lose (failure) and what they stand to gain (success). Without stakes, there is no urgency. Without a vision of success, there is no aspiration.
- **The One-Liner**: A concise statement that captures the brand's story in a single sentence, structured as: problem, solution, result. This replaces the traditional elevator pitch.
- **The BrandScript**: A filled-out SB7 template that becomes the master document from which all marketing materials are derived.
- **Noise vs. Music**: Most marketing is noise—self-focused, unclear, and overwhelming. Story-based marketing is music—clear, customer-focused, and structured in a pattern the brain naturally follows.
- **Aspirational Identity**: The success element should paint a picture of who the customer becomes, not just what they get. Transformation of identity is more powerful than acquisition of things.

## Application to Marketing

- **Website Wireframe**: Miller provides a specific website structure based on the SB7 framework: hero image with clear header text (stating the value proposition), stakes section (failure), value proposition (success), the plan (3 easy steps), explanatory paragraph (guide empathy and authority), direct CTA (repeated), and testimonials (proof of guide authority). This wireframe has become one of the most replicated website structures in small business marketing.
- **Email Sequences**: The SB7 elements map directly onto email nurture sequences. Email 1: empathy with the customer's problem. Email 2: establish authority and introduce the plan. Email 3: address failure stakes. Email 4: paint the success picture. Email 5: direct CTA. Each email addresses one element of the framework.
- **Sales Conversations**: Sales teams can structure their conversations around the SB7 elements: identify the customer's problem (all three levels), position themselves as the guide (empathy + authority), present the plan, call to action, and articulate both failure and success stakes.
- **Keynote and Presentation Structure**: Presentations structured with SB7 open with the audience's problem, establish the speaker as guide, present a clear plan, call the audience to action, and close with a vision of success. This outperforms the traditional "about us" → "our product" → "features" → "pricing" structure.
- **Internal Alignment**: The BrandScript becomes a single source of truth that aligns marketing, sales, customer success, and product teams around the same customer story. Internal alignment around a shared narrative eliminates the inconsistency that confuses customers.
