# Story Circle Resources
> Dan Harmon, Channel 101 Essays and Podcast References

## Summary

Dan Harmon, creator of *Community* and *Rick and Morty*, developed the Story Circle as a simplified, practical adaptation of Joseph Campbell's monomyth for use in television writing. Harmon first articulated the system in a series of essays published on the Channel 101 website, a collaborative short-film project he co-founded in Los Angeles. These essays, along with his podcast *Harmontown* and various interviews, constitute the primary source material for the Story Circle methodology.

The Story Circle distills Campbell's seventeen-stage hero's journey into eight steps arranged around a circle, creating a tool that is simple enough to internalize and apply in real time during a writers' room session. The eight steps are: (1) A character is in a zone of comfort, (2) But they want something, (3) They enter an unfamiliar situation, (4) They adapt to it, (5) They get what they wanted, (6) But pay a heavy price for it, (7) They return to their familiar situation, (8) Having changed.

The circle is divided by a horizontal line: the top half represents the ordinary world (order, consciousness, the known), and the bottom half represents the special world (chaos, the unconscious, the unknown). Crossing the line—at steps 3 and 7—marks the major threshold moments of the story.

Harmon's key contribution to narrative theory is the concept of "embryo theory"—the idea that story structure is not an arbitrary convention but something biological, hard-wired into human cognition because it mirrors the fundamental rhythm of how organisms interact with their environment: equilibrium, disruption, adaptation, new equilibrium.

He also popularized the "but/therefore" test as a diagnostic for narrative drive versus episodic meandering: scenes connected by "but" and "therefore" create causally driven stories, while scenes connected by "and then" create lists of events that feel shapeless and inert.

The Story Circle has become influential far beyond television. Its simplicity makes it applicable to any narrative unit—a single scene, an episode, a season arc, a brand campaign, a sales email. Harmon himself demonstrated its fractal nature by showing how individual scenes within an episode each complete their own mini-circle while contributing to the episode's larger circle.

## Central Thesis

Harmon's central argument is that story structure is not a set of rules invented by academics or Hollywood executives but a pattern that emerges naturally from the way human beings process experience. We are creatures who seek comfort, are disrupted by desire, venture into the unknown, are transformed, and return changed.

This pattern repeats at every scale of human life—a single conversation, a day at work, a career, a lifetime—and stories that map onto this pattern feel satisfying because they mirror the rhythm of lived experience.

The practical implication is that storytelling is a learnable skill, not a mystical gift. Anyone who can trace the eight steps around the circle for their narrative—and honestly assess whether each step is present and functioning—can diagnose and fix most story problems without advanced training in screenwriting theory.

## Key Concepts

- **The 8-Step Story Circle**: (1) You (comfort zone), (2) Need (desire/want), (3) Go (enter unfamiliar situation), (4) Search (adapt, struggle), (5) Find (get what you wanted), (6) Take (pay the price), (7) Return (go back), (8) Change (having been transformed). Each step has a specific emotional and structural function.

- **The Horizontal Line**: The circle is bisected horizontally. The top half is order, consciousness, the known world. The bottom half is chaos, the unconscious, the unknown. Crossing the line (steps 3 and 7) are the major threshold moments that define the story's structure.

- **Embryo Theory**: Story structure mirrors biological rhythms. An organism in equilibrium encounters a stimulus, moves toward or away from it, integrates the experience, and reaches a new equilibrium. Stories feel "right" when they follow this pattern because they resonate with the body's own experience of being alive.

- **But/Therefore vs. And Then**: A diagnostic test for narrative drive. If your scenes are connected by "but" (contradiction) and "therefore" (consequence), your story has causal momentum. If they are connected by "and then," you have a sequence of events without narrative propulsion. This test is applicable to any narrative form.

- **Fractal Structure**: The Story Circle operates at every scale. A single scene can complete a mini-circle. An episode contains multiple scene-circles nested within an episode-circle. A season contains episode-circles nested within a season-circle. This fractal property makes the tool infinitely scalable.

- **Death and Rebirth at the Bottom**: Step 5 (Find) sits at the very bottom of the circle—the point farthest from the ordinary world. Harmon describes this as the "death" point, where the character symbolically dies to their old self. Everything that follows is rebirth and return.

- **The Want vs. The Need**: Steps 2 and 8 are linked: what the character wants (step 2) is rarely what they need (step 8). The journey transforms the want into the need. The character gets what they need, which may or may not be what they originally wanted.

- **TV Structure Application**: Harmon used the Story Circle to structure every episode of *Community*, demonstrating that it works not just for feature films but for the compressed, recurring demands of episodic television where characters must change within each episode yet remain recognizable across a season.

- **The Audience's Journey**: The circle describes not just the character's journey but the audience's. The audience enters the story in comfort, is disrupted by the character's need, travels into unfamiliar territory, experiences transformation vicariously, and returns to their own life changed.

- **Simplicity as Feature**: Harmon deliberately kept the system to eight steps because he wanted it to be simple enough to hold in working memory during a fast-paced writers' room discussion. Complexity is the enemy of practical application.

## Application to Marketing

- **The Story Circle for Content Planning**: Each piece of content in a marketing campaign can be mapped to the Story Circle. A blog post: (1) reader's current situation, (2) their unmet need, (3) entering the problem space, (4) exploring solutions, (5) finding the insight/product, (6) what it costs (money, effort, change), (7) returning to their work/life, (8) being measurably better off. This ensures every piece of content has narrative completeness.

- **But/Therefore for Copy Editing**: Apply the but/therefore test to marketing copy. If your sentences and paragraphs are connected by "and then" logic, the copy will feel like a list of features. Rewriting connections as "but" (introducing tension) and "therefore" (showing consequence) creates copy with momentum that pulls readers forward.

- **Fractal Campaigns**: Use the fractal property of the Story Circle to design campaigns that work at multiple scales. Each social media post completes a micro-circle. Each email in a sequence completes a mid-scale circle. The entire campaign completes a macro-circle. This creates coherence across touchpoints while keeping each individual piece satisfying.

- **The Want/Need Gap for Positioning**: The gap between what customers want (step 2) and what they actually need (step 8) is a powerful positioning tool. Customers think they want more leads (step 2); they actually need a better conversion process (step 8). Marketing that bridges this gap positions the brand as genuinely insightful, not merely responsive.

- **Bottom-of-the-Circle Content**: The most transformative marketing content lives at the bottom of the circle—the "death and rebirth" zone. This is where customers confront uncomfortable truths about their current approach, let go of assumptions, and open themselves to genuine change. Content that operates here (challenging blog posts, myth-busting webinars, honest assessments) drives the deepest engagement.

## Notable Quotes

- "Once you understand story structure, you realize it's not something humans invented. It's something humans discovered—in themselves."
- "If your scene transitions are 'and then... and then... and then,' you don't have a story. You have a grocery list."
- "The character descends into the cave. At the very bottom, they die. Then they rise. That's every story ever told."
- "You don't need to know Campbell. You don't need to know Vogler. You need to know the circle, and you need to be honest about whether you've gone all the way around."
- "Story structure is a rhythm. It's the heartbeat. You can't get rid of it and still be alive."
- "The top half of the circle is where you're comfortable. The bottom half is where you grow. Most people spend their whole lives trying not to cross the line."
- "Every scene needs to go around the circle or it has no reason to exist."
