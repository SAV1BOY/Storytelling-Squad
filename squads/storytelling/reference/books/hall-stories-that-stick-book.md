# Stories That Stick: How Storytelling Can Captivate Customers, Influence Audiences, and Transform Your Business
> Kindra Hall, 2019

## Summary

Stories That Stick is a practical business storytelling framework built around four specific story types and a repeatable story formula. Kindra Hall, a professional storyteller and communication strategist, argues that most businesses know they should be telling stories but have no systematic method for doing so. The book provides that method, moving storytelling from an abstract aspiration to a concrete, teachable practice.

Hall identifies four story types that every business needs: the Value Story (demonstrating the value of your product or service), the Founder Story (explaining why the business exists), the Customer Story (showing how real people benefit), and the Purpose Story (articulating the larger mission). Each type serves a different strategic function, and together they form a complete narrative toolkit for any organization. Hall provides detailed templates and examples for each type, making them immediately usable.

The book's story formula consists of three components: an Identifiable Character (someone the audience can see themselves in), Authentic Emotion (real feelings that create connection), and a Significant Moment (a specific turning point that gives the story its power). Hall argues that most business stories fail because they lack one or more of these components, usually the Significant Moment. Without a turning point, the story is just a description.

Hall also introduces the Gap-Bridge framework, a structural tool for organizing any story. Every effective story begins with a Normal (the way things are), introduces an Explosion (the event that disrupts the normal), and arrives at a New Normal (the transformed state). This three-beat structure is simple enough to be used by anyone and robust enough to carry genuine emotional weight. It provides the skeleton upon which the story formula's flesh is hung.

The book draws on Hall's experience as a strategist for brands ranging from startups to Fortune 500 companies and her background in competitive storytelling. It combines academic rigor with practical accessibility and has become a go-to reference for marketing teams, sales organizations, and executives who need to systematize storytelling across their organizations.

## Central Thesis

Hall's central argument is that there is a "storytelling gap" in most businesses: a space between the stories a company should be telling and the stories it is actually telling (if any). This gap costs businesses revenue, loyalty, and differentiation. The solution is not to hire better writers or produce more content but to adopt a systematic approach to identifying, crafting, and deploying the right types of stories in the right contexts.

She contends that storytelling is not about talent but about method. Anyone can learn to identify an Identifiable Character, surface Authentic Emotion, and locate the Significant Moment. The four story types provide strategic direction, and the formula provides structural discipline. Together, they close the storytelling gap. Hall is emphatic that the "finding" problem, not the "crafting" problem, is what holds most businesses back, and her systematic approach to story discovery is as valuable as her structural framework.

## Key Concepts

- **The Four Story Types**: Value Story (proves the product works by showing its impact), Founder Story (explains why the company exists through the founder's pivotal moment), Customer Story (provides social proof through narrative transformation), Purpose Story (connects the business to a larger mission that transcends profit).
- **The Story Formula**: Identifiable Character + Authentic Emotion + Significant Moment = a story that sticks. All three elements are required. Missing one produces something that looks like a story but fails to resonate.
- **Identifiable Character**: A character the audience can recognize, relate to, or empathize with. This does not mean the character must be similar to the audience, only that the audience can understand the character's situation and stakes.
- **Authentic Emotion**: Real feelings that create genuine connection. Hall distinguishes between manufactured sentimentality and authentic emotion, which comes from specificity and truth. The audience can always tell the difference.
- **Significant Moment**: The turning point, the specific event or realization that changes everything. Without it, the story is just an anecdote. The Significant Moment is what makes the story memorable and shareable.
- **The Gap-Bridge Framework (Normal to Explosion to New Normal)**: A three-beat narrative structure. Normal establishes the status quo, Explosion disrupts it, and New Normal shows the transformed reality. This structure applies to any story at any length.
- **The Storytelling Gap**: The difference between the stories a company could be telling and the stories it actually tells. Most businesses have far more story material than they realize, but they lack the systems to capture and deploy it.
- **Story Collection Systems**: Processes for systematically gathering stories from customers, employees, and founders. Hall provides interview templates and prompts designed to surface the Significant Moments that make stories powerful.
- **The "Finding" Problem**: Most businesses believe their challenge is crafting stories. Hall argues the real challenge is finding stories. They are happening every day in customer interactions, team meetings, and product experiences, but nobody is capturing them.
- **Story Placement Strategy**: Matching the right story type to the right marketing or sales context. Value Stories work in sales conversations, Customer Stories work on websites, Founder Stories work in media, Purpose Stories work in recruiting and culture building.
- **The Vulnerability Principle**: The most effective business stories include moments of doubt, failure, or uncertainty. Polished perfection is the enemy of connection because it signals inauthenticity.
- **The Specificity Principle**: General stories are forgettable. Specific stories are memorable. The more specific the character, moment, and emotion, the more universal the story's appeal, which is counterintuitive but consistently true.

## Application to Marketing

1. **Website and Landing Page Copy**: Each page or section of a website can be mapped to a story type. The About page calls for a Founder Story, the testimonials section for Customer Stories, the product page for Value Stories, and the mission or impact page for the Purpose Story. This mapping ensures every page has a narrative job.

2. **Sales Enablement**: Value Stories and Customer Stories can be packaged as sales enablement tools. When a salesperson can tell a specific story about a customer whose situation mirrors the prospect's, conversion rates improve dramatically. Hall's templates make it possible to build a story library that sales teams can actually use.

3. **Content Calendar Architecture**: The four story types provide a natural content rotation. A marketing team can ensure balanced storytelling by cycling through Value, Founder, Customer, and Purpose stories across their content calendar. This prevents the common problem of telling only one type of story.

4. **Email Sequences**: The Normal-Explosion-New Normal structure maps perfectly to email marketing. The first email establishes the Normal (the reader's current situation), the second introduces the Explosion (the problem or opportunity), and the third presents the New Normal (the solution and transformed outcome).

5. **Interview Templates for Story Mining**: Hall's story collection systems can be deployed by marketing teams to systematically extract stories from customers and internal stakeholders. This solves the "we don't have stories" problem that plagues many content teams. The templates are designed to surface Significant Moments, not just satisfaction quotes.
