# Brand Bewitchery: How to Wield Story to Bewitch, Beguile & Bewitch Your Brand Into Prosperity
> Park Howell, 2020

## Summary

Brand Bewitchery presents Park Howell's Story Cycle System, a 10-step strategic framework for building brand narratives that resonate deeply with audiences. Howell, a veteran advertising strategist turned story consultant, developed the system by combining Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey with the pragmatic demands of modern brand strategy. The book argues that brands fail not because they lack good products but because they lack a compelling narrative that positions them meaningfully in the lives of their customers.

The Story Cycle System distills the monomyth into ten actionable steps that brands can use to craft their core narrative. Howell walks readers through each step with examples from real brands he has worked with, ranging from startups to Fortune 500 companies. The system moves from establishing the backstory (the world as it is) through escalating challenges and transformative experiences to a resolution that redefines the brand's relationship with its audience.

A distinctive feature of the book is its emphasis on making the brand the guide rather than the hero. Like Donald Miller's StoryBrand, Howell positions the customer as the protagonist. However, Howell's framework goes deeper into narrative structure, incorporating elements like the "road of trials," "the ordeal," and "the reward" that give brand stories more dramatic texture and emotional range. The ten steps provide more narrative granularity than simpler frameworks, making it particularly useful for brands with complex stories or multi-stage customer journeys.

The book also integrates Howell's well-known ABT (And, But, Therefore) narrative framework as a micro-storytelling tool that operates within the larger Story Cycle. The ABT serves as the Swiss Army knife of narrative: it can be applied to a tweet, a pitch deck, or a brand manifesto. Within the Story Cycle, it provides the sentence-level tension and resolution that keeps each step of the larger narrative engaging.

Howell makes the case that story is not a marketing tactic but a strategic operating system. When a brand's narrative is clear and compelling, it aligns everything from product development to customer service to hiring. The brand story becomes the decision-making filter for the entire organization, and every touchpoint becomes a chapter in the same coherent narrative.

## Central Thesis

Howell's central argument is that brands that operate from a clear story outperform those that operate from features, benefits, or clever advertising alone. The Story Cycle System provides a repeatable, structured method for discovering and articulating that story. The thesis rests on the claim that human brains are wired for narrative, and brands that tap into universal story patterns gain an unfair advantage in attention, memory, and emotional connection.

He further argues that most brand storytelling fails because it is superficial, bolted on as a marketing layer rather than embedded in the brand's strategic DNA. Brand Bewitchery insists that the story must come first and that marketing, messaging, and content should all flow from a single, coherent narrative architecture. When the story is right, everything downstream becomes easier and more effective.

## Key Concepts

- **The 10-Step Story Cycle System**: A structured journey modeled on the Hero's Journey, adapted for brand strategy: (1) Backstory, (2) Hero/Audience, (3) Stakes, (4) Disruption, (5) Antagonist, (6) Mentor/Guide, (7) Journey, (8) Victory, (9) Moral, (10) Ritual. Each step has specific strategic questions and deliverables.
- **ABT Framework (And, But, Therefore)**: A micro-narrative structure for any communication. "We do X AND Y, BUT the problem is Z, THEREFORE we offer W." Cuts through noise with narrative tension. Usable at every level from tagline to brand narrative.
- **The Brand as Guide**: The brand occupies the mentor/guide role in the customer's story, not the hero role. The customer is always the protagonist whose transformation the brand enables.
- **The Backstory as Foundation**: Every brand narrative begins with the world as it is before the brand intervenes. This establishes context, relatability, and the status quo that the brand challenges.
- **Stakes Definition**: Clarifying what the customer stands to lose if the problem is not solved. Without stakes, there is no urgency and no reason for the customer to engage.
- **The Antagonist Force**: Identifying the specific villain, which may be a competitor, an outdated way of doing things, a systemic problem, or an internal obstacle, that the brand helps the customer overcome. The antagonist gives the brand story its energy and direction.
- **The Road of Trials**: The journey is not a straight line. Brands must acknowledge the challenges and setbacks their customers face rather than pretending the solution is effortless.
- **The Moral of the Story**: Every brand story has a takeaway, a value statement that encapsulates what the brand stands for and what it believes about the world.
- **The Ritual**: The repeatable action that keeps the customer engaged in the brand story over time (subscription, community participation, regular use, brand events). The ritual turns a transaction into an ongoing relationship.
- **Story as Operating System**: The brand narrative is not a marketing layer but a strategic framework that informs all business decisions, from product features to hiring criteria to customer service protocols.
- **Narrative Transportation**: When audiences are absorbed in a story, their critical defenses lower and they become more receptive to the message. Brand stories that achieve narrative transportation bypass rational objections.
- **The Story Cycle Canvas**: A visual template for mapping all ten steps of the Story Cycle for a specific brand, creating a single-page strategic narrative document.

## Application to Marketing

1. **Brand Positioning**: The Story Cycle System provides a structured method for developing brand positioning that goes beyond taglines and value propositions. By working through all ten steps, marketers create a rich narrative foundation that informs every touchpoint and ensures consistency across channels.

2. **Content Strategy Backbone**: The ten steps of the Story Cycle map directly to content pillars. Backstory content establishes context, stakes content creates urgency, journey content provides value, and victory content delivers proof. This gives content teams a strategic framework rather than an editorial calendar of disconnected topics.

3. **ABT for Everything**: The And-But-Therefore structure works for social media posts, email subject lines, pitch decks, product descriptions, and ad copy. It is the most versatile narrative tool in the book and can be taught to any team member in minutes. It serves as both a drafting tool and an editing test.

4. **Competitive Differentiation Through Antagonist Definition**: Clearly naming the antagonist (not necessarily a competitor, but often a way of doing things) sharpens the brand's positioning and gives the audience a reason to choose sides. The best brand antagonists are beliefs or behaviors, not companies.

5. **Customer Onboarding as Narrative Journey**: The Story Cycle can structure customer onboarding sequences, mapping each touchpoint to a stage of the journey so that the customer feels a sense of progression and purpose rather than a series of disconnected tasks.

## Strengths and Limitations

The ten-step Story Cycle provides more narrative granularity than simpler frameworks like StoryBrand's seven-step model. This makes it particularly valuable for brands with complex stories, multi-stage customer journeys, or audiences that require deeper narrative engagement.

The ABT framework is genuinely one of the most useful micro-tools in the storytelling world. Its simplicity makes it teachable to anyone in minutes, and its versatility means it applies to every communication format. The combination of ABT (micro-structure) and Story Cycle (macro-structure) gives practitioners tools at both scales.

The book benefits from Howell's decades of agency experience. The examples feel real and grounded in actual brand challenges, not theoretical scenarios.

A limitation is that the ten-step system can feel heavy for small businesses or solopreneurs who need a quicker path to a brand story. Not every brand needs all ten steps fully developed, and the book could provide clearer guidance on which steps are essential versus optional for different contexts.

The Hero's Journey foundation, while powerful, carries the risk of formulaic storytelling. Brands that follow the system too literally may produce narratives that feel archetypal but generic. The best results come from using the system as a discovery tool rather than a template.

The book's tone occasionally veers toward the promotional, reflecting Howell's marketing background. Readers looking for academic rigor may find it lacking, though the practical utility compensates.
