# The Story Grid
> Shawn Coyne, 2015

## Summary

Shawn Coyne's *The Story Grid* is a systematic, analytical methodology for evaluating and editing stories. Coyne, a veteran book editor with decades of experience at major publishing houses, developed the system out of frustration with the vagueness of most editorial feedback. Rather than telling writers their story "doesn't work" and leaving them to guess why, Coyne created a framework that allows editors and writers to diagnose story problems with the precision of a medical examination.

The system centers on two tools: the Story Grid Spreadsheet and the Story Grid Infographic. The spreadsheet is a scene-by-scene analysis that tracks, for every scene in a manuscript, the story event (what happens), the value shift (what changes and in which direction), the polarity shift (positive to negative or vice versa), the turning point (the moment that causes the shift), and the point of view.

The infographic is a visual representation of the entire story's value shifts over time, creating a kind of EKG reading that reveals whether the story has sufficient dramatic movement or flatlines.

Coyne's approach is heavily genre-driven. He argues that every story operates within a specific content genre (action, horror, love, performance, morality, status, society, worldview) and that each genre has its own set of obligatory scenes and conventions that must be present for the story to satisfy its audience. These are not optional stylistic choices but structural requirements that the audience expects, whether consciously or not.

A thriller without a "hero at the mercy of the villain" scene will feel incomplete regardless of how well-written it is. A love story without a "lovers break up" scene will feel like it skipped a step.

The Five Commandments of Storytelling—inciting incident, progressive complication, crisis, climax, resolution—must be present in every unit of story, from the individual scene to the global story arc. This fractal requirement ensures that every unit of the narrative is doing meaningful dramatic work.

## Central Thesis

Coyne's central argument is that story quality is not subjective—it is measurable, diagnosable, and fixable using systematic analysis. Stories fail for identifiable structural reasons: missing obligatory scenes, insufficient value shifts, absent or weak turning points, genre convention violations, or failure to execute the Five Commandments at every level.

An editor or writer armed with the Story Grid methodology can identify exactly where and why a story fails and prescribe specific remedies, much as a doctor diagnoses illness and prescribes treatment.

This stands in contrast to the mystification of storytelling craft that treats quality as ineffable and taste-dependent. Coyne does not deny the role of voice, style, and inspiration, but he insists that these qualities cannot save a story that lacks structural integrity, just as beautiful paint cannot save a house built on a cracked foundation.

## Key Concepts

- **The Five Commandments of Storytelling**: Inciting incident (the event that upsets the status quo), progressive complication (escalating obstacles), crisis (the dilemma the protagonist must face), climax (the decision and action the protagonist takes), resolution (the new status quo). Must be present in every scene and every act.

- **The Story Grid Spreadsheet**: A scene-by-scene analytical tool tracking: story event, value at stake, value shift (with polarity), turning point type (action or revelation), and POV character. This turns intuitive editorial judgment into structured analysis.

- **The Story Grid Infographic**: A visual plot of value shifts across the entire story, showing the dramatic shape at a glance—peaks, valleys, flatlines, and the overall trajectory. Flatlines indicate scenes or sequences that need revision.

- **Content Genre**: The primary genre that defines the core value at stake (Life/Death for Action, Love/Hate for Love, Life/Unconsciousness for Horror, etc.). Each content genre has specific obligatory scenes and conventions that function as structural load-bearing walls.

- **Obligatory Scenes**: Scenes that must appear in a given genre for the story to work. In a thriller: the hero discovers the villain's MacGuffin, the hero is at the mercy of the villain, the hero's initial strategy fails. These are not clichés—they are essential structural elements.

- **Conventions**: The genre-specific narrative devices audiences expect: the love story needs lovers meeting, a secret, and a ritual. These are distinct from obligatory scenes—conventions are setups, obligatory scenes are payoffs.

- **Value Shifts**: Every scene must turn—must move from one value state to another (e.g., life to death, love to hate, freedom to captivity). A scene with no value shift is a flat scene and should be cut or rewritten.

- **Turning Points**: The specific moment within a scene that causes the value to shift. Turning points come in two types: action (something happens) and revelation (something is learned). The best scenes combine both.

- **Controlling Idea/Theme**: The one-sentence statement that expresses the story's argument about the human condition, formulated as: "[Value] prevails when [cause]" or "[Value] is lost when [cause]."

- **Global Story vs. Subplots**: The global story operates in the primary content genre; subplots operate in secondary genres. A thriller (global: action) might have a love subplot. Both must fulfill their own genre's requirements independently.

- **The Editor's Six Core Questions**: What is the genre? What are the conventions and obligatory scenes? What is the point of view? What are the objects of desire? What is the controlling idea? What is the beginning hook, middle build, and ending payoff?

- **Progressive Complications**: Obstacles must escalate. Each complication must be more challenging than the last, creating an upward ramp of difficulty that forces the protagonist to grow or break.

## Application to Marketing

- **The Five Commandments for Marketing Content**: Every piece of marketing content—from a blog post to a sales page—should contain all five commandments: an inciting incident (the problem that disrupts the reader's status quo), progressive complications (why the problem is harder than they thought), a crisis (the choice they face), a climax (the recommendation/offer), and a resolution (the transformed future).

- **Value Shifts as Engagement Predictors**: Content that produces clear value shifts (ignorance to knowledge, confusion to clarity, anxiety to confidence) outperforms content that merely delivers information without a felt shift. Before publishing, ask: what value does the reader start at, and what value do they end at? If the answer is the same, the content is flat.

- **Genre Analysis for Brand Content Strategy**: Identifying your brand's content genre gives you a checklist of obligatory scenes and conventions. A B2B SaaS brand operating in the "performance" genre has different obligatory content than a luxury brand in the "status" genre. Genre analysis prevents the common mistake of creating content that doesn't match your audience's underlying value stakes.

- **The Spreadsheet for Content Audits**: The Story Grid Spreadsheet can be adapted into a content audit tool. For each piece of content in your library, track: the story event, the value shift, and the turning point. Content without clear entries in these columns is underperforming.

- **Controlling Idea as Brand Thesis**: Every brand should be able to articulate its controlling idea: "[Value] prevails when [cause]." For example: "Business growth prevails when companies clarify their message and treat the customer as the hero." This controlling idea then becomes the test for every piece of content.

## Notable Quotes

- "If a scene doesn't turn, it's not a scene. It's a static display. Cut it or fix it."
- "Genre is not a marketing label. Genre is a set of promises to the reader about the emotional experience they will have."
- "The controlling idea is the argument your story makes about life. If you don't know your argument, your story doesn't know what it's about."
- "Obligatory scenes are the load-bearing walls of your story. You can renovate everything else, but remove a load-bearing wall and the structure collapses."
- "Every unit of story—scene, sequence, act, global—must have all five commandments. No exceptions."
- "The Story Grid is not a creative tool. It is a diagnostic tool. Create first, diagnose second, repair third."
