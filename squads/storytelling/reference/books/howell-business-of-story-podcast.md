# Business of Story Podcast: Key Episodes Index and Framework Reference
> Park Howell, Ongoing (launched 2015)

## Summary

The Business of Story podcast, hosted by Park Howell, is one of the longest-running and most focused podcasts on the intersection of storytelling and business strategy. Over hundreds of episodes, Howell interviews brand storytellers, marketers, authors, neuroscientists, and executives about how narrative drives business outcomes. The podcast serves as both a living laboratory for Howell's ABT (And, But, Therefore) framework and a broad survey of the storytelling-in-business landscape.

Howell uses the podcast as a platform to test, refine, and extend the ideas in his books and consulting practice. Many episodes feature live demonstrations of the ABT framework, where Howell helps guests restructure their brand messages in real time. These live coaching segments are among the most practically valuable content in the storytelling podcast space because they show the framework in action rather than just describing it theoretically. Listeners hear the "before" version of a message, the coaching conversation, and the "after" version in a single episode.

The guest roster spans the storytelling world, including repeat appearances from figures like Donald Miller, Kindra Hall, Matthew Dicks, and Nancy Duarte, as well as lesser-known practitioners who bring fresh perspectives from specific industries. Recurring themes include the neuroscience of story, the failure of "and, and, and" communication (what Howell calls the "And Plague"), and the application of narrative structure to sales, leadership, and content marketing.

The podcast also functions as an ongoing case study library. Howell frequently features brand leaders who share how they applied story principles to solve real business problems, from repositioning a struggling brand to launching a new product category to transforming internal culture. These episodes provide concrete evidence that narrative strategy produces measurable business results, not just softer outcomes like engagement or brand sentiment.

The format is consistent and disciplined. Each episode opens with a brief framing of the guest and topic, moves into conversation that often includes live ABT coaching, and closes with actionable takeaways. This consistency makes the podcast itself a case study in content marketing through storytelling: it demonstrates sustained audience building through narrative coherence.

## Central Thesis

The podcast's through-line argument is that clarity of narrative is the single most underleveraged competitive advantage in business. Howell consistently demonstrates that most businesses suffer from what he calls the "And Plague," a communication style that strings together facts, features, and benefits with no narrative tension. The ABT framework is his antidote: a simple, teachable structure that introduces conflict and resolution into any message, making it inherently more engaging and memorable.

Across episodes, Howell builds the case that storytelling is not a soft skill or a creative luxury but a hard strategic tool with direct impact on revenue, retention, and organizational alignment. He supports this with guest testimony, case studies, and neuroscience research that collectively demonstrate the business value of narrative clarity.

## Key Concepts

- **The ABT Framework (And, But, Therefore)**: The recurring structural tool across all episodes. Every communication can be improved by introducing the "But" (tension/conflict) and resolving it with "Therefore" (action/resolution). This is the podcast's signature contribution.
- **The And Plague**: The tendency of businesses to communicate in long strings of "and" statements (features, benefits, capabilities) with no narrative tension. This is the enemy of engagement and the default mode of most corporate communication.
- **The AAA Framework (And, And, And)**: The anti-pattern. Communication that is all setup and no payoff. Howell identifies this as what happens when communicators try to include everything and end up saying nothing.
- **DHY Framework (Despite, However, Yet)**: A variation used to express opposition or counterargument within narrative structure. Useful for handling objections or presenting contrarian positions.
- **Live ABT Coaching**: Many episodes feature real-time restructuring of a guest's brand message using the ABT, demonstrating immediate improvement in clarity and persuasiveness. These segments show the framework applied to real businesses in real time.
- **Story Cycle System in Practice**: Episodes that walk through the 10-step system with real brands, showing how each step manifests in actual marketing and business contexts. These episodes complement the theory in Brand Bewitchery with applied examples.
- **Neuroscience of Narrative**: Recurring topic across multiple episodes, covering how stories trigger oxytocin (trust and bonding), dopamine (reward and attention), and cortisol (tension and alertness) responses that drive engagement and memory.
- **The Five Primal Elements of Story**: Characters, stakes, conflict, transformation, and resolution. Howell returns to these elements as the irreducible core of any effective story, regardless of medium or context.
- **Narrative Transportation Theory**: The psychological state in which an audience becomes absorbed in a story and lowers their critical resistance. Episodes explore how brands can achieve this state through content and communication.
- **Story-Powered Sales**: Episodes dedicated to how sales teams can use narrative structure in prospecting, pitching, and closing. These episodes bridge the gap between marketing narrative and sales conversation.
- **The Business of Story Canvas**: A strategic planning tool discussed in several episodes that helps brands map their narrative architecture on a single page, making the brand story visible and shareable across teams.
- **The Five-Minute Brand Audit**: A diagnostic exercise featured in multiple episodes where Howell helps brands quickly assess the clarity and coherence of their current narrative.

## Application to Marketing

1. **ABT for Content Creation**: The ABT framework can be applied to any content format. Blog post introductions, email subject lines, social media captions, video scripts, and ad copy all benefit from the tension-resolution structure. Multiple episodes provide specific templates for each format, making implementation immediate.

2. **Brand Message Clarification**: The live coaching segments provide a model for how marketing teams can workshop their brand messages. A team can take any existing piece of messaging and restructure it using ABT in under ten minutes, often with dramatic improvement in clarity. This makes for excellent team exercises.

3. **Podcast as Content Marketing Case Study**: The podcast itself is a masterclass in content marketing through storytelling. Howell demonstrates sustained audience building through consistent narrative themes, guest curation, and format discipline. Marketers can study his approach as a model for their own content programs.

4. **Sales Enablement Content**: Episodes on story-powered sales provide frameworks that marketing teams can package into sales enablement materials, helping bridge the gap between marketing narrative and sales conversation. The ABT structure translates directly to sales scripts and objection handling.

5. **Competitive Messaging**: The antagonist-definition episodes help marketers sharpen their competitive positioning by clearly naming the problem or enemy, not as a competitor but as a way of doing things that the brand challenges. This creates narrative energy without engaging in direct competitive attacks.

## Key Episode Themes

The podcast episodes cluster around several recurring themes that provide a useful index for practitioners:

- **ABT Deep Dives**: Episodes that demonstrate the ABT framework across specific contexts: nonprofit fundraising, B2B sales, product launches, investor pitches, and internal communication.
- **Neuroscience of Story**: Episodes featuring neuroscientists and psychologists who explain the biological mechanisms behind narrative engagement, including oxytocin research, mirror neuron theory, and cognitive load studies.
- **Brand Turnaround Stories**: Episodes featuring leaders who used narrative strategy to reposition struggling brands, often with specific metrics on the business impact.
- **Story-Powered Leadership**: Episodes on how executives use storytelling to lead teams, communicate vision, and manage change.
- **Content Marketing Through Story**: Episodes specifically addressing how content marketers can apply story principles to blogs, videos, social media, and email marketing.
- **Sales Narrative**: Episodes on integrating story into sales conversations, from discovery calls to final presentations.
- **Author Interviews**: Episodes featuring storytelling book authors who discuss their frameworks in conversation with Howell, often with live ABT demonstrations.
- **The And Plague Diagnosis**: Episodes that demonstrate in real time how to identify and cure the "And, And, And" pattern in existing brand messaging and marketing copy.
