# Stories Sell: Storyworthy Strategies to Grow Your Business
> Matthew Dicks, 2024

## Summary

Stories Sell extends Matthew Dicks's personal storytelling methodology into the world of business, sales, and marketing. After the success of Storyworthy, Dicks observed that business professionals were adapting his personal narrative techniques for commercial contexts, often with remarkable results. This book formalizes that adaptation, providing a bridge between the intimate craft of personal storytelling and the practical demands of selling, leading, and building brands.

The book opens with a critical distinction: business storytelling is not corporate storytelling. Dicks rejects the sanitized, committee-approved narratives that most companies produce. Instead, he argues that the same principles that make personal stories powerful on a Moth stage, vulnerability, specificity, a clear moment of change, are exactly what make business stories effective. The difference is in the strategic application, not in the underlying craft.

Dicks provides frameworks for identifying which business situations call for stories and which do not, for selecting the right story from your archive, and for calibrating the level of personal revelation appropriate for a business context. He addresses common objections head-on: that storytelling is manipulative, that business audiences do not want stories, that data is more persuasive than narrative. In each case, he provides evidence and counter-examples showing that human connection through story is not the opposite of rigor but its complement.

The book also covers storytelling across specific business functions: sales conversations, keynote presentations, team meetings, job interviews, fundraising pitches, and customer communications. For each context, Dicks offers adapted techniques and real-world examples of professionals who used personal stories to achieve business outcomes. The examples are specific and credible, drawn from real people with real results, which itself models the book's central teaching about the power of specificity.

A particularly valuable section addresses the story archive: the practice of building a personal library of stories indexed by theme, emotion, and application. Dicks argues that having the right story ready for the right moment is a competitive advantage that cannot be faked or rushed, and he provides a systematic method for building and maintaining this library over time.

## Central Thesis

Dicks argues that the gap between personal storytelling and business storytelling is much smaller than most people assume, and that closing this gap produces outsized results. Business professionals who learn to tell genuine, personal, human stories in commercial contexts build trust faster, close more deals, inspire stronger teams, and create more memorable brands. The thesis is that human connection is the fundamental unit of business, and story is the most efficient vehicle for creating that connection.

He insists that authenticity is non-negotiable. Fabricated stories, exaggerated stories, and stories that manipulate rather than connect will eventually undermine trust. The techniques in the book are designed to help people find and tell true stories that happen to serve business objectives, not to manufacture fictions that serve those objectives. The distinction between connection and manipulation is a moral line that Dicks draws clearly and repeatedly.

## Key Concepts

- **The Connection Economy**: Business success increasingly depends on human connection, and stories are the most efficient tool for building it. Data persuades the mind; stories persuade the heart. You need both, but most professionals neglect the heart.
- **The Story Archive for Business**: Building a personal library of stories indexed by theme, emotion, and business context. Having the right story ready for the right moment is a competitive advantage that compounds over time.
- **Strategic Vulnerability**: Revealing enough of yourself to create connection without oversharing or undermining credibility. Dicks provides calibration guidelines for different business contexts, from sales calls to keynotes to team meetings.
- **The Five-Second Moment in Business**: Applying the five-second moment concept to business stories. The moment of realization, decision, or change that anchors the story and makes it memorable. Business stories that lack this moment are forgettable.
- **Story Selection**: Not every situation calls for a story, and not every story fits every situation. Dicks provides a decision framework for when to tell a story, which story to tell, and when to simply present the facts. Knowing when not to tell a story is as important as knowing how.
- **The Bridge Technique**: Using a personal story to bridge into a business point. The story creates emotional engagement, and the bridge connects it to the professional message. The bridge must feel natural, not forced.
- **Homework for Life (Business Edition)**: Adapting the daily noticing practice to business contexts. Recording customer interactions, meeting moments, and workplace observations that could become future stories. This builds the archive over time.
- **The Anti-Pitch**: Using a story to create pull rather than push in sales contexts. Instead of pitching features, telling a story that makes the prospect recognize their own situation and ask for the solution.
- **Specificity as Credibility**: Vague stories feel fabricated. Specific details (names, dates, places, sensory details) signal authenticity and build trust. This applies to business stories as much as personal ones.
- **The Rule of One**: Each story should convey one point, create one emotion, and drive one action. Overloading a story with multiple messages dilutes all of them.
- **Emotional Residue**: The feeling that lingers after a story ends. This residue colors how the audience perceives everything that follows. Strategic storytellers choose stories whose emotional residue serves their next move.
- **The Permission Slip**: The first person in a meeting or conversation who tells a genuine story gives everyone else permission to do the same. This transforms the quality of communication across an entire organization.
- **Story Stacking**: The strategic sequencing of multiple stories within a presentation or meeting, where each story builds on the emotional foundation of the previous one to create a cumulative effect.

## Application to Marketing

1. **Sales Enablement Through Story Libraries**: Marketing teams can build shared story libraries organized by buyer persona, objection type, and sales stage. Each story in the library includes the five-second moment, suggested bridge to the business point, and context for appropriate use. This turns storytelling from an individual talent into an organizational capability.

2. **Founder Stories That Convert**: Many founders tell their origin stories poorly, either too self-aggrandizing or too generic. Dicks's techniques help founders find the specific, vulnerable, human moment that makes their origin story authentic and resonant. The five-second moment framework prevents founders from telling their whole biography when they should be sharing one pivotal instant.

3. **Case Studies as Real Stories**: Traditional case studies follow the Problem-Solution-Results formula and read like business documents. Applying Dicks's narrative techniques transforms them into genuine stories with characters, tension, and transformation that prospects actually want to read and share.

4. **Email Marketing with Personal Narrative**: The most effective marketing emails often open with a short personal story before transitioning to the business message. The bridge technique provides a structured method for doing this without feeling forced or manipulative.

5. **Video Testimonials with Emotional Truth**: Instead of scripted testimonials, marketers can coach customers to identify their five-second moment, the specific instant when they realized the product changed something for them. These moments create far more powerful testimonials than rehearsed endorsements because they carry the unmistakable ring of truth.
