# Community Language

## Purpose

Community language transforms an audience into a movement. Drawing on Marshall Ganz's public narrative framework, these phrases use "us," "we," and "together" to build shared identity, shared purpose, and shared urgency. This language is essential for brand communities, membership programs, cause marketing, internal culture content, and any context where the goal is collective action rather than individual purchase. The shift from "I/you" to "we/us" is one of the most powerful moves in marketing storytelling.

## Collection

### Story of Us (Shared Identity)

> Use to define who "we" are. These phrases create in-group identity by naming shared values, shared experiences, or shared characteristics. (Marshall Ganz: "The Story of Us communicates the values that move us as a community.")

- "We're the ones who stayed up late because the work mattered."
- "We didn't choose this path because it was easy. We chose it because it was right."
- "This is a community of builders. Not talkers. Builders."
- "We're united by a belief that [shared value]."
- "What connects us isn't a product. It's a perspective."
- "We come from different industries, different backgrounds, different time zones. But we share the same frustration and the same ambition."
- "You're here because you refuse to accept 'good enough.'"
- "We're the people who read the fine print, question the defaults, and build something better."
- "This isn't a customer base. It's a community of practitioners."
- "We've all felt it: that moment when you realize the old way doesn't work anymore."
- "We are the ones we've been waiting for."

### Story of Now (Shared Urgency)

> Use to catalyze action by framing the present moment as a critical juncture. (Marshall Ganz: "The Story of Now articulates the urgent challenge that demands action now.")

- "The moment is now. Not next quarter. Not next year. Now."
- "We've talked about this long enough. It's time to act."
- "The world isn't waiting for us to be ready. We have to move."
- "This is the inflection point. What we do in the next [timeframe] defines what comes next."
- "If not us, who? If not now, when?"
- "The window is open. But it won't stay open."
- "Everything we've been preparing for leads to this moment."
- "The change we've been asking for requires the action we've been avoiding."
- "We are at the beginning of something. Let's not waste it."
- "Today is the day the conversation becomes a commitment."

### Collective Action Language

> Use to move from shared identity to shared behavior. These phrases make the audience feel that their individual action is part of something larger.

- "When one of us wins, all of us win."
- "Every contribution moves us forward."
- "Together, we've already [collective achievement]."
- "Your story is part of our story."
- "None of us is as smart as all of us."
- "This works because we show up for each other."
- "The strength of this community is that nobody has to figure it out alone."
- "We share our playbooks. We share our failures. That's how we all get better."
- "What one person discovers, the whole community benefits from."
- "This isn't a solo mission. It never was."
- "Join the effort. Own a piece of the outcome."

### Inclusive Language Patterns

> Use to make every member feel seen and welcome, regardless of their stage, background, or entry point.

- "Whether you're just starting or you've been at this for years, there's a place for you here."
- "We don't gatekeep. We open doors."
- "Every expert here was once a beginner. We remember that."
- "Bring your questions. Bring your doubts. Bring your ambition."
- "You belong here. Not because of what you've accomplished. Because of what you're committed to."
- "We learn from each other. Titles don't matter here. Ideas do."
- "This space is built on respect, curiosity, and a shared commitment to getting better."
- "No prerequisites. No minimum credentials. Just a willingness to do the work."
- "Your perspective is exactly what this community needs."

### Movement-Building Phrases

> Use to frame the brand, product, or community as part of a larger cause. Movement language elevates a value proposition from commercial to cultural.

- "We're not building a company. We're building a movement."
- "This is bigger than any one of us. That's what makes it worth doing."
- "We're on the right side of this shift, and we're just getting started."
- "Movements start with a few people who refuse to accept the status quo. That's us."
- "The change we're making isn't just for our customers. It's for the entire industry."
- "Every person who joins accelerates the change for everyone."
- "We're proof that a better way is possible."
- "This started as an idea. It became a product. Now it's becoming a standard."
- "The future doesn't happen to us. We make it happen."
- "What we're building will outlast any one campaign, any one quarter, any one product launch."

### Gratitude and Recognition Language

> Use to honor the community's contributions. Recognition builds loyalty and reinforces participation. People repeat behaviors that are acknowledged.

- "This community made this possible. Not us. You."
- "Every idea in here came from someone just like you."
- "We built this together. We celebrate it together."
- "Thank you for showing up. It matters more than you know."
- "The best features in our product came from your feedback."
- "You didn't just use the product. You shaped it."
- "Your stories are the reason we do this work."
- "We see you. The late nights. The experiments. The persistence."
- "This milestone belongs to everyone who contributed, questioned, and believed."
- "We don't take this community for granted. Not for a single day."

### "We" vs. "You" Calibration

> Use these paired examples to understand when to use collective language ("we") vs. direct address ("you"). Both have power; the key is intentionality.

- **We (collective):** "We're going to figure this out together." vs. **You (direct):** "You're going to figure this out. And we'll be here when you do."
- **We (inclusive):** "We all face this challenge." vs. **You (empowering):** "You have everything you need to solve this."
- **We (movement):** "We're changing how this industry works." vs. **You (personal):** "You're about to change how you work."
- **We (belonging):** "We're glad you're here." vs. **You (recognition):** "You made this community better by joining."

## Usage Guidelines

- **Earn the "we."** Don't use collective language before you've built genuine shared experience. Premature "we" feels presumptuous. Start with "you," transition to "we" as the relationship deepens.
- **Be specific about shared values.** "We're all in this together" means nothing. "We believe that [specific value] matters more than [competing value]" means everything.
- **Include without excluding.** Community language that defines "us" by who we're against ("we're not like those people") is fragile. Define "us" by what we're for.
- **Recognize before asking.** Gratitude before a call to action is more effective than a call to action alone. Thank people for being here, then invite them to go further.
- **Use Marshall Ganz's three-story framework:** Story of Self (your personal why), Story of Us (our shared identity), Story of Now (the urgent call to action). In that order.
