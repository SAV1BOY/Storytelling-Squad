# Pitch Power Phrases

## Purpose

Pitches are high-stakes storytelling under time pressure. These phrases are designed for investor pitches, sales presentations, client proposals, and partnership conversations where you need to establish authority, maintain frame control, build intrigue, and leverage social proof, all while sounding natural, not scripted. The best pitch is a story with structure and power language baked in.

## Collection

### Frame Control Phrases

> Use to define the terms of the conversation. Whoever sets the frame controls the narrative. These phrases establish that you are the authority in the room.

- "Let me reframe the question."
- "The real issue isn't [X]. It's [Y]."
- "Before I answer that, let me give you the context that changes the question."
- "That's the wrong comparison. Here's the right one."
- "Most people look at this as a [X] problem. It's actually a [Y] problem."
- "I'm going to challenge the premise of that question."
- "The way the market thinks about this is outdated. Here's the current reality."
- "Let me show you what this looks like from our vantage point."
- "The question isn't whether this market is big enough. The question is whether the current players are good enough."
- "Everyone in this space is solving the wrong problem. Let me show you the right one."

### Intrigue Phrases

> Use to create curiosity and pull the audience toward your reveal. Intrigue phrases work best right before you share a key insight, metric, or differentiator.

- "What we discovered next changed our entire approach."
- "This is the part nobody else will tell you."
- "There's a hidden dynamic in this market that most people miss."
- "We found something in the data that surprised even us."
- "I'm going to show you something that isn't in any analyst report."
- "The insight that changed everything came from the most unlikely place."
- "We've been sitting on this for [time]. Today, we're sharing it."
- "What I'm about to show you is the reason we exist."
- "Every investor we've talked to has the same reaction to this slide."
- "This next number is going to reframe everything I've said so far."

### Authority Phrases

> Use to establish that you and your team are the ones who should be solving this problem. Authority in a pitch comes from specificity, track record, and conviction.

- "We've spent [time] going deeper on this problem than anyone else."
- "Our team has collectively [X years] in this exact space."
- "We didn't enter this market. We created it."
- "This is the third company I've built in [space]. The first two taught me everything I needed to know."
- "We have proprietary [data/technology/relationships] that can't be replicated."
- "The reason we're the right team is [specific reason]."
- "We've already done what most companies in this space are still planning."
- "Our unfair advantage is [specific, concrete thing]."
- "We're not theorizing. We've already proven this with [evidence]."
- "Nobody knows this customer better than we do. Here's why."

### Social Proof Framing

> Use to name-drop, reference traction, and cite results without sounding desperate. Social proof should feel like evidence, not bragging.

- "[Impressive client] came to us because [specific reason]."
- "In the last [time period], [number] companies have chosen us over [competitor]. Here's why."
- "Our NPS is [number]. For context, [benchmark comparison]."
- "When [credible person] saw our approach, they said [quote]."
- "[Metric] in [timeframe], with [team size/budget constraint]."
- "We were oversubscribed in our last round. This round, we're being selective."
- "The waitlist hit [number] before we launched."
- "Our customers don't just renew. They expand. Average expansion revenue: [%]."
- "We didn't seek out [big client]. They found us."
- "[Industry publication] called us [quote]. We think they understated it."

### Objection Preemption Phrases

> Use to address the most common concerns before they become objections. Preempting objections signals confidence and preparation.

- "You're probably wondering about [concern]. Let me address that directly."
- "The obvious question is [X]. Here's the answer."
- "If I were sitting where you are, I'd be asking [question]. Here's what I'd want to hear."
- "Yes, there are risks. Here are the three biggest ones, and here's how we've mitigated each."
- "The skeptics say [objection]. Here's what they're missing."
- "I know what you're thinking. And you're right to think it. But here's the data."
- "We've heard this concern before. In fact, we had it ourselves. Here's what changed our mind."
- "Let me save you a question: the answer is [X], and here's the proof."

### Closing / Ask Phrases

> Use to make the ask feel like a natural conclusion, not an awkward transition. The best pitch closes are confident, specific, and give the audience a clear next step.

- "We're raising [amount] to [specific purpose]. Here's how that maps to [outcome]."
- "The opportunity is now. Here's exactly what we need to capture it."
- "We're not asking you to bet on a vision. We're asking you to bet on traction."
- "I'd like to continue this conversation. What's the best next step for you?"
- "The deal is straightforward: [terms]. The upside is [specific]."
- "We're choosing our partners carefully. We'd like you to be one of them."
- "This is a [timeframe] window. Let me tell you why."
- "If what I've shown you resonates, let's talk about what working together looks like."

## Usage Guidelines

- **Frame before you pitch.** The first 60 seconds should establish the frame, not the product. If the audience is thinking about the problem the wrong way, no amount of features will convince them.
- **Use intrigue sparingly.** One or two intrigue moments per pitch. More than that feels manipulative.
- **Social proof is evidence, not decoration.** Every name, number, or quote should advance your argument. If it doesn't, cut it.
- **Preempt the top 3 objections.** Know what your audience is thinking and address it before they have to ask. This builds massive credibility.
- **Close with confidence.** The ask should feel inevitable, not sudden. If you've built the story correctly, the close is just the final beat.
