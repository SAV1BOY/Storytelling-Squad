# Words That Stick

## Purpose
Words and phrases that are memorable, shareable, and hard to forget — based on
Chip and Dan Heath's SUCCESs framework (*Made to Stick*) and broader memorability
research.

---

## The SUCCESs Framework Applied to Word Choice

### S — Simple
Core message stripped to its essence. One idea, clearly expressed.

**Sticky phrases:**
1. "Find your one thing."
2. "Confusion is the enemy of conversion."
3. "Story is strategy."
4. "If you confuse, you lose."
5. "Clear beats clever."
6. "Name the pain."
7. "One message. One audience. One action."
8. "Don't make them think."

**Why they stick:** Short. Rhythmic. Impossible to misunderstand.

### U — Unexpected
Break a pattern. Violate expectations. Create a knowledge gap.

**Sticky phrases:**
9. "The best marketing doesn't feel like marketing."
10. "Your biggest competitor isn't a company. It's indifference."
11. "Data doesn't convince. Stories that contain data convince."
12. "The most dangerous phrase in business is 'we've always done it this way.'"
13. "Your customer doesn't care about your product. They care about their problem."
14. "The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea."
15. "Comfort is the enemy of growth."
16. "The meeting that could have been an email could have been a sentence."

**Why they stick:** The brain flags the unexpected. Surprise = attention = memory.

### C — Concrete
Specific, sensory, tangible language. Not abstractions.

**Sticky phrases:**
17. "Put a face on the data."
18. "Don't say 'improved efficiency.' Say 'cut the process from 3 hours to 20 minutes.'"
19. "A story without a character is a report."
20. "Show me the receipt."
21. "Features tell. Stories sell."
22. "Numbers are the skeleton. Story is the skin."
23. "The best elevator pitch doesn't need an elevator."

**Why they stick:** The brain processes concrete images faster than abstractions.

### C — Credible
Language that earns trust through specificity, authority, or verifiability.

**Sticky phrases:**
24. "We tested this with 2,400 companies. Here's what we found."
25. "Don't take our word for it. [Name] at [Company] said…"
26. "The data doesn't lie."
27. "We've been wrong before. Here's what we learned."
28. "Tested. Measured. Proven."
29. "I can show you the spreadsheet."
30. "Zero spin. Here's exactly what happened."

**Why they stick:** Trust amplifies memorability. We remember what we believe.

### E — Emotional
Language that makes people feel something — not just understand something.

**Sticky phrases:**
31. "What would you tell your younger self?"
32. "The customer isn't a data point. She's a person who stayed up until 2 a.m. trying to make your product work."
33. "Behind every churn metric is a broken promise."
34. "This isn't about revenue. This is about someone's livelihood."
35. "Build something worth caring about."
36. "Make them feel seen."
37. "The goal isn't to be liked. The goal is to be needed."

**Why they stick:** Emotion encodes memories. We remember how things made us feel.

### S — Stories
Narrative structures that carry ideas.

**Sticky phrases (that are mini-stories):**
38. "She started with a laptop and a question. She ended with a company and an answer."
39. "The first investor said no. The fiftieth said yes. The first now works for her."
40. "He walked into the pitch with no deck. He walked out with a term sheet."
41. "They launched on a Tuesday. By Thursday, they had more users than their server could handle."
42. "The worst day of the company became the best chapter of the brand story."

**Why they stick:** Stories are how humans have transmitted knowledge for 100,000 years.

## Structural Patterns That Create Stickiness

### The Rule of Three
43. "Clarity. Brevity. Impact."
44. "Test. Learn. Repeat."
45. "Character. Problem. Transformation."
46. "Hook. Story. Offer."
47. "Stop. Think. Start."
48. "Listen. Learn. Lead."

### Rhyme and Rhythm
49. "If it doesn't fit, you must acquit." (Cochran)
50. "A stitch in time saves nine."
51. "Fail to plan, plan to fail."
52. "What gets measured gets managed."
53. "Innovate or evaporate."
54. "When you're clear, they'll hear."

### Contrast Pairs
55. "Think big. Start small."
56. "Move fast. Break things." (early Facebook)
57. "Simple can be harder than complex." (Jobs)
58. "Fewer meetings. More decisions."
59. "Less noise. More signal."
60. "Don't count the days. Make the days count."

### The Inverted Expectation
61. "The customer is not always right. But the customer is always the customer."
62. "Culture eats strategy for breakfast." (attributed to Drucker)
63. "The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second-best time is now."
64. "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity." (Seneca)
65. "If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together."

## Making Your Own Words Stick

### The Stickiness Checklist
Before publishing any key phrase, tagline, or headline, test it:

1. **Can you say it in one breath?** If not, shorten it.
2. **Does it contain a concrete image?** If not, add one.
3. **Would someone repeat it to a colleague?** If not, it's not sticky.
4. **Does it violate an expectation?** If not, it might be forgettable.
5. **Can you visualize it?** If not, it's too abstract.
6. **Does it sound good out loud?** Read it. Listen to the rhythm.
7. **Is it true?** Sticky lies are still lies.

### The "Bar Test"
Imagine explaining your idea at a noisy bar. What phrase would you shout?
That's your sticky version.

### The "T-Shirt Test"
Would someone wear this phrase on a t-shirt? If yes, it sticks.
If no, it's too corporate, too vague, or too long.
