# Opening Lines Bank

## Purpose

The opening line is the single most important sentence in any piece of content. It determines whether the audience stays or leaves. This bank contains curated opening lines organized by type, each designed for a specific narrative strategy. Unlike hook phrases (which are shorter, punchier attention-grabbers), opening lines are complete thoughts that set the tone, genre, and promise of the entire piece.

## Collection

### In Medias Res (Starting in the Middle)

> Use to drop the audience into an active scene. No preamble, no setup. The story is already happening. Best for: keynotes, long-form content, case studies, video scripts.

- "The server went down at 11:47 p.m. on a Tuesday, and by midnight, we'd lost everything."
- "I was standing in a conference room with twelve people who hated the idea, and I had four minutes to change their minds."
- "She slid the contract across the table and said, 'This is your last chance.'"
- "The phone rang at 3 a.m. It was our biggest client. And they weren't calling to say thank you."
- "We had $4,200 in the bank, a product that barely worked, and a demo scheduled with the CEO of [company] in six hours."
- "I was halfway through the presentation when I realized I was solving the wrong problem."
- "The investor looked at the deck, then at me, and said one word: 'Why?'"
- "The email had three words: 'We're pulling out.' I read it seven times."
- "I opened the analytics dashboard on launch day and the line was flat. Completely flat."
- "My co-founder walked into my office, closed the door, and said, 'We need to talk about the numbers.'"

### Question Openings

> Use to engage the audience's brain immediately. A well-crafted question creates an open loop that demands resolution. Best for: social media, emails, blog posts, ad copy.

- "What if everything you've been told about [X] is wrong?"
- "When was the last time a piece of content actually changed how you work?"
- "How many hours did you spend last week on something that didn't matter?"
- "What would your business look like if you could start over with everything you know now?"
- "Have you ever made a decision you knew was wrong, but couldn't stop yourself?"
- "What's the one metric you track that actually predicts growth?"
- "If your customers could tell you one honest thing, what would they say?"
- "Why do the companies with the best products sometimes lose?"
- "What if your biggest weakness is actually your most underused strength?"
- "How do you explain a feeling? That's the challenge we faced."

### Bold Statement Openings

> Use to stake a claim and establish authority immediately. Bold statements demand agreement or disagreement; either way, the audience is engaged. Best for: thought leadership, manifestos, LinkedIn, pitch decks.

- "Content marketing is dead. What replaced it is better."
- "The best companies don't have marketing departments. They have storytelling cultures."
- "Your brand story isn't about you. It never was."
- "There are only three strategies that actually work at scale. Everything else is noise."
- "The future of [industry] was decided five years ago. Most people just don't know it yet."
- "We are in the middle of the biggest shift in [industry] in fifty years."
- "Nobody needs another [common product]. They need a better [outcome]."
- "The companies that will dominate the next decade have one thing in common."
- "Storytelling isn't a soft skill. It's the hardest skill in business."
- "If your marketing requires a disclaimer, your marketing isn't working."

### Sensory Openings

> Use to create an immersive experience from the first sentence. Sensory openings make the audience see, hear, feel, or smell the scene. Best for: brand narratives, long-form essays, video voiceover, experiential content.

- "The fluorescent lights hummed. The whiteboard was covered in red ink. The coffee was cold."
- "You could hear the click of keyboards from three floors away on launch week."
- "The office smelled like burnt coffee and ambition."
- "It was the kind of silence that fills a room after someone says something true."
- "The inbox was full. The desk was clean. Both were lies."
- "Rain ran down the glass walls of the conference room while inside, the projector glowed blue."
- "The sound of the notification was small. The news it carried was not."
- "Sticky notes covered every surface. Each one held someone's best idea."
- "The first thing you notice is the noise. Then the energy. Then the focus."
- "The room was too hot, the chairs were too hard, and the idea was too good to ignore."

### Dialogue Openings

> Use to create immediacy and character. A line of dialogue drops the audience into a human interaction. Best for: case studies, origin stories, profile pieces, podcast show notes.

- "'That's impossible,' she said. Six months later, she proved herself wrong."
- "'We're shutting it down.' Those were the words. What happened next is the real story."
- "'Why should anyone care?' It was the hardest question anyone had asked us. And the most important."
- "'You're not ready.' He was right. We launched anyway."
- "'Show me the data.' Three words that changed how we tell our story."
- "'I don't believe you.' The investor leaned back in his chair. So I showed him."
- "'What makes you different?' I'd rehearsed the answer a hundred times. This time, I told the truth."
- "'This will never work.' Every founder hears it. Few talk about how it feels."
- "'Run the numbers again.' We did. They were even better the second time."
- "'If you had one sentence to describe what you do, what would it be?' I froze."

### Statistic / Data Openings

> Use when a number is genuinely surprising enough to stop the audience. The data must be counterintuitive or emotionally resonant. Best for: reports, whitepapers, data-driven blog posts, LinkedIn.

- "73% of companies have a brand story. 4% can actually tell it."
- "The average person sees 10,000 messages a day. They remember three."
- "We spent $2.3 million on marketing last year. One $0 blog post outperformed all of it."
- "It takes 7 seconds to make a first impression. This is what we do with ours."
- "90% of startups fail. But 90% of startup advice is written by people who've never built one."
- "Our customers spend an average of 11 minutes on our landing page. Industry average is 54 seconds."
- "In 2024, [number] of [thing] happened. In 2025, the number doubled."
- "The ROI wasn't 2x. It wasn't 5x. It was 47x. And here's the story behind it."

## Usage Guidelines

- **The opening line is a promise.** Whatever it implies, the content must deliver. An in medias res opening promises a story. A bold statement promises proof. Don't break the promise.
- **Match opening type to content type.** Data openings for reports. Dialogue openings for case studies. Sensory openings for brand narratives. Mismatched openings create cognitive dissonance.
- **Read it aloud.** If the opening line doesn't sound natural when spoken, rewrite it.
- **Cut everything before the real start.** Most first drafts bury the opening line in paragraph two or three. Find it. Move it to line one. Delete everything before it.
- **Test openings.** When possible, test two different opening types for the same piece. The data will tell you which one your audience prefers.
