# Dialogue Tags

## Purpose

Dialogue brings stories to life by putting real words in real mouths. But how you attribute and format that dialogue affects pacing, tone, and characterization. This file provides alternatives to the overused "said," guidance on dialogue formatting for written marketing stories, and techniques for making quotes feel alive rather than reported. Designed for case studies, brand stories, testimonial integration, and narrative marketing content.

## Collection

### Neutral Alternatives to "Said"

> Use for standard attribution where you want the dialogue to carry the weight, not the tag. These are invisible to the reader and don't draw attention to themselves.

- stated
- replied
- answered
- responded
- noted
- mentioned
- added
- continued
- explained
- observed
- remarked
- recalled
- acknowledged
- confirmed

### Action Tags (Replacing "Said" Entirely)

> Use to eliminate the dialogue tag and replace it with a character action. This is the most cinematic technique and the one used by the best narrative nonfiction writers.

- "She set down her coffee. 'The numbers don't lie.'"
- "He leaned forward, hands on the table. 'We're not doing this halfway.'"
- "She pulled up the dashboard. 'Look at this. Just look.'"
- "He closed his laptop. 'I've heard enough. Let's move.'"
- "She turned to the team. 'We launch Thursday. No more delays.'"
- "He paused, looked out the window. 'I almost didn't come back.'"
- "She tapped the whiteboard. 'This. This is the insight.'"
- "He checked his phone, then put it face-down. 'You have my full attention.'"
- "She stood up and walked to the door. 'Meeting's over. We have work to do.'"
- "He took a breath. 'Okay. Here's what really happened.'"

### Dialogue Formatting Patterns

> These are structural patterns for how to present dialogue in marketing content.

**Direct Quote (Standard):**
- "When I asked our CEO why she chose this path, she said, 'Because nobody else would.'"
- Use for exact, verified quotes. Highest credibility.

**Paraphrased Dialogue:**
- "She told me, in no uncertain terms, that the old approach was dead."
- Use when the exact wording isn't available or isn't impactful enough.

**Embedded Quote (Mid-Sentence):**
- "The phrase she kept using was 'relentless simplicity,' and it defined everything they built."
- Use to highlight a specific phrase or term within a narrative sentence.

**Dialogue Sequence (Back-and-Forth):**
- "'Can we afford this?' I asked. / 'Can we afford not to?' she replied."
- Use sparingly in marketing. Two exchanges maximum before returning to narrative.

**Reported Speech (Indirect):**
- "He explained that the market had shifted, that the old playbook was irrelevant, and that we had maybe six months to adapt."
- Use when the summary is more powerful than the exact words.

### Dialogue Tags for Testimonials

> Use when integrating customer quotes into narrative content. These make testimonials feel like story beats rather than appended reviews.

- "As [Name] put it..."
- "[Name] said something that stuck with me..."
- "In [Name]'s words..."
- "The way [Name] describes it..."
- "[Name] didn't mince words..."
- "When we asked [Name] about the results, the answer was immediate..."
- "[Name] paused before answering. Then..."
- "The phrase [Name] kept coming back to was..."
- "[Name] laughed when we asked about the old way of doing things."
- "'I wish I'd found this sooner.' That's [Name], and that's the reaction we hear most."

### Internal Dialogue

> Use to show what someone was thinking but not saying. Internal dialogue adds psychological depth to business storytelling.

- "I kept thinking: this can't be right."
- "The voice in my head said run. I stayed."
- "Somewhere in the back of my mind, I already knew the answer."
- "I told myself it was fine. It wasn't fine."
- "My first thought was: we're in trouble. My second thought was: good."
- "I was smiling on the outside. Inside, I was doing math."

## Usage Guidelines

- **"Said" is not the enemy.** It's invisible, and invisible is often what you want. Only replace "said" when the alternative adds genuine value.
- **Action tags > emotion tags.** "She slammed the report on the desk" tells you more about tone than "she said angrily." Show, don't tell.
- **Limit dialogue in marketing content.** One or two strong quotes per piece. More than that and you're writing a screenplay, not a marketing story.
- **Verify all direct quotes.** If you put words in quotation marks, those must be the actual words spoken. Paraphrase or use indirect speech if you're approximating.
- **Match tag energy to content energy.** A reflective story uses "mused" and "recalled." A high-energy launch story uses "declared" and "fired back."
- **Avoid adverb-heavy tags.** "He said quietly" is weaker than "He whispered." "She said angrily" is weaker than "She snapped." The verb should do the work.
