# Hook Phrases

## Purpose

Opening hooks grab attention in the first 3-7 seconds. These phrases are designed to stop the scroll, interrupt autopilot thinking, and pull the reader or listener into a story. Use them at the top of emails, presentations, ads, social posts, and video scripts.

## Collection

### Curiosity Hooks

> Use when you want the audience to lean in and wonder "what happens next?"

- "Nobody was supposed to find out about this."
- "I deleted the email three times before I finally hit send."
- "There's a reason nobody talks about this part."
- "The thing they don't tell you about [X] is..."
- "I wasn't going to share this, but..."
- "What I'm about to show you took me [time] to figure out."
- "Everyone in the room knew something was wrong. Nobody said a word."
- "I almost didn't [do X]. Here's why I'm glad I did."
- "The answer was hiding in plain sight the whole time."
- "There's a secret most [industry] insiders already know."

### Pattern Interrupt Hooks

> Use when the audience expects one thing and you deliver something unexpected.

- "Forget everything you've been told about [X]."
- "Stop. Before you read another word, answer this."
- "This is not another [common content type] about [topic]."
- "What if the opposite were true?"
- "The worst advice I ever received made me the most money."
- "I'm about to contradict every expert in this space."
- "You've been lied to about [X]. Here's the proof."
- "Everything changed the day I stopped trying to [common action]."
- "What if the problem isn't [obvious thing]? What if it's [unexpected thing]?"
- "The most dangerous phrase in business is 'we've always done it this way.'"

### Provocative Question Hooks

> Use to force the audience into active thinking. Questions create open loops the brain wants to close.

- "What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail?"
- "When was the last time you felt truly proud of your work?"
- "What if your biggest competitor already knows what I'm about to tell you?"
- "How much money did you leave on the table last quarter?"
- "What would your business look like with 10x the leads and half the effort?"
- "If your strategy is so good, why aren't you sleeping well at night?"
- "What's the one thing you'd change if you could start over tomorrow?"
- "Have you ever wondered why some brands grow effortlessly while you grind?"

### Bold Claim Hooks

> Use when you have the evidence to back it up. Bold claims without proof destroy credibility; with proof, they build authority.

- "We grew from $0 to $1M in [timeframe] with one strategy."
- "This single change doubled our conversion rate overnight."
- "I've tested 147 [things] and only 3 actually work."
- "In the next 5 minutes, I'll show you the most underused tactic in [industry]."
- "This framework has generated [specific result] for [number] companies."
- "Most [professionals] will never figure this out on their own."
- "By the time you finish reading this, you'll never see [X] the same way."
- "There are only two types of [businesses/people]: those who [do X] and those who wish they had."

### Scene-Drop Hooks

> Use to place the audience inside a moment. Concrete, sensory details make the opening cinematic.

- "It was 2 a.m. and my phone wouldn't stop buzzing."
- "The conference room went silent. Twelve people stared at the screen."
- "I was standing in a parking lot, staring at my phone, when the email came through."
- "The pitch deck was open. The investors were smiling. And I was about to blow it."
- "Rain hammered the office windows. Inside, the dashboard told a different story."
- "She looked across the table and said five words that changed everything."
- "I pulled into the driveway, turned off the engine, and sat there. I couldn't go inside yet."
- "The Slack notification popped up at 11:47 p.m.: 'We need to talk.'"

### Confession Hooks

> Use to immediately create intimacy and trust. Confessions lower the audience's guard because they signal honesty.

- "I have a confession: I almost gave up on this entire project."
- "Here's something I've never admitted publicly."
- "I used to believe [common belief]. I was completely wrong."
- "For years, I told everyone [X]. The truth was the opposite."
- "I'm going to tell you something that might cost me credibility. But it needs to be said."
- "I was part of the problem. Here's how I became part of the solution."
- "Let me tell you about my most expensive mistake."
- "I've been keeping this to myself for [time]. Not anymore."

## Usage Guidelines

- **Match the hook to the medium.** Scene-drops work best in long-form (email, blog, presentation). Pattern interrupts work well in short-form (social, ads). Curiosity hooks are universal.
- **Earn the hook.** If you open with a bold claim, the body must deliver on it. Clickbait erodes trust.
- **Test in pairs.** A/B test two hook types for the same piece of content to learn what your audience responds to.
- **Keep hooks under 15 words when possible.** Shorter hooks have higher completion rates.
- **Avoid stacking hooks.** One strong hook is better than three mediocre ones.
- **Read the hook aloud.** If it sounds unnatural when spoken, rewrite it. The best hooks work in both written and spoken contexts.
- **Context matters.** A hook that works on LinkedIn may not work in an investor email. Always consider the platform and the relationship.
