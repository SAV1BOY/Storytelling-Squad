# Story Spine Templates

## Purpose
Ready-to-use Story Spine templates based on Kenn Adams' original structure, adapted
for business, brand, and marketing storytelling. Fill in the blanks and build.

---

## The Original Story Spine (Kenn Adams)
```
Once upon a time, ______________________.
Every day, ______________________.
But one day, ______________________.
Because of that, ______________________.
Because of that, ______________________.
Because of that, ______________________.
Until finally, ______________________.
And ever since that day, ______________________.
The moral of the story is ______________________.
```

---

## Template 1: The Customer Transformation
For case studies, testimonials, and product stories.

```
Once upon a time, there was a [role] named [Name] at [company type].
Every day, [they struggled with / dealt with / tolerated] ________________________.
But one day, [inciting incident — what changed? what broke? what was discovered?].
Because of that, [first action they took].
Because of that, [the result of that action / unexpected consequence].
Because of that, [the deeper transformation — not just metric, but identity/feeling].
Until finally, [the new normal — quantifiable outcome + emotional resolution].
And ever since that day, [how their world is different].
The moral: [One-sentence insight the audience can apply to their own situation].
```

**Example (filled):**
> Once upon a time, there was a VP of Marketing named Priya at a mid-size SaaS company.
> Every day, she spent three hours pulling data from five dashboards to build a report nobody read.
> But one day, her CEO said, "If you can't show me the story behind the numbers by Friday, I'm cutting the budget."
> Because of that, Priya tried something different: she replaced the 40-page deck with a one-page narrative.
> Because of that, the CEO read the whole thing — for the first time in two years — and asked a question she'd been waiting to hear: "What do you need?"
> Because of that, Priya got the budget, the team, and the mandate to rebuild the reporting system.
> Until finally, the monthly report was a two-page story that took 30 minutes to create and drove real decisions.
> And ever since that day, Priya's team has been the model for data storytelling across the company.
> The moral: The data was never the problem. The format was.

## Template 2: The Founder Origin Story
For brand pages, pitch decks, and about sections.

```
Once upon a time, [founder name] was a [role/situation].
Every day, [they experienced / witnessed / struggled with] ________________________.
But one day, [the moment that changed everything — specific, vivid, real].
Because of that, [the first impulsive or deliberate action].
Because of that, [the early obstacle or failure].
Because of that, [the pivot, learning, or breakthrough].
Until finally, [the company / product / movement] was born.
And ever since that day, [what the company does / stands for / fights for].
```

## Template 3: The Industry Disruption
For thought leadership and keynotes.

```
For years, the [industry] believed ________________________.
Every company, every leader, every playbook said ________________________.
But one day, [a person / team / company] asked a different question: ________________________?
Because of that, [they discovered / built / proved] ________________________.
Because of that, [the old assumption collapsed / the market shifted].
Because of that, [a new standard / possibility / category emerged].
Until finally, [the new reality was undeniable].
And the [industry] was never the same.
```

## Template 4: The "Story of Us" (Movement / Nonprofit)
For fundraising, community building, and advocacy.

```
Once upon a time, each of us faced ________________________ alone.
Every day, we [tolerated / worked around / suffered through] ________________________.
But one day, we found each other — in [a room / a forum / a moment].
Because of that, we realized ________________________.
Because of that, we decided to ________________________.
Because of that, [what we built / started / changed].
Until finally, [the outcome — concrete and measurable].
And ever since that day, [how the community has grown / what we now stand for].
The story isn't over. The next chapter is ________________________.
```

## Template 5: The Product Story
For landing pages, product launches, and demos.

```
Before [Product], [target user] had to ________________________.
Every day, that meant ________________________.
Then [Product] arrived — and [one-sentence value proposition].
Now, instead of [old painful process], [user] simply [new easy process].
The result: [specific metric or emotional outcome].
```

## Template 6: The Micro-Story (Social Media / Email)
Compressed to 5 beats for short-form content.

```
[Character] had a problem: ________________________.
They tried ________________________. It didn't work.
Then they discovered ________________________.
Result: ________________________.
Lesson: ________________________.
```

## Template 7: The Pitch Spine
For investor and sales pitches.

```

Current solutions [fail because / miss / ignore] ________________________.
We built [Product] to ________________________.
It works because ________________________.
In [timeframe], [N] customers have [outcome].
We're raising $[X] to ________________________.
Join us and ________________________.
```

## Template 8: The "What If" Spine
For visionary content and brand manifestos.

```
What if [bold reimagining of the status quo]?
What if instead of [current reality], we could [desired reality]?
What if [specific example of the vision in action]?
That's not a dream. It's a decision.
Here's how we start: ________________________.
```

## How to Use These Templates

1. **Start ugly.** Fill in every blank with the first thing that comes to mind. Don't edit yet.
2. **Find the turn.** The "But one day" line is the most important. Make it specific and vivid.
3. **Test the chain.** Each "Because of that" must causally follow the previous beat. If it doesn't, the story breaks.
4. **End with insight.** The moral / lesson / takeaway should be surprising or reframing — not obvious.
5. **Expand selectively.** Once the spine is solid, expand the 1–2 beats that carry the most emotional weight.
6. **Cut the template language.** In the final version, "Once upon a time" becomes a real opening. The template is scaffolding, not the building.
