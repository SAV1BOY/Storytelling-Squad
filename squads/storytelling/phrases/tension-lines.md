# Tension Lines

## Purpose
50+ phrases that create, maintain, or escalate tension in storytelling. Tension is
what keeps the reader moving forward — the gap between what is and what could be,
between the question and the answer.

---

## Foreshadowing Tension
Signal that something is coming without revealing what.

1. "They didn't know it yet, but that was the last normal day."
2. "Looking back, the signs were everywhere."
3. "If she had checked her email that morning, everything would have been different."
4. "That decision would cost them more than they could imagine."
5. "The quiet wouldn't last."
6. "In three weeks, none of this would matter."
7. "What happened next, nobody saw coming."
8. "He should have asked one more question. He didn't."

## Ticking Clock Tension
Compress time to create urgency.

9. "They had 72 hours."
10. "The deadline was Friday. It was Wednesday evening."
11. "Every hour they waited cost $4,000."
12. "The investor said, 'You have until end of day. Then I'm out.'"
13. "The launch was in 48 hours, and the product wasn't ready."
14. "She had one shot. One meeting. Seventeen minutes."
15. "The contract expired at midnight. It was 11:15 p.m."
16. "Each day without a fix meant another hundred customers walking away."

## Stakes Elevation Tension
Raise what's at risk.

17. "This wasn't just about revenue anymore. It was about survival."
18. "If this failed, thirty-four people would lose their jobs."
19. "Everything — the funding, the team, the three years of work — hung on this one presentation."
20. "It wasn't a business problem. It was a promise she'd made to her daughter."
21. "Failure didn't mean a setback. It meant the end."
22. "The margin for error was zero."
23. "This was the last round of funding they could raise."

## Obstacle / Complication Tension
Introduce a new problem when one seemed solved.

24. "And then the biggest client pulled out."
25. "The fix created three new problems."
26. "Just when the numbers started moving, the algorithm changed."
27. "The prototype worked perfectly — in the lab. In the real world, it broke."
28. "They solved the technical problem. The human problem was harder."
29. "The good news: they got the meeting. The bad news: it was tomorrow."
30. "One email unraveled six months of progress."
31. "The competition launched first."

## Internal Conflict Tension
Tension within a character.

32. "She knew the right thing to do. She also knew she couldn't afford to do it."
33. "Part of him wanted to walk away. The other part had already started building."
34. "He believed in the product. He no longer believed in the strategy."
35. "She had the data to make the case. She didn't have the courage."
36. "Every instinct said yes. Every number said no."
37. "He could keep the client or keep his integrity. Not both."

## Information Gap Tension
The reader knows something the character doesn't (or vice versa).

38. "What the team didn't know was that the board had already made its decision."
39. "She was celebrating the deal. The cancellation email was already in her inbox."
40. "He thought the meeting went well. He was the only one."
41. "The answer was in the data from day one. Nobody thought to look."
42. "She had the solution. She just didn't recognize it yet."

## Silence and Absence Tension
What's NOT said or done creates pressure.

43. "He sent the proposal. Three days of silence."
44. "Nobody in the room would say what everyone was thinking."
45. "She asked the question. He looked at his hands."
46. "The phone didn't ring."
47. "For two weeks, the dashboard showed nothing. Flat line."
48. "The seat at the table was empty. Nobody mentioned it."

## Approaching Decision Tension
The moment of choice is near but hasn't arrived.

49. "The decision was coming. And once made, there was no going back."
50. "She had all the information she needed. Now she had to choose."
51. "Two paths. One led forward. One led back to safety."
52. "The cursor hovered over 'send.' He paused."
53. "They could play it safe or bet everything. The clock was ticking."
54. "She picked up the phone, put it down, picked it up again."
55. "Tomorrow, one of them would be wrong. Today, they were both still right."

---

## Usage Notes
- **Tension must resolve.** Every tension line creates a promise. Breaking that promise (by never resolving) destroys trust.
- **Vary the source of tension.** External obstacles, internal conflict, time pressure, and information gaps create different feelings.
- **Don't stack too much tension.** More than two unresolved tension lines at once creates anxiety, not engagement.
- **The strongest tension lines are specific.** "The deadline was Friday" beats "time was running out."
