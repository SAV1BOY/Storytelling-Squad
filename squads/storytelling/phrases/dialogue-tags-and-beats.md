# Dialogue Tags and Action Beats

## Purpose
Alternatives to "said" and action beats that replace attribution entirely. Use
sparingly — "said" remains the workhorse. These are for moments that need more.

## Action Beats: The "Said" Replacement

Action beats show what the character is doing while speaking, eliminating the
need for a tag entirely. They are the superior tool for most storytelling.

### Hands and Body
1. She set down her coffee mug. "We need to talk about the numbers."
2. He rubbed the back of his neck. "I don't know if I can do that."
3. She crossed her arms. "Try again."
4. He leaned forward, both palms flat on the table. "Listen to me."
5. She pushed her glasses up. "The data doesn't support that."
6. He drummed his fingers on the desk. "How long do we have?"
7. She uncapped a marker and turned to the whiteboard. "Here's what I'm thinking."
8. He loosened his tie. "That bad?"
9. She pointed at the screen. "There. That line. That's where we lost them."
10. He folded the printout in half, then in half again. "I've seen enough."

### Eyes and Face
11. She didn't look up from her laptop. "Go on."
12. He met her eyes. "I'm serious."
13. She raised an eyebrow. "And?"
14. His jaw tightened. "That's not what we agreed."
15. She smiled, but it didn't reach her eyes. "Of course."
16. He stared at the ceiling. "When did this start?"
17. She blinked. "Say that again."
18. He looked out the window. "It's not about the money."

### Movement and Position
19. She stood. "We're done here."
20. He paced the length of the room. "What if we're wrong?"
21. She turned her chair to face him. "Tell me everything."
22. He stopped mid-stride. "Wait. What did you just say?"
23. She leaned back in her chair. "Take your time."
24. He sat down slowly. "Start from the beginning."

### Objects and Environment
25. She closed the laptop. "This conversation is more important."
26. He picked up the prototype. "This is what three years looks like."
27. She slid the contract across the table. "Your move."
28. He put the phone on speaker. "Everyone needs to hear this."
29. She tore the sticky note off the wall. "This is the one."
30. He stacked the papers neatly and pushed them aside. "None of that matters now."

### Silence and Pause
31. She was quiet for a long time. "I don't have an answer."
32. He took a breath. "Okay."
33. She opened her mouth, closed it, then tried again. "I'm sorry."
34. A pause. "You're sure?"
35. He held up a hand. Waited. "Now I'm ready."

## Rhythm Patterns for Dialogue Scenes

### Short-Short-Long
> "No."
> "Why?"
> "Because the last time we rushed a launch, we spent six months apologizing to customers who never came back."

### Beat-Dialogue-Beat
> She set down her pen. "The problem isn't the product." She paused. "The problem is the story we're telling about it."

### Rapid Exchange (No Tags)
> "When?"
> "Tomorrow."
> "How?"
> "I have no idea."
> "That's reassuring."
> "I know."

### Dialogue-Narration-Dialogue
> "We lost the account," he said.
> The room went still. Fourteen people, and not one of them moved.
> "But I know how to get it back."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Adverb-laden tags.** "She said angrily." Let the words and beats convey the anger. If you need an adverb, the dialogue itself is weak.
2. **Exotic tags as style.** "He opined," "she extrapolated," "he pontificated." These call attention to the writer, not the character.
3. **Too many beats.** Not every line needs an action. Sometimes "said" is enough. Sometimes no tag at all is enough.
4. **Beats that contradict tone.** "She smiled. 'You're fired.'" — this works for dark humor but not for sincere moments.
5. **Consistent tag placement.** Vary where the tag falls: beginning ("She said, 'Fine.'"), middle ("'Fine,' she said, 'but don't expect me to like it.'"), and end ("'Fine.' She said it the way people say fine when nothing is fine.").

---

## Usage Notes
- In business storytelling, use dialogue sparingly — 2-3 exchanges per piece at most.
- "Said" should make up 80% of your tags. Alternatives are seasoning.
- Action beats are almost always better than tags for conveying emotion.
- Read the dialogue aloud. If it doesn't sound like speech, rewrite it.
