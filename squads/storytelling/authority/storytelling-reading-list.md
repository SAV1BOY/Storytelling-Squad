# Storytelling Reading List

## Curated Resources for Storytelling Practitioners

This reading list is organized by experience level and focus area. It is designed to support continuous development for every member of the Storytelling Squad, from new hires to senior strategists.

---

## How to Use This List

- **New team members**: Start with the Foundation tier. Complete it within your first 90 days.
- **Experienced practitioners**: Use the Intermediate and Advanced tiers to deepen specific skills.
- **Specialists**: Focus on the area-specific sections most relevant to your role.
- **Everyone**: Read at least one book per quarter from this list.

---

## Foundation Tier (Start Here)

These works establish the core concepts every storytelling practitioner must understand.

### Narrative Structure and Craft

1. **"Story" by Robert McKee**
   The definitive guide to narrative structure. Written for screenwriters but essential for anyone who constructs narratives. Focus on the principles of story design, the gap between expectation and result, and the role of conflict.

2. **"Building a StoryBrand" by Donald Miller**
   The most practical framework for applying storytelling to brand marketing. Introduces the SB7 framework based on the hero's journey. Required reading for all brand narrative work.

3. **"Storyworthy" by Matthew Dicks**
   A masterclass in finding and crafting stories from everyday experience. Essential for developing the storytelling instinct: knowing which moments are stories and which are anecdotes.

4. **"The Storytelling Animal" by Jonathan Gottschall**
   The science behind why humans are wired for story. Covers evolutionary psychology, neuroscience, and the cognitive function of narrative. Provides the intellectual foundation for why storytelling works.

5. **"Made to Stick" by Chip Heath and Dan Heath**
   Why some ideas survive and others die. The SUCCESs framework (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories) is directly applicable to marketing storytelling.

### Writing Craft

6. **"On Writing Well" by William Zinsser**
   The essential guide to clear, effective nonfiction writing. Every storyteller needs strong sentence-level craft, and Zinsser is the best teacher.

7. **"Bird by Bird" by Anne Lamott**
   On the process of writing: dealing with perfectionism, finding your voice, and showing up to do the work. As relevant to marketing storytelling as to literary writing.

## Intermediate Tier

These works deepen specific storytelling competencies.

### Persuasion and Influence

8. **"Influence" by Robert Cialdini**
   The six principles of persuasion, grounded in decades of research. Understanding these principles is essential for ethical storytelling that moves people to action.

9. **"Thinking, Fast and Slow" by Daniel Kahneman**
   How the human mind actually makes decisions. System 1 (fast, emotional, narrative-driven) and System 2 (slow, rational, analytical) thinking explains why stories are more persuasive than arguments.

10. **"The Righteous Mind" by Jonathan Haidt**
    How moral intuition shapes belief and behavior. Essential reading for storytellers working on values-driven brands, social impact, or politically sensitive topics.

### Brand and Marketing Narrative

11. **"Whoever Tells the Best Story Wins" by Annette Simmons**
    Practical techniques for business storytelling across contexts: sales, leadership, negotiation, and culture. Includes specific story types and when to deploy each.

12. **"All Marketers Are Liars" by Seth Godin**
    Reframed as "All Marketers Tell Stories" in later editions. On the power of worldview-consistent narratives and why authenticity matters more than perfection.

13. **"Contagious" by Jonah Berger**
    The science of why things go viral. The STEPPS framework helps storytellers design narratives with built-in sharing mechanics.

14. **"Positioning" by Al Ries and Jack Trout**
    The classic on competitive positioning. Understanding positioning is essential for crafting brand narratives that occupy a distinct space in the audience's mind.

### Customer and Human Stories

15. **"The Mom Test" by Rob Fitzpatrick**
    How to talk to customers and learn the truth. While written for product research, the interviewing principles are directly applicable to customer story gathering.

16. **"Interviewing Users" by Steve Portigal**
    A practical guide to conducting interviews that surface deep insights and authentic stories. Essential for anyone involved in customer story collection.

17. **"Working" by Studs Terkel**
    A classic of oral history. Demonstrates how to let subjects speak in their own voice while crafting compelling narrative. A masterclass in story gathering.

## Advanced Tier

These works push storytelling practice to the highest level.

### Narrative Strategy and Theory

18. **"Narrative Economics" by Robert Shiller**
    How stories spread and drive major economic events. Provides a macroeconomic framework for understanding narrative power. Essential for strategic-level storytelling.

19. **"The Hero with a Thousand Faces" by Joseph Campbell**
    The foundational work on the hero's journey and universal narrative archetypes. Dense but invaluable for understanding the deep structures of human storytelling.

20. **"Sapiens" by Yuval Noah Harari**
    Not a storytelling book per se, but its central thesis, that shared stories (myths, brands, nations) are what enable human cooperation at scale, is the ultimate case for narrative.

21. **"Wired for Story" by Lisa Cron**
    The neuroscience of storytelling. How the brain processes narrative and why certain story elements trigger specific cognitive and emotional responses.

### Advanced Craft

22. **"Save the Cat" by Blake Snyder**
    Screenplay structure distilled to its most practical form. The 15 "beats" framework is surprisingly applicable to long-form brand content and campaign narrative arcs.

23. **"The Art of Dramatic Writing" by Lajos Egri**
    Deep exploration of character, conflict, and premise in dramatic writing. Elevates storytelling beyond formula into genuine craft.

24. **"Craft in the Real World" by Matthew Salesses**
    Challenges Western narrative conventions and explores how cultural context shapes storytelling expectations. Essential reading for global and multicultural storytelling work.

### Data and Measurement

25. **"Storytelling with Data" by Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic**
    How to turn data into narrative. Essential for data storytelling, presentation design, and making the case for storytelling ROI.

26. **"Resonate" by Nancy Duarte**
    Narrative structure applied to presentations and keynotes. The framework for analyzing great speeches and applying their structure to business communication.

## Specialist Focus Areas

### For Video Storytellers

27. **"In the Blink of an Eye" by Walter Murch** - Film editing as storytelling
28. **"Rebel Without a Crew" by Robert Rodriguez** - Resourceful filmmaking
29. **"The Filmmaker's Handbook" by Steven Ascher and Edward Pincus** - Technical production

### For Audio and Podcast Storytellers

30. **"Out on the Wire" by Jessica Abel** - Behind the scenes at narrative audio shows
31. **"Reality Radio" edited by John Biewen and Alexa Dilworth** - The art of documentary audio

### For Data Storytellers

32. **"The Functional Art" by Alberto Cairo** - Information visualization as narrative
33. **"Data Story" by Nancy Duarte** - Connecting data to narrative structure

### For Leaders Using Storytelling

34. **"Leading with Story" by Paul Smith** - Storytelling for organizational leaders
35. **"The Leader's Guide to Storytelling" by Stephen Denning** - Narrative as a leadership tool

### For Ethical Storytelling

36. **"Weapons of Math Destruction" by Cathy O'Neil** - Ethics of algorithmic narrative
37. **"So You Want to Talk About Race" by Ijeoma Oluo** - Navigating identity in narrative

## Reading Group Guidelines

The Storytelling Squad runs a monthly reading group. Guidelines:

1. One book per month, selected from this list or nominated by a team member
2. Each session is 60 minutes: 15 minutes recap, 30 minutes discussion, 15 minutes application
3. Discussion focus: How does this apply to our current projects?
4. Each participant brings one specific takeaway they will apply to their work
5. Notes are documented and shared with the full team
