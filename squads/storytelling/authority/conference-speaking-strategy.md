# Conference Speaking Strategy

## Purpose
Speaking at conferences positions you as an authority, generates leads, and creates content. This strategy covers how to identify opportunities, craft proposals, and deliver memorable talks.

---

## Target Event Types

### Tier 1: High-Profile Events
- TEDx talks (local and regional)
- Industry flagship conferences (Content Marketing World, SXSW, HubSpot INBOUND)
- Executive leadership summits
- **Goal:** 1 per year. These are credibility anchors.

### Tier 2: Industry Events
- Marketing conferences, SaaS conferences, startup events
- Professional association annual meetings
- Regional business events
- **Goal:** 2-3 per year. These build consistent visibility.

### Tier 3: Niche and Community Events
- Meetups, local business groups, university guest lectures
- Podcast live recordings, virtual summits
- Company-hosted events and webinars
- **Goal:** 4-6 per year. These build grassroots following.

---

## Talk Topics That Work

### The Signature Talk
One 20-30 minute talk that represents your core thesis. Polished to excellence.
- Title should be specific and intriguing: "The One Question That Unlocks Any Story"
- Structure: Hook, 3 key principles with stories, closing callback
- Refine continuously based on audience response

### The Workshop Talk
A 45-90 minute interactive session where attendees practice a framework.
- Higher engagement, deeper connection, stronger leads
- Requires more preparation and materials
- Best for Tier 2 and 3 events

### The Panel Contribution
Prepare 3-5 memorable insights for panel discussions.
- Prepare one contrarian take that will stand out
- Have one story ready that illustrates your unique perspective
- Panels are less work but less memorable — supplement with signature talks

---

## Proposal Writing

### The Winning Proposal Formula
1. **Title:** Specific, benefit-driven, slightly provocative (under 10 words)
2. **Abstract:** 150 words maximum. What the audience will learn and why it matters NOW.
3. **Outline:** 3-5 bullet points covering the key segments
4. **Speaker bio:** Written in third person, focused on storytelling expertise and results
5. **Social proof:** Previous speaking engagements, audience sizes, testimonials

### Common Proposal Mistakes
- Title is too generic: "The Power of Storytelling" (thousands of these exist)
- Abstract focuses on the speaker, not the audience benefit
- No social proof of speaking ability
- Too broad: "Everything You Need to Know About Story" (impossible in 20 minutes)

---

## Pre-Event Preparation
- Research the audience: Who attends? What do they care about? What have previous speakers covered?
- Customize the talk: Adjust examples and language for the specific audience
- Prepare 3 versions: 15-minute, 30-minute, and 45-minute versions of your core talk
- Test equipment: Arrive early, check mic, screen, and lighting
- Prepare a takeaway: One-page framework, QR code to a resource, or follow-up email signup

## Post-Event Amplification
- Record the talk (request video from organizers)
- Write a blog post summarizing key points
- Share clips and insights on social media
- Follow up with attendees who connected
- Collect testimonials from organizers and attendees
