# Book and Publishing Strategy

## Purpose
A book is the ultimate authority asset in storytelling. It transforms you from a practitioner into a recognized thought leader. This strategy covers the path from concept to published work.

---

## Publishing Path Options

### Option 1: Traditional Publishing
**Timeline:** 18-36 months from proposal to shelf
**Pros:** Credibility, distribution, advance payment, editorial support
**Cons:** Slow, less control, competitive acquisition process
**Best for:** Those with existing platforms and proven market demand

**Process:**
1. Write a book proposal (50-80 pages): concept, market analysis, chapter outline, sample chapter
2. Secure a literary agent (query letter + proposal)
3. Agent shops proposal to publishers
4. Contract negotiation
5. Write the manuscript (typically 50,000-70,000 words)
6. Editorial process (6-12 months)
7. Publication and marketing

### Option 2: Self-Publishing
**Timeline:** 6-12 months from concept to launch
**Pros:** Speed, full control, higher royalty per copy
**Cons:** No advance, requires self-marketing, perceived credibility gap (shrinking)
**Best for:** Those with existing audiences who can drive sales

### Option 3: The Hybrid Approach
**Timeline:** 3-6 months for a focused, shorter work
**Pros:** Fast to market, builds toward a traditional deal, tests concepts
**Cons:** Shorter format may limit depth

**Format:** Start with a "big idea" book (20,000-30,000 words) that stakes out your territory. Use it to build demand for the full-length work.

## Book Concept Development

### The Core Thesis
Every business book needs one clear, contrarian, defensible thesis:
- "Story is business infrastructure, not marketing decoration"
- "Every brand failure is a narrative failure"
- "Retellability is the most undervalued metric in marketing"

### The Structure
Most successful business books follow one of these structures:
1. **Framework book:** Present a methodology with case studies (Made to Stick, StoryBrand)
2. **Narrative book:** Tell a story that embeds principles (Shoe Dog, The Hard Thing About Hard Things)
3. **Manifesto book:** Argue a position with evidence and passion (Start with Why, Rework)

### The Proof
Before writing the book, you should have:
- 10+ case studies or examples to draw from
- Original research or data that supports your thesis
- A proven framework that has been tested with real clients
- An existing body of content that demonstrates your expertise

## Pre-Book Marketing

Build the platform before the book:
- Grow your newsletter to 1,000+ engaged subscribers
- Build a social media following around your core themes
- Publish 20+ pieces of content that test book concepts
- Speak at events to refine your ideas in front of live audiences
- Collect endorsement candidates: who would blurb your book?

---

## Writing Process

### Phase 1: Outline (2-4 weeks)
- Chapter-by-chapter outline with key arguments and examples
- Identify gaps in your case studies and research
- Test chapter concepts with your audience

### Phase 2: First Draft (8-16 weeks)
- Write 1,000-2,000 words per day, 4-5 days per week
- Do not edit while drafting — momentum matters more than polish
- Send chapters to beta readers as you complete them

### Phase 3: Revision (4-8 weeks)
- Structural edit: Does each chapter earn its place?
- Line edit: Is the prose clear, engaging, and free of jargon?
- Fact check: Are all claims sourced and accurate?

---

## Launch Strategy
- Build a launch team of 50-100 advocates who will buy and review on day one
- Create a launch content calendar: excerpts, behind-the-scenes, endorsement reveals
- Pursue podcast interviews around launch date (20+ booked in advance)
- Host a launch event (virtual or in-person) with a talk based on the book
