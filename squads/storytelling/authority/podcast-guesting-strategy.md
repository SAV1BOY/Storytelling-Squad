# Podcast Guesting Strategy

## Purpose
Podcast appearances are high-leverage authority builders. A single 45-minute conversation can reach thousands of ideal prospects, build trust through long-form dialogue, and create evergreen content. This strategy covers how to identify, pitch, prepare for, and maximize podcast guest appearances.

---

## Target Podcast Selection

### Tier 1: Industry Flagships (5,000+ downloads per episode)
- Marketing, business, and leadership podcasts with established audiences
- Goal: 1-2 appearances per year
- Approach: Warm introductions, proven track record required

### Tier 2: Niche Leaders (1,000-5,000 downloads per episode)
- Storytelling, content marketing, sales, and branding podcasts
- Goal: 3-4 appearances per year
- Approach: Direct pitch with strong hook

### Tier 3: Emerging Shows (Under 1,000 downloads)
- New but growing shows in your space
- Goal: 4-6 appearances per year (especially when building initial credibility)
- Approach: Direct pitch; hosts are actively seeking guests

## The Guest Pitch

### Pitch Template
Subject line: "[Specific topic] — Guest Pitch for [Podcast Name]"

Body (under 200 words):
1. Acknowledgment of a specific episode you listened to and what resonated
2. Your proposed topic — stated as a benefit to their audience
3. Your unique angle — what makes your perspective different
4. One credibility signal — a specific result, not a title
5. Sign-off with availability

### Topic Angles That Get Booked
- Contrarian take: "Why most brand stories actually hurt the brand"
- Specific methodology: "The 5-commandment scene structure for business storytelling"
- Timely insight: "How AI is changing storytelling — and what it cannot replace"
- Behind-the-scenes: "What we learned rebuilding 200 pitch narratives"
- Failure story: "The $2M deal I lost because of bad storytelling"

## Preparation Protocol

### Before the Interview
1. Listen to 3 recent episodes — understand the host's style, pace, and interests
2. Prepare 5 key talking points with stories attached to each
3. Prepare 2-3 memorable one-liners that the host might quote
4. Have proof points ready: specific numbers, named examples, client results
5. Prepare your CTA: one specific thing listeners should do after the episode

### During the Interview
- Open with energy — the first 60 seconds set the tone for the whole conversation
- Tell stories, not theories. Every point should be illustrated with a specific example.
- Use the host's name and reference their questions — shows listening
- Vary pace and energy — monotone kills podcast engagement
- End with a clear, generous CTA (free resource, not a sales pitch)

### After the Interview
- Send a thank-you message within 24 hours
- Share the episode across all channels when published (tag the host)
- Write a blog post or social content that extends one idea from the episode
- Add the episode to your speaking/media portfolio

---

## Maximizing Each Appearance

Every podcast episode should generate 5-10 additional content pieces:
1. Full episode share on social media
2. 3-5 audiogram clips (60-90 seconds each)
3. One LinkedIn post expanding on a key insight
4. One newsletter mention with link to the episode
5. Blog post that goes deeper on one topic discussed
6. Quote graphics from memorable moments
