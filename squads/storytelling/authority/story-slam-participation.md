# Story Slam Participation

## Purpose
Story slams (The Moth, local storytelling events, Toastmasters competitions) are performance storytelling events where speakers tell personal stories to live audiences. Participation builds stage presence, narrative instincts, and a reputation as someone who practices the craft they teach.

---

## Why Participate

### Skill Development
- Forces you to craft stories under strict time constraints (usually 5-7 minutes)
- Develops stage presence, timing, and emotional delivery
- Builds comfort with vulnerability — personal stories require emotional exposure
- Sharpens narrative instincts that improve all professional storytelling

### Credibility Building
- "I practice what I preach" — demonstrated through live performance
- Wins or notable performances become social proof
- Connects you with storytelling communities beyond business
- Creates content for social media and thought leadership

### Creative Fuel
- Personal stories feed professional work — patterns transfer
- Exposure to diverse storytelling styles expands your toolkit
- Live audience feedback is immediate and honest

---

## Major Story Slam Organizations

### The Moth
- Premier storytelling organization with events in 25+ cities
- StorySLAMs (open mic, lottery-selected) and MainStage (invitation only)
- Theme-based events: "Forgiveness," "Turning Points," "Risk"
- Apply through open calls; attend StorySLAMs to get on the radar

### Local Storytelling Events
- Most cities have local storytelling nights at bars, theaters, or community centers
- Lower stakes, great for practice
- Search for "[your city] storytelling event" or check Meetup

### Toastmasters
- Not a slam but develops presentation storytelling skills
- Table Topics (impromptu speaking) builds narrative agility
- Competitions provide structured performance goals

## Story Slam Story Structure

### The 5-Minute Story Arc
1. **Opening hook (15 seconds):** One vivid moment that drops the audience into the story
2. **Context (45 seconds):** Who you were, where you were, what you wanted
3. **Rising action (2 minutes):** What happened, escalating tension
4. **Climax (30 seconds):** The moment everything changed
5. **Resolution (1 minute):** What you learned, how you changed
6. **Closing line (15 seconds):** Callback to the opening or a thematic punch

### Rules for Personal Storytelling
- Be specific: dates, names, places, sensory details
- Be honest: embellishment is fine, lying is not
- Be vulnerable: the audience connects with imperfection
- Be brief: 5 minutes passes faster than you think
- No notes: story slams require memorized, natural delivery

---

## Preparation Method

1. Choose a theme-appropriate personal story
2. Write it out in full (1,000-1,200 words for 5 minutes)
3. Identify the 5 key beats you must hit
4. Practice telling (not reading) the story — 10 times minimum
5. Time every practice session — cut ruthlessly if over time
6. Practice in front of one trusted person for feedback
7. On the night: arrive early, watch other performers, breathe

---

## Leveraging Story Slam Experience

- Record your performances (with permission) for social media
- Write about the experience of performing — meta-storytelling content
- Reference slam experience in professional contexts: "I've told stories to audiences of 500+ at live events"
- Use slam-developed stories in professional contexts where appropriate
