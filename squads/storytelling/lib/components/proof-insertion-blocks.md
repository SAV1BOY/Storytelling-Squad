# Proof Insertion Blocks

## Purpose
Proof blocks are pre-formatted evidence units that can be inserted into any narrative to increase credibility. Each block is self-contained and designed to slot into a story without disrupting narrative flow. Stories persuade; proof converts.

---

## Block Type 1: The Stat Drop

### Format
> [Transition sentence connecting to narrative] + [Single powerful statistic] + [Source attribution] + [Implication sentence]

### Example
"After implementing the framework, the team didn't just improve — they transformed. Customer retention jumped from 62% to 91% in a single quarter (Internal Q3 Report). That's the difference between a leaking bucket and a growing business."

### Rules
- One statistic per block. Multiple stats dilute impact.
- Always attribute the source, even if internal.
- The implication sentence translates the number into meaning.
- Place stat drops after a claim, never before.

---

## Block Type 2: The Named Testimony

### Format
> [Character introduction — name, role, context] + [Direct quote] + [Outcome sentence]

### Example
"Maria Chen, VP of Marketing at Ridgeline Health, had tried three agencies before. 'Within two weeks, we had a story our sales team actually wanted to tell,' she said. Pipeline meetings went from dreaded to energized."

### Rules
- Real names with permission. "A client" is weak; "Maria Chen, VP of Marketing" is strong.
- Quote must be conversational, not polished. Imperfect quotes feel authentic.
- Keep to 1-2 sentences of direct quote. Longer quotes lose the reader.
- Follow the quote with a concrete outcome, not another quote.

---

## Block Type 3: The Before/After Contrast

### Format
> **Before:** [Specific painful state with metrics] → **After:** [Specific improved state with metrics] → **Timeline:** [How long the change took]

### Example
> **Before:** Sales team using 47 different pitch versions. Win rate: 12%. Average deal cycle: 94 days.
> **After:** Single narrative framework. Win rate: 28%. Average deal cycle: 51 days.
> **Timeline:** 6 weeks from kickoff to full deployment.

### Rules
- Both states must include numbers. Vague befores weaken the contrast.
- The timeline creates believability. Unrealistic timelines trigger skepticism.
- Place before/after blocks at narrative turning points.

---

## Block Type 4: The Authority Citation

### Format
> [Principle or claim] + [Authority name and credential] + [Their finding/statement] + [Application to your narrative]

### Example
"This isn't just our experience. Robert McKee, who's trained writers at Pixar, Disney, and Microsoft, argues that story is 'the most powerful way to put ideas into the world today.' Our framework operationalizes that principle for B2B sales teams."

### Rules
- Authority must be recognized by the target audience. A PhD impresses executives; a practitioner impresses operators.
- Don't over-quote authorities. One per narrative section maximum.
- Always bridge from the authority back to your specific context.

---

## Block Type 5: The Micro Case Study

### Format
> [One-sentence situation] + [One-sentence action] + [One-sentence result] + [One-sentence lesson]

### Example
"A Series B fintech startup was losing every enterprise deal to incumbents. They rebuilt their pitch around a single founder story — the moment their CEO watched his grandmother get denied a loan. Enterprise win rate went from 8% to 34%. The story didn't change the product; it changed the frame."

### Rules
- Four sentences maximum. This is a proof block, not a full case study.
- The lesson sentence must generalize — it should apply beyond this specific case.
- Micro case studies work best in clusters of 2-3 for cumulative proof.

---

## Block Type 6: The Process Proof

### Format
> [Name the process/methodology] + [Number of times/clients it's been applied] + [Range of results] + [Why it works sentence]

### Example
"The Narrative Audit process has been applied to 200+ companies across 14 industries. Results range from 20% to 340% improvement in content engagement. It works because it identifies the gap between the story a brand tells and the story its customers actually hear."

### Rules
- Ranges are more credible than single numbers. "20% to 340%" beats "200% average."
- Include the number of applications to signal pattern, not luck.
- The "why it works" sentence shows understanding, not just results.

---

## Insertion Guidelines

### Where to Place Proof Blocks
1. **After a bold claim** — proof immediately follows assertion
2. **At the narrative midpoint** — when the audience is deciding whether to stay
3. **Before the call to action** — final credibility boost before the ask
4. **Never in the opening** — proof before story kills narrative momentum

### Density Rules
- **Short-form (under 500 words):** 1-2 proof blocks maximum
- **Medium-form (500-2000 words):** 3-4 proof blocks
- **Long-form (2000+ words):** 5-7 proof blocks, evenly distributed
- **Pitch decks:** 1 proof block per major claim slide

### Transition Phrases Into Proof
- "Here's what that looks like in practice..."
- "The numbers tell the story..."
- "Don't take our word for it..."
- "This pattern holds across industries..."
- "One example makes this concrete..."
