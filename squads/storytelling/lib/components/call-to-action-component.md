# Narrative CTA Component

## Purpose
A call to action is the bridge between story and behavior. Most CTAs fail because they are transactional interruptions grafted onto emotional experiences. A narrative CTA emerges organically from the story — it feels like the only logical next step, not an ask. This component provides structures for CTAs that convert without breaking immersion.

---

## CTA Anatomy

### 1. The Emotional Runway
The 2-3 sentences before the CTA that prime the audience's emotional state. The CTA itself is just the landing — the runway does the real work.

### 2. The Action
The specific, concrete behavior you want the audience to take. Must be singular and unambiguous.

### 3. The Continuity Frame
Language that frames the action as a continuation of the story, not a departure from it.

---

## CTA Types

### Type 1: The Next Chapter CTA
**Premise:** The story you just told is Chapter 1. The CTA is the start of Chapter 2, and the audience is the protagonist.

**Template:**
> [Story conclusion] + "That's their story. Yours starts [action]." + [Specific instruction]

**Example:**
"Maria's team went from 12% to 28% win rate in six weeks. That's their story. Yours starts with a 30-minute narrative audit. Book your slot and we'll map the gaps in your current story."

**Best for:** Consultative sales, high-consideration purchases

### Type 2: The Cliffhanger CTA
**Premise:** The story is deliberately incomplete. The CTA resolves it.

**Template:**
> [Story that stops before resolution] + "Want to know how it ended?" + [Action that delivers resolution]

**Example:**
"The founder walked into the board meeting with a completely new pitch — no slides, no deck, just a story. What happened next changed the company's trajectory. Download the full case study to see the outcome."

**Best for:** Content marketing, lead generation, email sequences

### Type 3: The Mirror CTA
**Premise:** The audience recognizes themselves in the story. The CTA acknowledges that recognition.

**Template:**
> [Story conclusion] + "If this sounds familiar..." + [Action framed as self-recognition]

**Example:**
"Every time she opened the CRM, she felt the gap between the story she wanted to tell and the one the data showed. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone — and you don't have to stay there. Let's talk."

**Best for:** Problem-aware audiences, empathy-driven brands

### Type 4: The Proof Stack CTA
**Premise:** The story built proof throughout. The CTA leverages accumulated credibility.

**Template:**
> [Final proof beat] + "We've done this [N] times. The pattern holds." + [Action framed as joining a proven pattern]

**Example:**
"Ridgeline Health. Apex Fintech. Cornerstone Logistics. Three industries, one framework, consistent results. We've done this 200+ times. The pattern holds. See if it applies to your business."

**Best for:** Risk-averse audiences, enterprise sales

### Type 5: The Urgency Narrative CTA
**Premise:** The story established time-sensitive stakes. The CTA inherits that urgency.

**Template:**
> [Time-sensitive element from story] + "The window [is closing / opens now]." + [Time-bound action]

**Example:**
"Every quarter without a clear narrative is a quarter your competitors use to define you. Q2 planning starts in three weeks. The window for reshaping your story is now. Schedule your audit before March 30th."

**Best for:** Seasonal offers, limited availability, momentum-driven sales

## CTA Design Rules

### Rule 1: One CTA per narrative
Multiple CTAs create decision paralysis. Choose the single most important action.

### Rule 2: Match CTA intensity to story intensity
- Light story (social post) → Low-friction CTA (comment, share, follow)
- Medium story (blog, email) → Medium-friction CTA (download, sign up)
- Deep story (keynote, long-form) → High-friction CTA (book a call, buy)

### Rule 3: Use the character's language
If the story featured a character who said "I just needed someone to map it out for me," the CTA should echo: "Let us map it out for you."

### Rule 4: Remove the risk
Every CTA should answer the unspoken question: "What if this doesn't work for me?"
- "30-minute audit — no commitment, no pitch"
- "Full refund if you don't see results in 90 days"
- "Free pilot for qualified teams"

### Rule 5: The CTA must be visually distinct
In written form: whitespace, bold, or a button. In spoken form: a pause before and after. The CTA must not blend into the surrounding narrative.

---

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Jarring Ask:** Story about empathy, CTA about pricing — tonal mismatch
2. **The Vague CTA:** "Learn more" or "Get in touch" — no specificity, no pull
3. **The Premature CTA:** Asking before the story has built enough emotional investment
4. **The Guilt CTA:** "After everything we've shared..." — manipulative and transparent
5. **The Cluttered CTA:** Three buttons, two links, a phone number — choose one path
