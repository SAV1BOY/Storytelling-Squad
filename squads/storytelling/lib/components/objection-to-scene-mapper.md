# Objection-to-Scene Mapper

## Purpose
Every customer objection is a story waiting to be told. This mapper connects common objections to narrative scenes that dissolve resistance through story rather than argument. Arguing against an objection triggers defensiveness; a well-placed scene bypasses it entirely.

---

## Core Principle
An objection is not a logical problem — it is an emotional state wearing a rational mask. The scene must address the emotion underneath, not the words on top.

---

## Price Objections

### "It's too expensive."
**Underlying emotion:** Fear of waste, feeling foolish
**Scene type:** The Regret Scene
- Character bought the cheap alternative. Show the hidden costs accumulating.
- Climax: The moment they realized they'd spent 3x more fixing the cheap choice.
- Resolution: "The most expensive option is the one that doesn't work."

### "We don't have the budget right now."
**Underlying emotion:** Competing priorities, fear of internal pushback
**Scene type:** The Opportunity Cost Scene
- Character delayed the decision by one quarter. Show what they lost while waiting.
- Use specific numbers: leads lost, competitors gained, team morale dropped.

### "Your competitor is cheaper."
**Underlying emotion:** Uncertainty about differentiation
**Scene type:** The Apples-to-Oranges Scene
- Two characters make parallel choices. Follow both timelines.
- Reveal the divergence point where depth of solution created different outcomes.

---

## Trust Objections

### "We've been burned before."
**Underlying emotion:** Betrayal, vulnerability
**Scene type:** The Redemption Scene
- Acknowledge the scar directly: "One of our clients, Sarah, said the exact same thing."
- Show Sarah's guarded entry, the small early win that cracked skepticism, the moment trust rebuilt.
- Key beat: The protagonist must earn trust through action, not promises.

### "How do I know this will work for us?"
**Underlying emotion:** Fear of being the exception
**Scene type:** The Mirror Scene
- Present a character whose situation mirrors the prospect's context precisely.
- Detail the specific doubts that character had. Let the prospect see themselves.
- Climax: The results that character achieved despite identical doubts.

### "We need to do more research."
**Underlying emotion:** Decision paralysis, fear of commitment
**Scene type:** The Analysis Paralysis Scene
- Character researched for six months. Show the cost of indecision.
- Contrast with a character who committed early and iterated.

---

## Timing Objections

### "Now isn't the right time."
**Underlying emotion:** Overwhelm, fear of adding complexity
**Scene type:** The Window Scene
- Show a time-sensitive opportunity. A character who acted in the window vs. one who waited.
- The window closed. The cost of "later" became permanent.

### "We're in the middle of another initiative."
**Underlying emotion:** Bandwidth anxiety
**Scene type:** The Compound Effect Scene
- Character layered the solution alongside their existing initiative.
- Show how the two efforts amplified each other rather than competing.

---

## Authority Objections

### "I need to check with my team/boss."
**Underlying emotion:** Fear of advocating alone
**Scene type:** The Internal Champion Scene
- Show a character who brought the story to their leadership.
- Provide the exact narrative they used internally — give the prospect a script.
- Climax: The moment leadership said yes, and why.

### "We have an internal solution."
**Underlying emotion:** Pride, sunk cost attachment
**Scene type:** The Evolution Scene
- Honor the internal solution: "What your team built got you here."
- Show a character whose internal tool hit a ceiling. Not failure — growth beyond capacity.
- Frame the external solution as the next chapter, not a replacement.

---

## Mapping Process

1. **Collect objections** from sales calls, support tickets, lost-deal interviews
2. **Identify the emotion** beneath each objection (use the emotion taxonomy)
3. **Select scene type** from the catalog above or create a new one
4. **Source a real story** from your story bank that fits the scene template
5. **Craft the scene** using the scene component (5 commandments)
6. **Test with sales** — does the scene dissolve the objection in live conversations?
7. **Measure conversion** — track close rates on deals where the scene was deployed

## Scene Delivery Formats

| Format | Best For | Length |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Verbal anecdote | Sales call | 60-90 seconds |
| Email story | Follow-up | 150-200 words |
| Case study section | Proposal | 300-500 words |
| Video testimonial | Website/deck | 60-120 seconds |
| Slide narrative | Pitch deck | 1-2 slides |
