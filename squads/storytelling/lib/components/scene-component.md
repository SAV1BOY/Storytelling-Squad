# Scene Component — Five Commandments (Coyne)

## Purpose
A scene is the fundamental unit of story. Based on Robert McKee's scene principles and refined by Shawn Coyne in The Story Grid, every functional scene must contain five elements. If any element is missing, the scene is broken — it may read well but won't move the audience. This component provides the template for building scenes that work.

---

## The Five Commandments of Storytelling

### 1. Inciting Incident
The event that upsets the balance and forces the character to respond. Without it, nothing happens.

**Two types:**
- **Causal:** Caused by a character's action ("She sent the proposal to the wrong client")
- **Coincidental:** Random or external event ("The market crashed overnight")

**In business narrative:**
- A customer discovers a critical flaw in their process
- A founder receives unexpected news — good or bad
- A market shift makes the current strategy obsolete

**Test:** Remove the inciting incident. Does the character still need to act? If yes, it's not a true inciting incident.

### 2. Progressive Complication (Turning Point)
A series of obstacles that escalate the difficulty, culminating in a turning point that makes the old approach impossible.

**Two types:**
- **Action turning point:** An event changes the situation ("The lead investor pulled out")
- **Revelation turning point:** New information changes understanding ("She discovered the metrics were wrong the entire time")

**Escalation rules:**
- Each complication must be harder than the last
- Complications alternate between external and internal pressure
- The final turning point must back the character into a corner

**In business narrative:**
- First complication: The initial solution doesn't work
- Second complication: The backup plan has an unexpected cost
- Turning point: A piece of information reframes the entire problem

### 3. Crisis (Dilemma)
The character faces an impossible choice between two paths, both with significant consequences.

**Two types:**
- **Best bad choice:** Both options are negative; which is least damaging?
  - "Fire the underperforming co-founder or let the company suffer?"
- **Irreconcilable goods:** Both options are positive; you can only pick one
  - "Take the safe enterprise deal or bet on the risky but larger opportunity?"

**Rules:**
- The crisis must be a genuine dilemma, not an obvious choice
- Both options must have real, stated consequences
- The audience should be able to argue for either side

### 4. Climax
The character makes their choice and acts. The climax is the action taken at the moment of crisis. It reveals character — what someone does under pressure is who they are.

**Rules:**
- The climax is a decision expressed through action, not thought
- It must be irreversible — once done, the world has changed
- The choice must have been genuinely difficult (established by the crisis)

**In business narrative:**
- The founder pivots the entire product direction in a single meeting
- The marketing leader kills the campaign that took three months to build
- The customer commits to the new approach despite internal resistance

### 5. Resolution
The new state of the world after the climactic action. The balance has shifted — things are measurably different from the inciting incident.

**Rules:**
- Resolution must show change, not just state it
- Include at least one concrete detail that proves the shift
- The resolution of one scene can be the inciting incident of the next

**In business narrative:**
- The metrics after the pivot: new numbers that prove the choice worked (or didn't)
- The team's energy after the decision: observable behavioral change
- The customer's results: specific, measurable outcomes

## Scene Template

```
SCENE: [Title]
VALUE SHIFT: [What changes? e.g., Trust → Betrayal, Ignorance → Understanding]
POLARITY: [Positive to negative, or negative to positive]

1. INCITING INCIDENT:
   [Type: Causal/Coincidental]
   [What happens that upsets the balance]

2. PROGRESSIVE COMPLICATIONS:
   - Complication 1: [Escalation]
   - Complication 2: [Escalation]
   - Turning Point: [Action/Revelation that backs character into corner]

3. CRISIS:
   [Type: Best Bad Choice / Irreconcilable Goods]
   [Option A with consequences] vs. [Option B with consequences]

4. CLIMAX:
   [What the character chooses and does]

5. RESOLUTION:
   [New state of the world — concrete detail proving change]
```

## Scene Value Shifts

Every scene must shift at least one value. Common value shifts in business narrative:

| From | To | Scene Type |
|------|----|------------|
| Ignorance | Understanding | Discovery scene |
| Doubt | Conviction | Proof scene |
| Fear | Courage | Decision scene |
| Mistrust | Trust | Credibility scene |
| Confusion | Clarity | Framework scene |
| Complacency | Urgency | Stakes scene |
| Isolation | Connection | Community scene |

---

## Scene Sequencing

Scenes chain together to form sequences, and sequences form acts:
- Each scene's resolution sets up the next scene's inciting incident
- Alternate between positive and negative value shifts to create rhythm
- The final scene of a sequence should have the largest value shift
