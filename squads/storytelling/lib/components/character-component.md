# Character Component (Want / Need / Lie / Flaw)

## Purpose
Characters are the vehicles through which audiences experience story. A character without internal architecture is a mannequin — the audience watches but doesn't feel. This component defines the four pillars that make characters dimensional, relatable, and narratively functional.

---

## The Four Pillars

### 1. The Want (External Goal)
What the character is consciously pursuing. This is the visible, stated objective.

- Must be **concrete and measurable**: "Close a $5M deal," not "be successful"
- Must be **active**: The character pursues it; it doesn't just happen
- Must be **under threat**: Something stands in the way
- Drives the **plot** — every scene should relate to the pursuit of the want

**Business narrative examples:**
- Founder wants to raise a Series B before cash runs out
- Marketing leader wants to double qualified leads by Q3
- Customer wants to reduce churn below 5% annually

### 2. The Need (Internal Growth)
What the character actually requires to become whole — usually invisible to the character at the start.

- Often **contradicts the want**: The character pursues the wrong goal
- Represents the **theme** of the story
- Fulfilling the need is what makes the ending satisfying
- The need is discovered through conflict, not instruction

**Business narrative examples:**
- Founder needs to trust her team instead of controlling everything
- Marketing leader needs to listen to customers instead of optimizing metrics
- Customer needs to change their process, not just their tools

### 3. The Lie (False Belief)
The incorrect belief the character holds that prevents them from fulfilling their need.

- The lie feels **true** to the character — it's usually rooted in past experience
- It is the **source of the character's blind spot**
- The story's job is to **shatter the lie** through experience, not lecture
- The lie creates **dramatic irony** — the audience may see it before the character does

**Business narrative examples:**
- "If I just work harder, the business will turn around" (Lie: effort alone is sufficient)
- "Data should drive every decision" (Lie: ignoring intuition and emotion in decision-making)
- "We can't afford to slow down" (Lie: speed is always better than deliberation)

### 4. The Flaw (Behavioral Manifestation)
The visible behavior that results from the lie. It is the lie made observable.

- Creates **empathy through imperfection** — flawless characters are unrelatable
- Provides **evidence** of the internal problem the audience can track
- The flaw **causes real consequences** in the narrative
- Growth is measured by the flaw diminishing or transforming

**Business narrative examples:**
- Micromanagement (from the lie "only I can do this right")
- Avoidance of difficult conversations (from the lie "conflict destroys teams")
- Over-promising to clients (from the lie "saying no means losing the deal")

## Character Architecture Template

```
CHARACTER: [Name and role]
WANT: [Concrete external goal with deadline or stakes]
NEED: [Internal growth required — what they must learn]
LIE: [False belief holding them back — stated as a belief]
FLAW: [Observable behavior caused by the lie]
GHOST: [Past experience that created the lie — the wound]
ARC DIRECTION: [Positive/negative/flat — does the character grow, fall, or test others?]
```

---

## Arc Directions

### Positive Arc (Growth)
Character moves from Lie to Truth. Flaw diminishes. Need is fulfilled.
- Most common in customer success stories, brand stories, founder journeys
- Satisfying and aspirational — audience sees a path forward

### Negative Arc (Fall)
Character clings to the Lie. Flaw intensifies. Want is achieved but need is not.
- Powerful for cautionary tales, failed pitch analyses, "what not to do" content
- Creates urgency — audience wants to avoid this fate

### Flat Arc (Testing)
Character already knows the Truth. The world around them holds the Lie. Character's job is to change the world.
- Ideal for positioning a brand or thought leader as the guide
- The "character" tests environments and proves the truth through action

## Character Roles in Business Narrative

### The Customer as Hero
- Want: Solve their business problem
- Need: Change their approach or mindset
- Lie: "The old way still works" or "We just need a better tool"
- Flaw: Resistance to change, over-reliance on legacy systems
- Your brand's role: The guide who helps them see and overcome the lie

### The Founder as Hero
- Want: Build a successful company
- Need: Become the leader the company requires at each stage
- Lie: Varies by founder — "I have to do everything myself" is common
- Flaw: Whatever behavior the lie produces
- Narrative function: Creates trust through vulnerability and growth

### The Brand as Guide (Flat Arc)
- Truth the brand holds: [Core brand thesis]
- Lie the market holds: [What the market incorrectly believes]
- Evidence: [How the brand proves the truth through action]
- Narrative function: Positions the brand as experienced mentor, not hero

## Empathy Accelerators

These techniques make audiences connect with characters faster:

1. **The Undeserved Misfortune:** Show the character suffering through no fault of their own
2. **The Relatable Frustration:** Show the character dealing with something universal
3. **The Competence Signal:** Show the character being good at something — anything
4. **The Vulnerability Reveal:** Show the character admitting weakness or fear
5. **The Stakes Attachment:** Show what the character will lose — make it specific and human
