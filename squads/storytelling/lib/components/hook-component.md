# Hook Component

## Purpose
The hook is the first 5-15 seconds (or 1-2 sentences) of any narrative. Its only job is to create an open loop the audience must close. A hook that fails means nothing else in the story matters — the audience is already gone.

---

## Hook Anatomy

Every effective hook contains three elements:
1. **Pattern interrupt** — breaks the audience's current mental state
2. **Curiosity gap** — opens a question the audience needs answered
3. **Relevance signal** — tells the audience "this is about you"

---

## Hook Variations

### Type 1: The Contrarian Hook
**Formula:** State the opposite of what the audience believes.
- "The best salespeople never talk about the product."
- "Your brand story is hurting you more than having no story at all."
- "The most successful pitch I ever gave had zero slides."

**Best for:** Thought leadership, keynotes, LinkedIn posts
**Risk level:** Medium — must deliver on the contrarian promise or lose trust

### Type 2: The Specificity Hook
**Formula:** Use an oddly specific detail that signals a real story.
- "At 2:47 AM on a Tuesday, our biggest client called to fire us."
- "The email was 11 words. It changed the trajectory of the company."
- "She had 37 slides. The investor gave her four minutes."

**Best for:** Case studies, founder stories, presentations
**Risk level:** Low — specificity itself creates credibility

### Type 3: The Question Hook
**Formula:** Ask a question the audience cannot answer but desperately wants to.
- "What if everything your customers say they want is a lie?"
- "Why do 94% of pitch decks fail in the first 90 seconds?"
- "What's the one story every billion-dollar brand tells?"

**Best for:** Webinars, blog posts, social content
**Risk level:** Low — but overused; must be genuinely provocative

### Type 4: The In Medias Res Hook
**Formula:** Start in the middle of the action. Context comes later.
- "The room was silent. Twelve investors, arms crossed, staring."
- "She clicked 'send' and immediately wished she hadn't."
- "We were three days from running out of money."

**Best for:** Video content, keynotes, brand stories
**Risk level:** Low — universally effective when executed well

### Type 5: The Data Shock Hook
**Formula:** Lead with a statistic that violates expectations.
- "73% of customers can't retell your brand story. Not even close."
- "The average person sees 10,000 brand messages a day and remembers three."
- "Companies with a documented origin story raise 2.3x more in Series A."

**Best for:** Pitch decks, reports, executive presentations
**Risk level:** Medium — stat must be sourced and genuinely surprising

### Type 6: The Confession Hook
**Formula:** Admit a failure, weakness, or uncomfortable truth.
- "I spent two years telling the wrong story. Here's what it cost me."
- "Our marketing was beautiful. It was also completely ineffective."
- "I used to think storytelling was fluff. Then I lost a $2M deal because of it."

**Best for:** Founder stories, authenticity-driven content, workshops
**Risk level:** Medium — must feel genuine, not performative

### Type 7: The Stakes Hook
**Formula:** Immediately communicate what's at risk.
- "If you can't tell your story in 30 seconds, someone else will tell it for you."
- "The next 10 minutes will determine whether your pitch lives or dies."
- "Every day you operate without a clear narrative, you're leaving revenue on the table."

**Best for:** Sales presentations, urgency-driven content
**Risk level:** Medium-high — can feel manipulative if stakes are inflated

## Hook Testing Protocol

### The 3-Second Test
Read the hook to someone unfamiliar with the topic. After 3 seconds, ask: "Do you want to hear more?" If the answer is anything other than immediate yes, rewrite.

### The Scroll Test
Post the hook as the first line of social content. Measure scroll-stop rate. Below 30% stop rate = rewrite.

### The Retelling Test
Read the hook to someone. Wait 24 hours. Ask them to repeat it. If they can recall the essence, the hook is sticky.

---

## Hook Assembly Rules

1. **One hook per opening.** Stacking hooks dilutes all of them.
2. **The hook must connect to the story.** Clickbait hooks that don't pay off destroy trust.
3. **Match hook type to audience temperature.** Cold audiences need Question or Data Shock hooks. Warm audiences can handle Contrarian or Confession hooks.
4. **Write the hook last.** Write the full story first, then extract the most compelling element as the hook.
5. **Test three hooks for every story.** The first hook you write is rarely the best one.
