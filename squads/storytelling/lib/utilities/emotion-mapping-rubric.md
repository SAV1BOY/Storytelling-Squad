# Emotion Mapping Rubric

## Purpose
Every narrative has an emotional arc — a sequence of emotional states the audience moves through from opening to close. This rubric provides a systematic method for mapping, measuring, and designing the emotional intensity of a story section by section. A story with a flat emotional line is a report. A story with an intentional emotional arc is a narrative that moves people to act.

---

## Emotional Intensity Scale (0-10)

### Level 0: Neutral
- No emotional engagement. The audience is processing information, not feeling.
- Appropriate for: Setup, transitions, technical context

### Level 1-2: Mild Interest
- Curiosity is engaged but stakes are not yet felt.
- Appropriate for: Opening context, establishing the world

### Level 3-4: Engagement
- The audience cares about the outcome. Attention is voluntary but sustained.
- Appropriate for: Early narrative development, introducing characters

### Level 5-6: Investment
- The audience is emotionally involved. They want to know what happens.
- Appropriate for: Conflict escalation, stakes raising, midpoint turns

### Level 7-8: Tension
- The audience feels the stakes in their body — leaning forward, holding breath.
- Appropriate for: Crisis moments, climactic builds, major revelations

### Level 9-10: Peak Emotion
- The audience is fully absorbed. This is the moment they'll remember and retell.
- Appropriate for: Climax, resolution, the single most important moment of the narrative

## Emotion Types by Narrative Function

### Engagement Emotions (Pull audience in)
| Emotion | Trigger | Intensity Range |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Curiosity | Open loop, unanswered question | 2-5 |
| Surprise | Unexpected information or turn | 4-8 |
| Recognition | Audience sees themselves in the story | 3-6 |
| Anticipation | Setup of an expected payoff | 3-7 |

### Tension Emotions (Hold audience in place)
| Emotion | Trigger | Intensity Range |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Anxiety | Uncertain outcome with stakes | 5-8 |
| Empathy | Character suffering or struggling | 4-8 |
| Frustration | Obstacle that seems unjust | 5-7 |
| Dread | Inevitable negative outcome approaching | 6-9 |

### Resolution Emotions (Release and satisfy)
| Emotion | Trigger | Intensity Range |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Relief | Crisis resolved positively | 6-8 |
| Joy | Character achieves meaningful goal | 7-9 |
| Inspiration | Vision of what's possible | 6-9 |
| Determination | Clear path forward revealed | 5-8 |
| Awe | Magnitude of transformation revealed | 8-10 |

### Cautionary Emotions (Motivate through avoidance)
| Emotion | Trigger | Intensity Range |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Fear | Consequences of inaction | 5-8 |
| Regret | Missed opportunity shown | 5-7 |
| Discomfort | Status quo made viscerally unpleasant | 4-7 |

## Mapping Process

### Step 1: Divide the narrative into sections
- For written content: Use paragraphs or natural section breaks
- For presentations: Use slides or time blocks (2-3 minute segments)
- For video: Use scenes or shot sequences

### Step 2: Identify the target emotion for each section
- What should the audience feel at the end of this section?
- Choose from the emotion types above

### Step 3: Assign intensity levels
- Use the 0-10 scale for each section
- Plot on a line graph — the shape reveals your emotional arc

### Step 4: Evaluate the arc shape
- Does it rise and fall with intention?
- Is there at least one peak moment (8+)?
- Are there rest points (3-4) between peaks?
- Does the arc end higher than it began?

### Step 5: Adjust for target
- If the arc is flat: Add conflict, raise stakes, increase specificity
- If the arc is erratic: Smooth transitions, build progressively
- If the arc peaks too early: Redistribute tension toward the climax
- If the arc never peaks: The story lacks a climactic moment — add one

## Ideal Arc Shapes by Format

### Pitch Deck (10-15 slides)
```
Intensity: 2 → 4 → 6 → 4 → 7 → 5 → 8 → 9 → 7
Shape: Gradual build with two peaks, second peak is the climax
```

### Case Study (800-1200 words)
```
Intensity: 3 → 5 → 7 → 4 → 8 → 6
Shape: Quick engagement, build to the turning point, resolve with proof
```

### Keynote (20 minutes)
```
Intensity: 5 → 3 → 5 → 7 → 4 → 6 → 8 → 5 → 9 → 7
Shape: Strong hook, build-rest-build rhythm, major climax near the end
```

### Email Sequence (5 emails)
```
Email 1: 4-5 (curiosity)
Email 2: 5-6 (recognition)
Email 3: 7-8 (tension)
Email 4: 6-7 (proof)
Email 5: 8-9 (inspiration + CTA)
```

---

## Common Mapping Errors

1. **Flat line:** Every section at the same intensity — no emotional movement
2. **Early peak:** The most emotional moment is in the first 20% — nowhere to go
3. **No rest:** Every section at 7+ intensity — the audience burns out
4. **Emotional whiplash:** Jumping from joy to fear to humor without transitions
5. **Mismatch:** The mapped emotion doesn't match what the content actually delivers
