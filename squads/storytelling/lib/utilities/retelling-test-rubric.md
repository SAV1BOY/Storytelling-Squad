# Retelling Test Rubric

## Purpose
The ultimate test of a story's quality is whether the audience retells it. A story that lives in one conversation and dies there has limited impact. A story that gets retold spreads your narrative without your involvement. This rubric provides a framework for testing and scoring story stickiness — the degree to which a story can survive the retelling process.

---

## The Retelling Test Protocol

### Step 1: Tell the story
Deliver the story to a test audience of 3-5 people who haven't heard it before. Use the intended delivery format (verbal, written, video).

### Step 2: Wait 24-48 hours
Do not discuss the story in the interim. The delay is essential — it tests long-term retention, not short-term memory.

### Step 3: Ask them to retell it
Prompt: "Remember the story I told you? Can you tell it back to me as if you were telling someone else?"

### Step 4: Score the retelling
Use the rubric below to evaluate what survived, what was lost, and what was distorted.

---

## Retelling Scoring Categories

### 1. Core Message Retention (0-25 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 21-25 | The reteller conveys the core message accurately. The "point" of the story is preserved even if details are changed. |
| 16-20 | Core message is mostly preserved but slightly diluted or generalized. |
| 11-15 | Core message is partially retained — the reteller gets the topic right but misses the specific insight. |
| 6-10 | Core message is distorted — the reteller has created a different interpretation. |
| 0-5 | Core message is lost. The reteller cannot articulate what the story was about. |

### 2. Key Details Survival (0-20 points)

**Test which specific details survived:**

| Detail Type | Points if Retained |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Character name | 3 |
| Specific number or metric | 4 |
| Emotional moment | 4 |
| Turning point or twist | 4 |
| Opening hook | 3 |
| Closing line or image | 2 |

Score the total retained out of 20. Details that are modified but spiritually preserved count at half value.

### 3. Emotional Tone Preservation (0-20 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 17-20 | The reteller conveys the same emotional tone as the original. They feel what you intended them to feel while retelling it. |
| 13-16 | Emotional tone is mostly preserved. The reteller hits the right notes but with less intensity. |
| 9-12 | Some emotional content survives but the retelling is more factual than emotional. |
| 5-8 | Emotional tone is flat — the retelling is purely informational. |
| 0-4 | Wrong emotional tone — the reteller conveys a different emotion than intended. |

### 4. Structure Retention (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | The reteller follows the same narrative structure — beginning, middle, end in the right order. Key beats are sequenced correctly. |
| 10-12 | Structure is mostly preserved. Minor reordering that doesn't affect meaning. |
| 7-9 | Partial structure. The reteller remembers the ending but is fuzzy on the middle. |
| 4-6 | Structure is fragmented. Key beats are out of order or missing. |
| 0-3 | No recognizable structure. The retelling is a jumble of disconnected points. |

### 5. Voluntary Elaboration (0-20 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 17-20 | The reteller adds their own commentary, connection, or enthusiasm. They've made the story their own. They want to tell it. |
| 13-16 | Some personal connection evident. The reteller relates it to their own experience. |
| 9-12 | The reteller completes the task but shows no personal investment. Dutiful retelling. |
| 5-8 | The reteller is unsure and hesitant. They struggle to see why the story matters. |
| 0-4 | The reteller cannot complete the retelling or shows visible disinterest. |

---

## Score Interpretation

| Total Score | Stickiness Rating | Assessment |
|-------------|-------------------|------------|
| 85-100 | Viral-ready | This story will spread. It retains meaning across multiple retelling generations. |
| 70-84 | Sticky | Strong retention. Will survive one retelling generation. Worth deploying widely. |
| 55-69 | Moderate | Core survives but details are lost. Strengthen the memorable moments. |
| 40-54 | Weak | Only fragments survive. The story needs a stronger spine — core message, key detail, emotional peak. |
| Below 40 | Not Sticky | The story doesn't transfer. Rebuild with retellability as a primary design goal. |

## What Makes Stories Retellable

### The Stickiness Factors (Heath Brothers — Made to Stick)

1. **Simple:** One core message, not five. The reteller can capture it in one sentence.
2. **Unexpected:** A moment of surprise that the reteller wants to reproduce for their audience.
3. **Concrete:** Specific details — names, numbers, sensory descriptions — that give the reteller handholds.
4. **Credible:** Proof that makes the reteller confident they won't look foolish repeating the story.
5. **Emotional:** A feeling that the reteller experienced and wants to share.
6. **Story:** A narrative structure that provides a natural retelling sequence.

### The Retellable Moment
Every sticky story has one moment that is disproportionately retold — the "Did you hear about...?" moment. Identify it, sharpen it, and make sure it carries the core message.

---

## Improving Stickiness

### If the core message was lost:
- Simplify. The message has too many components. Reduce to one sentence.
- State the message explicitly at least once, in addition to showing it through the story.

### If key details were lost:
- The details weren't vivid enough. Add sensory information or oddly specific numbers.
- Reduce the number of details. Five details compete; one detail sticks.

### If emotional tone was lost:
- The emotional peak wasn't high enough. Intensify the climactic moment.
- The emotional moment was too brief. Linger on it — let the audience feel before you move on.

### If structure was lost:
- The structure was too complex. Simplify to three beats: setup, turn, payoff.
- Add a clear callback — it gives the reteller a natural ending.
