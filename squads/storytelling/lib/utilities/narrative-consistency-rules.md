# Cross-Channel Narrative Consistency Rules

## Purpose
A brand's story is told across dozens of touchpoints — website, pitch decks, social media, sales calls, emails, videos, event presentations. Inconsistency across these channels doesn't just confuse the audience — it erodes trust. These rules ensure that the core narrative remains coherent regardless of where or how it's delivered.

---

## The Consistency Hierarchy

### Level 1: Sacred Elements (Never Change)
These elements must be identical across every channel and format:
- **Core origin story:** The foundational narrative of why the company exists
- **Brand thesis:** The single sentence that captures the brand's core belief
- **Character roles:** Who is the hero (customer) and who is the guide (brand)
- **Primary enemy:** The problem or force the brand fights against
- **Value promise:** What the customer's life looks like after working with the brand

### Level 2: Adaptive Elements (Flex, Don't Break)
These elements can be adjusted for context but must remain recognizably consistent:
- **Tone of voice:** Can shift from formal to casual but must stay within the brand's voice range
- **Proof points:** Can be rotated or selected by relevance but must all support the same thesis
- **Story examples:** Can be swapped based on audience but must illustrate the same principles
- **Metaphors and analogies:** Can vary but must point to the same underlying truth

### Level 3: Channel-Specific Elements (Free to Vary)
These elements should be optimized for each channel:
- **Length and format:** A tweet vs. a whitepaper — different containers, same essence
- **Hook type:** Different channels require different attention-capture mechanisms
- **Visual style:** Platform-native design while maintaining brand recognition
- **CTA specifics:** Different conversion goals per channel

---

## Consistency Rules

### Rule 1: The One-Story Test
All content across all channels must be answerable by one question: "What story are we telling?" If two pieces of content imply different stories, one of them is wrong.

### Rule 2: The Stranger Test
Show a stranger one piece of content from each channel. Ask them: "Is this the same company?" If they hesitate, there's a consistency problem.

### Rule 3: The Contradiction Scan
No two pieces of content should contain directly contradictory information, positioning, or tone. Common contradiction zones:
- Website says "We serve everyone" / pitch deck says "We're exclusively enterprise"
- Social media tone is playful / sales emails are formal and corporate
- Case study claims one methodology / webinar describes a different process

### Rule 4: The Timeline Rule
When the brand story references specific dates, milestones, or founding narratives, the facts must be identical everywhere. Common drift points:
- Founding year
- Number of customers or users
- Key metrics and results
- Founding story details

### Rule 5: The Character Consistency Rule
The brand character (voice, personality, values) must be recognizable across channels. A customer who follows you on LinkedIn and then gets a sales email should feel like they're talking to the same entity.

## Cross-Channel Audit Checklist

### Website vs. Pitch Deck
- [ ] Origin story matches in both
- [ ] Value proposition uses the same core language
- [ ] Customer persona is consistent
- [ ] Metrics and proof points align
- [ ] The enemy/problem is framed identically

### Social Media vs. Email
- [ ] Voice and personality are recognizably the same
- [ ] Key themes from social content appear in email sequences
- [ ] Proof points referenced in email are discoverable on social
- [ ] No contradictory claims between channels

### Sales Collateral vs. Marketing Content
- [ ] Sales team tells the same origin story as the website
- [ ] Competitive positioning is consistent
- [ ] Pricing narrative aligns (even if specifics differ by deal)
- [ ] Case studies used in sales match published versions

### Internal vs. External Narrative
- [ ] The story employees tell matches the story marketing tells
- [ ] Internal values language appears in external content
- [ ] Employee advocacy content aligns with brand messaging
- [ ] New hire onboarding includes the narrative framework

---

## Consistency Maintenance Process

### Monthly
- Spot-check one piece of content from each active channel
- Compare against the sacred elements
- Flag and fix any drift

### Quarterly
- Full cross-channel audit using the checklist above
- Update the narrative framework document if intentional changes have been made
- Brief all content creators on any updates

### Annually
- Deep narrative audit across all channels
- Refresh proof points and examples
- Ensure the brand thesis still reflects the company's actual position

---

## Drift Prevention

### Single Source of Truth
Maintain one master narrative document that all content creators reference. This document contains all Level 1 (sacred) and Level 2 (adaptive) elements. No content should be created without consulting it.

### Narrative Onboarding
Every new team member, agency partner, or freelancer should receive the master narrative document and a 30-minute briefing before creating any content.

### Review Gate
All content that references the brand story, origin, values, or positioning should be reviewed against the consistency rules before publication.
