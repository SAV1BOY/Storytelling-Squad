# Pitch Scoring Rubric

## Purpose
A specialized rubric for evaluating the quality of pitches — investor pitches, sales pitches, partnership pitches, and internal initiative pitches. While the general story scoring rubric evaluates narrative quality, this rubric evaluates pitch-specific effectiveness: Does this pitch create the conditions for a "yes"?

---

## Scoring Categories

### 1. Problem Framing (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Problem is viscerally felt by the audience within 30 seconds. Specific, quantified, and emotionally loaded. Audience nods in recognition or feels the pain. |
| 10-12 | Problem is clear and relevant to the audience. Quantified but lacks emotional charge. |
| 7-9 | Problem is stated but generic — could apply to any company or audience. |
| 4-6 | Problem is implied but never directly stated or quantified. |
| 0-3 | No clear problem. Pitch starts with solution or company history. |

### 2. Unique Insight (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Presents a non-obvious insight that reframes how the audience understands the problem. The "aha" moment is palpable. The insight is proprietary or hard-won. |
| 10-12 | Insight is present and differentiating. Audience learns something new. |
| 7-9 | Insight exists but is familiar — the audience has heard similar framing before. |
| 4-6 | Solution is described but without a unique angle. "We built a better X." |
| 0-3 | No insight. Pure feature description or generic value proposition. |

### 3. Narrative Flow (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Pitch follows a clear story arc — setup, tension, resolution. Each section builds naturally on the previous. Transitions are seamless. Sparkline rhythm is present. |
| 10-12 | Good flow with minor hitches. One transition feels forced. Overall arc is present. |
| 7-9 | Sections are logically ordered but feel like a list, not a story. No narrative momentum. |
| 4-6 | Disjointed — sections don't build on each other. Audience must work to follow. |
| 0-3 | No discernible flow. Random arrangement of information. |

### 4. Proof and Traction (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Multiple proof types: metrics, named customers, growth data, third-party validation. Proof is woven into the narrative at key moments. Numbers are specific and attributed. |
| 10-12 | Good proof present. At least 2-3 concrete data points. Some proof feels bolted on rather than integrated. |
| 7-9 | Some proof exists but is generic — percentages without baselines, testimonials without names. |
| 4-6 | Proof is thin. One vague reference to results. "Customers love us." |
| 0-3 | No proof. All claims are unsubstantiated promises. |

### 5. Audience Calibration (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Pitch is precisely tailored to this audience's concerns, language, and decision criteria. References specific audience context. Anticipates and addresses likely objections. |
| 7-8 | Pitch is generally relevant to the audience. Some tailoring present. |
| 5-6 | Generic pitch that could be given to any audience. Not offensive but not targeted. |
| 3-4 | Slight mismatch between pitch content and audience priorities. |
| 0-2 | Clear mismatch. Pitch talks about things this audience doesn't care about. |

### 6. Memorability and Retellability (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Contains at least one moment the audience will retell — a striking stat, a vivid story, a perfect analogy. The pitch's core message can be summarized in one sentence by the audience after. |
| 7-8 | One memorable moment. Core message is clear but not strikingly retellable. |
| 5-6 | Competent but forgettable. No single moment stands out. |
| 3-4 | Audience will struggle to recall specifics an hour later. |
| 0-2 | Completely forgettable. Blends into every other pitch the audience has heard. |

### 7. Ask Clarity (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | The ask is specific, singular, and clearly stated. The audience knows exactly what you want, what they get, and what happens next. Timeline and process are outlined. |
| 7-8 | Ask is clear. Minor ambiguity about next steps or timeline. |
| 5-6 | Ask is present but vague. "We'd love to explore working together." |
| 3-4 | Multiple asks or unclear ask. Audience isn't sure what they're being asked to do. |
| 0-2 | No ask. Pitch ends without a clear call to action. |

### 8. Confidence and Delivery (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Speaker owns the room. Pace varies naturally. Pauses are used for emphasis. Eye contact is sustained. No filler words. Body language signals conviction. |
| 7-8 | Confident delivery with minor issues — occasional filler, slightly rushed in one section. |
| 5-6 | Adequate delivery. Reads more than performs. Consistent pace without variation. |
| 3-4 | Nervous delivery. Frequent filler words, rushed pacing, limited eye contact. |
| 0-2 | Delivery undermines content. Reading from notes, monotone, visible discomfort. |

---

## Total Score Interpretation

| Score | Rating | Likely Outcome |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| 90-100 | Exceptional | Strong likelihood of advancing. Memorable pitch. |
| 80-89 | Strong | Good chance of advancing. One area to sharpen. |
| 70-79 | Competitive | In the mix but not differentiated. Needs polish. |
| 60-69 | Adequate | May advance on merit of idea, not pitch quality. |
| 50-59 | Below Standard | Unlikely to advance without significant revision. |
| Below 50 | Does Not Meet Standard | Rebuild from scratch. |

---

## Quick Assessment (Pre-Pitch Checkpoint)

Before any pitch, verify these five non-negotiables:
1. Can you state the problem in one sentence that makes the audience feel it?
2. Can you state your unique insight in one sentence that creates an "aha"?
3. Do you have at least three concrete proof points?
4. Is your ask specific and singular?
5. Can someone retell your pitch's core in one sentence after hearing it?

If any answer is no, the pitch is not ready.
