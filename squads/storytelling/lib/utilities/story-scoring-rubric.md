# Story Scoring Rubric (0-100)

## Purpose
A standardized rubric for evaluating story quality across all formats. This scoring system provides objective, repeatable assessment so that story quality can be measured, compared, and improved over time. Every story produced by the squad should be scored before delivery.

---

## Scoring Categories

### 1. Hook Strength (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Hook creates immediate, visceral curiosity. Audience cannot look away. Pattern interrupt + curiosity gap + relevance signal all present. |
| 10-12 | Hook engages interest. One of the three hook elements is weak but the opening works. |
| 7-9 | Hook is functional but generic. Could apply to many stories. Doesn't create urgency. |
| 4-6 | Hook is present but weak — no pattern interrupt, no open loop. |
| 0-3 | No hook. Story starts with context or background. Audience must choose to stay. |

### 2. Character Depth (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Character has clear want, need, lie, and flaw. Audience empathizes within the first 30 seconds or 3 sentences. Character feels real, not constructed. |
| 10-12 | Character has want and at least one internal dimension. Empathy is present but takes time to build. |
| 7-9 | Character is named and situated but lacks internal dimension. Functions as a case study subject, not a story character. |
| 4-6 | Character is generic — "a client" or "a company." No specificity, no empathy. |
| 0-3 | No identifiable character. The narrative is about ideas, not people. |

### 3. Conflict and Stakes (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | Conflict is specific, escalating, and creates genuine tension. Stakes are felt by the audience — they worry about the outcome. Dilemma is present. |
| 10-12 | Conflict is clear and stakes are stated. Tension exists but doesn't escalate significantly. |
| 7-9 | Problem is identified but not dramatized. Stakes are implied, not felt. |
| 4-6 | Conflict is mentioned in passing. "They had challenges." No specificity. |
| 0-3 | No conflict. The narrative moves from situation to solution without resistance. |

### 4. Transformation Clarity (0-15 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 13-15 | State A and State B are vivid, specific, and emotionally distinct. The bridge between them includes genuine struggle. The distance traveled is felt. |
| 10-12 | Before and after are clear with measurable change. Bridge exists but could be more detailed. |
| 7-9 | Results are stated but State A is vague. Transformation is told, not shown. |
| 4-6 | Only State B is described. No before state for comparison. |
| 0-3 | No transformation. The narrative describes a situation without change. |

### 5. Proof and Credibility (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Multiple proof types present (stats, quotes, named examples). Proof is woven into narrative, not appended. Sources are attributed. |
| 7-8 | At least two proof types present. Proof supports key claims. |
| 5-6 | One proof type present. Claims have some support but gaps exist. |
| 3-4 | Proof is generic — "clients love us" without specifics. |
| 0-2 | No proof. All claims are unsupported assertions. |

### 6. Emotional Arc (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Emotional intensity varies intentionally. At least one peak moment (8+ on emotion scale). Arc shape matches the format. Rest points exist between peaks. |
| 7-8 | Emotional variation is present. One clear peak. Some sections are emotionally flat. |
| 5-6 | Some emotional moments exist but arc is mostly flat. No intentional design. |
| 3-4 | Narrative is primarily informational. Emotion appears accidentally, not by design. |
| 0-2 | No emotional engagement. Could be a report or memo. |

### 7. Structure and Pacing (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Narrative has clear beginning, middle, and end. Pacing varies — fast through action, slow through emotion. No section feels too long or too short. Scene commandments are present. |
| 7-8 | Structure is solid. Minor pacing issues — one section drags or rushes. |
| 5-6 | Structure exists but is formulaic. Pacing is monotonous. |
| 3-4 | Structure is unclear. Reader/listener isn't sure where they are in the narrative. |
| 0-2 | No discernible structure. Stream of consciousness or disorganized points. |

### 8. Clarity and Concision (0-10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Passes the grunt test completely. No jargon, no ambiguity. Every sentence earns its place. Trim rules have been applied. |
| 7-8 | Mostly clear. 1-2 instances of jargon or unnecessary complexity. |
| 5-6 | Generally understandable but could be 20-30% shorter without losing meaning. |
| 3-4 | Frequent jargon, hedge words, or redundancy. Message is buried. |
| 0-2 | Fails grunt test. Audience cannot easily identify what's offered, why it matters, or what to do next. |

---

## Score Interpretation

| Total Score | Rating | Action |
|-------------|--------|--------|
| 90-100 | Exceptional | Ready for delivery. Archive as exemplar. |
| 80-89 | Strong | Minor refinements. One editing pass. |
| 70-79 | Good | Solid foundation. Needs targeted improvement in weak categories. |
| 60-69 | Adequate | Functional but not memorable. Significant revision recommended. |
| 50-59 | Below Standard | Major gaps. Rebuild weak sections using component templates. |
| Below 50 | Does Not Meet Standard | Fundamental rethinking required. Restart from story architecture. |

---

## Scoring Process

1. Score each category independently — don't let a strong hook inflate your conflict score
2. Write a one-sentence justification for each score
3. Identify the two lowest-scoring categories as priority improvement areas
4. Re-score after revisions to confirm improvement
5. Log scores over time to track squad quality trends
