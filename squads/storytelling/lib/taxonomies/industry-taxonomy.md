# Industry Taxonomy

## Purpose
Different industries have different storytelling patterns — what resonates in SaaS falls flat in healthcare, and what moves a nonprofit audience will bore an ecommerce buyer. This taxonomy maps each major industry vertical to its dominant storytelling patterns, audience expectations, regulatory considerations, and high-performing narrative structures, so that story assets can be calibrated for industry context rather than applied generically.

---

## Industries

### 1. SaaS (Software as a Service)
**Audience psychology:** Rational buyers making recurring-cost decisions. Need to justify spend to stakeholders. Fear vendor lock-in and implementation pain.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Before/after transformation stories anchored in measurable outcomes (time saved, revenue gained, costs cut)
- "Day in the life" stories showing workflow improvement
- Migration stories: the pain of the old way vs. the relief of the new way
- Scale stories: how the product grew with the customer
**Narrative risks:** Feature-listing disguised as storytelling. Jargon-heavy "solutions" language that says nothing.
**High-performing formats:** Case studies, product demo narratives, customer video testimonials, onboarding journey stories
**Voice/tone norms:** Confident but not arrogant. Technical credibility with human warmth. Conversational over corporate.

### 2. Ecommerce
**Audience psychology:** Emotional, impulse-driven and aspiration-driven purchasing. Brand identity matters. Social proof is currency. Visual-first consumption.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Product origin stories: where it comes from, who made it, why it exists
- Customer identity stories: "people like you use this"
- Unboxing and first-use experience narratives
- Seasonal and cultural moment stories tied to buying occasions
**Narrative risks:** Over-reliance on discounts as the story. Aesthetic without substance.
**High-performing formats:** Social media stories, influencer narratives, email sequences, video ads, user-generated content
**Voice/tone norms:** Aspirational, visual, personality-forward. Varies widely by brand positioning (luxury vs. value vs. niche).

### 3. Info Product (Courses, Coaching, Digital Products)
**Audience psychology:** Buying transformation, not information. Skeptical due to market saturation. Need to trust the person behind the product. Fear of buying another thing they won't finish.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Creator origin story: the personal journey that led to the product
- Student transformation stories: specific, named, detailed results
- "I was where you are" empathy narratives
- Contrarian insight stories: "Everyone says X, but here's what actually works"
**Narrative risks:** Hype without substance. Manufactured urgency. Testimonials that sound scripted.
**High-performing formats:** Long-form sales pages, webinar narratives, email sequences, social proof compilations, podcast interviews
**Voice/tone norms:** Personal, direct, authority earned through story rather than credentials. Vulnerability is a tool.

### 4. B2B (Business to Business)
**Audience psychology:** Multiple stakeholders in buying decisions. Long sales cycles. Risk-averse — nobody gets fired for buying the safe choice. Need internal champions who can retell the story.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- ROI narratives with named companies and specific numbers
- "Company like yours" analogy stories
- Implementation journey stories that address the fear of change
- Thought leadership stories that position the vendor as an industry thinker
**Narrative risks:** Death by committee — stories edited into blandness. Confusing "professional" with "boring."
**High-performing formats:** Case studies, white papers with narrative structure, conference keynotes, LinkedIn content, account-based storytelling
**Voice/tone norms:** Authoritative, data-informed, peer-to-peer. Human but measured. Avoid consumer-style emotional manipulation.

### 5. Nonprofit
**Audience psychology:** Values-driven. Giving is emotional. Donors need to feel their contribution matters. Compassion fatigue is real. Transparency builds trust.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Beneficiary stories: one person, one name, one specific change
- Donor impact stories: "Because you gave, this happened"
- Founder/mission origin stories anchored in a defining moment
- Progress narratives: not just need, but momentum and hope
**Narrative risks:** Poverty porn or trauma exploitation. Stories that remove agency from beneficiaries. Donor fatigue from relentless need-based appeals.
**High-performing formats:** Annual reports as narrative, email appeals, video stories, gala presentations, social media impact moments
**Voice/tone norms:** Warm, honest, hopeful without being naive. Respect for the dignity of story subjects. Gratitude without groveling.

### 6. Startup
**Audience psychology:** Multiple audiences simultaneously — investors, early customers, talent, press. Each audience needs a different version of the same core story. Momentum is the meta-narrative.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Founder origin stories: the moment of insight that started everything
- Vision stories: the future state the startup is building toward
- Traction stories: evidence that the vision is working (users, revenue, milestones)
- "Why now" stories: market timing narratives that explain urgency
**Narrative risks:** Vision without proof. Founder ego stories disguised as company stories. Hyperbole that erodes credibility.
**High-performing formats:** Pitch decks, launch announcements, founder interviews, culture stories for hiring, press narratives
**Voice/tone norms:** Bold, specific, urgent. Earned confidence, not borrowed. Humble about challenges, clear about direction.

### 7. Healthcare
**Audience psychology:** High stakes — decisions affect health and life. Trust is paramount. Patients want empathy; providers want evidence. Regulatory constraints are real.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Patient journey stories: diagnosis, treatment, outcome, life after
- Provider dedication stories: the human behind the white coat
- Innovation stories: how a new treatment or technology changes outcomes
- Community health stories: impact at population level
**Narrative risks:** HIPAA and privacy violations. Medical claims without evidence. Minimizing patient suffering for marketing effect. Promising outcomes that can't be guaranteed.
**High-performing formats:** Patient testimonial videos (with consent), provider profiles, health education content with narrative structure, community impact reports
**Voice/tone norms:** Empathetic, credible, careful. Never sensational. Clinical accuracy with human warmth. Always deferential to the patient's experience.

### 8. Finance
**Audience psychology:** Trust-dependent. Anxiety about money is universal. Regulatory scrutiny on claims. Audience ranges from sophisticated investors to first-time savers.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Financial milestone stories: first home, retirement funded, debt eliminated
- Advisor relationship stories: the human guidance behind the numbers
- "Weathering the storm" resilience narratives during market downturns
- Education stories: making complex topics accessible through analogy and narrative
**Narrative risks:** Regulatory violations (performance claims, guarantees). Tone-deafness about financial privilege. Oversimplification that misleads.
**High-performing formats:** Client success stories (anonymized if needed), educational content series, advisor profiles, market commentary with narrative structure
**Voice/tone norms:** Trustworthy, calm, knowledgeable. Never flashy. Authority earned through clarity, not jargon. Accessible without being patronizing.

### 9. Education
**Audience psychology:** Students want relevance and belonging. Parents want safety and outcomes. Institutions want prestige and enrollment. Lifelong learners want transformation.
**Dominant storytelling patterns:**
- Student transformation stories: who they were when they arrived, who they became
- Alumni impact stories: where education led and what it made possible
- Teacher/mentor dedication stories: the educator who changed the trajectory
- Campus experience stories: belonging, community, formative moments
**Narrative risks:** Elitism. Promise of outcomes the institution can't control. Ignoring the diversity of student experience. Nostalgia that excludes.
**High-performing formats:** Alumni profiles, campus video stories, student blogs, enrollment journey content, commencement narratives
**Voice/tone norms:** Inspiring, inclusive, forward-looking. Balance tradition with innovation. Respect for diverse paths and definitions of success.

## Industry Selection Guide

When developing stories for an industry vertical, consider:
1. **Regulatory environment** — Healthcare and finance have legal constraints on claims. Build compliance review into the story development process.
2. **Buyer complexity** — B2B and SaaS have multi-stakeholder decisions. Stories must be retellable by internal champions.
3. **Emotional register** — Nonprofit and healthcare stories carry inherent emotional weight. Ecommerce and startup stories often need to manufacture emotional stakes.
4. **Proof requirements** — B2B and SaaS audiences demand evidence. Info product and ecommerce audiences respond more to identity and aspiration.
5. **Trust baseline** — Finance and healthcare start with low trust. Nonprofit starts with high trust. Adjust vulnerability and transparency accordingly.
