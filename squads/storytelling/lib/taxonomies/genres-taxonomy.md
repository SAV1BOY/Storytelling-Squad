# Genres Taxonomy — Snyder 10 + Coyne Genres for Marketing

## Purpose
Genre is not just a label — it is a set of audience expectations. When you choose a genre, you are making a promise about the emotional experience the audience will have. This taxonomy maps Blake Snyder's 10 story genres and Shawn Coyne's Story Grid genre system to marketing and business storytelling contexts.

---

## Blake Snyder's 10 Genres (Adapted for Business)

### 1. Monster in the House
**Original:** A monster, a house, a sin that created the monster
**Business adaptation:** A threat (competitor, disruption, regulation) traps a company, and survival requires confronting the threat directly
**Use for:** Crisis narratives, competitive positioning, risk-focused pitches
**Example:** "A new regulation threatened to shut down 40% of the industry. Here's how one company not only survived but grew 300%."

### 2. Golden Fleece
**Original:** A quest, a road, a team
**Business adaptation:** A journey toward a difficult goal with obstacles along the way — the real treasure is what's learned, not what's found
**Use for:** Product development stories, market expansion narratives, transformation case studies
**Example:** "We set out to build a CRM. What we actually built was a new way of selling."

### 3. Out of the Bottle
**Original:** A wish granted, a lesson learned
**Business adaptation:** Sudden success or resource (funding, viral moment, key hire) that creates unexpected problems before yielding wisdom
**Use for:** Founder stories, rapid-growth narratives, cautionary tales about scaling
**Example:** "We went viral overnight. 2 million visitors. And our servers — and our team — completely collapsed."

### 4. Dude with a Problem
**Original:** An ordinary person, a sudden event, a life-or-death struggle
**Business adaptation:** An ordinary business facing an extraordinary challenge they weren't prepared for
**Use for:** Customer stories, underdog narratives, crisis response case studies
**Example:** "She was a one-person marketing team at a 50-person company. Then their competitor launched a $10M campaign."

### 5. Rites of Passage
**Original:** A life transition, a wrong way, a right way
**Business adaptation:** A company or leader going through a fundamental transition (growth stage, pivot, succession)
**Use for:** Brand evolution stories, leadership narratives, company milestone content

### 6. Buddy Love
**Original:** An incomplete hero, a counterpart, a complication
**Business adaptation:** Partnership stories — co-founders, brand-customer relationships, strategic alliances
**Use for:** Partnership announcements, co-founder origin stories, integration narratives

### 7. Whydunit
**Original:** A dark turn, a detective, a secret revealed
**Business adaptation:** Investigating why something failed (or succeeded), uncovering the hidden cause
**Use for:** Failure analysis content, "what we learned" posts, diagnostic case studies

### 8. The Fool Triumphant
**Original:** A fool, an establishment, a triumph
**Business adaptation:** The outsider or underdog who succeeds precisely because they didn't know the "rules"
**Use for:** Challenger brand positioning, disruptor narratives, fresh-perspective content

### 9. Institutionalized
**Original:** A group, a choice, a sacrifice
**Business adaptation:** A team or community that must choose between individual benefit and collective good
**Use for:** Culture stories, team narratives, community-building content

### 10. Superhero
**Original:** A special power, a nemesis, a curse
**Business adaptation:** A unique capability (technology, methodology, insight) that comes with a responsibility
**Use for:** Product narratives, thought leadership, brand purpose stories

## Shawn Coyne's Genre Categories (Story Grid)

### By Content Genre

| Genre | Core Emotion | Business Application |
|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| Action | Excitement | Product launch stories, competitive wins |
| Horror | Dread | Risk/threat content, cybersecurity narratives |
| Crime | Intrigue | Fraud prevention, investigative case studies |
| Thriller | Anxiety | Time-sensitive narratives, crisis content |
| Love | Intimacy | Brand affinity stories, community content |
| Performance | Admiration | Achievement stories, award narratives |
| Status | Respect | Career stories, thought leadership |
| Society | Indignation | Purpose-driven content, social impact stories |
| Worldview | Revelation | Educational content, paradigm-shift narratives |

### By Value Shift

Every genre has a core value that shifts during the narrative:
- **Action:** Life → Death (adapted: Survival → Failure)
- **Status:** Success → Failure (adapted: Recognition → Obscurity)
- **Worldview:** Ignorance → Knowledge (adapted: Confusion → Clarity)
- **Love:** Connection → Disconnection (adapted: Trust → Distrust)

## Genre Selection for Marketing

| Marketing Goal | Recommended Genre | Why |
|---------------|-------------------|-----|
| Build awareness | Worldview (revelation) | Audience needs to learn something new |
| Generate leads | Action or Thriller | Urgency drives conversion |
| Nurture prospects | Love or Performance | Trust and admiration build relationship |
| Close sales | Status or Action | Respect and excitement drive decisions |
| Retain customers | Society or Love | Belonging and connection reduce churn |
| Build authority | Worldview or Whydunit | Insight and investigation build credibility |
