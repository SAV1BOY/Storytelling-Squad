# Taxonomy of Narrative Emotions

## Purpose
Storytellers need a precise vocabulary for the emotions they design into narratives. Vague emotional targets ("make them feel something") produce vague stories. This taxonomy catalogs the emotions available to narrative designers, organized by function in the storytelling process.

---

## Category 1: Engagement Emotions
These emotions pull the audience into the narrative. They answer: "Why should I pay attention?"

### Curiosity
- **Trigger:** An open loop, unanswered question, or pattern interrupt
- **Physical signal:** Leaning forward, widening eyes, pausing other activity
- **Narrative use:** Hooks, mystery setups, unexpected data
- **Duration:** Short — must be fed or it fades to indifference

### Surprise
- **Trigger:** Violated expectation — something happens that shouldn't, or doesn't happen that should
- **Physical signal:** Raised eyebrows, intake of breath, smile or frown
- **Narrative use:** Revelations, twists, contrarian openings
- **Duration:** Momentary — must immediately transition to another emotion

### Fascination
- **Trigger:** Complexity that feels accessible — the audience senses depth they want to explore
- **Physical signal:** Stillness, focused attention, desire to learn more
- **Narrative use:** Expert storytelling, behind-the-scenes reveals, process narratives
- **Duration:** Medium — can sustain attention for extended sequences

### Recognition
- **Trigger:** The audience sees themselves or their situation in the narrative
- **Physical signal:** Nodding, verbal agreement, knowing smile
- **Narrative use:** Problem statements, character introductions, "Have you ever..." openings
- **Duration:** Medium — transitions naturally into empathy or hope

---

## Category 2: Tension Emotions
These emotions hold the audience in place. They answer: "What's going to happen?"

### Anxiety
- **Trigger:** Uncertain outcome with meaningful stakes
- **Narrative use:** Ticking clocks, rising complications, approaching deadlines
- **Intensity range:** 4-8 on emotion scale

### Empathy
- **Trigger:** A character suffers or struggles, and the audience shares the feeling
- **Narrative use:** Character vulnerability, undeserved misfortune, relatable failure
- **Intensity range:** 3-8

### Fear
- **Trigger:** A negative outcome feels imminent and consequential
- **Narrative use:** Stakes raising, risk framing, loss scenarios
- **Intensity range:** 5-9

### Frustration
- **Trigger:** An obstacle feels unjust or a character's effort goes unrewarded
- **Narrative use:** Problem dramatization, conflict escalation, systemic failure stories
- **Intensity range:** 4-7

### Indignation
- **Trigger:** A moral line is crossed — something is unfair, dishonest, or harmful
- **Narrative use:** Purpose stories, social impact narratives, brand enemy identification
- **Intensity range:** 5-9

---

## Category 3: Resolution Emotions
These emotions reward the audience. They answer: "How did it end?"

### Relief
- **Trigger:** A feared outcome is avoided or a crisis is resolved
- **Narrative use:** Post-climax moments, crisis resolution, close calls
- **Intensity range:** 5-8

### Satisfaction
- **Trigger:** A problem is solved, a goal is achieved, justice is served
- **Narrative use:** Results sections, happy endings, proof delivery
- **Intensity range:** 5-8

### Joy
- **Trigger:** Unexpected positive outcome, genuine human connection, triumph
- **Narrative use:** Customer delight stories, celebration moments, community wins
- **Intensity range:** 6-9

### Inspiration
- **Trigger:** A vision of what's possible, demonstrated by someone who achieved it
- **Narrative use:** Transformation reveals, vision casting, aspirational content
- **Intensity range:** 6-10

### Awe
- **Trigger:** The magnitude of something exceeds expectation — scale, beauty, or achievement
- **Narrative use:** Big-picture revelations, macro-level impact, legacy narratives
- **Intensity range:** 8-10

---

## Category 4: Connection Emotions
These emotions bond the audience to the storyteller. They answer: "Can I trust you?"

### Trust
- **Trigger:** Consistency between words and actions, vulnerability, demonstrated competence
- **Narrative use:** Proof beats, founder vulnerability, transparent process stories
- **Gradual build:** Trust is not a moment — it accumulates across the narrative

### Belonging
- **Trigger:** The audience feels included in a group, mission, or identity
- **Narrative use:** Community narratives, brand identity content, "people like us" framing
- **Sticky:** Once felt, belonging creates loyalty that transcends individual interactions

### Gratitude
- **Trigger:** The audience receives genuine value — an insight, a tool, a perspective
- **Narrative use:** Educational content, generous thought leadership, free frameworks
- **Reciprocal:** Gratitude naturally generates willingness to reciprocate

---

## Category 5: Motivational Emotions
These emotions drive action after the narrative ends. They answer: "What should I do?"

### Urgency
- **Trigger:** A time-sensitive opportunity or threat
- **Narrative use:** CTAs, launch narratives, limited-offer framing

### Determination
- **Trigger:** A clear path forward combined with confidence it's achievable
- **Narrative use:** Resolution sections, actionable frameworks, how-to narratives

### Ambition
- **Trigger:** A vision of a better future state that the audience now believes is possible
- **Narrative use:** Transformation stories, vision casting, competitive positioning

---

## Emotion Sequencing Rules

1. **Start with engagement emotions** — you cannot create tension without first creating attention
2. **Build through tension emotions** — tension sustains attention and creates the need for resolution
3. **Deliver resolution emotions** — unresolved tension creates frustration, not satisfaction
4. **Weave connection emotions throughout** — trust is built in parallel, not in a single moment
5. **End with motivational emotions** — the final emotion determines what the audience does next
