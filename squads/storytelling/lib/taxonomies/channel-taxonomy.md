# Channel Taxonomy

## Purpose
Every channel has its own storytelling physics — constraints on length, format, pacing, and audience attention that shape how a story must be told. A story that works beautifully in a keynote speech will fail as a tweet. This taxonomy maps each major marketing channel to its storytelling characteristics, so that story assets can be adapted correctly rather than simply copy-pasted across channels.

---

## Channels

### 1. Social Media
**Format constraints:** Short-form text, image, or video (platform-dependent). Character limits (X/Twitter), algorithmic feed placement, scroll-based consumption.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Hook must land in the first line — no slow builds
- Single-beat stories work best: one moment, one insight, one emotion
- Visual storytelling (image or video) outperforms text-only on most platforms
- Conversation-starting stories outperform conclusion-delivering stories
- Native formats win — threads on X, carousels on LinkedIn/Instagram, short video on TikTok/Reels
**Ideal story types:** Micro-stories, customer moments, behind-the-scenes glimpses, before/after transformations
**Pacing:** Immediate hook, compressed middle, punchy resolution
**Typical length:** 50-280 words (text), 15-90 seconds (video)

### 2. Email
**Format constraints:** Subject line is the gateway. Skimmable layout. Competes with 100+ other emails per day. Read on mobile 60%+ of the time.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Subject line must create a story gap (curiosity, tension, specificity)
- Opening line continues the story the subject line started — never restart
- One story per email performs better than multiple
- Personal, first-person voice outperforms corporate tone
- Story should lead to a single, clear call to action
**Ideal story types:** Founder stories, customer transformation stories, lesson-learned narratives, value stories
**Pacing:** Subject line hook, quick scene-setting (2-3 sentences), tension, resolution tied to CTA
**Typical length:** 200-600 words

### 3. Video
**Format constraints:** Production spectrum from raw selfie to cinematic. Platform-dependent length norms. Sound-on vs. sound-off viewing. Thumbnail is the first impression.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- First 3 seconds determine whether the viewer stays
- Visual storytelling carries the narrative — don't rely on narration alone
- Show, don't tell — video punishes exposition
- Emotional peaks should be visible on faces (close-ups matter)
- Music and pacing create emotional architecture independent of words
**Ideal story types:** Customer testimonials, origin stories, day-in-the-life, product transformation stories
**Pacing:** Cold open with tension or intrigue, escalating stakes, emotional climax, clear resolution
**Typical length:** 30-90 seconds (short-form), 2-8 minutes (medium), 10-30 minutes (long-form/documentary)

### 4. Deck (Presentation/Slides)
**Format constraints:** Slide-based, sequential, often presented live with narration. Visual-heavy. Audiences read ahead and skim.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- One idea per slide — the story unfolds slide by slide
- The presenter's voice carries the narrative; slides are visual anchors, not scripts
- Build tension across slides using progressive disclosure
- Data becomes story when it has a before, an after, and a "so what"
- Slide transitions are pacing tools — use them deliberately
**Ideal story types:** Pitch narratives, case study walkthroughs, strategic vision stories, problem-solution arcs
**Pacing:** Situational setup (3-5 slides), problem/tension (5-8 slides), solution/resolution (5-8 slides), call to action (1-2 slides)
**Typical length:** 10-25 slides (pitch), 25-50 slides (keynote)

### 5. Pitch (Investor/Sales)
**Format constraints:** Time-boxed (often 5-15 minutes). High-stakes. Audience is evaluating, not just absorbing. Q&A follows.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Open with a story, not a statistic — earn the right to share numbers
- The customer's pain is the protagonist, not the product
- Specificity builds credibility: real names, real numbers, real moments
- Anticipate objections and weave answers into the narrative
- Close with vision: paint the future state the audience wants to inhabit
**Ideal story types:** Founder origin stories, customer pain stories, market insight stories, traction narratives
**Pacing:** Hook story (60 seconds), problem landscape, solution reveal, proof points, vision close
**Typical length:** 5-15 minutes spoken, supported by 10-15 slides

### 6. Webinar
**Format constraints:** Live or recorded, 30-60 minutes. Screen-shared slides or demos. Audience multitasking is the norm. Chat/Q&A interaction available.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Must re-earn attention every 5-7 minutes with a new story beat or interaction
- Teaching stories work best — the audience came to learn something
- Interactive moments (polls, Q&A) are narrative breathing room
- Open with a relatable problem story, not an agenda slide
- Close with a transformation story that mirrors the audience's desired outcome
**Ideal story types:** Teaching stories, case studies, expert journey narratives, live demonstrations as narrative
**Pacing:** Opening story (3-5 min), teaching block with embedded stories (20-40 min), closing story + CTA (5 min)
**Typical length:** 30-60 minutes

### 7. Ad (Paid Media)
**Format constraints:** Extreme brevity. Audience did not choose to watch/read. Must interrupt a pattern and earn attention instantly. Platform-specific specs.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- The entire story arc must compress into seconds
- Pattern interruption is the opening move — break the scroll
- Emotion over information — ads that make you feel outperform ads that make you think
- One message, one emotion, one action — no room for complexity
- Repetition across impressions builds narrative over time (campaign as story)
**Ideal story types:** Micro-transformation stories, single-moment customer stories, before/after, problem-agitation
**Pacing:** Instant hook (0-2 sec), compressed tension (2-10 sec), resolution + CTA (2-5 sec)
**Typical length:** 6-30 seconds (video), 5-25 words (text), single image + headline (static)

### 8. Blog
**Format constraints:** Long-form text. SEO considerations. Skimmable (headers, bullets, images). Reader chose to click — earned attention.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Headlines and subheads carry the story skeleton — a skim-reader should get the arc
- Opening paragraph must establish the story question or tension
- Blend narrative with utility — stories earn the right to teach
- Specific, concrete details build credibility and readability
- Internal linking extends the narrative across multiple posts
**Ideal story types:** Deep-dive case studies, lesson narratives, expert analysis stories, how-we-did-it stories
**Pacing:** Hook paragraph, scene-setting, rising tension/complexity, insight/climax, practical takeaway, CTA
**Typical length:** 800-2500 words

### 9. Podcast
**Format constraints:** Audio-only. Intimate, conversational. Consumed during commutes, workouts, chores. Long attention spans if earned.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Voice carries everything — tone, pacing, pauses matter enormously
- Conversational storytelling outperforms scripted reads
- Listeners form parasocial relationships — authenticity is non-negotiable
- Scene-painting through sensory details replaces visual aids
- Cliffhangers before ad breaks maintain retention
**Ideal story types:** Interview-driven stories, serialized narratives, behind-the-scenes stories, expert dialogue
**Pacing:** Cold open with a compelling moment, context-building, long narrative arcs with multiple beats, reflective close
**Typical length:** 20-60 minutes

### 10. Print
**Format constraints:** Physical medium. No hyperlinks, no interactivity. Higher perceived authority. Longer shelf life. Design and typography are storytelling tools.
**Storytelling characteristics:**
- Every word earns its space — print punishes bloat
- Layout, whitespace, and typography create narrative rhythm
- Tactile experience adds emotional weight (paper quality, binding, format)
- Longevity advantage — print stories get kept, displayed, passed along
- Photography and illustration carry narrative weight equal to text
**Ideal story types:** Brand manifestos, annual reports as narrative, case study collections, thought leadership pieces
**Pacing:** Visual hook (cover/layout), deliberate opening, measured build, satisfying close
**Typical length:** Variable — 500 words (brochure) to 10,000+ (report/book)

## Channel Selection Guide

When choosing where to deploy a story, consider:
1. **Audience location** — Where does the target audience already spend attention?
2. **Story complexity** — Simple stories compress well (ad, social). Complex stories need room (blog, podcast, video).
3. **Emotional register** — Intimate stories work in email and podcast. Aspirational stories work in video and deck.
4. **Conversion proximity** — How close is this channel to a buying decision? Pitch and email are close. Blog and podcast are further.
5. **Reusability** — A single story can be told across multiple channels if adapted to each channel's physics.
