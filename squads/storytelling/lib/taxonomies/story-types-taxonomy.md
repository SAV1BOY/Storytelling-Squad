# Story Types Taxonomy

## Purpose
Not all stories serve the same function. This taxonomy classifies the primary story types used in business and brand storytelling, based on Kindra Hall's framework (Stories That Stick) and extended for operational use. Selecting the right story type for the right context is the first strategic decision in any narrative project.

---

## Primary Story Types

### 1. The Value Story
**Function:** Demonstrates the tangible value of a product, service, or approach.
**Protagonist:** The customer or user
**Core movement:** From problem to solution to measurable result
**Key elements:**
- Specific, quantified pain before the solution
- The moment of decision to try the solution
- Implementation reality (not a fairy tale — show the friction)
- Measurable outcome with before/after comparison
- Emotional shift in the customer's experience

**When to use:** Sales conversations, case studies, product pages, proposals, testimonials

**Variations:**
- Quick-win value story (one metric, fast result)
- Deep-value story (multiple metrics, longer timeline, layered impact)
- Unexpected-value story (customer got results they didn't anticipate)

### 2. The Founder Story
**Function:** Creates emotional connection to the brand through the founder's personal journey.
**Protagonist:** The founder or founding team
**Core movement:** From personal wound to insight to mission
**Key elements:**
- The origin wound — the personal experience that made the problem unacceptable
- The epiphany — the insight that became the company's thesis
- The struggle — what it cost to pursue the insight
- The proof — early validation that the insight was right
- The invitation — why the audience should join the mission

**When to use:** About pages, investor pitches, keynotes, brand campaigns, hiring pages

**Variations:**
- Reluctant founder story (didn't plan to start a company)
- Serial founder story (this company is the culmination of previous attempts)
- Accidental founder story (stumbled into the solution)

### 3. The Customer Story
**Function:** Lets the audience see themselves in someone who's already succeeded.
**Protagonist:** A specific, named customer
**Core movement:** From skepticism or struggle to trust and transformation
**Key elements:**
- Customer's world before — specific, relatable, empathy-generating
- What triggered the search for a solution
- Why they chose this path (and what they considered instead)
- The journey, including early doubts and friction
- Results that are specific, attributed, and emotionally resonant

**When to use:** Website, sales collateral, social proof, email sequences, presentations

**Variations:**
- The skeptic-to-advocate story (strongest conversion tool)
- The underdog customer story (small company, big results)
- The enterprise customer story (large company, complex implementation)

### 4. The Purpose Story
**Function:** Communicates why the organization exists beyond profit.
**Protagonist:** The community, cause, or stakeholder group the brand serves
**Core movement:** From injustice or gap to action to impact
**Key elements:**
- The injustice or need that the brand addresses
- Concrete actions the brand takes (not just statements of values)
- Impact on real people, named and specific
- Invitation for the audience to participate in the purpose

**When to use:** Brand campaigns, social media, CSR communications, culture content, hiring

**Variations:**
- Community impact story (local, specific, intimate)
- Industry change story (the brand is changing how an industry works)
- Future vision story (what the world looks like when the mission succeeds)

---

## Secondary Story Types

### 5. The Behind-the-Scenes Story
**Function:** Humanizes the brand by showing process, failure, and real people
**Best for:** Social media, newsletters, brand affinity content

### 6. The Lesson-Learned Story
**Function:** Teaches through narrative — the storyteller made a mistake so the audience doesn't have to
**Best for:** Thought leadership, blog content, workshops

### 7. The Turning-Point Story
**Function:** Dramatizes a single moment of decision that changed everything
**Best for:** Keynotes, pitch narratives, brand milestone content

### 8. The Underdog Story
**Function:** Positions the brand or customer as overcoming disproportionate odds
**Best for:** Challenger brand positioning, crowdfunding, grassroots campaigns

### 9. The Vision Story
**Function:** Paints a picture of a future state that the audience wants to inhabit
**Best for:** Investor pitches, product launches, strategic planning, recruiting

---

## Story Type Selection Matrix

| Context | Primary Type | Secondary Type |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Sales meeting | Value Story | Customer Story |
| Investor pitch | Founder Story | Vision Story |
| Website homepage | Customer Story | Purpose Story |
| Social media | Behind-the-Scenes | Lesson-Learned |
| Keynote address | Turning-Point | Founder Story |
| Brand campaign | Purpose Story | Customer Story |
| Hiring page | Founder Story | Purpose Story |
| Email nurture sequence | Value Story | Lesson-Learned |
| Crisis communication | Purpose Story | Turning-Point |
| Product launch | Vision Story | Value Story |
