# Conflict Types Taxonomy

## Purpose
Conflict is the engine of narrative. This taxonomy classifies the fundamental types of conflict available to storytellers, with specific applications for business and brand storytelling. Each conflict type generates different emotional responses and serves different narrative goals.

---

## The Five Primary Conflict Types

### 1. Person vs. Self (Internal Conflict)
**Definition:** The character struggles against their own beliefs, fears, desires, or identity.

**Manifestations in business narrative:**
- Founder doubts their ability to lead at the next stage
- Employee torn between loyalty to the company and doing what's right
- Customer paralyzed by fear of change despite knowing change is necessary
- Leader's values conflict with what the market demands

**Emotional palette:** Anxiety, self-doubt, shame, eventual clarity or acceptance
**Narrative strength:** Creates deep empathy and psychological depth
**Best for:** Founder stories, vulnerability-driven content, internal transformation narratives

**Example:** "She built the company on the principle of saying yes to every client. But at 200 employees, saying yes to everyone was destroying the team. The hardest conversation wasn't with a client — it was with herself."

### 2. Person vs. Person (Interpersonal Conflict)
**Definition:** The character is in direct opposition to another specific character.

**Manifestations in business narrative:**
- Co-founders disagree on company direction
- Sales team versus a specific competitor in a deal
- Entrepreneur versus skeptical investor
- Manager versus resistant team member

**Emotional palette:** Frustration, determination, rivalry, respect
**Narrative strength:** Creates clear drama with identifiable stakes
**Best for:** Competitive positioning, negotiation stories, partnership origin stories

**Example:** "The investor leaned back and said, 'I've seen twenty pitches like yours this month.' She leaned forward and replied, 'No, you haven't. And I can prove it in three slides.'"

### 3. Person vs. Society (Systemic Conflict)
**Definition:** The character fights against an institution, cultural norm, or systemic force.

**Manifestations in business narrative:**
- Startup challenging industry incumbents and entrenched practices
- Brand taking a stand against industry norms
- Founder fighting regulatory barriers
- Company challenging societal assumptions about who their customer is

**Emotional palette:** Indignation, courage, solidarity, triumph or martyrdom
**Narrative strength:** Creates meaning and purpose beyond profit
**Best for:** Purpose stories, brand manifestos, social impact content, challenger brand positioning

**Example:** "The insurance industry had spent 50 years making claims processes deliberately complicated. They built a company on the radical idea that paying claims should take minutes, not months."

### 4. Person vs. Nature/Environment (Environmental Conflict)
**Definition:** The character contends with forces beyond anyone's control.

**Manifestations in business narrative:**
- Company navigating a pandemic, recession, or market crash
- Team dealing with natural disaster impact on operations
- Business adapting to climate change effects on their industry
- Startup surviving in a market that isn't ready for them

**Emotional palette:** Helplessness, resilience, adaptation, awe
**Narrative strength:** Creates urgency and tests character through forces no one controls
**Best for:** Crisis narratives, resilience stories, adaptation case studies

**Example:** "On March 13, 2020, their entire business model — built around in-person events — became impossible overnight. They had 72 hours to reinvent or die."

### 5. Person vs. Technology (Technological Conflict)
**Definition:** The character struggles against technology's capabilities, limitations, or consequences.

**Manifestations in business narrative:**
- Team racing to build technology before a competitor
- Company dealing with the unintended consequences of their own product
- Workers displaced or empowered by automation and AI
- Brand navigating the tension between technology and human connection

**Emotional palette:** Urgency, displacement, wonder, ethical tension
**Narrative strength:** Deeply relevant in the current era; creates rich moral complexity
**Best for:** AI and automation narratives, product development stories, future-of-work content

**Example:** "Their AI could write marketing copy in seconds. Their best copywriter could do it in hours. The question wasn't which was better — it was what 'better' even meant anymore."

---

## Compound Conflicts

The most compelling narratives combine multiple conflict types:

### Internal + Interpersonal
The character fights someone else while simultaneously fighting themselves.
- "She needed to fire the co-founder who was also her best friend."

### Systemic + Internal
The character challenges a system while questioning whether they have the right to do so.
- "He wanted to change the industry, but part of him wondered if the industry was fine and he was just arrogant."

### Environmental + Interpersonal
External forces create pressure that strains relationships.
- "The recession didn't just threaten the company — it turned the founding team against each other."

---

## Conflict Type Selection Guide

| Narrative Goal | Primary Conflict | Why |
|---------------|------------------|-----|
| Build empathy | Person vs. Self | Internal struggle is universally relatable |
| Create drama | Person vs. Person | Clear antagonist creates clear stakes |
| Inspire action | Person vs. Society | Fighting the system creates purpose |
| Demonstrate resilience | Person vs. Nature | Survival against uncontrollable forces |
| Explore ethics | Person vs. Technology | No easy answers create genuine thought |
| Maximum impact | Compound (2-3 types) | Layered conflict creates dimensional narrative |
