# Duarte Sparkline Contrast Patterns

## Purpose
Nancy Duarte's research on great presentations revealed a universal pattern: the most effective communicators alternate between "what is" and "what could be," creating a rhythm of contrast that propels the audience toward a new understanding. This file codifies those contrast patterns for use across all narrative formats.

---

## The Core Sparkline Structure

```
What Is → What Could Be → What Is → What Could Be → ... → New Bliss
```

Every presentation, pitch, or narrative oscillates between the current reality and the envisioned future. The gap between these two states creates tension. The audience's desire to close the gap creates momentum.

---

## Contrast Pattern 1: The Classic Sparkline

### Structure
1. **What Is:** Paint the current state with specific, felt detail
2. **What Could Be:** Show the possibility — make it tangible and desirable
3. **What Is (deeper):** Return to reality but go deeper into the pain
4. **What Could Be (higher):** Raise the vision — show more of what's possible
5. **What Is (urgent):** Make the status quo feel unbearable
6. **What Could Be (inevitable):** Make the future feel achievable and near
7. **New Bliss:** The call to action that bridges the final gap

### Example Application (Brand Pitch)
1. "Right now, your story is told in 47 different ways across 12 channels." (What Is)
2. "Imagine every touchpoint telling one clear, compelling narrative." (What Could Be)
3. "Last quarter, your team spent 200 hours creating content that contradicted itself." (What Is)
4. "A unified narrative would cut that to 40 hours — and triple engagement." (What Could Be)
5. "Your competitors have already figured this out. You're falling behind daily." (What Is)
6. "In six weeks, your entire team could be working from one narrative framework." (What Could Be)
7. "Let's start with a narrative audit this week." (New Bliss)

---

## Contrast Pattern 2: The Widening Gap

### Structure
Each oscillation increases the distance between What Is and What Could Be. The gap grows wider with each cycle until the audience can no longer tolerate the status quo.

### Escalation Method
- Cycle 1: Gap is informational (facts vs. possibility)
- Cycle 2: Gap is emotional (frustration vs. relief)
- Cycle 3: Gap is existential (obsolescence vs. relevance)

### Best For
High-stakes presentations where the audience is complacent. The widening gap creates urgency by making inaction feel increasingly dangerous.

---

## Contrast Pattern 3: The Convergence

### Structure
What Is and What Could Be start far apart and gradually converge as the speaker provides the path. Each cycle brings reality closer to the vision.

### Convergence Method
- Cycle 1: Wide gap — problem feels distant from solution
- Cycle 2: Medium gap — first proof that the gap can close
- Cycle 3: Narrow gap — concrete plan makes the future feel imminent
- Final: The two lines meet — the audience believes transformation is achievable now

### Best For
Solution presentations, product demos, and implementation pitches where the audience needs to believe the vision is practical, not just aspirational.

---

## Contrast Pattern 4: The Reversal

### Structure
Begin in What Could Be (paint the positive future first), then reveal What Is (show the audience they're not there yet). This reversal creates a productive shock.

### Example
1. "Every sales call opens with a story so compelling the prospect asks to keep talking." (What Could Be)
2. "Now look at your last 10 sales calls. How many opened with a feature list?" (What Is)

### Best For
Audiences who don't know they have a problem. The reversal creates awareness by letting them experience the vision before confronting reality.

---

## Contrast Pattern 5: The Testimony Sparkline

### Structure
Alternate between the speaker's narrative and customer/third-party testimony that validates each claim.

- Speaker: "What Is" statement
- Customer quote: Confirming the pain
- Speaker: "What Could Be" statement
- Customer quote: Confirming the transformation

### Best For
Audiences with low trust in the speaker. The external voices carry the contrast while the speaker provides structure.

## Building Contrast Slides

### The What-Is Slide
- Dark or muted color palette
- Concrete data showing the current state
- Language: "today," "currently," "right now," "the reality is"
- Emotional tone: tension, frustration, concern

### The What-Could-Be Slide
- Bright or aspirational color palette
- Specific vision of the improved state
- Language: "imagine," "picture this," "what if," "here's what's possible"
- Emotional tone: hope, excitement, clarity

### Transition Language
- Into What Is: "But here's the reality..." / "And yet today..."
- Into What Could Be: "Now imagine..." / "But it doesn't have to be this way..."
- Into New Bliss: "And here's how we get there..." / "The path is clear..."

---

## Contrast Ratio Guidelines

| Format | Recommended Cycles | Ratio (What Is : What Could Be) |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| 5-minute pitch | 2-3 cycles | 40:60 (lean toward vision) |
| 20-minute keynote | 4-5 cycles | 45:55 |
| 45-minute presentation | 6-8 cycles | 50:50 |
| Blog post (1000 words) | 2-3 cycles | 40:60 |
| Email sequence | 1 cycle per email | 50:50 per email |
