# Klaff Frame Control Execution Patterns

## Purpose
Oren Klaff's frame theory (from Pitch Anything) argues that every social interaction is a collision of frames — mental structures through which people interpret reality. The strongest frame absorbs the weaker one. In storytelling and pitching, frame control determines whether your narrative shapes the audience's reality or their default frame neutralizes your message.

---

## Core Frames

### The Power Frame
**Held by:** Authority figures, gatekeepers, executives who believe their time is more valuable than yours.
**Their behavior:** Checking phones, interrupting, making you wait, asserting dominance through body language.

**Counter-frame: The Prize Frame**
Reposition yourself as the prize — the audience should feel they are being evaluated, not the other way around.
- Signal scarcity: "We only take on four narrative engagements per quarter."
- Ask qualifying questions: "Before I continue, I need to understand if this is the right fit."
- Control pacing: If interrupted, pause and wait. Do not rush to fill silence.

### The Time Frame
**Held by:** Anyone who compresses your time. "You have five minutes."
**Their behavior:** Setting arbitrary deadlines, signaling impatience, clock-watching.

**Counter-frame: The Intrigue Frame**
Make the content so compelling that the time frame dissolves naturally.
- Open with an in medias res hook that creates an open loop
- Build a tension pattern that makes interruption feel costly
- Never acknowledge the time constraint — instead, make every second so valuable they want more

### The Analyst Frame
**Held by:** Detail-oriented evaluators who want to reduce your story to data points.
**Their behavior:** Asking granular questions mid-narrative, requesting spreadsheets, challenging methodology.

**Counter-frame: The Moral Authority Frame**
Redirect from analysis to values and meaning.
- "The numbers matter, and I'll share them. But first, let me tell you why we built this."
- Lead with the story of who benefits and why it matters
- Provide data within narrative context, not as standalone slides

### The Prizing Frame
**Held by:** Anyone who positions themselves as doing you a favor by listening.
**Their behavior:** "We're very selective," "What makes you different from the other 50 pitches we've heard?"

**Counter-frame: The Mutual Evaluation Frame**
Establish that both parties are evaluating fit.
- "We're selective too. Not every company is ready for this level of narrative work."
- Ask them questions about their commitment level, timeline, and willingness to change
- Walk away if the fit isn't right — demonstrated willingness to leave is the ultimate frame control

---

## Frame Collision Resolution

When two frames collide, one absorbs the other within 30-60 seconds. The winner is determined by:

1. **Conviction:** The person with deeper belief in their frame wins
2. **Status:** Higher perceived status strengthens the frame (but can be shifted)
3. **Novelty:** An unexpected frame disrupts the established one
4. **Emotional intensity:** The frame with stronger emotional charge dominates

---

## Frame Stacking in Narrative

### The Three-Frame Stack
For maximum narrative control, stack three frames in sequence:

**Frame 1: The Intrigue Frame (0-90 seconds)**
- Open with a hook that creates an open loop
- The audience's analytical frame cannot engage because they need to know what happens next

**Frame 2: The Prize Frame (90 seconds - 5 minutes)**
- Establish your unique position and scarcity
- The audience shifts from evaluating to wanting

**Frame 3: The Moral Authority Frame (5 minutes onward)**
- Connect your narrative to values, purpose, and impact
- The audience is now operating within your frame entirely

## Frame Control in Specific Contexts

### In Pitch Meetings
- Arrive with a frame: "I'm going to show you something that will change how you think about X"
- If challenged, reframe: "That's a good question for later. Right now, the story needs this next piece."
- Close with a prizing frame: "If this resonates, we should talk about next steps. If it doesn't, that's valuable information for both of us."

### In Content Marketing
- Frame the content as exclusive insight, not commodity information
- Use language that positions the reader as selected: "For the few who take storytelling seriously..."
- End with a frame that invites the reader into a selective community

### In Sales Conversations
- Never accept the buyer's frame of "convince me"
- Reframe to mutual discovery: "Let's figure out if this is the right fit"
- Use narrative to shift the conversation from price to value to meaning

---

## Frame Control Ethics

Frame control is a power tool. Ethical use requires:
1. **Genuine value:** Your frame must be backed by real substance
2. **Mutual benefit:** The frame you establish should serve both parties
3. **Authenticity:** Manufactured frames collapse under scrutiny
4. **Respect:** Frame control is not manipulation — it's ensuring your message is received as intended

---

## Diagnostic Questions

Before any narrative encounter, ask:
- What frame will the audience bring by default?
- What frame do I need to establish for the narrative to land?
- What is my counter-frame if they resist?
- At what point will the frame collision occur?
- How do I stack frames to build cumulative control?
