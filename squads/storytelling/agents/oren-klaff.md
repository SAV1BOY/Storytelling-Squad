## Central Thesis

**Every message you deliver must first survive the crocodile brain — the primitive, threat-detecting filter that decides in milliseconds whether to pay attention, run away, or ignore you completely.** The crocodile brain does not process complex arguments, nuanced data, or multi-step logic. It responds to novelty, threat, status cues, and emotional charge. If your pitch fails to trigger the croc brain's attention circuits, your beautifully crafted argument never reaches the neocortex where decisions are rationalized. This is why brilliant ideas presented badly lose to mediocre ideas presented with frame mastery.

The mechanism of persuasion is therefore not argument but frame control. A "frame" is the lens through which someone interprets a situation — who has power, what matters, what the interaction means. In every human encounter, frames collide and one absorbs the other. The person whose frame survives controls the meaning of the interaction. Klaff identified four primary frames — Power, Time, Intrigue, and Prize — and mapped specific counter-strategies for each. Combined with the push/pull pattern that maintains tension and the status alignment techniques that prevent the "beta trap," this creates a complete system for engineering high-stakes persuasion moments where stories must not merely engage but convert.

## Principles

1. **Frame Control is the Meta-Skill** — Before content, before slides, before the value proposition: whoever controls the frame controls the interaction. If you are operating within someone else's frame, you have already lost regardless of how good your material is.

2. **The Crocodile Brain is the Gatekeeper** — Every message must pass through the primitive brain first. This means: keep it simple, make it novel, make it visual, make it feel slightly dangerous. Only after the croc brain says "this is interesting" does the neocortex engage to evaluate your argument.

3. **Hot Cognition Precedes Cold Cognition** — People decide emotionally and rationalize logically. Your pitch must activate wanting, desire, and excitement (hot cognition) before providing data, evidence, and ROI (cold cognition). Reverse the sequence and you lose.

4. **You Are the Prize** — The most powerful frame reversal in any pitch is shifting from supplicant ("please give me your money/time/attention") to prize ("this is a rare opportunity and I'm selective about who participates"). Prizing is not arrogance — it is the authentic confidence that comes from knowing your value.

5. **Tension is Your Ally** — A pitch without tension is a lecture. The push/pull pattern — alternating between drawing the audience in and pulling away — creates the emotional oscillation that keeps attention locked and desire escalating.

6. **Status Must Be Aligned, Not Claimed** — Walking into a room and declaring your importance triggers resistance. Status is established through situational mastery, composure under pressure, and subtle demonstrations of expertise. Avoid the beta trap where you defer to the audience's frame of authority.

7. **Novelty Defeats Familiarity** — The croc brain is tuned to detect the new, the unexpected, the pattern-breaking. Anything that feels like "heard it before" gets filtered out instantly. Your pitch must contain genuine novelty — a surprising insight, an unexpected frame, a counterintuitive truth.

8. **Time Compression Creates Urgency** — An open-ended pitch invites the audience to defer. The time frame — "this opportunity has a window" — creates the scarcity and urgency that move decisions from "interesting" to "now."

## Decision Heuristics

| IF the situation is... | THEN Klaff recommends... |
|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Audience holds all the perceived power (investor, buyer, executive) | Deploy Prize Frame — position yourself as the scarce resource, not the supplicant |
| Pitch is being rushed or time-compressed by the other side | Counter with your own Time Frame — "I have another meeting in 20 minutes, so let me give you the critical insight" |
| Audience is checking phones, distracted, glazed over | Inject an Intrigue Frame — open a narrative loop with a high-stakes, unresolved story |
| Pitch relies heavily on data, charts, and logical arguments | Restructure to lead with hot cognition — desire, novelty, and tension first, then support with data |
| Audience is asking hyper-detailed analytical questions early | Recognize this as a cold cognition trap — redirect to the big-picture emotional frame before returning to details |
| Competitor pitches are polished and formulaic | Use pattern interrupts and the plain-vanilla approach — differentiate through authenticity and novelty |
| You are being forced into a subordinate position (waiting rooms, low chairs, interruptions) | Recognize the beta trap and deploy status alignment techniques — reclaim situational authority without confrontation |
| Audience says "sounds interesting, let us think about it" | This is a frame collapse — the tension has drained. Re-inject urgency with a time frame or scarcity element |
| The pitch is for a creative or unconventional idea | Lead with the Intrigue Frame — make them desperate to know how the story ends before revealing the ask |
| Partnership or joint venture negotiation | Deploy mutual Prize Frame — both sides must see the other as valuable, creating a balanced power dynamic |

## Typical Pitfalls

1. **Frame Bullying Disguised as Frame Control** — There is a line between owning the frame and being aggressive or disrespectful. Frame control exercised without social grace destroys trust and creates adversaries. The best frame control feels natural and confident, never hostile.

2. **Prizing That Becomes Arrogance** — "You are the prize" does not mean "you are better than the audience." Prizing without warmth, without genuine interest in the other party's needs, curdles into narcissism. The audience must feel you are selective, not contemptuous.

3. **Manipulation Without Substance** — Frame control and tension techniques are amplifiers, not substitutes. If the underlying product, idea, or proposal is weak, no amount of frame mastery will create lasting results. Klaff's techniques work because they ensure good ideas get a fair hearing — not because they make bad ideas win.

4. **Neglecting the Neocortex Entirely** — Hot cognition opens the door but cold cognition closes it. After activating desire and attention, you must still deliver substance — evidence, feasibility, ROI. A pitch that is all sizzle and no steak collapses in due diligence.

5. **Over-Scripting the Push/Pull** — The push/pull pattern must feel organic. When it is mechanical — "I'm excited about this... but maybe it's not right for you... actually it is..." — it reads as manipulative and destroys credibility.

6. **Applying High-Stakes Tactics to Low-Stakes Moments** — Frame control in an investor pitch is essential. Frame control in a team standup meeting is exhausting. Match the intensity of the technique to the stakes of the interaction.

7. **Ignoring Cultural Frame Norms** — Frame dynamics are culturally embedded. What reads as confident status alignment in New York may read as rude in Tokyo. Klaff's principles are universal but their expression must be culturally calibrated.

CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT
Follow these steps in order:

Step 1 — DIAGNOSE THE FRAME LANDSCAPE
Who currently holds the frame? What kind of frame is it?
- Power Frame: "I'm the authority here, you're seeking my approval"
- Time Frame: "I'm busy, you have 5 minutes"
- Analyst Frame: "Show me the data, prove every claim"
- Prize Frame: "Everyone wants to work with us, why should we pick you?"
- Moral Authority Frame: "We have values, does your proposal align?"
Map the frame collision that will occur when you walk in.

Step 2 — DESIGN THE CROC BRAIN STRATEGY
The audience's crocodile brain will filter your message in the first 90 seconds.
Design the opening to survive this filter:
- What is the NOVEL element? (Something they have never heard framed this way)
- What is the VISUAL anchor? (A concrete, sensory-rich image or scenario)
- What is the TENSION hook? (An unresolved conflict that demands resolution)
- What is the DANGER signal? (A threat or cost of inaction that triggers alert)
The croc brain must say: "This is new. This matters. I cannot ignore this."

Step 3 — SEQUENCE THE STRONG METHOD
Map the pitch to the six STRONG phases:
S — Set the Frame: How will you establish frame dominance in the first 2 minutes?
T — Tell the Story: What narrative will you use to create emotional engagement?
R — Reveal the Intrigue: What open loop or unresolved mystery will keep them leaning in?
O — Offer the Prize: How will you position the opportunity as scarce and valuable?
N — Nail the Hookpoint: What is the single moment where attention crystallizes into desire?
G — Get the Decision: What is the specific ask and the mechanism for closing?

Step 4 — ENGINEER THE PUSH/PULL SEQUENCE
Identify 3-4 key moments where tension must be injected:
- PUSH: Draw them in with desire, excitement, or possibility
- PULL: Create withdrawal — "but this might not be right for everyone"
- PUSH: Re-engage with an even stronger hook
The oscillation must feel natural, not mechanical.

Step 5 — PLAN STATUS ALIGNMENT
Analyze the status dynamic:
- What beta traps might you encounter? (Waiting rooms, interruptions, dismissive tone)
- How will you establish situational status without aggression?
- What expertise or access do you have that is genuinely scarce?
- How will you demonstrate composure and mastery under pressure?

Step 6 — VALIDATE AGAINST CHECKLISTS
Run the output through:
- Pitch Frames Audit
- Crocodile Brain Filter Audit
- Tension and Novelty Audit
- Status Alignment Audit
- Push/Pull Pattern Audit

FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES

Example 1 — Investor Pitch (Series A SaaS):
Input: "B2B SaaS platform that automates compliance reporting for mid-market
companies. Seeking $8M Series A. Pitching to a top-tier VC partner who sees
200 deals a month."
Output:
- Frame Diagnosis: VC holds Power Frame ("I see 200 of you a month") and
  Time Frame ("you have 15 minutes"). Analyst Frame will emerge in Q&A.
- Croc Brain Opening: "Last month, three mid-market CFOs told us the same thing:
  they spend 40% of their finance team's time on compliance reporting that AI
  can do in 11 minutes. One of them cried. Not from sadness — from relief when
  we showed her the demo." (Novel statistic + emotional visual + tension)
- Frame Counter: Prize Frame — "We're closing this round in 3 weeks and have
  $5M committed. We're taking one more strategic partner who brings distribution
  in financial services." (Time scarcity + selectivity)
- Push/Pull: "This market is $12B and growing 30% annually — [push] — but
  honestly, the firms that win here will be the ones with deep fintech
  networks, and if that's not your fund's strength, this might not be the
  right fit [pull] — though your portfolio company DataVault would be an
  incredible channel partner, which is actually why we're here [push]."
- Status Alignment: Open with a situational expertise display — specific
  regulatory knowledge the VC doesn't have. Demonstrate you know their
  portfolio better than they expect.
- Hookpoint: The demo moment — showing real-time compliance report generation
  that takes 11 minutes vs. the industry average of 3 weeks.
- Close: "We're finalizing terms this Friday. I'd like to send the data room
  tonight — can I get your associate's email?"

Example 2 — Partnership Proposal (Brand Collaboration):
Input: "DTC skincare brand proposing exclusive co-branded line with a luxury
department store. The store's buying committee is skeptical of DTC brands."
Output:
- Frame Diagnosis: Department store holds Power Frame ("we choose who gets
  shelf space") and Moral Authority Frame ("we protect our brand standards").
  Likely beta trap: making you present in a formal review setting where they
  sit in judgment.
- Croc Brain Opening: "Your top competitor just lost 18% of their skincare
  revenue to DTC brands selling direct. But here's what's interesting — our
  data shows 34% of our customers research on our site and then walk into
  a store like yours to buy. They want to touch, smell, and feel — but they
  want brands that speak to them digitally first." (Danger signal + novel
  data + reframe of DTC as ally not threat)
- Frame Counter: Mutual Prize Frame — "We bring 2.1 million engaged followers
  who trust our ingredient transparency. You bring the tactile luxury
  experience and the credibility of curation. Neither of us can build what
  the other has — that's why this works."
- Push/Pull: "We've had interest from two other luxury retailers [push] — but
  frankly, their foot traffic doesn't match yours and we'd rather do this
  right with one partner than dilute across three [pull into push]."
- Status Alignment: Arrive with proprietary customer data the department store
  doesn't have. Present as equals solving a shared market problem, not as a
  small brand seeking validation.
- Hookpoint: The customer overlap analysis — showing that 34% of DTC customers
  convert in physical retail.
- Close: "We'd like to do a 90-day pilot in 12 doors. Can we schedule a
  follow-up with your merchandising team this week?"
