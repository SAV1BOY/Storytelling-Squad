# Hall's Gap-Bridge Framework
> Every sticky story follows one structure: Normal, then Explosion (the gap), then New Normal (the bridge) — the universal pattern for narratives that create lasting change.

## Overview

Kindra Hall's Gap-Bridge Framework is a narrative structure that reduces storytelling to its most essential architecture. Every story that sticks — every story that audiences remember, retell, and act on — follows a three-part pattern: a state of normal is established, an explosion disrupts that normal and creates a gap, and a new normal is established as the bridge across that gap.

This framework is both a construction tool (how to build a story) and a diagnostic tool (how to identify why a story is not working). When a story feels flat, it is almost always because one of the three elements is missing or underdeveloped: the normal was not established (so the disruption has no contrast), the explosion was not surprising enough (so there is no gap to bridge), or the new normal was not concrete enough (so the story has no resolution).

Hall's framework is intentionally simple. It does not prescribe the number of steps, the type of protagonist, or the emotional register. It provides the minimum structural requirement that all effective stories share, regardless of context, channel, or purpose. This makes it versatile enough to apply to a 30-second testimonial, a 10-minute keynote story, or a year-long brand campaign.

## Core Methodology

### The Three Elements

**Normal (The Before)**
Normal is the established state of the world before the story begins. It is the status quo, the baseline, the way things are. Normal must be specific and concrete enough for the audience to see it, feel it, and recognize it. The more vividly the normal is established, the more powerful the disruption.

In business storytelling, Normal is:
- The customer's life before the product
- The company's trajectory before the pivot
- The market's assumptions before the disruption
- The team's culture before the change
- The individual's beliefs before the realization

Normal serves a psychological function: it gives the audience something to hold on to. Humans are pattern-recognition machines. When you establish a pattern (normal), the audience unconsciously begins predicting what comes next. This prediction-making is what makes the explosion powerful — it violates the prediction.

Critical rule: Normal must feel stable, not temporary. If the normal already contains the seeds of its own destruction, the audience will not be surprised by the explosion. Establish normal as if it could last forever. Then break it.

**Explosion (The Gap)**
The explosion is the moment when normal is shattered. Something happens that creates a gap between the old normal and whatever comes next. The gap is the space where the story lives — the space where tension, emotion, uncertainty, and change reside.

The explosion can be:
- An external event (a market crash, a customer complaint, a competitor move)
- An internal realization (a belief is proven wrong, a hidden truth is revealed)
- A decision (a risk taken, a line crossed, a commitment made)
- A collision (two normals that were running in parallel suddenly intersect)
- An absence (something expected fails to happen, and the silence is deafening)

The quality of the explosion determines the quality of the story. A predictable explosion produces a predictable story. A surprising, specific, earned explosion produces a story that audiences remember.

Characteristics of a powerful explosion:
- **Specific:** Not "things changed" but "on March 14, at 2:47 PM, the largest customer called and said three words: 'We are leaving.'"
- **Consequential:** The explosion must have real stakes. Something valuable is at risk.
- **Irreversible:** After the explosion, the old normal cannot be restored. The gap cannot be unfilled.
- **Emotional:** The explosion triggers a felt response, not just an intellectual acknowledgment.

**New Normal (The Bridge)**
The new normal is the state that emerges after the gap is bridged. It is not a return to the old normal — that is impossible after a genuine explosion. It is a fundamentally different state that incorporates the lessons, changes, and consequences of the explosion.

The new normal must be:
- **Concrete:** Show the new behavior, belief, or circumstance. Do not abstract it.
- **Earned:** The audience must feel that the new normal was achieved through effort, not luck.
- **Different:** The distance between the old normal and the new normal is the measure of the story's power. Apply Dicks' Beginning/End Rule here.
- **Stable:** Like the old normal, the new normal should feel like it could last. It is a new equilibrium, not a temporary state.

In business storytelling, New Normal is:
- The customer's life after the transformation
- The company's trajectory after the pivot succeeded
- The market's new understanding after the disruption settled
- The team's culture after the change took root
- The individual's new beliefs and behaviors

### The Gap as the Story

The most important insight in Hall's framework is that the story is not the Normal or the New Normal — the story is the Gap. The gap is where all the drama, emotion, decision-making, and transformation happen. Normal and New Normal are the bookends; the Gap is the book.

When crafting a story, spend the most time on the gap:
- What did the protagonist feel when normal shattered?
- What choices did they face?
- What did they try that failed?
- What unexpected help or insight appeared?
- What did they sacrifice?
- What was the moment when the bridge became visible?

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Establish the Normal

Write 2-3 sentences that paint the "before" state. Use sensory detail. Make it specific to a person, place, and time. Avoid generalities.

**Weak normal:** "The company was growing steadily."
**Strong normal:** "Every Monday at 9 AM, the team gathered in the same conference room, reviewed the same dashboard, and confirmed that the numbers were going up. It had been like this for eleven quarters. The meeting took twelve minutes."

The strong version establishes a pattern (same room, same dashboard, same result) that the audience will unconsciously expect to continue. This expectation is what the explosion will violate.

### Step 2: Detonate the Explosion

Write the single moment when normal breaks. Be precise about the who, what, when, and where. The explosion should land like a punch — short, specific, and irreversible.

**Weak explosion:** "Then the market changed and things got difficult."
**Strong explosion:** "On the twelfth Monday, the dashboard was red. Not a little red. Every metric. The VP of Sales closed her laptop and said, 'I think we need a different meeting.'"

### Step 3: Live in the Gap

This is the body of the story. Write the experience of being in the gap: the confusion, the attempts, the failures, the insights, the turning point. Use but/therefore structure (from Howell and Dicks) to maintain narrative tension.

**Gap narrative beats:**
- First reaction (denial, panic, determination)
- First attempt to bridge the gap (often fails)
- Complication that deepens the gap
- Unexpected insight, resource, or perspective
- Decision to try a different approach
- The moment when the bridge becomes visible
- The cost of crossing the bridge

### Step 4: Land the New Normal

Write 2-3 sentences that establish the new equilibrium. Mirror the structure of the Normal section to create a sense of resolution, but make the content distinctly different to demonstrate transformation.

**Weak new normal:** "Things improved and the company got back on track."
**Strong new normal:** "The Monday meeting still happens at 9 AM. But the dashboard is different now — half the metrics did not exist a year ago. The meeting takes forty-five minutes. And the VP of Sales has not closed her laptop since."

The mirroring (Monday meeting, 9 AM, dashboard, VP of Sales) creates a satisfying structural echo, while the differences (different metrics, longer meeting, open laptop) demonstrate genuine transformation.

### Step 5: Verify the Structure

Check each element:
- **Normal test:** Could this state have continued indefinitely if the explosion had not happened?
- **Explosion test:** Is this moment specific, surprising, and irreversible?
- **Gap test:** Does the gap contain genuine struggle, choices, and stakes?
- **New Normal test:** Is the new state fundamentally different from the old state? Is the difference concrete and demonstrable?
- **Bridge test:** Does the audience understand how the protagonist got from the old normal to the new one?

## Examples

### Example 1: Customer Testimonial for a Project Management Tool

**Normal:** "Our agency ran on email, spreadsheets, and the creative director's memory. Projects shipped on time. Clients were happy. We had 12 people and it worked."

**Explosion:** "Then we won the Meridian account — our first Fortune 500 client. On the first day of the engagement, they sent us a Gantt chart with 340 tasks, 47 dependencies, and a deadline that assumed we had a system. We did not have a system."

**Gap:** The agency tried to manage Meridian using their existing email-and-spreadsheet approach. Within two weeks, three deliverables were late. The creative director forgot a client review meeting. A junior designer worked on the wrong version of a file for three days. The founder stayed up until 2 AM manually reconciling project status across six email threads and realized the agency was about to lose its biggest client before the first invoice.

She researched project management tools over a weekend, chose [Product], and spent the following Monday migrating every active project. The team resisted. The creative director called it "corporate bureaucracy." But within a month, the resistance faded — not because the tool was perfect, but because nobody forgot a meeting again.

**New Normal:** "We still have 12 people. We now manage three Fortune 500 accounts. The creative director runs the Monday status meeting using the same dashboard the client sees. Last quarter, Meridian's CMO told us we were the most organized agency they had ever worked with. The creative director smiled and said nothing."

### Example 2: Brand Repositioning Story for an Annual Report

**Normal:** "For twenty years, we were a printing company. We printed brochures, business cards, annual reports, and the occasional banner. Our clients called us when they needed ink on paper. We answered. It was a good business."

**Explosion:** "In 2022, our three largest clients — representing 40% of revenue — moved their annual reports to digital-only within the same quarter. Not because of cost. Because their audiences had stopped reading paper. Our sales pipeline did not decline gradually. It fell off a cliff."

**Gap:** The company spent six months in denial, then six months in panic. The CEO considered selling. The head of production proposed buying cheaper presses to compete on price. A junior account manager — who had been with the company for eight months — presented a different idea at an all-hands meeting: the company's real expertise was not printing. It was visual communication. Printing was one medium. The company could serve its clients across any visual medium if it was willing to learn.

The CEO gave the account manager a budget and 90 days. She hired two digital designers, partnered with a web development firm, and went back to the three clients who had left. She said: "We are not a printing company anymore. We are a visual communication partner. Let us show you what that means."

Two of the three clients came back.

**New Normal:** "We are still a visual communication company. We still have presses — they run three days a week instead of six. But 60% of our revenue now comes from digital and experiential design. The junior account manager is now the VP of Client Services. And last year, for the first time in company history, a client asked us to design something we had never done before. We said yes before we knew how."

## When to Use

- Structuring any narrative that involves transformation: testimonials, case studies, brand stories, presentations
- Diagnosing why an existing story feels flat (identify which element is missing)
- Simplifying a complex narrative into its essential structure
- Teaching storytelling to non-storytellers who need the simplest possible framework
- Video testimonial scripts where clear structure is essential for production
- Sales enablement content that needs a clear before/during/after arc
- Proposal writing where the client's current state, challenge, and future state must be mapped

## When NOT to Use

- When you need to choose which story to tell (use Story Selection Matrix first)
- When you need to identify the 4 types of stories your brand needs (use Stories That Stick)
- When you need a daily practice for finding stories (use Homework for Life)
- When the narrative does not involve transformation (some content is informational, and that is fine)
- When the story is more about a moment of realization than a journey through disruption (use Storyworthy, which centers the 5-second moment)

## Integration

- **Hall's Stories That Stick:** Each of the 4 story types can be structured using Gap-Bridge. The framework provides the architecture; the story types provide the strategic purpose.
- **Hall's Story Formula:** The formula (Character + Emotion + Moment) ensures the Gap-Bridge structure is populated with the right human elements.
- **Hall's Story Selection Matrix:** Use the matrix to choose which story to tell, then use Gap-Bridge to structure it.
- **Dicks' Beginning/End Rule:** The Normal and New Normal sections should be as opposite as possible, directly applying Dicks' rule.
- **Dicks' Storyworthy:** The explosion in Gap-Bridge often coincides with or leads to the 5-second moment in Storyworthy. Use Storyworthy to find the emotional core; use Gap-Bridge to provide the structural frame.
- **Howell's ABT Framework:** Gap-Bridge maps directly to ABT. Normal + AND, Explosion + BUT, New Normal + THEREFORE. Use ABT to compress a Gap-Bridge story into a single sentence.
- **Howell's Story Cycle:** Gap-Bridge is the compressed version of the 10-step cycle. Normal = Steps 1-3, Explosion = Steps 4-5, Gap = Steps 6-7, New Normal = Steps 8-10.
