# Video Script Narrative Framework
> Design video narratives with hook, promise, delivery, and CTA beats mapped to retention curve optimization.

## Overview
Video is the most demanding storytelling format. Every second is a decision point where the viewer can leave. This framework provides a beat-by-beat narrative structure for video content — from 15-second ads to 60-minute long-form content — designed to maximize retention and drive action. The core insight is that video narrative is not linear storytelling; it is retention curve design. Every beat exists to earn the viewer's attention for the next beat. The framework maps story beats to the four critical segments of any video: hook (stop the scroll), promise (create commitment), delivery (fulfill the promise), and CTA (convert attention to action).

## Core Methodology
The Video Script Narrative Framework operates on four sequential beats, each aligned with viewer psychology:

1. **Hook (0-3 seconds)** — Stop the scroll. Interrupt the viewer's default behavior with pattern disruption: a surprising visual, a provocative statement, a recognizable emotion, or a question that demands an answer. The hook earns the next 7 seconds.

2. **Promise (3-10 seconds)** — Create a contract with the viewer. Explicitly or implicitly state what they will get by continuing to watch. The promise earns the next 2-5 minutes.

3. **Delivery (Bulk of video)** — Fulfill the promise through story, education, demonstration, or entertainment. The delivery must exceed the promise. Structure delivery using story beats that create micro-hooks every 30-60 seconds.

4. **CTA (Final 5-15 seconds)** — Convert the viewer's attention and goodwill into a specific action. The CTA must feel like the natural conclusion of the narrative, not an interruption.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Define the Video's Purpose and Format
- Determine the platform: YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn, website, ads
- Determine the length: short-form (15-60s), medium (2-10m), long-form (10-60m)
- Determine the goal: awareness (views), engagement (comments/shares), conversion (clicks/signups)
- Determine the viewer's context: feed scrolling, intentional search, embedded on a page, pre-roll ad
- Each platform and length has different retention curve expectations — design accordingly

### Step 2: Craft the Hook (0-3 Seconds)
- **Visual hook**: An unexpected image, a dramatic before/after, a face expressing strong emotion
- **Verbal hook**: A provocative claim, a relatable frustration, a question. "This one mistake costs most startups $50K."
- **Pattern interrupt**: Something that violates the visual or auditory norms of the platform. On TikTok, this might be unusual framing. On YouTube, it might be jumping mid-sentence into the most interesting part.
- **Identity hook**: Speak directly to the target audience. "If you run an e-commerce brand doing $1-5M, this is for you."
- Test multiple hooks for the same video. The hook has the highest leverage on total performance.
- Never begin with a logo animation, intro music, or "Hey guys." Start with the most compelling moment.

### Step 3: Design the Promise (3-10 Seconds)
- Explicitly state what the viewer will learn, see, or experience
- Frame the promise as a transformation: "By the end of this video, you will know exactly how to..."
- Include a credibility marker: "After working with 200+ brands..." or "This strategy generated $2M in..."
- The promise must be specific. "Marketing tips" is not a promise. "Three ad hooks that doubled our ROAS" is a promise.
- For short-form video, the hook and promise merge into the first 3-5 seconds

### Step 4: Structure the Delivery
- **For short-form (15-60s)**: One insight, one proof point, one CTA. No fat. Every word earns its place.
- **For medium-form (2-10m)**: 3-5 story beats, each with its own mini-hook. Use open loops: "The third technique is the one that changes everything — but first, let me show you the foundation."
- **For long-form (10-60m)**: Full narrative arc with 7-12 story beats. Include "retention resets" — moments of humor, surprise, or viewer interaction that re-engage attention.
- Build in "curiosity gaps" every 30-60 seconds: tease upcoming content to prevent drop-off
- Use visual variety to maintain attention: change camera angle, add B-roll, use graphics at transition points
- Each beat should advance the narrative AND deliver standalone value

### Step 5: Design Retention Mechanics
- **Open loops**: Introduce a question or tease early that you resolve later. "I will show you the exact template in a moment, but first..."
- **Progress indicators**: "We are on step 2 of 5" or visual progress bars. Viewers stay longer when they know where they are.
- **Pattern changes**: Shift energy, pacing, or visual style every 60-90 seconds for short-form, every 3-5 minutes for long-form
- **Social proof drops**: Insert a brief testimonial, result, or credibility marker at predicted drop-off points
- **Preview of payoff**: "The case study I am about to show you is the most dramatic result we have ever seen." This buys 60-90 seconds of attention.

### Step 6: Write the CTA
- **Direct CTA**: "Click the link below to start your free trial." Clear, specific, immediate.
- **Soft CTA**: "If this was helpful, subscribe — I post videos like this every Tuesday." Relationship-building.
- **Story CTA**: "Sarah watched this video six months ago. Today, her agency does $200K/month. Your story could be next." Narrative resolution.
- Place the primary CTA at the natural story conclusion, not as an appendix
- For YouTube: include a verbal CTA at 70% of the video (for viewers who leave early) and at 100%
- For ads: the CTA IS the destination. The entire video is a funnel to this moment.

## Examples

### Example 1: YouTube Educational Video (8 Minutes) — Marketing Agency
- **Hook (0-3s)**: "This client was about to fire us. Then we did something that changed their entire business." [Visual: tense meeting room]
- **Promise (3-10s)**: "I am going to show you the exact 3-step framework we used to turn a failing campaign into $1.2M in revenue in 90 days. And I will give you the template."
- **Delivery Beat 1 (10s-2m)**: The story — what went wrong, why the client was frustrated, what we discovered in the data
- **Delivery Beat 2 (2m-4m)**: Step 1 of the framework, explained with the client's real data (anonymized), before/after
- **Delivery Beat 3 (4m-6m)**: Steps 2-3, with a screen share of the actual template
- **Delivery Beat 4 (6m-7m)**: The results — $1.2M revenue, client reaction quote, what we learned
- **CTA (7m-8m)**: "Download the template in the description. If you want us to build this for you, book a free strategy call."

### Example 2: TikTok/Reels Short-Form (30 Seconds) — E-commerce Brand
- **Hook (0-2s)**: "Stop scrolling if you have bought skincare that did nothing." [Direct eye contact, relatable frustration]
- **Promise (2-5s)**: "I wasted $2,000 on products before I learned this one thing from a dermatologist."
- **Delivery (5-22s)**: The insight: "Most skincare fails because of application order, not product quality. Here is the exact order that changed my skin in 2 weeks." [Quick visual demonstration]
- **CTA (22-30s)**: "Our starter kit comes with a numbered routine card so you never get the order wrong. Link in bio."

## When to Use
- YouTube content creation (educational, promotional, brand)
- Social media video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)
- Video ads (Facebook, YouTube pre-roll, connected TV)
- Product demonstrations and walkthroughs
- Testimonial videos and customer story videos
- Sales videos and VSLs (video sales letters)

## When NOT to Use
- When the content is purely informational and does not require narrative structure (screen recordings, tutorials)
- When the audience will watch regardless of retention design (internal training, compliance videos)
- When audio/video production quality is too low to execute the script — a great script with bad production underperforms a simple script with good production
- When the message is too complex for video and would be better served by written content
