# The Miller Marketing Roadmap
> A five-step implementation sequence that deploys StoryBrand messaging through the entire marketing funnel — from one-liner to referral engine.

## Overview

The Marketing Roadmap is Miller's answer to the question every business asks after completing their BrandScript: "Now what?" Having a clear brand message is necessary but not sufficient. The message must be deployed systematically across every customer touchpoint — and in a specific sequence.

Most businesses make the mistake of investing in tactics (social media, paid ads, SEO) before building the foundational assets that make those tactics work. They run Facebook ads that link to a confusing website. They build an email list but have no nurturing sequence. They get referrals by accident rather than by design.

The Marketing Roadmap prescribes five steps, in order, each building on the previous:

1. **Create your One-Liner** — Clarify the message
2. **Build your website** — Create the conversion hub
3. **Create a lead generator** — Capture interested prospects
4. **Build a nurturing email sequence** — Develop trust over time
5. **Build a referral system** — Turn customers into advocates

Miller insists on this sequence because each step is a prerequisite for the next. A website without a clear One-Liner will be confusing. A lead generator without a functioning website has nowhere to send traffic. An email sequence without leads has no audience. A referral system without satisfied customers who went through the funnel has no advocates.

This is not a marketing strategy in the traditional sense — it is a marketing *infrastructure*. Once these five assets exist, every future tactic (content marketing, paid ads, social media, partnerships, events) becomes dramatically more effective because the underlying system catches and converts attention.

## Core Methodology

### The Funnel Logic

The five steps map to a customer journey:

```
AWARENESS ──> INTEREST ──> TRUST ──> PURCHASE ──> ADVOCACY

Step 1: One-Liner ──────────────────────────────────────────>
Step 2: Website ──────> Landing + Conversion
Step 3: Lead Generator ──────> Email Capture
Step 4: Nurture Sequence ──────> Trust Building
Step 5: Referral System ──────────────────────> Advocacy Loop
```

Each step addresses a different psychological need:
- **One-Liner:** "Do I understand what this company does?" (Clarity)
- **Website:** "Is this relevant to me? Can I trust them?" (Relevance + Credibility)
- **Lead Generator:** "Is this worth my email address?" (Value exchange)
- **Nurture Sequence:** "Do they understand me? Are they competent?" (Relationship)
- **Referral System:** "Would I recommend this? Is there a reason to?" (Advocacy)

### The Compounding Effect

These five assets create a compounding system. Each new piece of content, each ad, each social media post feeds into the existing infrastructure. Without the infrastructure, marketing efforts are isolated events. With it, every effort compounds.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Create Your One-Liner

**What it is:** A ~30-word statement following the Problem + Solution + Result formula (see `miller-one-liner.md` for deep methodology).

**Why it comes first:** The One-Liner forces the most extreme clarity. If you cannot compress your BrandScript into 30 words, your messaging is not clear enough. The One-Liner becomes the "seed" that grows into every other asset.

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Complete BrandScript (see `miller-brand-script.md`)
- [ ] Draft 10+ versions of the One-Liner
- [ ] Test with 5+ people outside your industry
- [ ] Select the final version
- [ ] Deploy to all team email signatures
- [ ] Deploy to social media bios
- [ ] Deploy to voicemail greetings
- [ ] Print on business cards
- [ ] Brief every team member so they can deliver it verbally

**Common mistakes:**
- Writing a tagline instead of a One-Liner (taglines are clever; One-Liners are clear)
- Skipping the problem and leading with the solution
- Using jargon or internal language
- Making it too long (over 40 words)
- Not testing it with real humans

### Step 2: Build a Wireframed Website

**What it is:** A website structured around the SB7 Framework, not around what the business wants to say. The homepage follows a specific section order designed to mirror the story arc and guide the visitor toward action.

**The StoryBrand Website Wireframe (section order):**

**Section 1 — The Header (Above the Fold)**
- A clear headline that passes the Grunt Test (see `miller-grunt-test.md`)
- A sub-headline that elaborates on the value
- A prominent call-to-action button (Direct CTA)
- An image showing the aspirational result (happy customer, not the product)
- Optional: Transitional CTA as a secondary link

**Section 2 — The Stakes**
- Identify the problem the customer faces
- Agitate the internal and philosophical problem
- 2-3 sentences maximum
- This section creates the emotional need for a solution

**Section 3 — The Value Proposition**
- What value do you provide?
- How does life improve with your product/service?
- Use bullet points or a grid showing before/after
- Focus on outcomes, not features

**Section 4 — The Guide**
- Show empathy: "We understand..."
- Show authority: testimonials, logos, statistics, awards
- Brief brand story if relevant (but keep the customer as the hero)

**Section 5 — The Plan**
- Display your 3-step process plan
- Name each step
- Add a brief description under each
- End with the Direct CTA again

**Section 6 — The Explanatory Paragraph**
- A longer paragraph (or short section) that provides more detail for analytical visitors
- Still written in BrandScript language
- Addresses common objections indirectly

**Section 7 — Failure and Success**
- Briefly hint at what is at stake (failure)
- Paint a vivid picture of the success
- Use the Before/After Grid from the BrandScript

**Section 8 — The Final CTA**
- Repeat the Direct CTA
- Repeat the Transitional CTA
- This is the "bottom of page" catch — many visitors scroll to the bottom before acting

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Wireframe the homepage using the section order above
- [ ] Write all copy from the BrandScript (do not invent new messaging)
- [ ] Apply the Grunt Test — show the wireframe to 5 people for 5 seconds each
- [ ] Ensure the Direct CTA appears at least 3 times on the page
- [ ] Ensure the Transitional CTA appears at least 2 times
- [ ] Optimize for mobile (most visitors are on phones)
- [ ] Add a simple navigation bar: your product/service, how it works, testimonials, CTA button

**Common mistakes:**
- Leading with the company story instead of the customer's problem
- Hiding the CTA below the fold
- Using carousel sliders (they dilute the message)
- Showing product features instead of customer outcomes
- Having more than 7 navigation items
- Using stock photos that feel generic

### Step 3: Create a Lead Generator

**What it is:** A free resource offered in exchange for an email address. This is your Transitional CTA made tangible. The lead generator captures people who are interested but not ready to buy.

**Effective lead generator formats:**
- **PDF Guide** — "The 5 Mistakes [Customer Type] Make With [Topic]"
- **Checklist** — "The Complete [Process] Checklist"
- **Video Series** — "3 Videos That Show You How to [Outcome]"
- **Webinar** — "How to [Achieve Outcome] Without [Pain]"
- **Quiz** — "What's Your [Topic] Score?" (provides personalized results)
- **Template** — "The [Process] Template We Use With Every Client"
- **Free Chapter or Sample** — Works well for courses, books, and premium content

**Rules for effective lead generators:**
1. **Solve a real problem** — The lead generator should provide genuine value, not just tease the paid product
2. **Be specific** — "The Ultimate Marketing Guide" is vague. "The 7-Email Welcome Sequence Template for E-Commerce Brands" is specific.
3. **Deliver quick wins** — The prospect should be able to implement something immediately after consuming it
4. **Demonstrate competence** — If the free resource is excellent, they will trust that the paid product is exceptional
5. **Naturally lead to the paid product** — The lead generator solves part of the problem; your product solves the whole thing

**The lead generator landing page structure:**
- Headline: What they will get (outcome-focused)
- 3-4 bullet points: Specific benefits of the resource
- Image: Mockup of the resource (PDF cover, video thumbnail)
- Email capture form: Name + Email (keep it minimal)
- Privacy statement: "We'll never share your email. Unsubscribe anytime."

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Choose the format based on your audience's preferences
- [ ] Write a specific, outcome-focused title
- [ ] Create the resource (aim for high quality — this is your first impression)
- [ ] Build a landing page following the structure above
- [ ] Set up email automation to deliver the resource immediately
- [ ] Add the lead generator CTA to your website (header, sidebar, pop-up, footer)
- [ ] Test the full flow: click CTA → land on page → submit email → receive resource

### Step 4: Build a Nurturing Email Sequence

**What it is:** An automated email sequence that delivers value, builds trust, and moves subscribers toward a purchase decision. This is not a newsletter — it is a strategic sequence with a defined arc.

**The Nurture Sequence Structure (Miller's recommendation):**

**Email 1: Deliver the Lead Generator**
- Subject: "Here's your [resource name]"
- Deliver the promised resource immediately
- Brief introduction to who you are (one paragraph)
- No hard sell

**Email 2: The Problem (sent 2-3 days later)**
- Subject: Address the core problem
- Agitate the problem with a story or example
- Show empathy — demonstrate you understand
- End with a soft mention of your solution

**Email 3: Social Proof (sent 2-3 days later)**
- Subject: Feature a customer success story
- Tell a specific customer's before/after story
- Include concrete results (numbers, quotes)
- Position the customer as the hero, your brand as the guide

**Email 4: Value and Education (sent 2-3 days later)**
- Subject: Teach something useful
- Provide a genuinely valuable tip or insight related to their problem
- Build authority by demonstrating expertise
- Mention your product as one of many resources

**Email 5: Overcoming Objections (sent 2-3 days later)**
- Subject: Address the most common reason people do not buy
- Name the objection directly ("You might be thinking...")
- Address it with evidence, story, or reframing
- Include a soft CTA

**Email 6: The Direct Ask (sent 2-3 days later)**
- Subject: Clear and direct offer
- Summarize: the problem, your solution, the result
- Include a strong Direct CTA
- Add urgency if genuine (limited availability, time-limited offer)
- Include a P.S. with the Transitional CTA for those not ready

**Ongoing: Weekly or Biweekly Nurture Emails**
After the initial sequence, continue with regular emails that alternate between:
- Value-driven content (tips, insights, how-tos)
- Customer stories and testimonials
- Soft CTAs back to the product

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Write the 6-email sequence
- [ ] Set up automation triggers (subscribe → email 1 immediately, email 2 at day 3, etc.)
- [ ] Write subject lines that promise value (not clickbait)
- [ ] Include one clear CTA per email (not three)
- [ ] Test email deliverability (check spam scores)
- [ ] Set up tracking for open rates and click-through rates
- [ ] Plan ongoing weekly/biweekly content

### Step 5: Build a Referral System

**What it is:** A deliberate system that turns satisfied customers into active advocates who refer new business. Most referrals happen by accident — this step makes them happen by design.

**Why most businesses do not have referral systems:**
They assume happy customers will refer naturally. Some will. Most will not — not because they are not satisfied, but because they have never been asked, given a reason, or provided a mechanism.

**Miller's referral system components:**

**1. Identify Existing Advocates**
- Survey customers with one question: "How likely are you to recommend us to a colleague?" (NPS)
- Segment 9-10 scorers as advocates
- Personally thank them and invite them into the referral program

**2. Create a Referral Incentive**
Options:
- Discount on future purchases for both referrer and referee
- Free month of service
- Exclusive content or access
- Gift cards or credits
- Charitable donation in their name
- Recognition (featured case study, social media shoutout)

The incentive does not need to be large. Often, the act of being asked and given a simple mechanism is enough.

**3. Make Referral Frictionless**
- Provide a unique referral link or code
- Create shareable content (email templates, social posts) they can forward
- Build a dedicated referral landing page for referred leads
- Automate the tracking and reward fulfillment

**4. Systematize the Ask**
Referral asks should be triggered by positive moments:
- After a successful project completion
- After a positive customer service interaction
- After a renewal or repeat purchase
- After they leave a positive review
- In the email sequence (email 8-10, after the initial nurture)

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Survey existing customers to identify advocates
- [ ] Design a referral incentive
- [ ] Build a referral landing page
- [ ] Create shareable referral assets
- [ ] Set up automation for referral tracking and rewards
- [ ] Train customer-facing team to ask for referrals at positive moments
- [ ] Measure: referrals generated, conversion rate, revenue attributed to referrals

## Examples

### Example 1: Online Course Business — Full Roadmap Implementation

**Step 1 — One-Liner:** "Most aspiring freelancers waste months guessing how to land clients. Our 8-week program gives them a proven client acquisition system — so they replace their salary within 6 months."

**Step 2 — Website:** Homepage opens with "Stop guessing. Start landing clients." + CTA "Enroll Now." Sections follow the SB7 wireframe: the struggle of freelancing without a system, the program as the guide, the 3-phase plan (Foundation → Outreach → Scale), student testimonials, failure stakes ("Another year of inconsistent income"), success vision ("A full client roster and the freedom you went freelance for").

**Step 3 — Lead Generator:** "The Freelancer's First Client Checklist — 15 Steps to Landing a Paying Client This Month" (PDF). Landing page with email capture.

**Step 4 — Nurture Sequence:** 6 emails over 2 weeks. Email 1 delivers checklist. Email 2 tells the story of a freelancer who struggled for a year before finding a system. Email 3 features a student who landed 3 clients in her first month. Email 4 teaches one cold outreach tactic. Email 5 addresses "but I'm not experienced enough" objection. Email 6 presents enrollment offer with limited-time bonus.

**Step 5 — Referral System:** After course completion, graduates receive: unique referral link giving their friend $200 off + the referrer gets access to an exclusive alumni mastermind for every 2 referrals.

### Example 2: Local Accounting Firm — Full Roadmap Implementation

**Step 1 — One-Liner:** "Most small business owners dread tax season because they're never sure if they're doing it right. We handle everything from bookkeeping to filing, so they save money and sleep at night."

**Step 2 — Website:** Header: "Tax Season Doesn't Have to Be Stressful." Plan section: "1. Send us access to your books. 2. We clean up and optimize. 3. We file on time, every time." Authority: "Serving 400+ small businesses in [City]. Average client saves $4,200/year."

**Step 3 — Lead Generator:** "The Small Business Tax Deduction Checklist — Are You Missing Any of These 27 Write-Offs?" (PDF). Promoted on website and social media.

**Step 4 — Nurture Sequence:** Email 1 delivers checklist. Email 2 describes the anxiety of DIY tax filing. Email 3 features a client who discovered $8,000 in missed deductions. Email 4 explains one commonly missed deduction in detail. Email 5 addresses "but I can't afford an accountant" objection with ROI math. Email 6 offers free 30-minute tax review.

**Step 5 — Referral System:** After tax season, clients receive a handwritten thank-you card with two referral cards. Each card offers the referred business a free tax health check. For every referral who becomes a client, the referrer receives a $100 credit toward next year's fees.

## When to Use

- **When the BrandScript is complete and you need to activate it** — this is the natural next step
- **When marketing feels scattered** — the Roadmap provides a clear, sequential plan
- **When you are spending on tactics without infrastructure** — stop, build the 5 assets, then resume tactics
- **When onboarding a new marketing hire or agency** — the Roadmap gives them a checklist
- **Annual or quarterly planning** — audit which of the 5 steps are strong and which need work

## When NOT to Use

- **Before the BrandScript exists** — the Roadmap deploys the BrandScript; without one, you are deploying confusion
- **For brand awareness campaigns** — the Roadmap is a conversion infrastructure, not a brand awareness play
- **For product-led growth (PLG) businesses** — PLG companies may need a modified funnel where the product itself replaces steps 3-4
- **When the product is not yet validated** — building a full marketing funnel for a product nobody wants is a waste. Validate first.

## Integration

The Marketing Roadmap is the implementation layer of the Miller system:

- **Deploys** the BrandScript (`miller-brand-script.md`) across all channels
- **Starts with** the One-Liner (`miller-one-liner.md`) as Step 1
- **Structures the website using** the SB7 Framework (`miller-storybrand-sb7.md`)
- **Validates each step with** the Grunt Test (`miller-grunt-test.md`)

The Roadmap also connects to Duarte frameworks at Step 3 (lead generators) and Step 4 (nurture sequences). A webinar lead generator can use the Sparkline structure (`duarte-resonate-sparkline.md`). A video in the nurture sequence can use a S.T.A.R. Moment (`duarte-star-moment.md`).

## Agent Routing

**Route TO this framework when:**
- A user has their messaging figured out and asks "what do I do with it?"
- A user asks about website structure, lead magnets, email sequences, or referral programs
- A user says their marketing is not working despite having a clear message
- A user needs a marketing plan or funnel strategy

**Route AWAY from this framework when:**
- The user does not yet have clear messaging (route to `miller-storybrand-sb7.md` or `miller-brand-script.md`)
- The user needs a presentation, not a marketing funnel (route to Duarte frameworks)
- The user needs to audit existing messaging (route to `miller-grunt-test.md`)
- The user only needs an elevator pitch (route to `miller-one-liner.md`)
