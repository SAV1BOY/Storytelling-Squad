# Coyne's Controlling Idea
> The single thematic sentence that every scene in a story must argue for or against, serving as the narrative's philosophical spine and the ultimate measure of story coherence.

## Overview

Shawn Coyne adopted and refined Robert McKee's concept of the "controlling idea"—a one-sentence statement that expresses the story's ultimate meaning, proven by the climactic action. The controlling idea is not a theme (too vague), not a moral (too prescriptive), and not a logline (too plot-focused). It is the philosophical argument the story makes through its events.

The controlling idea follows a strict formula: **[Value] is achieved/lost when [cause].** For example: "Justice prevails when the protagonist sacrifices personal safety to expose the truth." Or its negative form: "Tyranny reigns when good people remain silent out of fear."

For marketers, the controlling idea is the most powerful alignment tool available. When every piece of content argues for (or dramatizes the consequences of violating) a single controlling idea, the brand develops philosophical coherence that transcends individual campaigns. The controlling idea is the DNA strand from Harmon's Embryo Theory made explicit and actionable.

## Core Methodology

The controlling idea operates on three levels:

1. **Prescriptive (for new content):** Define the controlling idea before writing. Every scene, section, or touchpoint must either advance the positive case for the idea or dramatize the consequences of its opposite. Content that does neither is cut.

2. **Diagnostic (for existing content):** Extract the controlling idea from the climax. What value is at stake in the final scene? What cause led to that value being achieved or lost? If the extracted idea doesn't match the brand's intended message, the content has drifted.

3. **Strategic (for brand architecture):** Define a master controlling idea for the brand that all campaign-level controlling ideas nest within. The brand's controlling idea is the philosophical ground truth; campaign ideas are specific expressions of it.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Value at Stake
Every genre has a primary value spectrum. Coyne's system maps these precisely:
- **Action:** Life / Death
- **Thriller:** Life / Damnation (fate worse than death)
- **Love:** Love / Hate (or Connection / Isolation)
- **Performance:** Respect / Shame
- **Status:** Success / Failure (social standing)
- **Worldview:** Meaning / Meaninglessness (or Sophistication / Naivete)
- **Morality:** Selflessness / Selfishness (Good / Evil)
- **Society:** Freedom / Subjugation

For marketing, the most common values are: Performance (Respect/Shame), Status (Success/Failure), Worldview (Understanding/Ignorance), and Love (Connection/Isolation).

Identify which value your story's climax resolves. That is your primary value.

### Step 2: Determine Positive or Negative Charge
Does the story end on the positive or negative pole of the value?
- **Positive (prescriptive):** "Respect is earned when..." — the story proves that the positive value is achievable under certain conditions.
- **Negative (cautionary):** "Shame results when..." — the story warns that the negative value is the consequence of certain behaviors.

Most marketing content uses positive controlling ideas. But negative forms are powerful for awareness campaigns, competitive positioning, and cautionary content ("Here's what happens when you ignore this").

### Step 3: Identify the Cause
The cause is the "when" clause—the specific condition that produces the value outcome. This is where the controlling idea becomes unique and arguable. Generic causes produce generic stories. Specific, counterintuitive causes produce compelling ones.

**Generic:** "Success is achieved when you work hard." (True but boring—no one argues against it.)
**Specific:** "Success is achieved when you publicly embrace your team's failures as learning investments." (Arguable, specific, actionable.)

The cause should be:
- **Specific** enough to be actionable
- **Counterintuitive** enough to be interesting
- **True** enough to be proven by evidence
- **Arguable** enough that someone could disagree

### Step 4: Validate Against the Climax
The controlling idea must be proven by the story's climactic moment—not stated in exposition, not argued in dialogue, but demonstrated through action and consequence. Ask: "Does the climax dramatize this exact idea?"

For a case study with the controlling idea "Market leadership is achieved when a company prioritizes customer education over short-term revenue," the climax must show the company choosing education over revenue AND achieving market leadership as a result. If the climax shows something else proving the point, the controlling idea doesn't match.

### Step 5: Test Every Scene Against the Idea
For each scene or content section, ask one of two questions:
- "Does this scene argue FOR the controlling idea?" (Shows the positive value being achieved through the specified cause)
- "Does this scene argue AGAINST the controlling idea?" (Shows the negative value resulting from violating the specified cause)

Scenes that do neither are thematically disconnected. They may be entertaining or informative in isolation, but they weaken the narrative's coherence.

### Step 6: Define the Counter-Controlling Idea
Every strong controlling idea has an equally compelling opposite. Define it explicitly. This becomes the antagonist's philosophy, the cautionary tale, or the "what if we're wrong" that gives the story intellectual honesty.

**Controlling Idea:** "Trust is built when brands show vulnerability alongside expertise."
**Counter-Controlling Idea:** "Trust is destroyed when brands confuse vulnerability with weakness, undermining their authority."

The best content engages with its counter-idea rather than ignoring it. This creates the dialectical tension that makes narratives feel mature and honest.

## Examples

### Example 1: Enterprise Software Brand
**Master Controlling Idea:** "Operational excellence is achieved when teams replace heroic individual effort with systematic collaboration."
**Counter-Idea:** "Burnout and failure result when organizations reward individual heroics instead of building systems."

**Campaign Applications:**
- Case study: "Revenue growth is sustained when sales teams share intelligence systematically rather than hoarding contacts." (Nests within master idea.)
- Thought leadership: "Innovation accelerates when cross-functional teams have shared visibility into each other's work." (Nests within master idea.)
- Product launch: "Deadlines are met when project dependencies are visible to everyone, not trapped in one manager's head." (Nests within master idea.)

Every piece of content argues for systematic collaboration over individual heroism. The brand develops a philosophical identity beyond its product features.

### Example 2: Health and Wellness Brand
**Master Controlling Idea:** "Lasting health is achieved when people pursue consistency over intensity."
**Counter-Idea:** "Injury and burnout result when people chase dramatic transformations through extreme effort."

**Campaign Applications:**
- Social content: Before/after posts that celebrate 12-month gradual transformations, not 30-day crash programs
- Email sequence: Each email demonstrates one small, sustainable habit rather than a complete lifestyle overhaul
- Product positioning: Supplements framed as "daily foundations" not "miracle boosters"

The controlling idea dictates not just what the brand says but what it refuses to say. This brand will never run a "get ripped in 30 days" campaign because it violates the controlling idea.

## When to Use

- Brand strategy development (defining the philosophical core)
- Campaign brief writing (the controlling idea IS the brief's north star)
- Content audits (extracting controlling ideas from existing content reveals drift and inconsistency)
- Editorial decision-making (does this piece argue for our controlling idea? If not, why are we creating it?)
- Competitive differentiation (your controlling idea should be different from competitors')
- Internal alignment (the controlling idea gives every team member a decision-making heuristic)

## When NOT to Use

- Purely transactional content (order confirmations, shipping updates)
- When the brand hasn't yet defined its value proposition (do positioning work first)
- Content that is intentionally exploratory or questioning rather than argumentative
- When the audience expects objectivity (journalism, academic research)
- Very early stage ideation where premature commitment to a controlling idea constrains creativity

## Integration

The Controlling Idea integrates with:
- **Coyne's Story Grid** — The controlling idea is what the Grid evaluates every scene against
- **Coyne's Genre Clover** — The external genre determines the value spectrum from which the controlling idea is drawn
- **Coyne's Five Commandments** — The crisis in every scene should force a choice that relates to the controlling idea
- **Harmon's Embryo Theory** — The controlling idea IS the narrative DNA that must be present in every cell
- **Miller's StoryBrand SB7** — StoryBrand's "philosophical failure" maps to the counter-controlling idea
- **Ganz's Values and Action** — Ganz's values identification process complements the controlling idea's value selection

## Agent Routing

Route to Controlling Idea when:
- The user needs to define the philosophical core of a brand or campaign
- Content feels scattered and needs a unifying thesis
- The user asks about theme, message, or "what are we really saying?"
- An editorial audit reveals inconsistent messaging across touchpoints
- The user mentions McKee's or Coyne's controlling idea concept
- Brand strategy requires articulating a unique philosophical position

Do NOT route here when:
- The user needs structural execution (route to Story Grid, Save the Cat, or Hero's Journey)
- The focus is on character identity (route to Campbell Archetypes)
- The user needs presentation mechanics (route to Duarte)
- The task is pitch or persuasion mechanics (route to Klaff)
- The user needs to select which stories to tell (route to Hall Story Selection Matrix)
