# The Grunt Test
> The ultimate clarity diagnostic: show your website to a caveman for 5 seconds and they should be able to grunt what you offer, how it helps, and what to do next.

## Overview

The Grunt Test is Donald Miller's simplest and most ruthless framework. It cuts through every debate about design, copy, and branding with one question: **If a caveman looked at your website (or any marketing piece) for five seconds, could they grunt the answers to three questions?**

1. **What do you offer?**
2. **How will it make my life better?**
3. **What do I need to do to buy it?**

If the answer to any of these is "no" or "I'm not sure," the messaging fails. It does not matter how beautiful the design is, how clever the copy is, or how much money was spent. Confused people do not buy. They leave.

The Grunt Test is named provocatively for a reason. It is not testing whether a sophisticated consumer understands your value proposition after careful reading. It is testing whether a distracted, impatient, slightly annoyed human being — which is what every website visitor is — can grasp the essentials in the time it takes to decide whether to stay or bounce.

The average website visitor makes a stay-or-leave decision in 3-8 seconds. In those seconds, their brain is not reading — it is scanning for survival cues: "Is this relevant to me? Can this help me? What do I do?" The Grunt Test ensures your messaging answers all three before the visitor's thumb hits the back button.

Miller positions the Grunt Test as the diagnostic that validates whether the SB7 Framework and BrandScript have been applied correctly. You can have a brilliant BrandScript, but if it does not translate into clear, scannable, actionable messaging on the page, it has failed.

## Core Methodology

### The Three Questions

**Question 1: What do you offer?**

This is the most basic and most frequently failed test. The visitor should be able to identify in 5 seconds:
- What category of product or service this is
- Who it is for
- What it does

**Passing examples:**
- "Project management software for marketing teams"
- "Meal kits delivered to your door"
- "Leadership coaching for new executives"

**Failing examples:**
- "Empowering tomorrow's innovators" (What does that mean?)
- "Synergistic solutions for the modern enterprise" (Jargon)
- "Welcome to [Company Name]" (This tells me nothing)
- A homepage with a giant hero image and no text above the fold

The number one reason websites fail Question 1 is that the company uses their name or a clever tagline as the header instead of a clear statement of what they offer. "Acme Solutions" tells the visitor nothing. "Accounting software that files your taxes automatically" tells them everything they need.

**Question 2: How will it make my life better?**

Once the visitor knows what you offer, they need to know why they should care. This is the value proposition — stated in terms of the customer's life, not the product's features.

**Passing examples:**
- "Save 10 hours a week on project management"
- "Healthy dinners in 20 minutes, no planning required"
- "Lead with confidence within 90 days"

**Failing examples:**
- "AI-powered, cloud-native, enterprise-grade" (Features, not benefits)
- "We're passionate about what we do" (Nobody cares about your passion)
- "Industry-leading solution" (What does "industry-leading" mean to my life?)

The key distinction is **features vs. outcomes**. Features describe the product. Outcomes describe the customer's transformed life. The Grunt Test demands outcomes.

**Question 3: What do I need to do to buy it?**

The call to action must be obvious, prominent, and unambiguous. The visitor should not have to search for how to take the next step.

**Passing examples:**
- A bright, contrasting "Start Free Trial" button above the fold
- "Schedule a Call" button in the top-right navigation
- "Buy Now" button directly below the product description

**Failing examples:**
- CTA buried below three screens of scrolling
- Ambiguous button text: "Learn More" (learn more about what?)
- Multiple competing CTAs of equal prominence (which one do I click?)
- No CTA visible without scrolling

### The 5-Second Rule

The Grunt Test is specifically a 5-second test. This is not arbitrary — it reflects actual browsing behavior:

- **Seconds 0-2:** The brain registers the visual hierarchy. What is the biggest text? Where is the eye drawn?
- **Seconds 2-4:** The brain processes the headline and subheadline. It forms a preliminary judgment: relevant or irrelevant.
- **Seconds 4-5:** The brain looks for an action to take or a reason to scroll. If neither is clear, the visitor leaves.

Everything that matters must be communicated in this window. Content below the fold, paragraphs of explanation, and secondary pages are all irrelevant to the Grunt Test because most visitors never reach them.

## Detailed Steps

### How to Administer the Grunt Test

**Step 1: Choose Your Test Subjects**

Select 5-10 people who are NOT familiar with your business. They should represent a range:
- People in your target market (ideal)
- People completely outside your industry (also useful — they test pure clarity)
- A mix of ages and technical comfort levels

Do NOT test with team members, investors, or friends who already know what you do. Their prior knowledge contaminates the test.

**Step 2: Prepare the Test**

For a website:
- Open the homepage on a laptop or phone (test both)
- Have the test subject look away while you load the page
- Tell them: "I'm going to show you a website for 5 seconds. After 5 seconds, I'll close it. Then I'll ask you three questions."

For a print piece (brochure, flyer, business card):
- Hold the piece face-down
- Flip it over for 5 seconds, then flip it back

For a video or ad:
- Play the first 5 seconds only, then pause

**Step 3: Show for 5 Seconds**

Exactly 5 seconds. Use a timer. Do not let them linger. Do not explain anything. Do not point at anything.

**Step 4: Ask the Three Questions**

Close or hide the material, then ask:
1. "What does this company offer?"
2. "How would it make your life better?"
3. "What would you do if you wanted to buy it?"

Record their exact words. Do not prompt, help, or clarify. If they say "I'm not sure," that is a data point. If they get it wrong, that is a data point. If they nail it, that is validation.

**Step 5: Score**

For each question, score:
- **Pass (3 points):** They got it right, quickly and confidently
- **Partial (2 points):** They got the gist but were uncertain or vague
- **Fail (1 point):** They were wrong, confused, or could not answer

A perfect score is 9/9 across all three questions for a single test subject. Average across all test subjects.

- **7-9:** Your messaging passes. Minor refinements may help.
- **5-6:** Your messaging partially works. Identify which question(s) fail and rewrite.
- **3-4:** Your messaging fails. Return to the BrandScript and restructure the page.

**Step 6: Diagnose and Fix**

For each failed question, trace back to the root cause:

| Failed Question | Likely Root Cause | Fix |
|----------------|------------------|-----|
| What do you offer? | Header is vague, clever, or uses jargon | Rewrite the headline to state clearly what you sell and who it's for |
| How will it help me? | Copy focuses on features, company story, or process | Add a sub-headline or bullet points stating specific outcomes |
| What do I do to buy? | CTA is not visible, not prominent, or uses weak language | Add a high-contrast CTA button above the fold with direct language |

### Beyond the Website: Applying the Grunt Test Everywhere

The Grunt Test applies to any piece of marketing communication:

**Email subject lines + preview text:**
- Can the recipient grunt what the email is about in 5 seconds?
- Does the subject line + preview text pass all three questions?

**Social media profiles:**
- Your LinkedIn headline, Instagram bio, or Twitter bio
- 5-second scan: Do they know what you do, how it helps, and how to engage?

**Sales decks and pitch presentations:**
- The first slide (title slide) should pass the Grunt Test on its own
- If someone only sees slide 1, do they know what you do, why it matters, and what you are asking for?

**Business cards:**
- Beyond name and title, does the card communicate what you offer and how to engage?

**Trade show booths:**
- From 10 feet away, walking past at a brisk pace, can someone grunt the three answers?

**Video thumbnails and first frames:**
- Before someone clicks play, does the thumbnail pass the Grunt Test?

**App store listings:**
- The icon, title, and first line of description — 5 seconds of scanning in a list of apps

## Examples

### Example 1: SaaS Website — Before and After

**BEFORE (Fails Grunt Test):**

Header: "Welcome to Nimbus"
Sub-header: "Transforming the way teams collaborate"
Hero image: Abstract blue gradient with floating geometric shapes
CTA: Small gray "Learn More" link in the navigation

Grunt Test Results:
- Q1 (What do you offer?): "Some kind of tech company? Collaboration software maybe?" — PARTIAL
- Q2 (How will it help me?): "I have no idea." — FAIL
- Q3 (What do I do to buy?): "I'd have to look around... maybe click 'Learn More'?" — FAIL
- Score: 4/9

**AFTER (Passes Grunt Test):**

Header: "Project Management for Marketing Teams"
Sub-header: "Plan, execute, and deliver campaigns — on time, every time. No more missed deadlines."
Hero image: Screenshot of the product showing a marketing campaign dashboard
CTA: Large green button "Start Your Free Trial" + smaller link "Watch 2-Minute Demo"

Grunt Test Results:
- Q1: "It's project management software for marketing teams." — PASS
- Q2: "It helps me deliver campaigns on time and not miss deadlines." — PASS
- Q3: "I'd click the green button to start a free trial." — PASS
- Score: 9/9

### Example 2: Professional Services — Before and After

**BEFORE (Fails Grunt Test):**

Header: "Elevate Your Potential"
Sub-header: "Johnson & Associates — Excellence. Integrity. Results."
Hero image: City skyline at sunset
CTA: "Contact Us" buried in the footer

Grunt Test Results:
- Q1: "A consulting firm? Law firm? I honestly can't tell." — FAIL
- Q2: "Something about potential? No clue." — FAIL
- Q3: "I'd scroll down and look for a phone number maybe." — FAIL
- Score: 3/9

**AFTER (Passes Grunt Test):**

Header: "Financial Planning for Business Owners Who Want to Retire on Their Own Terms"

Hero image: Photo of a relaxed business owner on a boat (aspirational result)

Grunt Test Results:
- Q1: "Financial planning for business owners." — PASS
- Q2: "They help me retire well and stop worrying about money." — PASS
- Q3: "I'd click the button to schedule a review." — PASS
- Score: 9/9

### Common Grunt Test Failures by Industry

**Tech startups:** Over-index on innovation language ("disrupting," "reimagining," "next-gen") that sounds impressive but communicates nothing specific.

**Professional services (law, accounting, consulting):** Default to credentialism ("Founded in 1987. Partners from Big 4 firms.") without stating what they do or how it helps the client.

**E-commerce:** Prioritize brand aesthetic over clarity. Beautiful photography, minimalist design, but no clear statement of what they sell or why it is better.

**Nonprofits:** Lead with the organization's mission and history instead of the donor's or volunteer's role and impact.

**B2B enterprise:** Drown the homepage in jargon, acronyms, and feature matrices that only existing customers understand.

## When to Use

- **After building or redesigning a website** — the Grunt Test is the final quality check before launch
- **Before any major marketing spend** — ensure the landing page passes before driving traffic to it
- **Quarterly messaging audits** — run the Grunt Test on your top 5 most-visited pages
- **When conversion rates are low** — the first diagnostic should always be the Grunt Test
- **When onboarding new team members** — have them Grunt Test the website on day one for a fresh perspective
- **When evaluating agency or freelancer work** — apply the Grunt Test to any creative deliverable before approving it
- **When reviewing competitors** — Grunt Test competitor websites to identify their clarity gaps (and your opportunities)

## When NOT to Use

- **For deep content pages** (blog posts, documentation, knowledge bases) — these are for people who have already passed the initial clarity threshold
- **For brand advertising meant to build awareness over time** — some brand campaigns intentionally trade clarity for curiosity or emotional resonance
- **For artistic or creative portfolios** — a photographer's website communicates through imagery, not headlines
- **For audiences who already know you** — existing customers returning to your site for support or account management do not need the Grunt Test experience
- **As the ONLY diagnostic** — the Grunt Test measures clarity but not persuasion, trust, or emotional resonance. A page can pass the Grunt Test and still fail to convert if it lacks social proof, addresses the wrong problem, or has a weak offer.

## Integration

The Grunt Test is the validation layer across all Miller frameworks:

- **Validates** the SB7 Framework (`miller-storybrand-sb7.md`) — if SB7 has been applied correctly, the Grunt Test passes
- **Validates** the BrandScript (`miller-brand-script.md`) — if BrandScript messaging is on the page, the Grunt Test passes
- **Validates** the One-Liner (`miller-one-liner.md`) — a strong One-Liner in the header usually guarantees Q1 and Q2 pass
- **Validates** the Marketing Roadmap (`miller-marketing-roadmap.md`) Step 2 (website) — the Grunt Test is the acceptance criteria for the website wireframe

The Grunt Test also applies to Duarte framework outputs. A presentation's opening slide should pass the Grunt Test. The first 5 seconds of a presentation should answer: "What is this about? Why should I care? What are you asking me to do?"

## Agent Routing

**Route TO this framework when:**
- A user asks to review or audit their website, landing page, or any marketing piece
- A user says their conversion rate is low or people "don't get" what they do
- A user has built messaging and wants to validate it
- A user shares a URL and asks "what do you think?"
- A user wants to evaluate competitor messaging

**Route AWAY from this framework when:**
- The user needs to CREATE messaging (route to `miller-storybrand-sb7.md` or `miller-brand-script.md`)
- The user needs to implement messaging (route to `miller-marketing-roadmap.md`)
- The user needs a presentation structure (route to Duarte frameworks)
- The user needs a quick pitch (route to `miller-one-liner.md`)
