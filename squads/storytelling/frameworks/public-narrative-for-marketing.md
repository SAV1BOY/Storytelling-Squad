# Public Narrative for Marketing
> Marshall Ganz's Self/Us/Now framework adapted for commercial marketing — connecting personal story, community identity, and urgent action.

## Overview

Marshall Ganz developed the Public Narrative framework at Harvard for organizing and social movements. It structures persuasion into three nested stories: the Story of Self (why I am called to act), the Story of Us (why we as a community share this calling), and the Story of Now (why we must act immediately). Together, they create a complete persuasive arc from individual motivation to collective urgency.

This framework adapts Ganz's model for commercial marketing. In the original context, the "self" is an organizer and the "us" is a movement. In marketing, the "self" is the brand (or founder), the "us" is the community of customers and believers, and the "now" is the market moment that demands action.

The adaptation works because great marketing and great organizing share the same challenge: motivating people to move from passive awareness to active participation. Both require emotional connection (Self), shared identity (Us), and urgency (Now).

This framework is particularly effective for mission-driven brands, community-based businesses, social enterprises, and any brand where the product is inseparable from the values behind it.

## Core Methodology

**The Three Stories:**

**Story of Self:** Why does this brand exist? What personal experience, frustration, or conviction drove its creation? This is the origin story, told not as corporate history but as a values statement made vivid through specific narrative moments.

**Story of Us:** Who are we — the brand and its customers — as a community? What do we believe? What do we stand for? What makes us different from the mainstream? This is the tribal identity story that makes customers feel they belong to something larger than a transaction.

**Story of Now:** Why must we act now? What is the challenge, opportunity, or moment that makes inaction unacceptable? This is the urgency story that converts belief into behavior.

**The Nesting Principle:**
The three stories are not sequential speeches — they nest inside each other. The Story of Self establishes credibility that makes the Story of Us believable. The Story of Us establishes community that makes the Story of Now powerful. And the Story of Now gives the other two stories purpose.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Craft the Story of Self

**For the brand/founder:**
- What moment made the founder (or founding team) realize this needed to exist?
- What personal experience of the problem created the conviction to solve it?
- What values drove the decision, and how were those values formed?

The Story of Self is not a resume. It is a choice point — a moment where someone could have done nothing and chose to act instead. That choice reveals values more powerfully than any values statement.

**Template structure:**
1. The moment of encounter (I saw/experienced/discovered...)
2. The emotional response (I felt...)
3. The choice (I decided to...)
4. The values revealed (Because I believe...)

**Marketing example:** "I spent seven years in corporate wellness, watching companies spend millions on programs their employees ignored. The breaking point was a Tuesday afternoon in 2019 — I watched a VP present 'employee wellness results' to a board while, two floors down, half the team was eating lunch at their desks because they were afraid to take a real break. I quit that Friday. Not because I gave up on wellness — because I realized the entire model was backwards. We were designing programs for companies, not for people."

### Step 2: Craft the Story of Us

**For the brand community:**
- What shared experience unites your customers?
- What common frustration, aspiration, or belief defines the group?
- What makes this community different from the mainstream or from competitors' customers?
- What language, rituals, or symbols does this community share?

The Story of Us turns individual customers into a collective. It answers "am I the only one who thinks this?" with "no — we all do, and here's what we're doing about it."

**Template structure:**
1. The shared experience (We have all...)
2. The shared frustration or aspiration (We are tired of... / We believe...)
3. The emerging identity (We are the ones who...)
4. The collective strength (Together, we...)

**Marketing example:** "If you are reading this, you probably have something in common with our 12,000 members. You believe wellness is not a perk — it is a right. You are tired of wellness programs that are really productivity programs in disguise. You think people should be trusted to know what they need. You are not anti-work. You are anti-pretending that a meditation app makes up for a broken culture. That is who we are. And that clarity is our strength."

### Step 3: Craft the Story of Now

**For the call to action:**
- What is the current moment that makes action urgent?
- What is at stake if the audience does not act?
- What specific, concrete action can the audience take right now?
- What will that action lead to?

The Story of Now transforms shared identity into shared action. It creates a choice: act now, or accept the status quo. The urgency must be genuine — manufactured urgency (fake countdown timers, artificial scarcity) violates the trust that Self and Us have built.

**Template structure:**
1. The moment (Right now...)
2. The stakes (If we don't... / What's at risk is...)
3. The choice (We can either... or...)
4. The action (Here is what to do, specifically)
5. The vision (When enough of us do this...)

**Marketing example:** "Right now, 73% of employees say their company's wellness program does not address their actual needs. That is not a statistic — it is 73 out of every 100 people spending eight hours a day in an environment that claims to care about them but demonstrably does not. This quarter, we are launching the Open Wellness Standard — a free framework any company can adopt to redesign their wellness program around what employees actually want. We need 500 companies to pilot it by June. If your company is one of them, the people on your team will know you actually listened. Join the pilot. It takes 10 minutes to register and zero budget to start."

### Step 4: Weave the Three Stories Together
In practice, Self/Us/Now are interwoven, not delivered in strict sequence:
- A landing page might lead with Us (community identification), embed Self (founder credibility), and close with Now (CTA).
- A keynote might open with Self, build through Us, and climax with Now.
- An email sequence might dedicate one email to each, building across three touchpoints.

The key is that all three are present and that each strengthens the others.

### Step 5: Adapt to Format

**Landing page:** Self (brief founder moment in the "about" section) → Us (hero section speaks to the community identity) → Now (CTA section creates urgency)

**Brand video:** Self (founder on camera, 30-40 seconds) → Us (community montage with shared language, 30-40 seconds) → Now (the current initiative, 20-30 seconds) → CTA

**Email sequence:** Email 1: Self (origin story, no sell) → Email 2: Us (community stories, social proof) → Email 3: Now (the offer, the urgency, the action)

**Social campaign:** Each post emphasizes one story, with the campaign arc moving from Self to Us to Now across posts.

## Examples

### Example 1: DTC Sustainable Fashion Brand

**Self:** "Our founder grew up in a textile manufacturing town in Bangladesh. She saw firsthand what 'fast fashion' costs the people who make the clothes. She moved to New York, worked in fashion for a decade, and spent every day complicit in the system she grew up watching. In 2020, she stopped being complicit."

**Us:** "You are part of a growing community of people who believe your closet should not come at someone else's expense. You are not anti-fashion — you are pro-honesty. You want to know who made your clothes, what they were paid, and whether the fabric will outlast the trend. There are 40,000 of us now. And the industry is starting to notice."

**Now:** "This month, we are publishing our full supply chain — every factory, every wage, every material source — in a public, searchable database. We are the first brand in our category to do this. We are asking every brand you buy from to do the same. Share the database. Tag the brands. Ask the question: 'Who made my clothes?' The more of us who ask, the harder it becomes to stay silent."

### Example 2: B2B Data Privacy Platform

**Self:** "Our CTO spent 15 years building data systems for enterprises. She was good at it. She was also increasingly uncomfortable with what she was building — systems designed to collect everything and delete nothing. The final straw was a project where she was asked to build a user profiling system for a children's education platform. She declined the project and started building the opposite."

**Us:** "We serve companies that believe data privacy is not a compliance checkbox — it is a competitive advantage. Our customers are the CTOs and CPOs who read the regulation and think 'finally' instead of 'here we go again.' There are 800 of us now, across 14 countries, and we are proving that privacy-first companies outperform surveillance-first companies."

**Now:** "New regulation takes effect in Q4. Most companies will scramble. Ours will be ready. If you want to be ready too, join our Privacy-Ready cohort starting April 1. Twelve weeks, live implementation support, and a community of peers who are solving the same problems. 200 spots. Registration closes March 25."

## When to Use

- Mission-driven brands where values are central to the brand proposition
- Community-building campaigns that need to galvanize collective identity
- Launch campaigns that need to create urgency around a specific initiative
- Fundraising and social enterprise marketing
- Brand manifestos and positioning work
- Employer branding where company values attract talent
- Any marketing where the audience cares about why the brand exists, not just what it sells

## When NOT to Use

- Purely transactional products where values are irrelevant to purchase decisions
- When the brand does not genuinely have a values-driven origin (the Story of Self will ring false)
- When urgency is manufactured rather than genuine (Now must be real)
- Quick conversion-focused ads where there is no room for the full Self/Us/Now arc
- When the brand's values are controversial in a way that would alienate rather than attract the target audience

## Integration

- **Brand Story Framework** — Self/Us/Now is a lens through which to organize the brand story
- **Founder Story Framework** — the Story of Self is an extended founder story
- **Universal Story Archetypes** — Self often follows Rebirth; Us follows Quest; Now follows Overcoming the Monster
- **Emotional Arc Mapping** — Self creates empathy, Us creates belonging, Now creates urgency — a designed emotional arc
- **Content Marketing Story Framework** — Self/Us/Now can structure an entire content program (early content = Self, mid = Us, mature = Now)
- **Hiring/Employer Brand Story** — the three stories adapt directly for recruitment marketing

## Agent Routing

| Signal in brief/prompt | Route to |
|---|---|
| "Mission-driven brand" or "values-based marketing" | Public Narrative for Marketing as primary |
| "Community building" or "movement marketing" | Public Narrative (emphasis on Story of Us) |
| "Brand manifesto" or "brand positioning" | Public Narrative + Brand Story Framework |
| "Fundraising" or "social enterprise" | Public Narrative as primary structure |
| "Why we exist" or "brand purpose" | Public Narrative (Story of Self) |

**Routing logic:** This framework activates when the brand's values, mission, or community identity are central to the marketing objective. It provides the macro narrative structure, while other frameworks (format, technique) handle execution details. It is most powerful when all three stories are present; if only one is relevant, route to the more specific framework (Founder Story for Self alone, Community/Social for Us alone).
