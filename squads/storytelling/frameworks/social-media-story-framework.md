# Social Media Story Framework
> Craft platform-specific micro-stories, narrative threads, carousel stories, and short-form video stories for social media.

## Overview
Social media is where stories go to be compressed, remixed, and shared. The attention constraints are extreme — you have 1-3 seconds to earn a pause and 10-30 seconds to deliver a narrative. But the opportunity is equally extreme: a great micro-story can reach millions and create more brand impact than a polished campaign. This framework provides platform-specific storytelling structures for text threads, carousels, short-form video, and single-post micro-stories. The core principle is that social stories are not shorter versions of long stories — they are a fundamentally different narrative form optimized for interruption, emotion, and shareability.

## Core Methodology
Social storytelling operates on four format types, each with distinct narrative mechanics:

1. **Micro-Stories** — Complete narratives in a single post (1-3 paragraphs or 280 characters). These rely on compression: every word carries narrative weight. The structure is setup-tension-payoff with no room for filler.

2. **Narrative Threads** — Multi-post stories (Twitter/X threads, LinkedIn series) that unfold across 5-15 connected posts. Each post must work alone AND advance the arc. The thread format creates built-in cliffhangers.

3. **Carousel Stories** — Visual narratives across 5-10 slides (Instagram, LinkedIn). Each slide is a story beat with text and image working together. Carousel stories combine the serial tension of threads with visual impact.

4. **Short-Form Video Stories** — 15-60 second video narratives (TikTok, Reels, Shorts). These compress the full video narrative arc into seconds using visual storytelling, voiceover, and text overlay.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Choose the Format Based on Platform and Goal
- **Twitter/X**: Micro-stories for engagement, threads for authority and reach
- **LinkedIn**: Micro-stories for engagement, carousels for lead generation, threads for thought leadership
- **Instagram**: Carousels for saves and shares, Reels for reach, Stories for intimacy
- **TikTok**: Short-form video for reach and brand awareness
- **Facebook**: Micro-stories and video for engagement within communities
- Match format to goal: reach (video, threads), engagement (micro-stories), saves/shares (carousels), conversion (carousels with CTA)

### Step 2: Craft Micro-Stories
- **Structure**: One sentence of context. One sentence of tension. One sentence of resolution. Optional: one sentence of reflection.
- **Opening line**: Must stop the scroll. Use specificity, emotion, or surprise. "I got fired on a Tuesday" beats "I once lost a job."
- **Compression technique**: Remove everything that is not setup, tension, or payoff. No throat-clearing, no qualifiers, no "I think that maybe."
- **Emotional anchor**: Every micro-story needs one specific emotional detail. Not "I was nervous" but "My hands were shaking so hard I put them under the table."
- **Payoff options**: Insight ("Here is what I learned"), reversal ("Turns out, that was the best thing that happened"), invitation ("Has this ever happened to you?")
- Target length: 150-280 characters for Twitter/X, 200-600 words for LinkedIn

### Step 3: Build Narrative Threads
- **Post 1 — The Hook**: The most compelling moment from the story. Not the beginning — the most interesting part. "I was 48 hours from shutting down my company when I got the email that changed everything."
- **Posts 2-4 — The Setup**: Go back and provide context. Who, what, when, why. Build the world.
- **Posts 5-8 — The Conflict**: The problem, the stakes, the failed attempts, the escalating tension.
- **Posts 9-11 — The Turn**: The insight, the decision, the action taken.
- **Posts 12-14 — The Resolution**: What happened. Results, lessons, transformation.
- **Final Post — The Takeaway**: What the reader should learn or do. CTA if applicable.
- Each post must end with a reason to read the next: question, tease, incomplete thought
- Number your posts ("1/12") to signal commitment and encourage follows through the full thread

### Step 4: Design Carousel Stories
- **Slide 1 — Cover**: Bold statement or question that earns the swipe. "The pricing mistake that cost us $200K"
- **Slide 2 — Context**: Brief setup for the story or framework
- **Slides 3-7 — Core Content**: One beat per slide. For stories: one event per slide. For frameworks: one step per slide.
- **Slide 8-9 — Proof or Examples**: Results, testimonials, before/after
- **Slide 10 — CTA**: Clear next step. "Save this for later" or "Follow for more" or "Link in bio"
- Design rules: large readable text, consistent visual style, each slide scannable in 3 seconds
- Visual storytelling: use images, diagrams, or charts that reinforce the narrative on each slide
- Carousel arcs work because the swipe mechanic creates micro-commitment — each swipe deepens investment

### Step 5: Script Short-Form Video Stories
- Apply the Video Script Narrative Framework in compressed form
- **0-1s**: Visual or verbal hook that stops the scroll
- **1-5s**: Setup — who, what, why this matters
- **5-20s**: Story — the conflict, the journey, the turn
- **20-25s**: Payoff — the result, the insight, the lesson
- **25-30s**: CTA — follow, save, visit link
- Use text overlays for silent viewers (85% of social video is watched without sound)
- Trending audio can serve as a hook if relevant to the story
- Native, unpolished footage often outperforms produced content on short-form platforms

### Step 6: Build a Story Content System
- Create a story bank: collect micro-moments, customer quotes, team anecdotes, founder memories
- Categorize stories by theme: origin, customer win, lesson learned, behind-the-scenes, contrarian insight
- Repurpose across formats: one customer story becomes a micro-story (LinkedIn), a thread (Twitter/X), a carousel (Instagram), and a Reel (TikTok)
- Establish posting cadence: stories should appear at least 3x per week across platforms
- Track which story types and formats generate the highest engagement per platform

## Examples

### Example 1: LinkedIn Micro-Story — Agency Founder
"Two years ago, I fired my biggest client. They were 40% of our revenue. My team thought I was insane. But that client called at midnight. Sent 47 revision requests on a single deliverable. Made my best designer cry in a meeting. The month after we let them go, we signed three new clients who respected our process. Revenue went up 20%. Sometimes the best growth strategy is subtraction."

### Example 2: Twitter/X Thread — SaaS Product Story
"Thread: How a single customer support ticket led to a feature that now drives 30% of our revenue. (1/8)"
"A customer named David submitted a ticket in March 2023. He was not complaining. He was asking: 'Is there a way to see all my data in one view?' (2/8)"
"We did not have that feature. But David's question nagged at us. We pulled the data and found that 340 other customers had asked similar questions in the past 6 months. (3/8)"
[Thread continues through the decision to build, the MVP, David's reaction, the broader rollout, and the revenue impact]

### Example 3: Instagram Carousel — DTC Brand
- Slide 1: "I spent $12,000 on skincare in 3 years. Here is what actually worked."
- Slide 2: "First, what DID NOT work: the 10-step routines, the trendy ingredients, the celebrity brands."
- Slides 3-7: One lesson per slide with personal before/after context
- Slide 8: "The 3 products that actually changed my skin (and yes, ours is one of them)"
- Slide 9: Customer results photos with quotes
- Slide 10: "Save this carousel. Follow for weekly skin science. Link in bio for our starter kit."

## When to Use
- Daily social media content creation
- Building audience and brand presence on social platforms
- Repurposing longer content (case studies, blogs, webinars) for social distribution
- Community engagement and conversation starting
- Product launches and campaign support on social channels

## When NOT to Use
- When the story requires more depth than the format allows (use blog or long-form video)
- When the audience is not active on social platforms
- When the content is sensitive and could be misinterpreted without full context
- When the brand voice has not been established and social experimentation could create inconsistency
