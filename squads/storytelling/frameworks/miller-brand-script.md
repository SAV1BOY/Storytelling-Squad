# The BrandScript
> The foundational messaging document that maps all seven SB7 elements in detail, serving as the single source of truth for every piece of marketing a brand produces.

## Overview

The BrandScript is the fully expanded version of the StoryBrand SB7 Framework. While the SB7 provides the structure and the One-Liner provides the compression, the BrandScript is the complete document — the "bible" of your brand messaging. Every email, webpage, social post, sales script, and advertisement should be traceable back to the BrandScript.

Think of the BrandScript as a screenplay for your brand's relationship with the customer. It defines the character (customer), the conflict (their problem at three levels), the guide (your brand's positioning), the plan (how you reduce risk), the call to action (what you ask them to do), the stakes (what happens if they do not act), and the resolution (the life they will live after choosing you).

Most companies operate without a BrandScript, which means every team member, agency, and freelancer invents messaging from scratch. The result is inconsistency: the website says one thing, sales says another, social media says a third, and the customer receives a fragmented, confusing signal. The BrandScript eliminates this by giving everyone the same foundational story to draw from.

A complete BrandScript typically takes 2-4 hours to draft, involves key stakeholders (founders, sales leads, customer-facing team members), and should be revisited quarterly as the business evolves.

## Core Methodology

The BrandScript is organized into seven sections corresponding to the seven SB7 elements. Each section contains specific prompts, fields, and decision frameworks that force clarity.

### The BrandScript Document Structure

```
BRANDSCRIPT FOR: [Company Name]
DATE: [Date Created]
VERSION: [Version Number]
OWNER: [Who maintains this document]

1. CHARACTER
2. PROBLEM (External, Internal, Philosophical)
3. GUIDE (Empathy + Authority)
4. PLAN (Process Plan + Agreement Plan)
5. CALL TO ACTION (Direct + Transitional)
6. FAILURE (Stakes)
7. SUCCESS (Transformation)

APPENDIX: One-Liner, Before/After Grid, Key Phrases
```

### The BrandScript Workshop Process

Miller recommends creating the BrandScript in a facilitated session with 3-7 key stakeholders. The process:

1. **Pre-work (1 week before):** Each participant interviews 3 customers and collects verbatim quotes about their problems, frustrations, and desired outcomes. Bring these quotes to the workshop.
2. **Session 1 — Character and Problem (90 minutes):** Define the customer persona and map the three levels of problem using real customer language.
3. **Session 2 — Guide, Plan, CTA (90 minutes):** Position the brand as guide, design the plan, and choose CTAs.
4. **Session 3 — Failure and Success (60 minutes):** Define stakes and paint the success vision.
5. **Synthesis (post-session):** One person assembles the final document, derives the One-Liner, and builds the Before/After Grid.

## Detailed Steps

### Section 1: Character Profile

The character is your customer. Not a demographic profile — a *narrative* profile. You need to understand what they want at a primal level.

**Fields to complete:**

- **Who is the character?** Write a one-sentence description. ("A mid-career marketing director at a B2B SaaS company with a team of 5-12 people.")
- **What do they want as it relates to your brand?** One specific desire. ("They want their team to produce more campaigns without adding headcount.")
- **What survival category does this desire map to?** (Financial resources, time, social networks, status, resources, generosity, meaning)
- **What is the story question?** Every story creates a question in the audience's mind: "Will the hero get what they want?" Define yours. ("Will this marketing director be able to scale output without burning out the team?")

**Critical rule:** You may serve multiple customer types, but each BrandScript focuses on ONE character. If you have three distinct customer types, you create three BrandScripts.

**Verbatim customer quotes to include:**
Collect 3-5 direct quotes from real customers that express the desire. These quotes become raw material for all messaging.

### Section 2: Problem Mapping

Map the problem at three levels. This is the most important section of the BrandScript because it is where emotional resonance is built.

**External Problem:**
- What is the tangible, visible problem the customer faces?
- How would they describe it to a coworker?
- What is the villain causing this problem? (Every external problem has a villain — a root cause that can be named. "Manual processes." "Outdated tools." "Information overload.")

**Internal Problem:**
- How does the external problem make them *feel*?
- What emotion do they experience most frequently because of this problem?
- Common internal problem emotions: frustration, overwhelm, embarrassment, anxiety, inadequacy, guilt, confusion, exhaustion
- Write the internal problem as an "I" statement from the customer's perspective: "I feel like I'm always behind and my team is losing confidence in me."

**Philosophical Problem:**
- Why is this problem *wrong*? What "should" or "ought" statement applies?
- Frame it as a moral or justice issue: "Teams that work this hard deserve tools that work just as hard for them."
- The philosophical problem gives customers permission to act — choosing your product is not just practical, it is *right*.

**The Villain:**
- Name the root cause of all three problems. This is the antagonist of the story.
- The villain should be a force, not a person. "Complexity." "Outdated legacy systems." "The myth that more hours equals more output."
- The villain should be relatable — your customer should nod and say "yes, THAT is the problem."

### Section 3: Guide Positioning

Your brand enters the story here. Complete two subsections:

**Empathy Statements (write 3-5):**
- "We understand what it's like to..."
- "We've seen firsthand how [problem] affects..."
- "You shouldn't have to choose between [painful tradeoff]..."
- "We built [product] because we were tired of [the same problem]..."

Pick the strongest 1-2 for primary messaging.

**Authority Proof Points:**
- **Testimonials:** Collect 5-10 customer quotes that demonstrate transformation. Rank by specificity — "We increased conversions by 34%" beats "Great product!"
- **Statistics:** What numbers prove your competence? Users served, success rates, years in business, customer satisfaction scores.
- **Awards and Recognition:** Industry awards, media mentions, certifications, partnerships.
- **Credentials:** Team expertise, proprietary methodology, patents, research.

**Guide Persona:**
- In one sentence, describe the "personality" of your brand as a guide. Are you the wise mentor (calm, experienced), the energetic coach (motivating, action-oriented), or the fellow traveler (relatable, peer-level)?
- This persona should inform tone of voice across all messaging.

### Section 4: The Plan

Design both plan types:

**Process Plan (required):**
Create a 3-step (maximum 4-step) plan that shows the customer exactly how to engage.

Rules:
- Each step should start with a verb
- Each step should be achievable and non-threatening
- The first step should be extremely low-friction
- Name the steps — named steps feel more official and trustworthy

Example:
1. **Discover** — Schedule a free 15-minute assessment call
2. **Design** — Receive a custom strategy tailored to your goals
3. **Deliver** — Launch your first campaign within 30 days

**Agreement Plan (recommended):**
List 3-5 commitments your brand makes to reduce perceived risk.

Example:
- "We'll never lock you into a long-term contract."
- "Every deliverable goes through two rounds of revision."
- "If we miss a deadline, you don't pay for that month."
- "Your data stays yours — we'll delete everything if you leave."

### Section 5: Calls to Action

**Direct CTA:**
- What is the single most important action you want the customer to take?
- Write the exact button text or verbal ask
- Where will this CTA appear? (Website, emails, sales calls, ads)
- Miller's rule: repeat the direct CTA at least 3 times on every webpage

**Transitional CTA:**
- What value will you offer in exchange for an email address?
- What format? (PDF guide, video series, quiz, webinar, template, checklist)
- What is the title? (The title should promise a specific outcome)
- How does the transitional CTA connect to the direct CTA? (The lead generator should naturally lead to wanting the paid product)

### Section 6: Failure / Stakes

**Failure scenarios (write 3-5):**
Describe specific negative outcomes if the customer does NOT act.

- What will they continue to experience?
- What will they miss out on?
- What will get worse over time?
- What is the cost of inaction — in money, time, opportunity, or emotion?

**Select 1-2 for primary messaging.** These should be specific and credible, not hyperbolic.

**The "what's at stake" statement:**
Write one sentence that captures the risk of inaction. This sentence will appear on sales pages, in email sequences, and in sales conversations.

### Section 7: Success / Transformation

**Success outcomes (write 5-10):**
Describe specific positive outcomes after the customer acts.

- What will their day look like?
- What will others notice about them?
- How will they feel?
- What numbers will change?
- What will they stop doing?
- What will they start doing?

**The Before/After Grid:**

| Category | BEFORE Your Product | AFTER Your Product |
|----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| They have... | [current painful state] | [improved state] |
| They feel... | [negative emotion] | [positive emotion] |
| Their day looks like... | [frustrating routine] | [improved routine] |
| Their status is... | [current perception] | [elevated perception] |
| They spend time on... | [wasteful activity] | [valuable activity] |

**The transformation statement:**
Write one sentence that captures the full arc from problem to success. This is the emotional climax of the BrandScript.

## Examples

### Example 1: B2B Email Marketing Platform

**Character:** E-commerce marketing managers responsible for email revenue, managing 50K-500K subscriber lists, under pressure to grow revenue without increasing ad spend.

**External Problem:** Their email campaigns take hours to build, and they cannot personalize at scale.
**Internal Problem:** They feel like they are wasting their most profitable channel because they cannot execute fast enough.
**Philosophical Problem:** Marketers who drive real revenue should not be held back by clunky tools.
**Villain:** Bloated, outdated email platforms built for developers, not marketers.

**Guide Empathy:** "We know the frustration of having a strategy you believe in but a tool that slows you down."
**Guide Authority:** "Used by 3,200 e-commerce brands. Average 28% increase in email revenue within 90 days. Shopify Plus Certified Partner."

**Process Plan:** 1. Connect your store (takes 5 minutes). 2. Choose from proven e-commerce templates. 3. Launch your first automated flow today.
**Agreement Plan:** "No long-term contracts. Free migration from your current platform. Dedicated onboarding specialist for every account."

**Direct CTA:** "Start your free 14-day trial"
**Transitional CTA:** "Download: The E-Commerce Email Playbook — 12 Flows That Drive Revenue on Autopilot"

**Failure:** "Every day without proper email automation, you're leaving revenue on the table. Your competitors are already sending the abandoned cart, browse abandonment, and post-purchase flows that you haven't built yet."

**Success:** "Your email channel becomes your most profitable marketing investment. Flows run automatically while you sleep. Your CEO asks how you grew email revenue by 30% without increasing headcount."

### Example 2: Leadership Development Consultancy

**Character:** HR directors at mid-market companies (500-5,000 employees) tasked with developing the next generation of leaders but lacking internal capability to do it.

**External Problem:** High-potential employees are being promoted into leadership without training, and the company has no structured development program.
**Internal Problem:** The HR director feels exposed — they know the gap exists and fear a key leader will leave or fail publicly before they can fix it.
**Philosophical Problem:** People who earn leadership roles deserve to be set up for success, not left to figure it out alone.
**Villain:** The "sink or swim" management culture that treats leadership as an innate talent rather than a learnable skill.

**Guide Empathy:** "We've watched talented people get promoted and then slowly drown because nobody invested in their growth. It doesn't have to be that way."
**Guide Authority:** "15 years developing leaders at 200+ companies. 94% of participants report measurable improvement in team performance. ICF-certified coaches."

**Process Plan:** 1. Assess — We evaluate your current leadership bench and identify gaps. 2. Design — We build a custom 6-month development track for your top 20. 3. Transform — Your leaders graduate with real skills, real confidence, and a peer network.
**Agreement Plan:** "We guarantee measurable improvement in 360-feedback scores. If we don't deliver, we extend the program at no cost."

**Direct CTA:** "Schedule a leadership gap assessment"
**Transitional CTA:** "Download: The Leadership Readiness Scorecard — Rate Your Team in 10 Minutes"

**Failure:** "Without structured development, your best people will leave for companies that invest in them. The ones who stay will underperform, and the cost of replacing a failed leader is 2-3x their salary."

**Success:** "Your leadership bench is deep and ready. Succession planning becomes a strength, not a scramble. Your best people stay because they're growing, and they build teams that perform."

## When to Use

- **Before any marketing initiative** — the BrandScript must exist before the website, the campaign, or the sales deck
- **When onboarding a new agency or freelancer** — hand them the BrandScript as the messaging brief
- **When the team cannot agree on messaging** — the BrandScript workshop resolves internal disagreements through structure
- **When the company is rebranding or repositioning** — the BrandScript should be the first artifact of the rebrand
- **When sales and marketing are misaligned** — a shared BrandScript creates a single language
- **Quarterly reviews** — revisit and update the BrandScript as the market, product, and customer evolve

## When NOT to Use

- **As a public-facing document** — the BrandScript is an internal strategy document, not customer-facing copy
- **For product documentation** — the BrandScript informs tone but is not a substitute for technical accuracy
- **When you have no customer data** — a BrandScript built on assumptions rather than customer interviews will be fiction, not strategy. Do the interviews first.
- **For very early-stage startups still searching for product-market fit** — the BrandScript assumes you know who your customer is and what problem you solve. If those are still open questions, focus on discovery first.

## Integration

The BrandScript is the central document in the Miller framework ecosystem:

- **Built on** the SB7 Framework (`miller-storybrand-sb7.md`), which provides the theoretical structure
- **Produces** the One-Liner (`miller-one-liner.md`), which is a compressed version of the BrandScript
- **Feeds into** the Marketing Roadmap (`miller-marketing-roadmap.md`), which deploys BrandScript messaging across channels
- **Validated by** the Grunt Test (`miller-grunt-test.md`), which checks whether the BrandScript has been applied clearly

The BrandScript also serves as source material for Duarte frameworks. When building a presentation using the Sparkline (`duarte-resonate-sparkline.md`), pull "what is" from the problem section and "what could be" from the success section. When designing a S.T.A.R. Moment (`duarte-star-moment.md`), draw the emotional story from the character and failure sections.

## Agent Routing

**Route TO this framework when:**
- A user needs comprehensive brand messaging or a messaging guide
- A user has completed the SB7 and wants to build the full document
- A user is briefing an agency, freelancer, or new team member on brand voice
- A user says their messaging is inconsistent across channels
- A user is preparing for a rebrand or major campaign

**Route AWAY from this framework when:**
- The user just needs a quick pitch (route to `miller-one-liner.md`)
- The user needs the theoretical framework explained (route to `miller-storybrand-sb7.md`)
- The user needs to implement messaging across channels (route to `miller-marketing-roadmap.md`)
- The user needs to audit existing messaging (route to `miller-grunt-test.md`)
- The user needs a presentation, not a messaging document (route to Duarte frameworks)
