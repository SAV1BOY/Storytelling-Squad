# Discovery Layer
> Research layer for audience interviews, belief mapping, objection identification, story mining, and competitor narrative audit.

## Overview
Every great story begins with discovery — not writing, not strategy, not structure, but the disciplined act of learning what stories to tell, to whom, and why they matter. The Discovery Layer is the research foundation of the entire storytelling stack. It answers the questions that every subsequent layer depends on: Who is the audience? What do they believe? What do they fear? What objections will they raise? What stories already exist in the market? And what untold stories are waiting to be found? Without rigorous discovery, storytelling becomes guesswork — and guesswork produces content that sounds good to the company and means nothing to the customer.

## Core Methodology
The Discovery Layer operates through five interconnected research streams:

1. **Audience Interviews** — Direct conversations with customers, prospects, and lost deals to understand their language, motivations, objections, and decision-making processes. Interviews surface the emotional truth that surveys and analytics miss.

2. **Belief Mapping** — Charting the audience's current beliefs about the problem, the solution category, and the brand. Beliefs determine what stories will resonate and what stories will be rejected. You cannot change behavior without first understanding belief.

3. **Objection Identification** — Cataloging the specific reasons people do not buy, do not engage, or do not share. Every objection is a story gap — a narrative that has not been told or told poorly.

4. **Story Mining** — Systematically extracting stories from internal sources: customer success teams, sales conversations, support tickets, founder experiences, employee perspectives. Most companies are sitting on a goldmine of stories they have never documented.

5. **Competitor Narrative Audit** — Analyzing how competitors tell their stories: their promises, their enemies, their proof, their voice. Understanding the competitive narrative landscape reveals gaps you can own.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Conduct Audience Interviews
- Interview 12-20 people across three segments: current customers, recent prospects (won and lost), and ideal prospects who have never engaged
- Use a semi-structured format: prepared questions with flexibility to follow interesting threads
- **Core question set**:
  - "Tell me about the last time you tried to solve [problem]. What happened?"
  - "What was the moment you realized you needed to change something?"
  - "What did you try before you found us? What worked and what did not?"
  - "When you were evaluating options, what were you most worried about?"
  - "If you were explaining what we do to a friend, how would you describe it?"
  - "What almost stopped you from buying/signing up?"
- Record and transcribe every interview. The exact words matter more than summaries.
- After 12 interviews, patterns emerge. After 20, you have a comprehensive language map.

### Step 2: Build the Belief Map
- From interview transcripts, extract belief statements: what does the audience believe about the problem, the solution category, and your brand?
- Categorize beliefs as: Helpful (beliefs that lead toward your solution), Harmful (beliefs that lead away), and Neutral (beliefs that do not affect the decision)
- **Helpful beliefs**: "I believe this problem is costing me more than I realize." "I believe technology can solve this." These are beliefs to reinforce.
- **Harmful beliefs**: "I believe switching tools is too risky." "I believe all solutions in this category are the same." These are beliefs to challenge.
- **Neutral beliefs**: "I believe this industry is growing." These can be ignored in the narrative.
- For each harmful belief, identify the story that would change it. This becomes your content roadmap.
- Map belief intensity: some beliefs are lightly held and easily shifted; others are identity-level and resist change. Focus storytelling resources on the most consequential, most shiftable beliefs.

### Step 3: Catalog Objections
- Sources: lost deal interviews, sales call recordings, support tickets, social media comments, review sites
- Categorize objections by type:
  - **Trust objections**: "I do not believe you can deliver." → Needs proof stories
  - **Value objections**: "I do not think this is worth the price." → Needs ROI stories
  - **Timing objections**: "Now is not the right time." → Needs urgency and cost-of-delay stories
  - **Risk objections**: "What if it does not work?" → Needs risk-reversal and guarantee stories
  - **Inertia objections**: "My current solution is good enough." → Needs contrast and aspiration stories
- For each objection, write the story that resolves it. These stories become sales assets, email content, and ad angles.
- Rank objections by frequency and deal impact. Address the top 5 first.

### Step 4: Mine Internal Stories
- **Customer success team**: "Tell me about a customer interaction in the last month that made you proud." "Tell me about a customer who almost churned but stayed. What happened?"
- **Sales team**: "Tell me about the last deal you won that surprised you. Why did they choose us?" "Tell me about the best objection response you have ever given."
- **Support team**: "Tell me about a ticket that turned into a happy customer. What changed?"
- **Founders/executives**: "What is the origin moment you never share publicly?" "What is the hardest decision you have made for the company?"
- **Product team**: "What feature was inspired by a customer request? Tell me that story."
- Catalog stories by type: origin, transformation, obstacle overcome, unexpected result, human connection
- Store stories in a central repository accessible to the entire marketing and sales organization

### Step 5: Audit Competitor Narratives
- For each primary competitor, analyze:
  - **Their promise**: What transformation do they claim to deliver?
  - **Their enemy**: What do they position themselves against?
  - **Their proof**: What evidence do they cite? Customer stories, data, endorsements?
  - **Their voice**: How do they sound? Professional, casual, authoritative, rebellious?
  - **Their gaps**: What stories are they NOT telling? What audiences are they NOT addressing?
- Map the narrative landscape: if every competitor promises "efficiency," the opportunity might be "joy" or "creativity" or "simplicity"
- Identify the narrative white space — the stories no competitor is telling that your audience needs to hear
- Document the competitive narrative landscape in a single visual: a positioning map with narrative dimensions instead of feature dimensions

### Step 6: Synthesize Discovery into Story Strategy
- Combine all five streams into a discovery brief:
  - **Audience language glossary**: The exact words and phrases the audience uses (for all content creation)
  - **Belief map**: Current beliefs, target beliefs, and the stories needed to bridge the gap
  - **Objection response playbook**: Each objection paired with its resolving story
  - **Story bank**: All mined stories, categorized and rated for narrative potential
  - **Competitive narrative map**: Competitor positions and the white space available
- The discovery brief becomes the foundational document for the Strategy Layer
- Update the discovery brief quarterly with new interviews, new stories, and new competitive intelligence

## Examples

### Example 1: SaaS Discovery for Marketing Automation Platform
- **Interview finding**: "Prospects consistently describe their current state as 'duct-taped together.' Seven of twelve interviewees used the word 'Frankenstein' to describe their tech stack."
- **Belief map finding**: "Harmful belief: 'Switching platforms will take 6 months and break everything.' This belief blocks 40% of qualified prospects."
- **Objection**: "The most common lost-deal reason is 'We just renewed our contract with [competitor].' This is a timing objection, but interviews revealed it is actually a risk objection — they fear the migration, not the timing."
- **Story mined**: "Customer success found a story about a company that migrated in 3 weeks with zero downtime. This story directly addresses the migration fear."
- **Competitive gap**: "Every competitor talks about features and integrations. Nobody is telling the story of what happens AFTER implementation — the day-to-day experience of using the tool."

### Example 2: DTC Discovery for Wellness Brand
- **Interview finding**: "Customers describe their previous experience as 'throwing money at the problem.' Eight of fifteen interviewees expressed guilt about past purchases that did not work."
- **Belief map finding**: "Helpful belief: 'I deserve a solution that actually works, not just marketing.' This belief is strong and should be reinforced in all messaging."
- **Objection**: "The top objection is 'I have tried everything and nothing works.' This is an inertia objection born from repeated disappointment."
- **Story mined**: "Founder has a personal story of the same cycle of disappointment that led to creating the product. This story directly mirrors the audience's experience."
- **Competitive gap**: "Competitors sell aspiration and lifestyle. Nobody is acknowledging the frustration and skepticism that the audience actually feels. Honesty about the problem is the narrative white space."

## When to Use
- Before any new marketing initiative, campaign, or content strategy
- When entering a new market segment or launching a new product
- When messaging is not resonating and you need to understand why
- Quarterly refresh of audience understanding and story inventory
- When a new competitor enters the market and changes the narrative landscape
- Before building or rebuilding a brand narrative

## When NOT to Use
- When you have already completed recent discovery (within the last quarter) and the market has not changed
- When the task is purely tactical and does not require audience insight (formatting existing content, for example)
- When time constraints prevent proper research — rushed discovery produces false confidence

## Integration
- Discovery findings feed directly into the **Strategy Layer** for story selection and prioritization
- Audience language informs the **Drafting Layer** for voice and tone
- Objection catalog informs the **Sales Story Framework** and **Ad Storytelling Framework**
- Story bank feeds every application framework across the storytelling stack
- Competitive audit informs the **Brand Story Framework** enemy definition and the **Thought Leadership Story Framework** contrarian positioning

## Agent Routing
- **Research Agent**: Conducts interviews, transcribes, and extracts patterns
- **Analysis Agent**: Builds belief maps, categorizes objections, and synthesizes findings
- **Competitive Intelligence Agent**: Monitors competitor narratives and identifies shifts
- **Documentation Agent**: Maintains the discovery brief, story bank, and language glossary
- Route to this layer when beginning any new storytelling initiative or when existing messaging is underperforming and the root cause is unclear
