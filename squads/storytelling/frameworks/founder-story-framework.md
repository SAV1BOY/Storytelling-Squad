# Founder Story Framework
> Craft the founder's journey from personal pain through insight to mission, building, impact, and future vision.

## Overview
The founder story is the origin myth of a company. It answers the question every customer, investor, employee, and journalist eventually asks: "Why does this company exist, and why should I care?" A well-crafted founder story does more than explain history — it creates emotional investment in the company's mission. The founder becomes a proxy for the audience's own struggles and aspirations. This framework provides a six-beat narrative arc that transforms biographical facts into a compelling mission narrative, positioning the founder as a relatable hero on a purposeful journey.

## Core Methodology
The Founder Story Framework follows six sequential beats that create escalating emotional investment:

1. **Personal Pain** — The founder's direct, lived experience with the problem. Not market research. Not an opportunity spotted. Real, felt, personal frustration or suffering that made the status quo intolerable.

2. **Moment of Insight** — The specific instant when the founder saw a different possibility. This is the "aha moment" — but it must be grounded in a concrete scene, not an abstraction. Where were they? What triggered it? What did they feel?

3. **Mission Born** — The crystallization of insight into purpose. The moment the founder decided to act. This beat transitions from personal experience to universal mission — the problem is not just theirs; it belongs to a community they will serve.

4. **Journey of Building** — The honest, unglamorous reality of creation. Obstacles overcome, sacrifices made, early failures, pivots, and the people who joined along the way. This beat earns credibility through vulnerability.

5. **Current Impact** — Where the company stands today. Not vanity metrics, but meaningful evidence that the mission is being fulfilled. Real customers, real transformations, real scale of impact.

6. **Future Vision** — Where the founder sees the mission going. This is not a product roadmap; it is a vision of the world the company is building toward. The audience is invited to be part of that future.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Excavate the Personal Pain
- Interview the founder with focus on the emotional origin, not the business origin
- Ask: "Before the company, what was the moment you were most frustrated by this problem?"
- Look for the universal in the personal — the founder's pain must resonate with the audience's pain
- Identify sensory details: place, time, physical sensations, specific events
- Resist the temptation to sanitize or professionalize the pain — rawness creates connection
- If there are multiple founders, find the single most compelling personal origin

### Step 2: Capture the Insight Moment
- Ask: "What was the specific moment you realized it could be different?"
- This must be a scene, not an idea. "I was sitting in a hospital waiting room" is a scene. "I realized the market was underserved" is not.
- The insight should feel inevitable in hindsight but surprising in the moment
- Connect the insight directly to the personal pain — the insight is the answer to the pain
- Keep it simple: one insight, one sentence, one moment

### Step 3: Articulate the Mission
- Translate the personal insight into a collective purpose
- Format: "I realized that [I/we] could [solve this] so that [audience] would never have to [experience this pain] again"
- The mission must be larger than the product — it is the reason the company will keep going even when the product changes
- Test the mission against authenticity: does it sound like the founder or like a marketing team?
- The mission should create an us-vs-them dynamic — who are we fighting for, and what are we fighting against?

### Step 4: Narrate the Building Journey
- Select 2-3 key moments from the building phase that demonstrate character: a near-death moment, a breakthrough, a sacrifice
- Include at least one failure or pivot — perfection is not believable and not relatable
- Introduce key people who joined the mission and why they chose to join
- Show the founder's growth, not just the company's growth
- Use specific details: "We had $400 in the bank and a demo that crashed during the pitch" is stronger than "We faced financial challenges"

### Step 5: Quantify Current Impact
- Choose metrics that reflect mission fulfillment, not just business growth
- "We have served 50,000 families" is more compelling than "We have 50,000 users"
- Include one specific customer story that embodies the impact
- Connect current impact back to the original personal pain — show the circle closing
- Be honest about what has been achieved and what remains to be done

### Step 6: Cast the Future Vision
- Describe the world the company is building toward, not the features it plans to ship
- Make the vision ambitious but believable given the current trajectory
- Explicitly invite the audience into the vision: "And this is where you come in"
- The vision should feel like the natural extension of the mission, not a pivot
- End with energy and forward momentum — the story is not over

## Examples

### Example 1: Health Tech Startup Founder
- **Personal Pain**: "My mother was diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes in 2016. She had three doctors, none of whom talked to each other. Her medication list was a mess of contradictions. I watched the smartest woman I know become confused and afraid because the system treated her like a file number, not a person."
- **Insight**: "I was building her a spreadsheet to track her medications when I realized: if I, a software engineer, needed a spreadsheet to manage one person's health — what was everyone else doing?"
- **Mission**: "Every patient deserves a single source of truth for their health. Not a portal. Not a chart. A story of their health that makes sense to them."
- **Building**: "The first version was an app I built for my mom. She showed it to her doctor. The doctor asked for ten copies. We had no copies — it was a prototype held together with duct tape. That was our first validation and our first panic."
- **Impact**: "Today, 120,000 patients use the platform. Hospital readmission rates dropped 23% across our partner systems. My mom still uses it. She calls it 'my health story.'"
- **Vision**: "We are building toward a world where no patient ever has to be their own medical detective. Where your health story follows you, makes sense to you, and helps every doctor help you better."

### Example 2: EdTech Founder
- **Personal Pain**: "I failed algebra twice in high school. Not because I was not smart enough — because I was too embarrassed to ask questions in a room of 30 kids who seemed to get it. I sat in silence and fell further behind every day."
- **Insight**: "Years later, tutoring my niece, I watched her light up when she could ask a question without anyone watching. She whispered her question to me, like it was a secret. That whisper changed everything for me."
- **Mission**: "Every student deserves a safe space to ask the question they are afraid to ask in class."
- **Building**: "We built the first version as a chat tool where students could ask anonymous questions. Teachers told us it was a distraction. Then one teacher showed us her data — participation went from 8 students per class to 28. She cried when she told us. We almost cried too."
- **Impact**: "2,400 schools use the platform. Students have asked over 11 million questions. The most common first message is 'Is this a dumb question?' It never is."
- **Vision**: "We are building toward classrooms where curiosity is never punished and silence is never confused with understanding."

## When to Use
- Company about page and brand narrative
- Investor pitches and fundraising decks
- Media interviews and press kits
- Recruiting and employer brand materials
- Keynote presentations and conference talks
- Customer-facing content that needs to establish trust and mission alignment

## When NOT to Use
- When the founder's story is not genuinely connected to the company's mission
- When the founder prefers to remain private and the story would feel forced
- When the company has pivoted so far from the original mission that the founder story creates confusion
- When the audience cares more about outcomes than origins (use Case Study Framework instead)
- When multiple founders have conflicting origin narratives that have not been resolved

## Integration
- Feeds into the **Brand Story Framework** as the origin component of the brand narrative
- Connects to the **Pitch Story Framework** for investor presentations
- Adapts to the **Presentation Narrative Framework** for keynote talks
- Provides source material for the **Social Media Story Framework** as serialized content
- Informs the **Hiring/Employer Brand Story** for recruiting narratives
- Uses the **Discovery Layer** for the initial founder interview process

## Agent Routing
- **Discovery Agent**: Conducts the deep founder interview, captures raw material
- **Structure Agent**: Identifies the six beats from interview material and maps the arc
- **Drafting Agent**: Writes the founder story in the appropriate voice and format
- **Editing Agent**: Ensures authenticity, removes corporate sanitization, strengthens emotional beats
- **Strategy Agent**: Determines where and how the founder story should be deployed
- Route to this framework when the task involves telling the story of why the company or product exists through the lens of its creator
