# Delivery Layer
> Adapt stories for format, channel, visual design, and production — preparing the story for its medium.

## Overview
A great story told in the wrong format for the wrong channel is a wasted story. The Delivery Layer is where narrative meets medium. It takes edited, publication-ready content and adapts it for the specific format, channel, and delivery mechanism that will carry it to the audience. A case study that works as a 1,200-word blog post needs different treatment as a 2-minute video, a 10-slide carousel, a sales one-pager, or an email excerpt. The Delivery Layer ensures that every story is optimized for how the audience will actually experience it — on what device, in what context, with what attention level, and through what sensory channels.

## Core Methodology
The Delivery Layer operates through four dimensions of adaptation:

1. **Format Adaptation** — Converting narrative content from one format to another while preserving the story's core emotional arc. A blog post becomes a video script. A case study becomes a sales slide. A webinar becomes a blog series. Each conversion requires rethinking structure, not just reformatting text.

2. **Channel Optimization** — Tailoring the delivery for the specific platform's constraints, audience behaviors, and algorithmic preferences. LinkedIn favors different content than Instagram. Email works differently than a landing page. Each channel has rules that affect how stories are consumed.

3. **Visual Design** — Creating the visual layer that supports the narrative. Typography, color, imagery, layout, and data visualization all affect how the story is received. Visual design should amplify the narrative, not compete with it.

4. **Production Notes** — Specifying the technical requirements for audio, video, or interactive delivery. Shot lists, music cues, animation briefs, voiceover direction, and interactive design specifications.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Map the Delivery Matrix
- For each story, identify all target formats and channels:
  - **Primary format**: The hero asset (e.g., a full blog post, a keynote presentation, a video)
  - **Derivative formats**: Adapted versions for other channels (e.g., social excerpts, email summary, slide deck, one-pager)
- Create a delivery matrix: Story x Format x Channel x Audience x Timeline
- Prioritize by impact: which format-channel combination will reach the most people in the target audience?
- Sequence deliverables: hero asset first, derivatives follow. The hero asset establishes the source of truth that all derivatives reference.

### Step 2: Execute Format Adaptation
- **Blog → Video Script**: Identify the 3-5 most visual and emotional moments from the blog. Build the video around scenes, not paragraphs. Add visual direction for B-roll, graphics, and transitions. Cut word count by 50-60% — video moves faster than reading.
- **Blog → Social Posts**: Extract the single most shareable insight from each section. Compress to platform-specific length. Add a hook optimized for the platform's feed algorithm. Link back to the full piece.
- **Blog → Email**: Pull the opening hook and the most compelling proof point. Frame as a personal message, not a newsletter. Include the CTA from the blog but reduce friction (one click, not two).
- **Blog → Sales One-Pager**: Restructure around the buyer's decision framework: problem, solution, proof, pricing, next step. Cut all narrative exploration — keep only the beats that directly serve the sales conversation.
- **Blog → Carousel**: One key point per slide. Design for scanning (3 seconds per slide). Use large text and supporting visuals. First slide is the hook, last slide is the CTA.
- **Presentation → Blog**: Expand speaker notes into full paragraphs. Add context that the speaker provides verbally. Replace images with descriptions. Add the proof detail that slides only hint at.
- Each adaptation should feel native to its format. If it feels like a reformatted blog post, the adaptation failed.

### Step 3: Optimize for Channel
- **Website/Blog**: SEO optimization (title tags, meta descriptions, header structure). Internal linking to related stories. CTAs placed at 50% and 100% of scroll depth. Mobile-responsive design.
- **Email**: Subject line optimization (6-10 words, curiosity or benefit-driven). Preview text that extends the subject, does not repeat it. Single-column layout for mobile. One primary CTA, one secondary at most.
- **LinkedIn**: Hook in the first 2 lines (before the "see more" fold). No external links in the post text (algorithmic penalty). Use document posts (PDFs/carousels) for higher reach. Post between 7-9 AM audience local time.
- **Twitter/X**: Thread format for longer stories (numbered posts). First tweet is the hook. Last tweet is the CTA + retweet of the first tweet. Use line breaks for readability.
- **Instagram**: Visual-first. Carousel for educational content. Reels for personality and reach. Caption hook in first line. Hashtag strategy in comments, not caption.
- **YouTube**: Thumbnail optimization (faces, contrast, text overlay). Title with keyword + curiosity. First 30 seconds determine retention. End screens and cards for CTA.
- **Paid Ads**: Platform-specific aspect ratios and length requirements. Sound-off design with text overlays. Clear CTA button. Landing page alignment with ad narrative.

### Step 4: Design the Visual Layer
- **Typography**: Headlines should command attention. Body text should be effortless to read. Choose 2 fonts maximum. Use font weight and size to create hierarchy.
- **Color**: Use brand colors consistently but strategically. Reserve accent colors for CTAs and key data points. Ensure sufficient contrast for accessibility (WCAG AA minimum).
- **Imagery**: Select images that advance the narrative, not decorate the page. Customer photos, product screenshots, and data visualizations are narrative. Stock photos of people smiling are decoration.
- **Data visualization**: Follow the Data Storytelling Framework for chart design. Narrative titles. Annotated callouts. Progressive revelation in presentations.
- **Layout**: Guide the reader's eye through the story. Use white space to create emphasis. Break long text blocks with pull quotes, images, or section headers. Design for scanning AND reading.
- **Accessibility**: Alt text for all images. Captions for all video. Sufficient color contrast. Screen-reader-friendly document structure.

### Step 5: Prepare Production Notes
- **Video production**: Shot list (scene, framing, duration, audio), talent direction, B-roll list, music selection and licensing, graphics and animation brief, color grading notes
- **Audio/Podcast production**: Script with speaker notes, music cues (intro, transition, outro), sound effect notes, guest preparation questions, editing notes (cut points, emphasis moments)
- **Interactive content**: User flow diagram, interaction triggers, conditional logic (branching), loading states, mobile adaptation, fallback for unsupported browsers
- **Print/PDF**: Bleed and margin specifications, color mode (CMYK for print, RGB for digital), font embedding, image resolution (300 DPI minimum for print)
- Production notes should be detailed enough that a production team can execute without additional creative direction

### Step 6: Quality Assurance Before Publishing
- **Technical QA**: All links work. All images load. All videos play. All forms submit. All CTAs function.
- **Cross-device testing**: Content displays correctly on desktop, tablet, and mobile. Touch targets are large enough. Scroll behavior is smooth.
- **Brand QA**: Visual design aligns with brand standards. Voice and tone are consistent. Logos and colors are correct.
- **Narrative QA**: The story's emotional arc survived the adaptation. The proof still lands. The CTA still makes sense in this format.
- **Accessibility QA**: Screen reader test. Color contrast check. Caption accuracy for video. Alt text for images.

## Examples

### Example 1: Case Study Delivery Matrix
- **Hero asset**: 1,200-word blog post with pull quotes and data visualization
- **Derivative 1**: 2-minute customer testimonial video (interview footage + B-roll of product in use)
- **Derivative 2**: 10-slide LinkedIn carousel (one key beat per slide, metric callouts)
- **Derivative 3**: Sales one-pager (PDF, problem/solution/proof/CTA format)
- **Derivative 4**: Email excerpt for nurture sequence (hook + key metric + link to full case study)
- **Derivative 5**: 3 social posts (micro-story, key quote, metric highlight)

### Example 2: Webinar Delivery Adaptation
- **Hero asset**: 45-minute live webinar with slides
- **Derivative 1**: Full replay video with chapter markers
- **Derivative 2**: 3 blog posts (one per teaching point from the webinar)
- **Derivative 3**: 5 short-form video clips (key moments, 30-60 seconds each, for social)
- **Derivative 4**: Slide deck as downloadable PDF (with expanded notes)
- **Derivative 5**: Email summary for registrants who did not attend (key takeaways + replay link)

## When to Use
- After the Editing Layer has produced publication-ready content
- When adapting existing content for new channels or formats
- When planning a multi-channel content release
- When redesigning content for improved performance
- Before any content goes live on any platform

### Delivery Pitfalls
- **Lazy reformatting**: Taking a blog post and pasting it into a carousel without rethinking the narrative for the visual, swipe-based format. Each format has its own narrative grammar. Respect it.
- **Ignoring mobile**: Designing for desktop when 60-80% of content consumption happens on mobile. If the font is too small, the images are too wide, or the CTA is too far down, the mobile audience is lost.
- **One-size production**: Applying the same production quality to every piece. A customer testimonial video benefits from polished production. A behind-the-scenes social clip benefits from authenticity. Match production level to format and platform expectations.
- **Missing accessibility**: Publishing content without alt text, captions, or sufficient contrast. Accessibility is not optional — it affects both audience reach and legal compliance.
- **Skipping QA**: Publishing content with broken links, missing images, or formatting errors. Every quality issue erodes trust. Test everything on every target device before publication.

## When NOT to Use
- For content that lives on a single channel in a single format (the editing pass is sufficient)
- When adapting would require so much rewriting that starting from the Structure Layer is more efficient
- For real-time content (live social posts, live video) where delivery IS the creation
- When the original piece has not been through the Editing Layer — delivering unedited content across multiple channels multiplies errors

## Integration
- Receives edited content from the **Editing Layer**
- References channel specifications from the **Strategy Layer**
- Applies visual standards from the **Brand Story Framework**
- Outputs to publishing platforms and the **Performance Layer** (for live delivery)
- Feeds performance data to the **Measurement Layer**

## Agent Routing
- **Delivery Agent**: Manages the adaptation matrix and coordinates across formats
- **Design Agent**: Creates visual assets, layouts, and data visualizations
- **Production Agent**: Prepares video, audio, and interactive production briefs
- **Technical Agent**: Handles platform-specific optimization, SEO, and accessibility
- **QA Agent**: Performs technical, brand, narrative, and accessibility quality assurance
- Route to this layer when content is ready for its final form and needs to be prepared for publication across one or more channels

### Delivery Quality Checklist
- Has every derivative asset been reviewed for narrative coherence with the hero asset?
- Have all assets been tested on mobile, tablet, and desktop?
- Are all links, CTAs, and forms functional across all channels?
