# Ad Storytelling Framework
> Tell complete stories in 15-60 seconds using 3-second hooks, micro-narratives, and platform-specific CTA design.

## Overview
Advertising is storytelling under the most extreme constraint: you must capture attention, create emotion, and drive action in 15-60 seconds, often competing with the viewer's thumb hovering over the skip button. This framework provides a systematic approach to ad storytelling across Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, and other digital platforms. The core insight is that ads fail not because they are too short for stories but because they try to tell the wrong kind of story. An ad story is not a compressed feature film; it is a flash fiction narrative — a single emotional beat delivered with maximum intensity and minimum waste.

## Core Methodology
The Ad Storytelling Framework operates on a three-beat structure with platform-specific adaptations:

1. **3-Second Hook** — The moment that earns the viewer's attention. On every platform, the first 3 seconds determine whether the rest of the ad exists for the viewer. The hook must pattern-interrupt, create curiosity, or trigger identification.

2. **Micro-Story (5-45 seconds)** — A compressed narrative that creates emotional movement. Not a product description. A story with a character, a tension, and a shift. The micro-story creates the emotional context in which the product makes sense.

3. **CTA (3-10 seconds)** — The action request, positioned as the resolution of the micro-story's tension. The CTA is not appended to the story; it completes the story.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Define the Ad's Single Emotion
- Every effective ad creates one dominant emotion: relief, excitement, belonging, fear of missing out, vindication, surprise, recognition
- Choose the emotion that most directly connects the audience's pain to the product's promise
- Write the emotion on a card and test every creative decision against it: "Does this increase [emotion]?"
- Avoid trying to create multiple emotions — a 30-second ad has room for one emotional arc, not three
- The emotion should match the platform's native tone: TikTok rewards surprise and humor, LinkedIn rewards validation, Facebook rewards recognition

### Step 2: Craft the Hook (0-3 Seconds)
- **Identity Hook**: "If you are a [specific audience], stop scrolling." Direct, qualifying, immediate.
- **Outcome Hook**: "[Specific result] in [specific timeframe]." Lead with the payoff.
- **Conflict Hook**: "I was about to [give up/fail/quit] when..." Start mid-story.
- **Visual Hook**: A dramatic before/after, an unexpected image, a face showing strong emotion.
- **Curiosity Hook**: "Nobody talks about this, but..." or "This is the [thing] that [surprising claim]."
- Test 5-10 hook variations for every ad. The hook is the single highest-leverage creative element.
- The hook must be understandable without audio (most social video starts muted)

### Step 3: Build the Micro-Story (5-45 Seconds)
- **Character**: Introduce a relatable person in 1-2 seconds. The character can be the speaker, a customer, or an archetype the audience identifies with.
- **Tension**: Show the problem in 3-5 seconds. Specific, visual, emotionally resonant. Not "marketing is hard" but "I was spending $5K/month on ads that were not converting."
- **Shift**: The moment the product enters the story. Not as a pitch but as a turning point. "Then I discovered..." or "That is when I tried..."
- **Transformation**: Show the result in 3-5 seconds. Before/after, metric, emotional change, specific moment of realization.
- Keep the micro-story focused on ONE transformation. Multiple benefits dilute emotional impact.

### Step 4: Platform-Specific Adaptations

#### Facebook/Instagram Feed Ads
- Format: 1:1 or 4:5 ratio, 15-30 seconds optimal
- Hook style: Identity or outcome hooks perform best
- Story style: Testimonial-driven or UGC (user-generated content) style
- CTA placement: On-screen text CTA at the transformation moment, verbal CTA at the end
- Captions are essential — design for sound-off viewing

#### YouTube Pre-Roll/In-Stream
- Format: 16:9, 15-second (non-skippable) or 30-60 second (skippable)
- Hook style: Must be compelling within 5 seconds (skip button appears at 5s for skippable ads)
- Story style: More produced, can use fuller narrative arcs for 30-60s spots
- CTA placement: Verbal CTA with on-screen overlay and companion banner
- For skippable ads: deliver the core message by second 5 in case they skip

#### TikTok
- Format: 9:16, 15-30 seconds optimal
- Hook style: Pattern interrupt, curiosity, or trending format adaptation
- Story style: Native, creator-led, UGC aesthetic. Produced ads underperform.
- CTA placement: End card with text overlay, or spoken CTA that feels conversational
- Match the platform energy: fast cuts, trending sounds, authentic tone

#### LinkedIn
- Format: 1:1 or 16:9, 15-30 seconds
- Hook style: Insight or data-driven hooks. "We analyzed 500 companies and found..."
- Story style: Professional, insight-driven, thought-leadership adjacent
- CTA placement: Subtle — "Learn more" or "Download the report"
- LinkedIn ads should feel like valuable content, not advertisements

### Step 5: Write the Script
- Write the script as three columns: Timestamp | Visual | Audio/Text
- Every second must be accounted for. A 30-second ad has 30 seconds, not "about 30 seconds."
- Read the script aloud and time it. Spoken scripts always run longer than you expect.
- Cut anything that does not serve the hook, the micro-story, or the CTA
- Include text overlay directions for sound-off viewing
- Write 3-5 script variations: same structure, different hooks and angles

### Step 6: Test and Iterate
- Test hooks independently: same body, different hooks, measure thumb-stop rate (3-second view rate)
- Test stories independently: same hook, different story angles, measure completion rate
- Test CTAs independently: same ad, different CTA text and placement, measure click-through rate
- Budget allocation: spend 70% of testing budget on hook variations — hooks have the highest leverage
- Winning formula: the hook with the highest stop rate + the story with the highest completion rate + the CTA with the highest click rate

## Examples

### Example 1: Facebook Ad — SaaS Tool for E-commerce (30 Seconds)
- **Hook (0-3s)**: [Visual: founder at desk, stressed] "I was spending $8,000 a month on ads and had no idea which ones actually worked."
- **Micro-Story (3-25s)**: "My dashboard was a mess of metrics. Every platform showed different numbers. I was guessing. Then I connected [Product] and saw something I had never seen: every dollar, every ad, every conversion — in one view. Within a week, I cut $3,000 in wasted spend and doubled my ROAS."
- **CTA (25-30s)**: "See your real numbers. Start free." [On-screen: URL and free trial CTA]

### Example 2: TikTok Ad — DTC Fitness Brand (15 Seconds)
- **Hook (0-2s)**: [Creator holds up two items] "Left: $200 gym bag I bought. Right: what I actually needed."
- **Micro-Story (2-12s)**: "I spent years buying gear I did not need because fitness brands made me think I had to. Then I found [Brand]. Three products. Everything you need. Nothing you do not." [Quick cuts showing simplicity of the products in use]
- **CTA (12-15s)**: "Link in bio. Starter kit is $49." [Text overlay with arrow to profile]

### Example 3: YouTube Pre-Roll — B2B Service (15 Seconds Non-Skippable)
- **Hook (0-3s)**: "Your competitors are reading this report. You are not." [Visual: report cover]
- **Micro-Story (3-12s)**: "We surveyed 1,000 B2B buyers on what makes them choose a vendor. The #1 answer was not price, not features — it was something 80% of companies never even mention on their website."
- **CTA (12-15s)**: "Download the free report. Link below." [URL overlay, end card]

## When to Use
- Paid advertising campaigns across digital platforms
- Retargeting campaigns where the audience has some brand familiarity
- Product launches where awareness is the primary goal
- Testing new messaging angles before committing to larger campaigns
- Any scenario where you need to tell a story within severe time constraints

## When NOT to Use
- When the product requires extensive education before the audience can understand the value (use webinar or content marketing)
- When the audience is already deep in the funnel and needs detailed information, not a story
- When ad budgets are too small to run meaningful creative tests
- When the product's story cannot be told visually or in under 60 seconds
