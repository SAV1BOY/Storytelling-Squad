# Howell's ABT (And/But/Therefore) Framework
> The minimum viable narrative structure — a three-part sentence that transforms any statement from boring to compelling, championed by Park Howell from Randy Olson's work.

## Overview

The ABT framework is the simplest narrative structure that still qualifies as a story. Created by scientist-turned-filmmaker Randy Olson and championed by Park Howell in the Business of Story methodology, ABT stands for And/But/Therefore. It is the "narrative litmus test" — if you cannot express your message as an ABT, you do not yet have a story.

The structure works because it contains the three essential elements of narrative: agreement (AND), contradiction (BUT), and consequence (THEREFORE). These three beats activate the brain's pattern-recognition and problem-solving circuits, making any message more memorable, persuasive, and shareable.

Olson contrasts the ABT with two dysfunctional alternatives:
- **AAA (And/And/And):** A list of facts with no tension. "We do this AND this AND this AND this." This is the default mode of most corporate communication. It is accurate and boring.
- **DHY (Despite/However/Yet):** An overly hedged, academic structure that buries the point. Common in policy documents and committee-written content.

The ABT sits in the productive middle: enough structure to create tension, simple enough to use everywhere.

## Core Methodology

### The ABT Formula

```
[Context/Agreement] AND [Additional Context],
BUT [Problem/Contradiction/Tension],
THEREFORE [Action/Solution/Consequence].
```

Each component serves a specific narrative function:

**AND (Agreement):** Establish the world. Set up what the audience already knows or can easily accept. The AND clause builds common ground. It should feel obvious, familiar, or consensus-driven. If the audience disagrees with your AND clause, the rest of the structure collapses.

**BUT (Contradiction):** Introduce tension. The BUT is the pivot point of the entire structure. It disrupts the comfortable agreement established by AND and creates a gap that the audience's brain needs to resolve. The BUT should be surprising, specific, and consequential. Weak BUTs produce weak stories.

**THEREFORE (Consequence):** Resolve the tension. The THEREFORE provides the action, solution, or insight that addresses the BUT. It should feel inevitable in hindsight but not predictable in advance. The best THEREFOREs change the audience's understanding or behavior.

### The Narrative Litmus Test

Howell uses ABT as a diagnostic tool. Before investing in any piece of content, campaign, or presentation, test it with this question: "Can I express this as a single ABT statement?"

- If yes, you have a narrative core. Build from there.
- If no, you have information but not a story. Rework until you find the ABT.

This test works at every scale: a tweet, an email subject line, a presentation, a brand strategy, or an annual report.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Agreement

Ask: "What does my audience already know or believe that is relevant to my message?"

The AND clause is not the place for new information. It is the place for shared reality. Common sources of agreement:
- Industry trends everyone acknowledges
- Customer pain points that are well-known
- Cultural moments or shared experiences
- Data points that are widely accepted
- Aspirations that are universally held

**Example:** "Every marketing team wants to produce more content AND reach wider audiences..."

### Step 2: Find the Contradiction

Ask: "What is the tension, problem, or surprise that disrupts this agreement?"

The BUT clause is where your story lives. Without it, you are just stating facts. The contradiction can be:
- An unexpected obstacle
- A counterintuitive finding
- A market failure or gap
- A hidden cost or consequence
- A paradox in the current approach

**Example:** "...BUT most teams are producing more content that reaches fewer people because algorithmic noise drowns out undifferentiated messaging..."

### Step 3: Deliver the Consequence

Ask: "Given this contradiction, what must happen / what did happen / what should we do?"

The THEREFORE clause resolves the tension. It can be:
- A call to action
- A strategic recommendation
- A product/service positioning
- A lesson learned
- A changed perspective

**Example:** "...THEREFORE we built a platform that uses narrative intelligence to ensure every piece of content has a story structure that cuts through noise."

### Step 4: Pressure-Test the ABT

Run these checks:
- **Agreement test:** Would 80%+ of the target audience nod along with the AND clause?
- **Tension test:** Does the BUT genuinely surprise or challenge? Does it create a gap the brain wants to close?
- **Resolution test:** Does the THEREFORE follow logically from the BUT? Is it specific enough to act on?
- **Retell test:** Could someone who hears this ABT retell it to a colleague without notes?
- **Single-breath test:** Can you say the entire ABT in one breath? If not, trim it.

### Step 5: Scale the ABT

Once you have a core ABT, it becomes the seed for larger content:
- **Expand to a paragraph:** Each clause becomes 2-3 sentences.
- **Expand to a presentation:** Each clause becomes a section with supporting evidence.
- **Expand to a campaign:** The AND is the awareness phase, the BUT is the consideration phase, the THEREFORE is the conversion phase.
- **Expand to a full Story Cycle:** The ABT maps directly to Howell's 10-step system (AND = Steps 1-3, BUT = Steps 4-5, THEREFORE = Steps 6-10).

## Examples

### Example 1: SaaS Product Launch Email

**ABT:** "Your sales team is closing deals AND hitting quarterly targets, BUT 68% of those deals take twice as long as they should because reps spend more time in the CRM than on calls, THEREFORE we built an AI layer that auto-logs every interaction so your team never touches data entry again."

**Expanded subject line:** "Your reps are selling, BUT they're drowning in data entry"
**Expanded email body:** The AND becomes the opening acknowledgment ("Congrats on a strong Q3..."), the BUT becomes the problem section with data and empathy, the THEREFORE becomes the product introduction and CTA.

### Example 2: Nonprofit Annual Report Opening

**ABT:** "In 2025, our community donated over $4 million AND our volunteer base grew by 30%, BUT the families we serve are facing a housing crisis that has tripled wait times for shelter placement, THEREFORE this year we are launching an emergency housing fund and restructuring our intake process to cut wait times in half."

This ABT works because the AND establishes positive momentum (avoiding the typical nonprofit doom-and-gloom opening), the BUT introduces the real tension, and the THEREFORE commits to specific action.

### Example 3: Executive Summary for Board Deck

**ABT:** "We entered the year with strong product-market fit AND a 140% net revenue retention rate, BUT our customer acquisition cost has increased 3x in 18 months due to market saturation in our primary channel, THEREFORE we are pivoting our growth strategy to partnerships and product-led acquisition with a target CAC reduction of 40% by Q3."

### Example 4: Social Media Post (LinkedIn)

**ABT:** "I spent 15 years building brands AND won every award the industry hands out, BUT I realized last month that my own kids couldn't tell me what I do for a living. THEREFORE I am rewriting my entire personal narrative starting with: what would a 10-year-old understand?"

## When to Use

- Elevator pitches and networking introductions
- Email subject lines and opening paragraphs
- Executive summaries and board presentations
- Social media posts (especially LinkedIn and Twitter/X)
- Sales call opening statements
- Presentation thesis statements
- Internal alignment — getting a team to agree on the core narrative before building content
- Content briefs — the ABT becomes the brief's narrative thesis
- Ad copy and headline writing
- Pitch decks — the ABT is the story spine of the entire deck
- Any situation where you have 30 seconds or less to make a point

## When NOT to Use

- When you need a full narrative arc with character development, journey, and transformation (use the full Story Cycle)
- When the audience needs extensive context before they can understand the contradiction (build up to the ABT)
- When the message is purely informational with no persuasive intent (a status update, a factual report)
- When the emotional stakes require a longer, more nuanced story (use Dicks' Storyworthy approach)
- When you are selecting between multiple stories for a specific context (use Hall's Story Selection Matrix first)

## Integration

The ABT framework integrates with nearly every other storytelling methodology:

- **Howell's Story Cycle:** The ABT is the compressed version of the full 10-step cycle. AND = Backstory + Hero + Stakes. BUT = Disruption + Antagonists. THEREFORE = Mentor + Journey + Victory + Moral + Ritual. Use the ABT to test the narrative integrity of each cycle step.
- **Howell's Story Strategy Canvas:** The canvas should have a master ABT at the top that captures the entire brand narrative in one statement.
- **Dicks' Storyworthy:** Every storyworthy "5-second moment" can be expressed as a BUT. The moment of realization is the contradiction between what the hero believed AND what turned out to be true.
- **Dicks' Beginning/End Rule:** The AND clause and THEREFORE clause should be as opposite as possible, mirroring the rule of opposites.
- **Hall's Gap-Bridge Framework:** The ABT maps directly: AND = Normal, BUT = Explosion/Gap, THEREFORE = New Normal/Bridge.
- **Hall's Story Formula:** The ABT provides the structure; Hall's formula provides the character and emotional criteria.
