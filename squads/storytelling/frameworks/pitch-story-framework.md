# Pitch Story Framework
> Structure persuasive pitch narratives using frame setting, value proposition, proof, urgency, and next step.

## Overview
A pitch is a story told under constraint — limited time, skeptical audience, high stakes. Most pitches fail not because the idea is bad but because the narrative is wrong. This framework combines Oren Klaff's frame control methodology with Donald Miller's StoryBrand clarity to produce pitches that capture attention, establish authority, deliver value, prove claims, and drive action. Whether the pitch is a 60-second elevator conversation, a 10-minute investor presentation, or a 45-minute sales meeting, the underlying narrative architecture is the same. Only the depth of each beat changes.

## Core Methodology
The Pitch Story Framework operates on five sequential beats, each with a specific psychological function:

1. **Frame Setting** — Establish the lens through which the audience will interpret everything that follows. The person who controls the frame controls the conversation. Frame setting determines whether you are perceived as a supplicant asking for attention or an authority offering value.

2. **Value Proposition** — Articulate the transformation you deliver in terms the audience cares about. This is not a feature list. It is a before/after contrast that creates desire. The value proposition answers: "Why should I care?"

3. **Proof** — Demonstrate that the promised transformation is real, repeatable, and relevant to this specific audience. Proof must be layered: social proof, data proof, demonstration proof.

4. **Urgency** — Create a legitimate reason to act now rather than later. Urgency is not artificial scarcity; it is a truthful articulation of why delay has a cost. What does the audience lose by waiting?

5. **Next Step** — Provide a single, clear, low-friction action the audience can take immediately. The next step must feel like a natural continuation of the conversation, not a sales maneuver.

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Set the Frame (First 10% of Pitch Time)
- **Power Frame**: Establish yourself as the expert with a contrarian insight or unexpected data point. "Most companies believe X. The data shows Y."
- **Time Frame**: Set the terms of engagement. "I have three things to share with you. Each one will change how you think about [topic]." This signals control and respect for the audience's time.
- **Intrigue Frame**: Open with a story that creates curiosity. A customer scenario, a market anomaly, a personal experience that sets up the problem.
- **Analyst Frame**: For skeptical audiences, lead with data. "We analyzed 500 companies and found a pattern nobody is talking about."
- Choose ONE frame. Do not layer frames — it creates confusion. Match the frame to the audience's decision-making style.

### Step 2: Deliver the Value Proposition (Next 25%)
- State the problem in the audience's language, not yours
- Quantify the cost of the problem: "This costs companies like yours $X per year"
- Present the transformation: "What if instead of [current pain], you could [desired state]?"
- Describe the mechanism briefly: "We do this through [method], which works because [logic]"
- Anchor the value to something the audience already values: time, money, status, risk reduction
- Never describe more than three value dimensions — the audience cannot hold more in working memory

### Step 3: Prove the Claim (Next 30%)
- **Layer 1 — Social Proof**: "Companies like [name], [name], and [name] use this." Choose names the audience respects and identifies with.
- **Layer 2 — Data Proof**: Specific, verifiable metrics. "Average ROI of 340% in the first 90 days across 47 implementations."
- **Layer 3 — Demonstration Proof**: Show, do not tell. A live demo, a walkthrough, a before/after visual, a case study excerpt.
- **Layer 4 — Risk Reversal**: Guarantee, trial, or pilot offer that reduces perceived risk. "If you do not see results in 30 days, you pay nothing."
- Sequence proof from most emotionally resonant to most logically rigorous
- Each proof layer should answer the objection raised by the previous one

### Step 4: Create Urgency (Next 20%)
- **Market Urgency**: "The companies adopting this now will have 18 months of advantage. The window is closing."
- **Cost of Delay**: "Every month without this solution costs you approximately $X based on the data we discussed."
- **Capacity Urgency**: "We onboard 5 new clients per quarter. We have 2 spots remaining this quarter."
- **Competitive Urgency**: "Your competitors are already moving on this. Here is what we are seeing in the market."
- Choose ONE urgency mechanism. Stacking multiple urgency tactics feels manipulative and erodes trust.
- The urgency must be truthful. If discovered to be fabricated, trust is permanently destroyed.

### Step 5: Define the Next Step (Final 15%)
- The next step should require minimal commitment: a follow-up call, a pilot, a review of a proposal
- Remove friction: "I will send you a 1-page summary. If it resonates, we will schedule a 30-minute deep dive."
- Make the next step specific: date, time, format, participants
- Confirm verbally and follow up in writing within 2 hours
- Never end with "What do you think?" — end with "Here is what I suggest we do next."

## Examples

### Example 1: SaaS Sales Pitch to VP of Marketing
- **Frame**: "Your team is producing more content than ever, but conversion rates are flat. We spent six months studying why, and the answer surprised us." (Analyst Frame)
- **Value Proposition**: "The problem is not content quality — it is content sequencing. Your best content is hitting prospects at the wrong stage. Our platform maps content to buyer journey stages and sequences delivery automatically. Companies using this approach see 2.3x higher conversion rates."
- **Proof**: "HubSpot, Drift, and Outreach all use this methodology. Here is a 60-second walkthrough of what the dashboard looks like for a company your size. And here is a case study from an agency that went from 1.2% to 3.8% conversion in 90 days."
- **Urgency**: "Your Q3 campaigns launch in 6 weeks. Every campaign that runs without sequencing optimization is leaving conversion on the table."
- **Next Step**: "I would like to run a free 15-minute audit of your current content sequence. I will show you exactly where the gaps are. Can we do that Thursday at 2?"

### Example 2: Investor Pitch for Seed Round
- **Frame**: "Everyone in healthcare AI is building for doctors. We built for the 80 million Americans who manage a family member's health and have zero tools designed for them." (Intrigue Frame)
- **Value Proposition**: "Family caregivers make 70% of healthcare decisions but use consumer tools designed for healthy people. We built the first care coordination platform for non-professional caregivers. We simplify medication management, appointment scheduling, and provider communication into one app."
- **Proof**: "12,000 users in 8 months with zero paid acquisition — all word of mouth. Net Promoter Score of 78. We have partnerships with 3 hospital systems for referral pilots launching in Q2. Our retention at 6 months is 64%, which is 3x the health app average."
- **Urgency**: "The caregiver demographic is growing 10% annually as the population ages. The first platform to own this space will define the category. We are raising $3M to lock in our hospital partnerships and expand to 50,000 users before a competitor enters."
- **Next Step**: "I will send our full deck and data room access today. I would like to schedule a follow-up with you and your health tech partner for next week."

## When to Use
- Investor pitches and fundraising conversations
- Sales presentations to prospects
- Partnership proposals
- Internal budget requests and project proposals
- Conference talks with a call to action
- Any scenario where you must persuade someone to take a specific action within a limited time

### Common Pitch Mistakes
- **Feature dumping**: Listing every capability instead of telling a transformation story. The audience does not need to know everything your product does — they need to know what it does for them.
- **Weak opening**: Starting with "Thanks for having me" or "A little about our company." Start with the frame. The first 30 seconds determine whether you have 10 more minutes.
- **Stacking urgency**: Using multiple urgency mechanisms simultaneously ("limited time AND limited spots AND price increase"). One honest urgency mechanism is more persuasive than three that feel manufactured.
- **Missing the CTA**: Ending with "any questions?" instead of a specific next step. The pitch should culminate in a clear, low-friction action.
- **Ignoring the audience**: Delivering the same pitch regardless of who is in the room. A CFO cares about different proof than a VP of Engineering. Customize the proof layers for every audience.
- **Apologizing for selling**: Prefacing the ask with "I know this might seem pushy but..." Confidence in your value proposition is not arrogance — it is respect for the audience's intelligence.

## When NOT to Use
- When the goal is education, not persuasion (use Webinar or Content Marketing frameworks)
- When the audience is already convinced and needs implementation guidance, not a pitch
- When you are exploring a relationship and a pitch would feel premature
- When you lack sufficient proof to back claims — pitching without proof destroys credibility
- When the audience has explicitly asked for a different format (demo, Q&A, technical deep dive)

- **Discovery Agent**: Researches the specific audience, their priorities, objections, and decision-making style
- **Strategy Agent**: Selects the appropriate frame, proof layers, and urgency mechanism for this audience
- **Structure Agent**: Maps the five beats to the available time and format
- **Drafting Agent**: Writes the pitch script or talking points
- **Editing Agent**: Sharpens language, eliminates filler, ensures every sentence advances the narrative
- Route to this framework when the task involves a time-constrained persuasive narrative with a specific audience and desired action

### Pitch Preparation Checklist
- Audience research complete: decision-making style, priorities, objections, and competitors identified
- Frame selected and opening rehearsed (deliver in under 60 seconds)
- Value proposition articulated in audience's language, not yours
- 3+ proof layers prepared and sequenced from emotional to rational
- Urgency mechanism identified and verified as truthful
- Next step defined: specific date, time, format, and participants
- Backup plan prepared for 50% time reduction
- Objection responses prepared for top 3 expected objections
- Practice delivery timed and within allocated slot
