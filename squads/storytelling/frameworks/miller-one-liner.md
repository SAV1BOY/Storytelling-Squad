# The Miller One-Liner
> A concise formula — Problem + Solution + Result — that distills your entire brand message into roughly 30 words that pass the cocktail party test.

## Overview

The One-Liner is the compression layer of the StoryBrand framework. While the full SB7 can take pages to articulate, the One-Liner captures the essence in a single memorable statement. It answers the question every business owner dreads at a networking event: "So, what do you do?"

Most people answer this question with their job title ("I'm a financial advisor") or their company description ("We make cloud-based HR software"). Both answers kill the conversation because they position the speaker as the hero and provide no narrative tension. The One-Liner flips this by leading with a problem — which immediately activates the listener's curiosity — then offering a solution, and closing with a desirable result.

The One-Liner is not a tagline, a mission statement, or an elevator pitch in the traditional sense. A tagline is clever and ambiguous ("Think Different"). A mission statement is internal and aspirational. The One-Liner is specific, external, and action-oriented. It is designed to make people say, "Tell me more."

Miller considers the One-Liner the single most important piece of marketing collateral a business can create. It appears in email signatures, social media bios, the header of your website, your podcast intro, your team's voicemail greeting, and the first slide of every presentation.

## Core Methodology

### The Three Components

**1. The Problem**
Open with a pain point that your ideal customer recognizes immediately. The problem creates an open story loop in the listener's brain — they want to know how it resolves.

Rules for the problem statement:
- Use language your customer uses, not industry jargon
- Be specific enough to feel real, but broad enough to apply to your target market
- Frame it as a common human experience, not a niche technical issue
- Start with "Most..." or "A lot of..." to normalize the pain and include the listener

**2. The Solution**
Bridge from problem to resolution by introducing what you provide. This is not a product description — it is the mechanism of change.

Rules for the solution statement:
- Position your brand as the guide, not the hero
- Describe the action or tool, not the features
- Keep it to one clause — if you need a comma, you are being too complex
- Use active verbs: "we provide," "we help," "we created"

**3. The Result**
Close with the specific, desirable outcome. This is the "new bliss" — the transformed reality after the problem is solved.

Rules for the result statement:
- Make it vivid and concrete, not vague and aspirational
- Focus on the internal or status transformation, not just the external outcome
- End on the emotional payoff whenever possible
- This should be something the listener can picture themselves experiencing

### The Cocktail Party Test

The One-Liner must pass one critical test: **Can you say it in casual conversation and have people lean in rather than glaze over?**

If your One-Liner sounds like marketing copy, it fails. If it sounds like something a human would say to another human at a dinner party, it passes.

Signals that it passes:
- The listener asks a follow-up question
- The listener says "Oh, I know someone who needs that"
- The listener nods and says "That's a real problem"
- The listener remembers it and repeats it to someone else later

Signals that it fails:
- The listener says "Oh, that's nice" and changes the subject
- The listener looks confused and asks "So what does that mean exactly?"
- You feel awkward saying it out loud
- It takes more than 15 seconds to deliver

## Detailed Steps

### Step 1: Start With Your BrandScript

The One-Liner is derived from the full SB7 BrandScript (see `miller-brand-script.md`). Before writing your One-Liner, you need clarity on your character, problem, solution, and success. If you skip the BrandScript and jump straight to the One-Liner, the result will be shallow and generic.

Pull these elements from your BrandScript:
- The character's primary desire
- The internal or philosophical problem
- Your brand's solution mechanism
- The most compelling success vision

### Step 2: Draft the Problem Opening

Write 5-10 versions of the problem statement. Experiment with:
- Starting with "Most [customer type]..." — "Most small business owners struggle with..."
- Starting with a universally recognized frustration — "Nobody likes spending their Sunday evening on..."
- Starting with a surprising statistic — "87% of new managers receive zero leadership training..."
- Starting with "Have you ever..." — creates immediate identification

Test each version by saying it aloud. Does it sound like something you would actually say to another person? Does it create curiosity?

### Step 3: Draft the Solution Bridge

Write 3-5 versions of the solution. The solution should be one clause that connects the problem to the result. Common structures:

- "...we provide [mechanism]..."
- "...we help [customer type] [verb]..."
- "...[product name] gives them [tool/approach]..."
- "...we created a [simple description]..."

Avoid feature lists. "We provide a cloud-based platform with AI-powered analytics and real-time collaboration" is a feature dump. "We give teams a single place to track every project" is a solution.

### Step 4: Draft the Result Close

Write 5-10 versions of the result. The result should feel like a relief, a victory, or a transformation. Common structures:

- "...so they can [desirable action] without [previous pain]"
- "...which means [concrete outcome]"
- "...and finally [emotional resolution]"
- "...giving them [status transformation]"

### Step 5: Assemble and Edit

Combine the strongest problem, solution, and result. Read it aloud. Time it — it should be under 15 seconds. Edit ruthlessly:

- Cut every word that does not earn its place
- Replace jargon with plain language
- Replace abstract words with concrete ones
- Replace passive voice with active voice
- Ensure the three components flow as one natural sentence

### Step 6: Test With Real Humans

Say the One-Liner to five people who are NOT in your industry. Observe:
- Do they ask a follow-up question? (Good)
- Do they nod and change the subject? (Bad)
- Can they repeat it back to you roughly? (Great)
- Do they look confused? (Rewrite)

### Step 7: Deploy Everywhere

Once validated, the One-Liner should appear in:
- Email signature (every team member)
- Website header or hero section
- Social media bios (LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram)
- Business cards
- Voicemail greetings
- Podcast and webinar introductions
- First slide of presentations
- Sales call opening
- Trade show booth signage
- Proposal cover pages

## Examples

### Example 1: Cybersecurity Training Platform (B2B)

**Problem:** Most companies discover their employees are a security risk only after a breach happens.

**Solution:** We provide simulated phishing exercises and micro-training that turn your team into your first line of defense.

**Result:** So you can stop worrying about the email that slips through and start trusting the people who click on it.

**Full One-Liner:** "Most companies discover their employees are a security risk only after a breach happens. We provide simulated phishing exercises and micro-training that turn your team into your first line of defense — so you stop worrying about the email that slips through and start trusting the people who click on it."

**Word count:** 54 (slightly long — could be compressed for casual use to: "Most companies only find out their team is a security risk after a breach. We train employees to spot threats before they click — so the people are the firewall, not the vulnerability.")

### Example 2: Executive Coaching Practice

**Problem:** Most new executives feel like impostors pretending to know what they're doing.

**Solution:** We pair them with a coach who has led teams through the same challenges.

**Result:** Within 90 days, they lead with confidence and their teams actually want to follow them.

**Full One-Liner:** "Most new executives feel like impostors pretending to know what they're doing. We pair them with a coach who's been in their shoes — and within 90 days, they lead with real confidence and their teams actually want to follow them."

### Example 3: Residential Solar Installation

**Problem:** Most homeowners want to go solar but get paralyzed by confusing quotes and hidden costs.

**Solution:** We handle everything — from a transparent quote to a fully installed system.

**Result:** They start saving on day one with zero surprise fees.

**Full One-Liner:** "Most homeowners want to go solar but get paralyzed by confusing quotes and hidden costs. We handle everything from a transparent quote to a fully installed system, so they save from day one with zero surprises."

### Industry-Specific Templates

**SaaS:** "Most [user type] waste [time/money] on [painful task]. [Product] automates [the task] so they can [desirable outcome] without [previous pain]."

**Professional Services:** "Most [client type] struggle with [specific challenge]. We [service mechanism] so they [measurable result] and [emotional payoff]."

**E-commerce:** "Most [customer type] are frustrated by [shopping pain]. We [differentiator] so every [product category] they buy [result promise]."

**Nonprofit:** "Every [timeframe], [alarming statistic about the problem]. We [intervention mechanism] so that [beneficiary] can [transformed outcome]."

**Agency:** "Most [client type] are tired of [agency frustration]. We [approach differentiator] so they finally get [the result they've been paying for]."

## When to Use

- **Networking events and conferences** — answer "what do you do?" compellingly
- **Team alignment** — ensure every employee can articulate the brand's value
- **Email signatures** — passive marketing in every communication
- **Website hero section** — the first thing visitors read
- **Social media bios** — maximum impact in limited characters
- **Sales call openings** — frame the conversation from the first sentence
- **Investor pitches** — open with clarity before diving into details
- **Podcast and media appearances** — introduce your brand efficiently
- **Hiring and recruiting** — attract candidates who resonate with the mission

## When NOT to Use

- **When you need depth** — the One-Liner is a hook, not an argument. Follow it with SB7-based content for conversion.
- **When speaking to experts in your field** — they may find the simplified framing reductive. Adjust the language to match their sophistication.
- **In legal or regulatory contexts** — the One-Liner's persuasive framing may conflict with required disclaimers or accuracy standards.
- **As a replacement for brand strategy** — the One-Liner summarizes strategy; it does not replace it. Build the BrandScript first.
- **When the product is genuinely complex and the audience needs complexity** — enterprise sales to technical buyers may require a more nuanced opening.

## Integration

The One-Liner is the most compressed artifact in the Miller system:

- **Derived from** the BrandScript (`miller-brand-script.md`), which is the expanded SB7 document
- **Validates** the SB7 (`miller-storybrand-sb7.md`) — if you cannot compress SB7 into a One-Liner, your SB7 is not clear enough
- **Feeds into** the Marketing Roadmap (`miller-marketing-roadmap.md`) as Step 1 of the 5-step implementation
- **Tested by** the Grunt Test (`miller-grunt-test.md`) — the One-Liner should answer all three Grunt Test questions

The One-Liner also pairs with Duarte's frameworks: use it as the opening statement before launching into a Sparkline presentation (`duarte-resonate-sparkline.md`), or as the "repeatable sound bite" in a S.T.A.R. Moment (`duarte-star-moment.md`).

## Agent Routing

**Route TO this framework when:**
- A user asks "how do I explain what my business does?"
- A user needs an elevator pitch, bio, tagline, or positioning statement
- A user is preparing for a networking event, podcast, or media appearance
- A user says their BrandScript or SB7 is done and they need to summarize it
- A user's website header is vague or generic

**Route AWAY from this framework when:**
- The user has not yet defined their SB7 or BrandScript (route to `miller-storybrand-sb7.md` or `miller-brand-script.md` first)
- The user needs full messaging, not a summary (route to `miller-brand-script.md`)
- The user needs to implement the One-Liner across channels (route to `miller-marketing-roadmap.md`)
- The user needs a presentation structure (route to `duarte-resonate-sparkline.md`)
