# Dialogue and Voice Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that dialogue sounds natural, reveals character, and carries subtext

## Purpose
Gates the quality of dialogue and distinct voice in narratives that include spoken words, quotes, or character speech. Use for scripts, case studies with customer quotes, founder stories, presentation narratives, or any content where characters speak. Ensures dialogue does double duty: advancing plot while revealing character.

## Prerequisites
- A narrative draft containing dialogue, quotes, or character speech exists
- Characters or speakers are identified with their roles and motivations
- The scene context for each dialogue exchange is established
- Tone and brand voice guidelines are available

## Checklist

### Natural Speech Patterns
- [ ] **Read-aloud test passed** — Every line of dialogue has been read aloud; lines that feel awkward, stilted, or written-sounding have been revised
- [ ] **Contractions used naturally** — Characters use contractions in casual contexts ("don't" not "do not") unless formality is intentional for that character
- [ ] **Sentence length varies** — Dialogue includes a mix of short, punchy lines and longer thoughts; no character speaks in uniformly long or short sentences
- [ ] **Filler and false starts used sparingly** — Dialogue includes occasional natural speech artifacts (hesitation, self-correction) without overdoing it
- [ ] **No exposition dumps** — Characters do not explain things they would both already know purely for the audience's benefit ("As you know, Bob...")

### Character Distinction
- [ ] **Characters sound different** — Covering speaker names, a reader can identify which character is speaking based on word choice, rhythm, and style
- [ ] **Vocabulary matches character** — Each character's word choices reflect their background, expertise, age, and personality
- [ ] **Status dynamics present** — Dialogue reveals power relationships: who defers, who interrupts, who asks questions vs. makes statements
- [ ] **Emotional state audible** — The reader can infer how the character feels from how they speak, not just from narration describing their feelings

### Subtext and Depth
- [ ] **Subtext present in key exchanges** — At least one important dialogue exchange has characters saying one thing while meaning another; not everything is on-the-nose
- [ ] **Dialogue serves dual purpose** — Each significant exchange both advances the plot/argument and reveals something about the character
- [ ] **Conflict in conversation** — Key dialogue scenes contain opposing wants; characters are not simply agreeing or narrating at each other
- [ ] **What is unsaid matters** — At least one moment relies on what a character does not say, creating tension or meaning through omission

### Voice Consistency and Appropriateness
- [ ] **Brand voice maintained** — If the narrative represents a brand, the overall dialogue tone aligns with brand guidelines without making characters sound like marketing copy
- [ ] **Tone matches context** — Serious scenes have appropriately weighted dialogue; light scenes have natural ease; no tonal mismatch
- [ ] **Cultural sensitivity verified** — Dialogue does not use stereotypical speech patterns, and any dialect or colloquialism serves character authenticity rather than caricature
- [ ] **Quotation accuracy confirmed** — If using real quotes (from interviews, testimonials), the original speaker has been accurately represented and attributed

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Read-aloud test passed
- Characters sound different
- No exposition dumps
- Subtext present in key exchanges

Any CRITICAL failure requires dialogue revision.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to opening/close review (opening-and-close-quality.md) and format-specific checklist.
- **On Fail:** Revise dialogue with the read-aloud method. Reference Johnstone's improv principles (squads/storytelling/checklists/johnstone/) for status dynamics and natural offers, or Dicks' storytelling techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for authentic voice. Consider interviewing real people to capture genuine speech patterns for case studies and testimonials.
