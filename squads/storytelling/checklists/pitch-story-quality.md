# Pitch Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a pitch narrative persuades, frames, and drives a decision

## Purpose
Gates the narrative effectiveness of any pitch: investor pitch, sales pitch, partnership
proposal, internal budget request, grant application, or board presentation. Use after the
pitch narrative is drafted and before rehearsal or delivery. Ensures the pitch controls the
frame from the first moment, establishes value through story rather than bullet points,
creates and sustains tension, and makes the next step so obvious that inaction feels like
a missed opportunity.

## When to Use
- After a pitch narrative or deck script is drafted and before rehearsal
- Before any high-stakes meeting: investor pitch, board presentation, sales demo, partnership proposal
- When a pitch receives feedback like "too much information" or "I didn't feel compelled"
- When adapting a pitch for a different audience segment or decision-maker level
- Before pitch competitions, demo days, or public presentations

## Prerequisites
- A complete pitch narrative or deck script exists
- The audience (who is being pitched) is identified with their priorities, concerns, and decision criteria
- The ask (investment amount, purchase decision, partnership terms, budget approval) is defined
- Competitive alternatives are understood from the audience's perspective
- Available proof, credentials, and traction metrics are catalogued
- Time constraint for the pitch is known

## Checklist

### Frame Control
- [ ] **Pitch sets the frame first** — The narrative establishes how the audience should interpret everything that follows within the first 15 seconds or 2 slides; the pitcher defines the category
- [ ] **Frame is big-picture** — The pitch starts with a macro trend, market shift, or fundamental change that creates the opportunity, not with the product, company history, or team slide
- [ ] **Prize frame established** — The pitch positions the opportunity as something the audience would be fortunate to participate in, not something the pitcher desperately needs them to approve
- [ ] **Status frame managed** — The pitcher maintains peer-level status with the audience throughout; the narrative does not grovel, over-explain, seek validation, or apologize for taking time
- [ ] **Analyst frame prevented** — The pitch does not trigger spreadsheet-thinking prematurely; it maintains storytelling mode through the narrative arc before inviting analytical scrutiny
- [ ] **Reframe prepared** — The pitch includes at least one moment that reframes an expected objection or assumption before the audience can voice it

### Value and Differentiation
- [ ] **Problem is validated first** — The narrative proves the problem exists and is significant before introducing the solution, using data, an anecdote, or the audience's own experience
- [ ] **Value proposition stated in one sentence** — A clear, jargon-free statement explains what is offered and why it matters, deliverable in under 20 words and under 10 seconds
- [ ] **Differentiation is structural** — The reason this solution wins is rooted in an approach, insight, technology, or position that competitors cannot easily replicate
- [ ] **"Why you" answered** — The narrative explains why this team, founder, or company is uniquely positioned to win, with proof from track record, domain expertise, or unfair advantages
- [ ] **Market timing justified** — The pitch explains why this opportunity exists now (not 3 years ago or 3 years from now) with evidence of market readiness

### Tension and Engagement
- [ ] **Tension present throughout** — The pitch does not simply present facts in sequence; it creates unresolved questions, contrasts, and stakes that pull the audience forward
- [ ] **Ticking clock or urgency** — A genuine reason for timely action is woven into the narrative: competitive window, market momentum, first-mover advantage, or regulatory change
- [ ] **Objections preempted** — At least two common objections are addressed within the narrative flow before the audience can raise them, reducing resistance without being defensive
- [ ] **Audience's self-interest engaged** — The pitch connects to what the specific audience wants (returns, efficiency, reputation, risk reduction, innovation credit), not just what the pitcher wants

### Ask and Next Step
- [ ] **Ask is specific** — The pitch requests a clear, concrete action: a specific investment amount, a defined pilot program, a meeting by a date, a decision with parameters
- [ ] **Ask is proportional** — The requested commitment matches the evidence and relationship stage; the pitch does not over-ask strangers or under-ask champions
- [ ] **Next step is obvious and easy** — The audience knows exactly what to do after the pitch ends, and the barrier to that first action is as low as possible
- [ ] **Closing creates urgency without desperation** — The final beat reinforces why acting now is in the audience's interest, using evidence and framing rather than pressure

### Narrative Quality
- [ ] **Pitch tells a story, not just presents information** — The narrative has characters, tension, causality, and resolution, not just a sequence of slides with data and bullet points
- [ ] **Memorable moment exists** — There is at least one image, analogy, demonstration, or story beat that the audience will remember and retell to colleagues who were not in the room
- [ ] **Pitch fits the time constraint** — The narrative is rehearsed to use 80% of the allotted time window, leaving 20% for questions and organic conversation

## Scoring
Must pass **17/22 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Pitch sets the frame first
- Value proposition stated in one sentence
- Ask is specific
- Pitch tells a story, not just presents information
- Problem is validated first

Any CRITICAL failure requires pitch revision before delivery.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to rehearsal and delivery preparation. Rehearse at least 3 times with timing.
  Run deck-story-quality.md if a slide deck accompanies the pitch. Prepare for likely questions
  using improv-readiness-quality.md.
- **On Fail:** Restructure the pitch from the audience's perspective: what do they care about,
  what are they afraid of, and what would make them act? Reference Klaff's Pitch Anything
  framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/klaff/) for frame control and tension, Miller's
  StoryBrand (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for message clarity and audience-as-hero
  positioning, or the pitch framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/pitch/) for overall pitch
  architecture. Rehearse with a hostile-audience simulation and revise based on where the
  pitch loses the room.
