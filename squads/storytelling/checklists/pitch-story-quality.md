# Pitch Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a pitch narrative persuades, frames, and drives a decision

## Purpose
Gates the narrative effectiveness of any pitch: investor pitch, sales pitch, partnership proposal, internal budget pitch, or grant application. Use after the pitch narrative is drafted and before rehearsal or delivery. Ensures the pitch controls the frame, establishes value, creates tension, and makes the next step obvious.

## Prerequisites
- A complete pitch narrative or deck script exists
- The audience (who is being pitched) is identified with their priorities and objections
- The ask (investment, purchase, partnership, approval) is defined
- Competitive alternatives are understood
- Available proof and credentials are catalogued

## Checklist

### Frame Control
- [ ] **Pitch sets the frame first** — The narrative establishes how the audience should interpret everything that follows within the first 15 seconds or 2 slides
- [ ] **Frame is big-picture** — The pitch starts with a macro trend, market shift, or fundamental change, not with the product or company
- [ ] **Prize frame established** — The pitch positions the opportunity as something the audience would be fortunate to participate in, not something the pitcher desperately needs
- [ ] **Status frame managed** — The pitcher maintains peer-level status with the audience; the narrative does not grovel, over-explain, or seek validation
- [ ] **Analyst frame prevented** — The pitch does not trigger spreadsheet-thinking; it maintains storytelling mode until the appropriate moment for details

### Value and Differentiation
- [ ] **Problem is validated** — The narrative proves the problem exists and is significant before introducing the solution, using data or a resonant anecdote
- [ ] **Value proposition stated in one sentence** — A clear, jargon-free statement explains what is offered and why it matters, in under 20 words
- [ ] **Differentiation is structural** — The reason this solution is different is rooted in an approach, insight, or position that competitors cannot easily copy
- [ ] **"Why you" answered** — The narrative explains why this team, founder, or company is uniquely positioned to win, with proof

### Tension and Engagement
- [ ] **Tension is present throughout** — The pitch does not simply present facts; it creates unresolved questions that pull the audience forward
- [ ] **Ticking clock or urgency** — A genuine reason for timely action is woven into the narrative (market window, competitive threat, momentum)
- [ ] **Objections preempted** — At least two common objections are addressed within the narrative flow before the audience can raise them
- [ ] **Audience's self-interest engaged** — The pitch connects to what the specific audience wants (returns, efficiency, reputation, risk reduction), not just what the pitcher wants

### Ask and Next Step
- [ ] **Ask is specific** — The pitch requests a clear, concrete action: a specific investment amount, a pilot program, a meeting, a decision by a date
- [ ] **Ask is proportional** — The requested commitment matches the evidence and relationship stage; the pitch does not over-ask or under-ask
- [ ] **Next step is obvious and easy** — The audience knows exactly what to do after the pitch ends, and the barrier to that action is low
- [ ] **Closing creates urgency without desperation** — The end of the pitch reinforces why acting now is in the audience's interest

### Narrative Quality
- [ ] **Pitch tells a story, not just presents information** — The narrative has characters, tension, and resolution, not just bullets and data
- [ ] **Memorable moment exists** — There is at least one image, analogy, or story beat that the audience will remember and retell
- [ ] **Pitch fits the time constraint** — The narrative is timed to use 80% of the allotted window, leaving room for dialogue

## Scoring
Must pass **15/20 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Pitch sets the frame first
- Value proposition stated in one sentence
- Ask is specific
- Pitch tells a story, not just presents information

Any CRITICAL failure requires pitch revision before delivery.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to rehearsal and delivery preparation. Run deck-story-quality.md if a slide deck accompanies the pitch.
- **On Fail:** Restructure the pitch. Reference Klaff's Pitch Anything framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/klaff/) for frame control and tension, Miller's StoryBrand (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for message clarity, or the pitch framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/pitch/) for overall pitch architecture. Rehearse with a hostile-audience simulation before revising.
