# Emotional Arc Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative delivers a deliberate, well-paced emotional journey

## Purpose
Gates the emotional design of a narrative. Stories succeed or fail based on how they make the audience feel, not just what they communicate. Use this checklist after structure and content are solid, to ensure the emotional experience is intentional, varied, and builds to a satisfying payoff. Applies to all narrative formats.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists with established structure and pacing
- The target emotional response (what the audience should feel at the end) is defined
- The audience's likely starting emotional state is understood
- The narrative has passed structural review

## Checklist

### Emotional Mapping
- [ ] **Emotional peaks identified** — At least two distinct high-emotion moments are present and can be pinpointed in the narrative (joy, anger, surprise, fear, hope, relief)
- [ ] **Emotional valleys identified** — At least one moment of lower emotional intensity exists between peaks, providing contrast and recovery
- [ ] **Emotional variety present** — The narrative evokes at least three different emotions across its length, not a single sustained note
- [ ] **Opening emotion established** — The first 10% of the narrative creates a specific emotional entry point (curiosity, concern, delight, tension) that is intentional, not accidental
- [ ] **Closing emotion designed** — The final emotional beat is the one the audience should carry away; it matches the strategic intent of the narrative

### Pacing and Rhythm
- [ ] **Tension and release alternate** — The narrative does not sustain high tension for more than 25% of its length without a release valve (humor, reflection, beauty, simplicity)
- [ ] **Pacing matches emotional intent** — High-emotion moments use shorter sentences, paragraphs, or cuts; reflective moments slow down with longer, more spacious language
- [ ] **No emotional monotone** — The narrative does not maintain the same emotional register for more than 3 consecutive sections or paragraphs
- [ ] **Breathing room exists** — At least one moment in the narrative gives the audience space to process what they have felt before the next emotional demand

### Payoff and Resolution
- [ ] **Emotional payoff earned** — The climactic emotional moment is set up by earlier beats; it does not arrive without groundwork (the audience must care before they can feel)
- [ ] **Peak-end rule applied** — The two most intense moments are (1) the emotional peak and (2) the very end; these are the most carefully crafted beats in the narrative
- [ ] **Resolution provides emotional closure** — The audience is not left in unresolved emotional tension unless that is a deliberate strategic choice (e.g., a cliffhanger for a series)
- [ ] **Catharsis present** — The narrative provides a release of built-up emotional tension through resolution, insight, or a decisive action moment

### Authenticity and Calibration
- [ ] **Emotions are authentic, not manipulative** — Emotional beats arise naturally from the story's events and characters, not from manipulation techniques (exploitative imagery, manufactured sentimentality)
- [ ] **Emotional intensity matches the stakes** — The level of emotional charge is proportional to what is actually at risk in the story; small stakes do not receive disproportionate emotional treatment
- [ ] **Audience emotional capacity respected** — The narrative does not overwhelm the audience with too many intense moments; emotional demands are calibrated to the format and context
- [ ] **Vulnerability is earned** — Any vulnerable or deeply emotional moment is preceded by enough trust-building that it feels appropriate, not forced

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Emotional peaks identified
- Closing emotion designed
- Emotional payoff earned
- Peak-end rule applied

Any CRITICAL failure requires an emotional arc revision.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to final editing (editing-and-trimming-quality.md) and format-specific review.
- **On Fail:** Map the emotional arc visually: plot emotion (y-axis) against narrative progression (x-axis) and identify flat spots or misplaced peaks. Reference Duarte's Sparkline (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) for contrast-driven emotional design, Snyder's Beat Sheet (squads/storytelling/checklists/snyder/) for beat-level emotional pacing, or Dicks' storytelling techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for authentic emotional moments. Test by reading aloud and noting where you feel nothing.
