# Emotional Arc Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative delivers a deliberate, well-paced emotional journey

## Purpose
Gates the emotional design of a narrative. Stories succeed or fail based on how they make the
audience feel, not just what they communicate. Information is forgotten; emotion is remembered.
Use this checklist after structure and content are solid, to ensure the emotional experience is
intentional, varied, and builds to a satisfying payoff. Applies to all narrative formats:
presentations, videos, emails, pitches, case studies, brand stories, and keynotes. If the
audience feels nothing, the narrative has failed regardless of how clear or well-structured it is.

## When to Use
- After structure and content pass their respective checklists
- When a narrative is technically correct but "doesn't move me" or "feels flat"
- For any narrative where emotional engagement drives the desired outcome
- Before finalizing keynotes, fundraising appeals, brand films, or campaign content
- During revision to diagnose why a well-structured narrative underperforms

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists with established structure and pacing
- The target emotional response (what the audience should feel at the end) is defined and documented
- The audience's likely starting emotional state is understood (excited, skeptical, bored, anxious, curious)
- The narrative has passed structural review (structure-and-pacing-quality.md)
- The key moments and turning points of the narrative have been identified

## Checklist

### Emotional Mapping
- [ ] **Emotional peaks identified** — At least two distinct high-emotion moments are present and can be pinpointed to specific locations in the narrative (moments of joy, anger, surprise, fear, hope, relief, or pride)
- [ ] **Emotional valleys identified** — At least one moment of lower emotional intensity exists between peaks, providing contrast, recovery, and room for the audience to process
- [ ] **Emotional variety present** — The narrative evokes at least three different emotions across its length; it does not sustain a single emotional note from start to finish
- [ ] **Opening emotion established** — The first 10% of the narrative creates a specific emotional entry point (curiosity, concern, delight, tension, recognition) that is intentional and designed, not accidental
- [ ] **Closing emotion designed** — The final emotional beat is the one the audience will carry away; it matches the strategic intent of the narrative (hope, determination, urgency, confidence, belonging)
- [ ] **Emotional map documented** — Someone on the team can draw the emotional arc: a rough plot of emotional intensity (y-axis) against narrative progression (x-axis) with named emotions at each peak and valley

### Pacing and Rhythm
- [ ] **Tension and release alternate** — The narrative does not sustain high tension for more than 25% of its length without a release valve: humor, reflection, beauty, simplicity, or a quiet human moment
- [ ] **Pacing matches emotional intent** — High-emotion moments use shorter sentences, faster cuts, or more direct language; reflective moments slow down with longer, more spacious language and imagery
- [ ] **No emotional monotone** — The narrative does not maintain the same emotional register for more than 3 consecutive sections, paragraphs, or slides without a shift
- [ ] **Breathing room exists** — At least one moment in the narrative gives the audience space to absorb and process what they have felt before the next emotional demand arrives
- [ ] **Escalation is graduated** — Emotional intensity builds progressively through the narrative; it does not spike to maximum in the first third and then plateau

### Payoff and Resolution
- [ ] **Emotional payoff earned** — The climactic emotional moment is set up by earlier beats; the audience must care about the characters, stakes, or outcome before the payoff can land
- [ ] **Peak-end rule applied** — The two most carefully crafted emotional moments are: (1) the highest emotional peak anywhere in the narrative and (2) the very last emotional beat; these two moments determine what the audience remembers
- [ ] **Resolution provides emotional closure** — The audience is not left in unresolved emotional distress unless that is a deliberate, strategic choice (e.g., a cliffhanger in a series, or urgency before a CTA)
- [ ] **Catharsis present** — The narrative provides a release of built-up emotional tension through resolution, insight, action, or a decisive moment that transforms accumulated tension into forward energy

### Authenticity and Calibration
- [ ] **Emotions are authentic, not manipulative** — Emotional beats arise naturally from the story's events, characters, and stakes, not from manipulation techniques (exploitative imagery, manufactured sentimentality, sob stories disconnected from the narrative)
- [ ] **Emotional intensity matches the stakes** — The level of emotional charge is proportional to what is actually at risk; minor topics do not receive disproportionate emotional treatment
- [ ] **Audience emotional capacity respected** — The narrative does not overwhelm the audience with too many intense moments in sequence; emotional demands are calibrated to the format, context, and audience expectations
- [ ] **Vulnerability is earned** — Any deeply vulnerable or emotional moment is preceded by enough trust-building and narrative context that it feels appropriate and brave, not forced, premature, or attention-seeking

### Emotional Mechanics
- [ ] **Specificity drives emotion** — Emotional moments use concrete, specific details rather than abstract generalizations; "her son's hand-drawn card was still on the fridge" beats "she missed her family"
- [ ] **Show before tell** — The audience feels the emotion before being told what to feel; the narration does not pre-label emotions ("It was a heartbreaking moment") before demonstrating them
- [ ] **Contrast amplifies impact** — The most powerful emotional moments are preceded by a contrasting emotional state: joy before loss, tension before relief, doubt before triumph
- [ ] **Humor is used strategically** — If humor appears, it serves the emotional arc (breaking tension, building connection, creating contrast for a serious beat) rather than undermining it

## Scoring
Must pass **18/25 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Emotional peaks identified
- Closing emotion designed
- Emotional payoff earned
- Peak-end rule applied
- Emotions are authentic, not manipulative

Any CRITICAL failure requires an emotional arc revision pass.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to final editing (editing-and-trimming-quality.md) and format-specific
  review. The emotional layer is solid and intentional.
- **On Fail:** Map the emotional arc visually: draw a graph plotting emotion against narrative
  progression. Identify flat spots (no emotion), spikes without setup (unearned emotion), and
  sustained monotone sections. Reference Duarte's Sparkline (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/)
  for contrast-driven emotional design, Snyder's Beat Sheet (squads/storytelling/checklists/snyder/)
  for beat-level emotional pacing, Dicks' storytelling techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/)
  for authentic emotional moments, or Campbell's Hero's Journey (squads/storytelling/checklists/campbell/)
  for archetypal emotional patterns. Test by reading aloud and noting exactly where you feel
  something and where you feel nothing; the "nothing" spots need work.
