# Improv Readiness Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a presenter, facilitator, or spokesperson is prepared for unscripted moments

## Purpose
Gates the readiness of individuals who will face unscripted, dynamic situations: Q&A sessions, live demos, panel discussions, media interviews, sales calls, or any scenario where the narrative must adapt in real time. Use before any high-stakes live appearance. Based on Johnstone's improv principles and applied storytelling techniques.

## Prerequisites
- The presenter/speaker has been identified
- The core message and key talking points are established
- The likely audience and their concerns are understood
- The format (Q&A, panel, interview, live pitch, workshop) is defined
- The presenter has had at least one practice session

## Checklist

### Status Awareness
- [ ] **Default status recognized** — The presenter can articulate their natural status tendency (high-status: commanding/rigid or low-status: deferring/nervous) and how it affects audience dynamics
- [ ] **Status flexibility demonstrated** — In practice sessions, the presenter can shift between high and low status intentionally: deferring to an expert question, then reasserting authority on their domain
- [ ] **Audience status read** — The presenter can identify whether the audience is hostile, supportive, skeptical, or disengaged and adjust approach accordingly
- [ ] **Status mismatch avoided** — The presenter does not default to high-status lecturing when the audience needs engagement, or low-status apologizing when the audience needs confidence

### Active Listening and Acceptance
- [ ] **"Yes, and" instinct present** — In practice Q&A, the presenter builds on audience input rather than blocking, correcting, or dismissing it
- [ ] **Questions are actually answered** — The presenter addresses the substance of questions asked, not the question they wish had been asked
- [ ] **Listening before responding** — The presenter pauses to fully hear questions before answering; no interrupting or pre-formulated response jumping
- [ ] **Hostile questions reframed, not fought** — The presenter can take an adversarial question, acknowledge its validity, and redirect to productive territory without being defensive

### Presence and Embodiment
- [ ] **Physical presence grounded** — The presenter demonstrates stable posture, deliberate movement, and appropriate eye contact; no pacing, fidgeting, or hiding behind the podium
- [ ] **Silence is comfortable** — The presenter can hold a 3-second pause without filling it with "um," "so," or nervous laughter
- [ ] **Energy matches the room** — The presenter reads the room's energy level and calibrates: not overly hyped in a serious setting, not flat in an energetic one
- [ ] **Authentic recovery from mistakes** — In practice, the presenter demonstrates the ability to acknowledge errors, missteps, or technical failures with grace rather than panic

### Rhythm and Adaptability
- [ ] **Key messages accessible under pressure** — The presenter can articulate the 3 core messages in any order, at any length (10 seconds, 30 seconds, 2 minutes) without notes
- [ ] **Bridge phrases practiced** — The presenter has rehearsed 3-5 bridging phrases to move from any topic back to key messages ("What I think is most important here is..." / "What that really comes down to is...")
- [ ] **Story bank loaded** — The presenter has at least 5 short anecdotes or examples ready to deploy for different types of questions or challenges
- [ ] **Time awareness demonstrated** — The presenter can expand or compress answers based on remaining time without losing coherence
- [ ] **Surprise scenario survived** — In a practice session, the presenter successfully handled at least one unexpected curveball (hostile question, technical failure, off-topic tangent) without losing composure

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- "Yes, and" instinct present
- Questions are actually answered
- Key messages accessible under pressure
- Surprise scenario survived

Any CRITICAL failure requires additional rehearsal before the live event.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to the live event with confidence. Brief the presenter on known audience dynamics and likely questions.
- **On Fail:** Schedule additional improv-style rehearsal sessions. Reference Johnstone's improv principles (squads/storytelling/checklists/johnstone/) for status work and offer acceptance, and Howell's techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/howell/) for presence and delivery. Conduct mock Q&A with a hostile questioner. Record and review practice sessions for unconscious habits.
