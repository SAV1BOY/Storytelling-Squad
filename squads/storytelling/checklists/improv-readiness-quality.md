# Improv Readiness Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a presenter or spokesperson is prepared for unscripted moments

## Purpose
Gates the readiness of individuals who will face unscripted, dynamic, or partially improvised
situations: Q&A sessions after presentations, live product demos, panel discussions, media
interviews, sales discovery calls, investor meetings, or any scenario where the narrative must
adapt in real time. Use before any high-stakes live appearance where the script ends but the
conversation continues. Based on Johnstone's improv principles applied to professional
storytelling and communication contexts.

## When to Use
- Before any live event with a Q&A, panel, or unscripted interaction component
- Before media interviews, podcast guest appearances, or press conferences
- Before sales demos or investor meetings where questions are expected
- When a presenter has struggled with live questions or unexpected situations in past events
- During spokesperson training or executive communications coaching

## Prerequisites
- The presenter/speaker has been identified and is available for assessment
- The core message, key talking points, and brand voice are established
- The likely audience, their concerns, and probable questions are understood
- The format (Q&A, panel, interview, live pitch, workshop, sales call) is defined
- The presenter has had at least one full practice session or mock run
- Known hostile questions or difficult topics have been listed

## Checklist

### Status Awareness
- [ ] **Default status recognized** — The presenter can articulate their natural status tendency (high-status: commanding, rigid, dominant; or low-status: deferring, nervous, over-qualifying) and how it reads to an audience
- [ ] **Status flexibility demonstrated** — In practice sessions, the presenter can shift between high and low status intentionally: deferring respectfully to an expert, then reasserting authority in their domain
- [ ] **Audience status read** — The presenter can identify in real time whether the audience is hostile, supportive, skeptical, confused, or disengaged, and names what they observe
- [ ] **Status mismatch avoided** — The presenter does not default to high-status lecturing when the audience needs engagement and dialogue, or low-status apologizing when the audience needs confidence and direction

### Active Listening and Acceptance
- [ ] **"Yes, and" instinct present** — In practice Q&A, the presenter builds on audience input rather than blocking, dismissing, or immediately correcting it, even when redirecting
- [ ] **Questions are actually answered** — The presenter addresses the substance of questions asked, including uncomfortable ones, not the question they wish had been asked instead
- [ ] **Listening before responding** — The presenter pauses to fully hear questions and process before answering; no interrupting, pre-formulated response jumping, or finishing the questioner's sentence
- [ ] **Hostile questions reframed, not fought** — The presenter can take an adversarial question, acknowledge its validity or the concern behind it, and redirect to productive territory without becoming defensive, dismissive, or combative
- [ ] **Follow-up questions welcomed** — The presenter does not shut down threads prematurely; they invite deeper engagement when the topic is productive

### Presence and Embodiment
- [ ] **Physical presence grounded** — The presenter demonstrates stable posture, deliberate movement, and appropriate eye contact; no pacing, fidgeting, crossed arms, or hiding behind the podium
- [ ] **Silence is comfortable** — The presenter can hold a 3-5 second pause after a question or before a key point without filling it with "um," "so," "like," nervous laughter, or throat-clearing
- [ ] **Energy matches the room** — The presenter reads the room's energy level and calibrates accordingly: not overly hyped in a serious board meeting, not flat in an energetic launch event
- [ ] **Authentic recovery from mistakes** — In practice, the presenter demonstrates graceful handling of errors, missteps, wrong slides, technical failures, or forgotten points without panic or overcompensating
- [ ] **Breathing is controlled** — The presenter does not rush through answers out of nervousness; their speech pace allows the audience to absorb information

### Rhythm and Adaptability
- [ ] **Key messages accessible under pressure** — The presenter can articulate the 3-5 core messages in any order, at any length (10 seconds, 30 seconds, 2 minutes) without consulting notes
- [ ] **Bridge phrases practiced** — The presenter has rehearsed 3-5 bridging phrases to move from any topic back to key messages ("What's most important here is..." / "That connects to the bigger point, which is..." / "I'd frame it slightly differently...")
- [ ] **Story bank loaded** — The presenter has at least 5 short anecdotes, examples, or analogies ready to deploy for different types of questions, objections, or audience interest areas
- [ ] **Time awareness demonstrated** — The presenter can expand or compress answers based on remaining time without losing coherence or critical information
- [ ] **Surprise scenario survived** — In a mock session, the presenter successfully handled at least one unexpected curveball (hostile question, technical failure, off-topic tangent, emotional audience member) without losing composure or the thread

### Preparation and Boundaries
- [ ] **No-go topics identified** — The presenter knows which topics, claims, or commitments are off-limits and has rehearsed graceful ways to decline or redirect without creating awkwardness
- [ ] **"I don't know" is comfortable** — The presenter can say "I don't have that data right now, but I'll follow up" without losing credibility or confidence; they do not bluff
- [ ] **Opening and closing are memorized** — Even if the middle is improvised, the presenter has the first 30 seconds and last 30 seconds internalized to ensure strong starts and endings
- [ ] **Technical contingency planned** — The presenter has a plan for what to do if slides fail, internet drops, or audio breaks: continue presenting from memory, switch to storytelling, or smoothly transition to Q&A

## Scoring
Must pass **17/23 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- "Yes, and" instinct present
- Questions are actually answered
- Key messages accessible under pressure
- Surprise scenario survived
- Authentic recovery from mistakes

Any CRITICAL failure requires additional rehearsal sessions before the live event.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to the live event with confidence. Brief the presenter on specific known
  audience dynamics and the 3 most likely difficult questions. Prepare a one-page cheat sheet
  of bridging phrases and key stats.
- **On Fail:** Schedule additional improv-style rehearsal sessions, ideally with an experienced
  facilitator. Reference Johnstone's improv principles (squads/storytelling/checklists/johnstone/)
  for status work, offer acceptance, and spontaneity. Reference Howell's techniques
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/howell/) for physical presence and vocal delivery. Conduct
  a mock Q&A with someone playing a hostile or skeptical questioner. Record practice sessions
  and review for unconscious habits (filler words, defensive body language, question avoidance).
  Consider a 2-hour improv workshop focused on professional scenarios.
