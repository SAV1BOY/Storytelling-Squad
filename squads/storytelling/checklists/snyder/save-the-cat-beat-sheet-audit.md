# Save the Cat Beat Sheet Audit
> Quality gate for verifying all 15 Blake Snyder beats are present, properly timed, and dramatically clear

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative follows the Save the Cat beat sheet structure with all 15 beats accounted for, each hitting at the right moment in the story and delivering clear turning points (viradas) that propel the audience forward.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft or detailed outline
- Familiarity with Blake Snyder's 15-beat structure
- Story length or runtime established for timing calculations

## Checklist

### Act One Beats
- [ ] **Opening Image Present (Beat 1)** — A single image or moment establishes tone, mood, and the "before" snapshot; it contrasts with the Final Image
- [ ] **Theme Stated (Beat 2)** — Someone states the thematic premise within the first 10% of the narrative, even if the hero does not yet understand it
- [ ] **Set-Up Complete (Beat 3)** — All major characters, the hero's world, and the "things that need fixing" are introduced before the Catalyst
- [ ] **Catalyst Arrives (Beat 4)** — A life-changing event occurs at roughly the 10-12% mark; it is external, specific, and undeniable
- [ ] **Debate Present (Beat 5)** — The hero questions whether to accept the call; this section lasts long enough to feel the weight of the decision

### Act Two Beats
- [ ] **Break into Two Clear (Beat 6)** — The hero makes an active choice to enter the new world at approximately the 20-25% mark; it is not forced upon them
- [ ] **B Story Introduced (Beat 7)** — A secondary storyline (often a relationship) begins that will carry the theme
- [ ] **Fun and Games Delivered (Beat 8)** — The "promise of the premise" is fulfilled; this section delivers what the audience came for
- [ ] **Midpoint Hits (Beat 9)** — A major event at the 50% mark raises stakes (false victory or false defeat); the story shifts from fun to serious
- [ ] **Bad Guys Close In (Beat 10)** — External pressures and internal doubts intensify between midpoint and All Is Lost; the walls close in
- [ ] **All Is Lost Moment (Beat 11)** — The hero hits bottom at roughly the 75% mark; there is a "whiff of death" (literal or metaphorical)
- [ ] **Dark Night of the Soul (Beat 12)** — The hero processes the loss; this beat has emotional depth and is not rushed past

### Act Three Beats
- [ ] **Break into Three Clear (Beat 13)** — A new idea or synthesis of A and B stories propels the hero into the finale with renewed clarity
- [ ] **Finale Delivers (Beat 14)** — The hero applies lessons learned, defeats the antagonist or solves the problem using growth from the journey
- [ ] **Final Image Present (Beat 15)** — A closing image mirrors and contrasts the Opening Image, proving transformation has occurred

### Timing and Viradas
- [ ] **Beat Timing Within Tolerance** — Each beat lands within 5% of its expected position, or deviation is justified by the narrative's needs
- [ ] **Viradas Are Clear** — Every major turning point (Catalyst, Midpoint, All Is Lost, Break into Three) creates an unmistakable shift the audience can feel
- [ ] **No Missing Beats Without Justification** — If any beat is intentionally omitted, the reason is documented and the narrative still functions

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 18 items checked; beats land with precision and emotional impact |
| Conditional | 14-17 items checked; beats present but timing or clarity needs adjustment |
| Fail | Fewer than 14 items, or more than 2 beats missing without justification |
