# Genre and Promise Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the genre is identified, its promise delivered, and obligatory moments present

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative identifies its genre (using Snyder's 10 genre types or equivalent), fulfills the implicit promise that genre makes to the audience, and includes the obligatory scenes and conventions audiences expect.

## Prerequisites
- Genre identified from Snyder's 10 types (Monster in the House, Golden Fleece, Out of the Bottle, Dude with a Problem, Rites of Passage, Buddy Love, Whydunit, The Fool Triumphant, Institutionalized, Superhero)
- Complete draft or detailed outline
- Audience expectations for the genre documented

## Checklist

### Genre Identification
- [ ] **Genre Explicitly Named** — The story's Snyder genre type is documented with a brief rationale
- [ ] **Genre Fits the Core Conflict** — The genre choice aligns with the fundamental nature of the conflict, not just surface elements
- [ ] **Not Genre-Confused** — The story commits to one primary genre; if it blends genres, the primary is dominant and the secondary is subordinate

### Promise of the Genre
- [ ] **Promise Articulated** — The specific experience the audience expects from this genre is written out (e.g., Monster in the House promises escalating dread and a final confrontation)
- [ ] **Promise Delivered in Full** — The narrative provides the emotional and dramatic experience the genre promises; no bait-and-switch
- [ ] **Fun and Games Section Fulfills Promise** — The "promise of the premise" section of the beat sheet specifically delivers genre-expected moments
- [ ] **Audience Satisfaction Test** — A genre-savvy reader would feel the story delivered what they signed up for

### Obligatory Scenes
- [ ] **Genre Obligatory Scenes Identified** — The specific scenes required by the genre are listed (e.g., Golden Fleece requires road milestones; Buddy Love requires a breakup)
- [ ] **All Obligatory Scenes Present** — Each identified obligatory scene exists in the narrative or its absence is deliberately justified
- [ ] **Obligatory Scenes Feel Fresh** — Required scenes are executed with originality; they meet expectations without being predictable
- [ ] **Emotional Beats of Obligatory Scenes Land** — Each obligatory scene delivers its expected emotional payload (fear, joy, tension, relief)

### Genre Conventions
- [ ] **Conventions Honored** — Genre conventions (character types, settings, tonal rules) are respected throughout
- [ ] **Subversions Are Intentional** — Any departure from convention is a deliberate creative choice with payoff, not an oversight
- [ ] **No Convention Violations Without Reward** — Breaking a convention creates a better experience for the audience than following it would have

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 14 items checked; genre promise is fulfilled with craft and freshness |
| Conditional | 10-13 items checked; genre is clear but some obligatory scenes need strengthening |
| Fail | Fewer than 10 items, or the genre promise is fundamentally undelivered |

## Next Steps
- If genre is confused, commit to the primary genre and restructure the conflicting elements
- List all obligatory scenes for the identified genre and verify each one against the draft
- If obligatory scenes feel stale, study three exemplars in the same genre for fresh approaches
