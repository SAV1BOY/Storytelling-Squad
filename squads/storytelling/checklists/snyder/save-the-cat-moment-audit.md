# Save the Cat Moment Audit
> Quality gate for verifying an empathy-building moment exists early and the audience roots for the hero

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative includes an early moment that makes the audience like, empathize with, or root for the protagonist. This "Save the Cat" moment is the foundation of audience investment; without it, no amount of plot will matter.

## Prerequisites
- First 15% of the narrative drafted
- Protagonist defined with clear strengths and flaws
- Target audience identified

## Checklist

### Empathy Moment Existence
- [ ] **Save the Cat Moment Exists** — There is a specific, identifiable scene or action in the first 10-15% where the hero does something that earns audience sympathy
- [ ] **Moment Is Active** — The hero performs an action (helps someone, shows vulnerability, makes a sacrifice) rather than passively receiving sympathy
- [ ] **Moment Is Early Enough** — The empathy beat occurs before the audience has a chance to disengage; it is not buried deep in the setup

### Likability Established
- [ ] **Hero Is Relatable** — The audience can see themselves in the hero's situation, emotions, or desires, even if the hero's circumstances are extraordinary
- [ ] **Hero Has a Recognizable Flaw** — The hero is imperfect in a way that makes them human, not annoying; the flaw creates empathy rather than frustration
- [ ] **Competence or Wit Shown** — The hero demonstrates at least one quality (skill, humor, intelligence, kindness) that makes the audience want to spend time with them
- [ ] **Underdog Quality Present** — The hero faces a disadvantage, injustice, or obstacle that makes the audience want them to succeed

### Audience Roots for Hero
- [ ] **Rooting Interest Clear** — By the end of the setup, the audience can articulate what they want for the hero, even if the hero cannot
- [ ] **Emotional Investment Testable** — A test reader reports feeling concerned about the hero's outcome before the main conflict begins
- [ ] **Villain or Obstacle Creates Sympathy** — The antagonist or opposing force is established in a way that makes the audience side with the hero instinctively
- [ ] **Not Just Pity** — The audience admires or likes the hero, not merely feels sorry for them; pity without respect does not sustain investment

### Craft and Integration
- [ ] **Moment Feels Organic** — The Save the Cat beat arises naturally from the story situation, not as an obvious audience-manipulation tactic
- [ ] **Moment Connects to Theme** — The empathy-building action hints at the hero's deeper nature or the story's thematic concern
- [ ] **Moment Is Specific and Concrete** — The beat involves a particular action in a particular setting, not a vague impression of niceness
- [ ] **Balance of Likability and Complexity** — The hero is likable without being bland; their Save the Cat moment coexists with rough edges

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the audience is emotionally invested before the Catalyst |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; empathy exists but could be stronger or earlier |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or no identifiable empathy moment in the first 15% |

## Next Steps
- If the moment feels manipulative, ground it in a character-specific action that only this hero would take
- If the hero is not likable enough, add a small act of unforced kindness or self-deprecating humor
- Test with three readers: ask them to describe the hero after reading only the first 15%
