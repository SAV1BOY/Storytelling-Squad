# Audience Journey Mapping Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the audience's emotional state is tracked from resistance through acceptance to action

## Purpose
Ensure the presentation is designed around the audience's emotional journey, not just the presenter's content. The audience moves from their initial state (often resistance, skepticism, or indifference) through understanding and acceptance to committed action. Every section of the presentation should advance this journey.

## Prerequisites
- Audience profile documented (knowledge level, emotional state, attitudes, motivations)
- Presentation outline or draft completed
- Desired audience state at the end of the presentation defined

## Checklist

### Audience Starting State
- [ ] **Initial Emotional State Documented** — The audience's likely mindset at the start is articulated: skeptical, curious, hostile, overwhelmed, hopeful, indifferent
- [ ] **Knowledge Level Assessed** — What the audience already knows (and does not know) is documented, preventing both over-explaining and under-explaining
- [ ] **Resistance Points Identified** — Specific objections, fears, or competing priorities the audience brings are listed and addressed in the presentation

### Resistance-to-Acceptance Arc
- [ ] **Resistance Acknowledged** — The presentation explicitly or implicitly validates the audience's concerns rather than ignoring or dismissing them
- [ ] **Empathy Before Persuasion** — The presenter demonstrates understanding of the audience's current situation before asking them to change
- [ ] **Evidence Addresses Specific Doubts** — Data, stories, and proof points are chosen to counter the identified resistance points, not generic selling
- [ ] **Gradual Shift Planned** — The presentation moves the audience in stages (awareness, interest, desire, action) rather than demanding immediate buy-in

### Acceptance-to-Action Arc
- [ ] **Tipping Point Designed** — A specific moment in the presentation is intended to shift the audience from considering to committing; it is identifiable
- [ ] **Confidence Built** — By the time the CTA arrives, the audience feels capable of taking the requested action, not just willing
- [ ] **Social Proof Provided** — Examples of others who took the same action and succeeded are included, reducing perceived risk
- [ ] **Urgency Is Authentic** — The reason to act now is genuine (deadline, opportunity cost, competitive pressure), not artificial

### Emotional State Tracking
- [ ] **Emotional Arc Mapped Slide by Slide** — Each section or slide has a note on the intended audience emotion: curious, concerned, hopeful, inspired, committed
- [ ] **Low Points Are Intentional** — If the audience will feel discomfort (during problem sections), it is deliberate and brief, not accidental or prolonged
- [ ] **Recovery Follows Tension** — Every moment of tension or discomfort is followed by a moment of possibility, relief, or humor
- [ ] **Final Emotional State Matches Goal** — The audience leaves feeling the intended emotion (energized, convinced, aligned, committed), verified by the design of the closing section

### Verification
- [ ] **Audience Proxy Reviewed** — At least one person representing the target audience has reviewed the presentation and confirmed the emotional journey lands
- [ ] **Journey Is Audience-Centered** — The presentation is structured around what the audience needs to hear in what order, not what the presenter wants to say in their preferred order

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 17 items checked; the audience journey is deliberately designed and verified |
| Conditional | 13-16 items checked; journey is planned but some transitions are rough or untested |
| Fail | Fewer than 13 items, or the presentation is structured around presenter logic rather than audience journey |

## Next Steps
- Create a two-column outline: left column is content, right column is intended audience emotion at each point
- If resistance is not addressed, insert an objection-handling section before the CTA
- Rehearse with a proxy audience and ask them to rate their engagement at each section on a 1-5 scale
