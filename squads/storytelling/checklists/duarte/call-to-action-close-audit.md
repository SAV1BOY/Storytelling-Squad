# Call to Action Close Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the CTA is clear, positioned at the emotional peak, and the next step is obvious

## Purpose
Ensure the presentation's closing call to action lands at the moment of maximum emotional resonance, is unambiguous in what it asks the audience to do, and makes the next step so obvious that inaction requires more effort than action.

## Prerequisites
- Complete presentation with CTA drafted
- Sparkline structure mapped (emotional arc identified)
- Audience decision-making context understood (authority to act, barriers to action)

## Checklist

### CTA Clarity
- [ ] **Single Primary CTA** — The presentation asks the audience to do one thing, not three; secondary asks are subordinate and clearly labeled
- [ ] **CTA Uses Specific Action Language** — The ask is a concrete verb phrase ("Sign the proposal by Friday," "Visit this URL and register," "Approve the budget for Q2") not vague ("Think about it," "Consider this")
- [ ] **CTA Is Achievable** — The action requested is within the audience's power, authority, and capacity to perform
- [ ] **CTA Has a Deadline or Timeframe** — The ask includes urgency: when the action should be taken, even if the deadline is self-imposed

### Emotional Positioning
- [ ] **CTA Follows the Emotional Peak** — The call to action is placed immediately after the most emotionally compelling moment in the presentation, not after a flat administrative section
- [ ] **Emotional Momentum Carries Into CTA** — The audience is still feeling the impact of the peak moment when the CTA lands; there is no deflating transition between them
- [ ] **CTA Resolves the Tension** — The presentation has built tension (problem, stakes, gap); the CTA is positioned as the release of that tension
- [ ] **Hope and Agency Present** — The audience feels empowered, not guilted or pressured, by the CTA; it inspires action through possibility

### Next Step Obviousness
- [ ] **Next Step Is One Step** — The CTA describes a single first action, not a multi-step process; "do this one thing" is the instruction
- [ ] **Friction Minimized** — Barriers to taking the next step (forms, approvals, complexity) have been reduced or addressed in advance
- [ ] **Resources Provided** — The audience has everything they need to act: URL, contact info, document, deadline, template, or reference
- [ ] **Follow-Up Planned** — A follow-up mechanism is in place (email, meeting, reminder) so the CTA does not depend entirely on audience initiative

### Reinforcement
- [ ] **CTA Restated at the End** — The last slide or the last spoken sentence reinforces the CTA so it is the final thing the audience carries away
- [ ] **CTA Connects to the Opening** — The call to action ties back to the problem or opportunity established at the beginning, creating narrative closure
- [ ] **Visual CTA Present** — The CTA is displayed visually (on screen, in a handout, or in a follow-up email) so it is not lost in the moment

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the CTA lands at the right moment and the path forward is crystal clear |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; CTA is present but timing or specificity needs adjustment |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or no clear CTA exists, or CTA is buried before the emotional peak |

## Next Steps
- If the CTA is positioned after administrative slides (agenda recap, Q&A), move it before those sections
- If the CTA is vague, rewrite it to include who does what by when
- Practice the transition from emotional peak to CTA until it feels seamless, not jarring
