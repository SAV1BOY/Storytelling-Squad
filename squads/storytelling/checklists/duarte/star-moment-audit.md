# S.T.A.R. Moment Audit
> Quality gate for verifying at least one planned memorable moment exists that is repeatable and emotionally resonant

## Purpose
Ensure the presentation contains at least one S.T.A.R. moment (Something They will Always Remember): a deliberately designed, emotionally resonant moment that the audience will recall and retell long after the presentation ends.

## Prerequisites
- Complete presentation draft
- Audience emotional arc mapped
- Understanding of Duarte's five S.T.A.R. moment types: Memorable Dramatization, Repeatable Sound Bite, Evocative Visual, Emotional Story, Shocking Statistic

## Checklist

### Moment Exists
- [ ] **At Least One S.T.A.R. Moment Planned** — A specific, identifiable moment in the presentation has been deliberately designed to be memorable; it is not left to chance
- [ ] **Moment Type Identified** — The S.T.A.R. moment is categorized: dramatization, sound bite, visual, story, or statistic; the type is chosen for audience impact
- [ ] **Moment Is Placed Strategically** — The S.T.A.R. moment occurs at a high-leverage point in the presentation (climax, turning point, or close), not buried in the middle

### Repeatability
- [ ] **Moment Is Retellable** — The audience could describe the moment to someone who was not present in 30 seconds or less
- [ ] **Sound Bite Quality** — If the moment involves language, the phrase is concise, rhythmic, and quotable; it sticks in memory
- [ ] **Shareable Format** — The moment could be captured in a tweet, photo, or short clip; it has viral potential in the audience's context
- [ ] **Simple Enough to Remember** — The moment does not require extensive context to make sense; its impact is self-contained

### Emotional Resonance
- [ ] **Moment Provokes a Physical Response** — The audience laughs, gasps, leans forward, nods, or has a visible emotional reaction during the moment
- [ ] **Emotional Connection to the Theme** — The S.T.A.R. moment directly connects to the presentation's core message; it is not a tangential gimmick
- [ ] **Surprise Element Present** — The moment contains an element of surprise, contrast, or revelation that disrupts the audience's expectations
- [ ] **Authenticity Maintained** — The moment feels genuine and earned, not manufactured or manipulative

### Craft and Execution
- [ ] **Rehearsed Specifically** — The S.T.A.R. moment has been practiced separately to ensure timing, delivery, and impact are optimized
- [ ] **Setup Precedes the Moment** — The presentation builds toward the S.T.A.R. moment so it lands with full impact, not as a non sequitur
- [ ] **Moment Does Not Overshadow CTA** — The S.T.A.R. moment enhances the call to action rather than distracting from it
- [ ] **Backup Plan Exists** — If the moment requires technology, a prop, or audience participation, a fallback plan exists in case of failure

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the audience will talk about this moment tomorrow |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; moment exists but needs stronger execution or better placement |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or no deliberately planned memorable moment exists |

## Next Steps
- If no S.T.A.R. moment exists, choose the most important point and design a moment around it using one of the five types
- Test the moment with a small audience and ask them to retell it; if they cannot, simplify
- Study exemplar S.T.A.R. moments: Bill Gates releasing mosquitoes, Steve Jobs pulling the MacBook Air from an envelope
