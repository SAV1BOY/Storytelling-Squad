# Sparkline Audit
> Quality gate for verifying "what is" vs "what could be" contrast is present, alternating, and builds momentum

## Purpose
Ensure the presentation follows Nancy Duarte's Sparkline structure: alternating between the current reality ("what is") and the envisioned future ("what could be"), creating increasing contrast that builds to a climactic "new bliss" or call to action.

## Prerequisites
- Complete presentation draft or outline
- Audience's current reality documented
- Desired future state articulated
- Understanding of Duarte's Sparkline framework

## Checklist

### What Is / What Could Be Contrast
- [ ] **"What Is" Established Early** — The presentation opens by grounding the audience in their current reality with specific, recognizable details
- [ ] **"What Could Be" Introduced as Contrast** — A compelling alternative future is presented that creates a gap between the current state and the possibility
- [ ] **Contrast Is Specific** — Both "what is" and "what could be" use concrete examples, data, or stories, not abstract generalities
- [ ] **Gap Creates Tension** — The difference between current reality and future possibility is large enough to motivate but not so large as to discourage

### Alternating Pattern
- [ ] **Pattern Alternates** — The presentation moves back and forth between "what is" and "what could be" at least three times, not just once
- [ ] **Each Alternation Deepens** — Successive cycles explore new facets of the contrast (emotional, practical, societal) rather than repeating the same point
- [ ] **Audience Feels the Rhythm** — The back-and-forth creates a discernible pattern that the audience can feel even if they cannot name it
- [ ] **No Extended Flatlines** — Neither "what is" nor "what could be" dominates for too long; the alternation maintains energy

### Momentum Building
- [ ] **Stakes Escalate** — Each cycle raises the stakes of the gap: what is lost by staying in "what is" and what is gained by reaching "what could be"
- [ ] **Emotional Intensity Increases** — The contrast becomes more emotionally charged as the presentation progresses
- [ ] **Evidence Accumulates** — Each cycle adds proof (data, stories, examples) that "what could be" is achievable, building credibility
- [ ] **Climax Is the Highest Contrast** — The final "what could be" is the most vivid, ambitious, and emotionally resonant of all cycles

### Resolution
- [ ] **"New Bliss" Described** — The presentation ends with a specific, compelling picture of the world when "what could be" is realized
- [ ] **Call to Action Bridges the Gap** — The audience is given a clear, actionable step to move from "what is" to "what could be"
- [ ] **Audience Leaves Changed** — The final state of the presentation is qualitatively different from the opening; the audience's perspective has shifted

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the Sparkline builds irresistible momentum toward the future state |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; contrast is present but pattern is inconsistent or does not escalate |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or "what is" / "what could be" contrast is absent or only appears once |

## Next Steps
- Map the presentation slide by slide, labeling each as "what is" or "what could be" and check for alternation
- If momentum is flat, reorder content so each cycle raises stakes higher than the previous one
- Study Duarte's analysis of Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" and Steve Jobs' iPhone launch as exemplars
