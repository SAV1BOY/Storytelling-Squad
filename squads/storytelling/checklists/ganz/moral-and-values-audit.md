# Moral and Values Audit
> Quality gate for verifying values are clear, authentic, and not performative

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative's moral foundation is built on genuinely held values that are demonstrated through action and story, not merely declared for strategic advantage. Performative values are detected by audiences instantly and destroy trust irreparably.

## Prerequisites
- Narrative or communication draft completed
- Organizational or speaker values documented
- Understanding of the difference between stated values and enacted values

## Checklist

### Values Clarity
- [ ] **Values Named Explicitly** — The core values driving the narrative are identified and documented, not assumed or left implicit
- [ ] **Values Are Specific** — Each value is defined concretely ("fairness means equal access to opportunity regardless of background") not generically ("we believe in fairness")
- [ ] **Few Values, Deeply Held** — The narrative focuses on 2-3 core values rather than listing many; depth trumps breadth
- [ ] **Values Are Prioritized** — When values conflict (growth vs. sustainability, speed vs. quality), the narrative makes clear which takes precedence

### Authenticity
- [ ] **Values Are Demonstrated** — Each stated value is backed by at least one specific story, action, or decision that proves it is enacted, not just proclaimed
- [ ] **Sacrifice Present** — The narrative shows moments where living the values cost something: money, time, convenience, popularity
- [ ] **Consistency Over Time** — The values expressed in this narrative are consistent with the speaker's or organization's past actions and communications
- [ ] **Imperfection Acknowledged** — The narrative does not claim perfect adherence to values; moments of falling short and course-correcting are included

### Not Performative
- [ ] **Values Exist Before the Narrative** — The values are part of the organization's or speaker's actual operating principles, not invented for this communication
- [ ] **No Virtue Signaling** — The narrative does not use values language primarily to gain social approval or avoid criticism
- [ ] **Actions Match Words** — A skeptical observer examining the speaker's or organization's behavior would find it consistent with the stated values
- [ ] **Uncomfortable Truths Included** — The narrative includes honest assessments of challenges, failures, or tensions related to the values, not just success stories

### Impact
- [ ] **Values Motivate Action** — The stated values are connected to specific actions the audience can take, not just feelings they should have
- [ ] **Values Create Belonging** — The narrative's values create a sense of "us" among people who share them, building community identity
- [ ] **Values Invite, Not Exclude** — The moral framework is presented as an invitation to share in purpose, not as a judgment on those who do not yet participate

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; values are clear, genuine, and demonstrated through action |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; values are present but some feel performative or undemonstrated |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or values are clearly performative or inconsistent with behavior |

## Next Steps
- For each stated value, list three specific actions that prove it; if you cannot, the value may be aspirational rather than actual
- If performative signals are detected, either align actions to values or change the stated values to match reality
- Ask a trusted critic: "Does this sound like us, or does it sound like what we wish we were?"
