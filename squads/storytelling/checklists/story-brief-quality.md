# Story Brief Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a story brief is complete, actionable, and ready for narrative development

## Purpose
Gates the completeness and clarity of the story brief before any narrative drafting begins.
Use this checklist every time a new brief is submitted or revised. This prevents wasted
creative cycles on underspecified or misaligned briefs. A strong brief is the single most
important predictor of a strong final narrative.

## Prerequisites
- A draft story brief document exists
- The requesting stakeholder has been identified
- Business context or campaign context is available
- Target audience has been at least loosely identified
- Any relevant brand guidelines or style guides are accessible

## Checklist

### Objective and Intent
- [ ] **Primary objective stated** — The brief contains a single, unambiguous sentence describing what the story must accomplish (e.g., "Convert trial users to paid within 7 days")
- [ ] **Success metric defined** — At least one measurable outcome is specified (e.g., click-through rate, sign-ups, shares, sentiment shift, revenue attributed)
- [ ] **Objective is behavior-oriented** — The goal describes a reader/viewer action or belief change, not just "raise awareness" or "educate"
- [ ] **Single focus confirmed** — The brief does not attempt to accomplish more than two objectives; if it does, it must be split into separate briefs
- [ ] **Strategic context provided** — The brief explains how this narrative fits into the larger campaign, funnel, or strategic initiative

### Audience Definition
- [ ] **Target audience named** — A specific audience segment is identified with demographic or psychographic detail, not "everyone" or "business professionals"
- [ ] **Current belief documented** — The brief states what the audience currently believes or feels about the topic before encountering the story
- [ ] **Desired belief documented** — The brief states the target belief or feeling after the story lands, expressed as a shift from current state
- [ ] **Audience knowledge level noted** — The brief specifies whether the audience is unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, or product-aware (using Schwartz awareness stages or equivalent)
- [ ] **Objections or resistance anticipated** — At least one known objection or skepticism point is listed with its expected intensity
- [ ] **Audience context described** — The brief notes where the audience will encounter this story (scrolling social, reading email, sitting in a conference, browsing a website) and their likely mental state at that moment

### Call to Action and Proof
- [ ] **CTA is specific and singular** — There is one clear next step the audience should take, worded as a concrete action (not "learn more" or "get in touch")
- [ ] **CTA friction assessed** — The brief notes what the audience must do, give up, or risk to take the CTA, and whether the story must overcome that friction
- [ ] **Available proof catalogued** — The brief lists what evidence exists: data points, testimonials, case studies, demonstrations, credentials, awards, media mentions
- [ ] **Proof gaps identified** — Any missing evidence the narrative team will need to source or create is flagged with a plan for how to fill the gap
- [ ] **Proof credibility rated** — Each proof asset is rated for strength: first-party data, third-party validation, anecdotal, or aspirational

### Tone and Channel
- [ ] **Tone guidance provided** — The brief includes at least three tone descriptors (e.g., "warm, authoritative, urgent") or references a brand voice guide document
- [ ] **Channel specified** — The delivery channel is named: email, video, stage presentation, social media (which platform), landing page, slide deck, print, podcast, or other
- [ ] **Format constraints documented** — Word count, video duration, slide count, image dimensions, or platform-specific limits are stated with hard vs. soft boundaries
- [ ] **Brand guidelines referenced** — The brief links to or names the applicable brand/style guide, including any visual identity requirements
- [ ] **Competitive context noted** — The brief identifies what competing messages the audience is also receiving in this channel or moment

### Context and Constraints
- [ ] **Timeline stated** — A deadline or milestone date is included, with distinction between first draft due date and final delivery date
- [ ] **Stakeholder approval chain identified** — The brief names who must approve the final narrative, how many rounds of review are expected, and who has final sign-off authority
- [ ] **Mandatory inclusions listed** — Any required elements (legal disclaimers, partner mentions, hashtags, regulatory language, logos) are documented
- [ ] **Exclusions listed** — Topics, claims, competitor mentions, or framings that are off-limits are noted explicitly
- [ ] **Budget and resource constraints noted** — Any limitations on production (no video budget, no custom photography, limited design support) are stated upfront

## Scoring
Must pass **20/26 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Primary objective stated
- Target audience named
- CTA is specific and singular
- Channel specified
- Timeline stated

Any CRITICAL failure requires brief revision before proceeding to narrative development.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Brief moves to narrative development. Assign to the narrative architect for story
  structure selection. The architect should select the appropriate story framework based on the
  brief's objective and channel.
- **On Fail:** Return the brief to the requesting stakeholder with specific gaps annotated.
  Reference the Miller StoryBrand framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for
  guidance on clarifying objective and audience. Use the audience definition worksheet to
  sharpen the target segment. Consult the Story Brief Agent for assisted revision. Do not
  begin narrative drafting until the brief passes this gate.
