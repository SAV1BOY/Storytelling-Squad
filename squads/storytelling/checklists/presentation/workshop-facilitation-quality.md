# Workshop Facilitation Quality
> Quality gate for verifying engagement is planned, exercises are embedded, and learning is captured

## Purpose
Ensure the workshop is designed as a participatory learning experience, not a lecture with activities bolted on. Engagement must be planned into the structure, exercises must produce meaningful outcomes, and learning must be captured in a form participants can use afterward.

## Prerequisites
- Workshop objectives defined with specific learning outcomes
- Participant profiles and group size known
- Time allocation and venue constraints documented
- Materials and supplies prepared

## Checklist

### Engagement Planned
- [ ] **First Engagement Within 10 Minutes** — Participants are actively doing something (not just listening) within the first 10 minutes of the workshop
- [ ] **Engagement Every 15-20 Minutes** — No stretch of more than 20 minutes passes without participant interaction: discussion, exercise, vote, reflection, share
- [ ] **Multiple Engagement Modes** — The workshop uses varied engagement: individual reflection, pair work, small group discussion, full group sharing, physical movement
- [ ] **Engagement Serves Objectives** — Every activity connects to a learning objective; no activity exists solely to "break things up" without educational purpose

### Exercises Embedded
- [ ] **Exercises Are Practice, Not Performance** — Participants practice skills or apply concepts in a safe environment rather than performing for judgment
- [ ] **Instructions Are Clear** — Every exercise has clear instructions: what to do, how long, what the output should look like, and how it connects to the concept
- [ ] **Exercises Build on Each Other** — Later exercises use skills or concepts from earlier ones; the workshop has a progressive learning arc
- [ ] **Time for Exercises Is Realistic** — Each exercise has enough time for meaningful completion; exercises are not rushed to fit the agenda

### Learning Captured
- [ ] **Takeaways Are Documented** — Key insights, frameworks, or commitments are recorded during the workshop (worksheets, shared documents, photos of boards)
- [ ] **Participants Leave With Artifacts** — Each participant has a tangible output: a completed worksheet, an action plan, a framework applied to their own situation
- [ ] **Reflection Moments Built In** — Structured pauses for participants to synthesize what they have learned and identify their key takeaways are included
- [ ] **Follow-Up Materials Planned** — Post-workshop resources (summary document, recording, templates, reading list) are prepared for distribution

### Facilitation Quality
- [ ] **Energy Management Planned** — The workshop design accounts for energy cycles: high-energy after breaks, lower energy before lunch, and structures activities accordingly
- [ ] **Difficult Moments Anticipated** — Potential friction points (controversial topics, dominant participants, quiet groups, technical failures) have contingency plans
- [ ] **Facilitator Talks Less Than 40%** — The majority of workshop time is participant activity, not facilitator presentation; the ratio is deliberately designed
- [ ] **Closing Commitment Present** — The workshop ends with each participant stating or writing one specific action they will take as a result of the learning

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the workshop is a designed learning experience with high engagement and captured outcomes |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; engagement is present but exercises or learning capture needs strengthening |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or the workshop is essentially a lecture with a Q&A at the end |

## Next Steps
- If engagement is front-loaded, redesign the middle and end sections with additional interactive elements
- If exercises feel disconnected, map each exercise to a specific learning objective and cut those that do not connect
- Do a time audit: calculate the minutes of facilitator talking vs. participant activity and adjust toward 40/60
