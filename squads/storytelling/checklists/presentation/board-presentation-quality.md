# Board Presentation Quality
> Quality gate for verifying data plus narrative plus decision, executive-appropriate format, and action orientation

## Purpose
Ensure board presentations combine rigorous data with compelling narrative to drive specific decisions. Board members have limited time, high expectations, and decision-making authority. Every minute must count, every slide must earn its place, and the presentation must end with a clear ask.

## Prerequisites
- Board meeting agenda and time allocation confirmed
- Financial and operational data current and verified
- Decision or approval being sought clearly defined
- Board member profiles and concerns understood

## Checklist

### Data
- [ ] **Data Is Current and Verified** — All metrics, financials, and KPIs are up-to-date and have been verified by the relevant team; no stale or estimated numbers
- [ ] **Data Is Contextualized** — Every number is presented with a benchmark, target, or comparison that tells the board whether performance is good, bad, or neutral
- [ ] **Trends Over Snapshots** — Data is presented as trajectories (quarter-over-quarter, year-over-year) not isolated data points; the direction matters more than the number
- [ ] **Data Density Is Appropriate** — Slides contain enough data to be substantive but not so much that they require line-by-line reading; key metrics are highlighted

### Narrative
- [ ] **Story Arc Present** — The presentation follows a logical narrative: here is where we are, here is what happened, here is what it means, here is what we should do
- [ ] **Strategic Context Provided** — Data is framed within the strategic context: how results connect to the company's strategy, competitive position, or market dynamics
- [ ] **Challenges Addressed Honestly** — Problems, misses, and risks are presented directly with root cause analysis and corrective actions, not buried or minimized
- [ ] **Wins Are Attributed** — Successes are connected to specific actions, decisions, or strategies, not just claimed as outcomes; the board understands causation

### Decision Orientation
- [ ] **Decision Requested Is Clear** — The specific decision, approval, or direction being sought from the board is stated explicitly, not implied
- [ ] **Options Presented** — When a decision is required, 2-3 options are presented with pros, cons, and the management recommendation
- [ ] **Risk Assessment Included** — Each option or recommendation includes an honest assessment of risks, downsides, and mitigation strategies
- [ ] **Management Recommendation Stated** — The presenting team clearly states their recommended path and the rationale behind it; the board is asked to decide, not to figure it out

### Executive-Appropriate Format
- [ ] **Time Respected** — The presentation is designed to fit the allocated time with room for discussion; it does not require rushing through the last third
- [ ] **Executive Summary Leads** — The first slide or opening minute contains the key message, the decision needed, and the recommendation; details follow for those who want them
- [ ] **Appendix Available** — Detailed data, supporting analysis, and backup slides are in an appendix for reference, not crammed into the main presentation
- [ ] **Pre-Read Distributed** — If the board expects it, a pre-read document with full detail is distributed in advance so the meeting focuses on discussion, not information transfer

### Action Orientation
- [ ] **Next Steps Defined** — The presentation concludes with specific next steps, owners, and timelines
- [ ] **Follow-Up Committed** — Any open questions or requested analysis is captured with a commitment to deliver by a specific date

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 18 items checked; the board presentation is a model of data-driven narrative decision-making |
| Conditional | 14-17 items checked; content is strong but format, timing, or decision clarity needs adjustment |
| Fail | Fewer than 14 items, or no clear decision is requested, or data is unverified |

## Next Steps
- If the presentation runs long, cut content and move it to the appendix; protect discussion time
- If no decision is requested, clarify with the CEO or board chair what the board expects from this item
- Rehearse with the CFO or a board-experienced executive who can flag content that will trigger unnecessary deep dives
