# Keynote Quality
> Quality gate for verifying a big idea, memorable arc, S.T.A.R. moment, and audience transformation

## Purpose
Ensure the keynote presentation delivers a transformative experience: a single big idea, a compelling narrative arc, at least one unforgettable moment, and an audience that leaves changed. Keynotes are performances, not presentations; they must move people.

## Prerequisites
- Keynote drafted with full script or detailed outline
- Audience profile and expectations documented
- Venue, timing, and context understood
- Rehearsal time allocated

## Checklist

### Big Idea
- [ ] **Single Big Idea Identified** — The keynote is organized around one central idea that can be stated in a single sentence
- [ ] **Big Idea Is Worth Spreading** — The idea is significant enough to deserve the audience's time; it challenges, inspires, or reframes their thinking
- [ ] **Big Idea Is Repeated** — The central idea is stated, reinforced, and returned to at least three times throughout the keynote in different ways
- [ ] **Big Idea Is New to This Audience** — The idea offers fresh perspective; it is not a restatement of what the audience already believes

### Memorable Arc
- [ ] **Clear Beginning, Middle, End** — The keynote has a definite opening that hooks, a middle that develops with tension and proof, and an ending that transforms
- [ ] **Sparkline Structure Present** — "What is" and "what could be" alternate throughout, building momentum toward the vision
- [ ] **Pacing Varies** — The keynote alternates between high-energy and reflective moments, between data and story, between tension and release
- [ ] **No Sag in the Middle** — The middle section contains a major turning point, surprise, or escalation that prevents audience disengagement

### S.T.A.R. Moment
- [ ] **Planned Memorable Moment Exists** — At least one moment is deliberately designed to be unforgettable: a demonstration, a story climax, a visual reveal, a shocking statistic
- [ ] **S.T.A.R. Moment Connects to Big Idea** — The memorable moment reinforces the central idea, not a tangent
- [ ] **S.T.A.R. Moment Is Rehearsed** — The moment has been practiced specifically for timing, delivery, and impact

### Audience Transformation
- [ ] **Audience Starting State Known** — What the audience thinks, feels, and believes before the keynote is documented
- [ ] **Desired Ending State Defined** — What the audience should think, feel, and believe after the keynote is explicitly planned
- [ ] **Transformation Is Achievable** — The distance between starting and ending state is bridged by the content and emotional arc of the keynote
- [ ] **Call to Action Present** — The audience is given a specific, actionable step to take that extends the keynote's impact beyond the room

### Performance Quality
- [ ] **Opening Is Commanding** — The first 60 seconds establish presence, authority, and curiosity; no throat-clearing, bio recitation, or agenda slides
- [ ] **Closing Is Powerful** — The last minute is the emotional peak or a powerful return to the big idea; the keynote ends strong, not with Q&A logistics
- [ ] **Delivery Matches Content** — Voice, movement, pacing, and energy are calibrated to support the narrative, not distract from it

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 18 items checked; the keynote is a transformative experience |
| Conditional | 14-17 items checked; content is strong but delivery, S.T.A.R. moment, or pacing needs work |
| Fail | Fewer than 14 items, or no clear big idea, no memorable moment, or audience is unchanged |

## Next Steps
- If the big idea is unclear, complete this sentence: "I want the audience to leave believing _______"
- If no S.T.A.R. moment exists, design one using Duarte's five types: dramatization, sound bite, visual, story, statistic
- Rehearse the full keynote at least three times, including once in front of a test audience that provides feedback
