# Structure and Pacing Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative has a clear architecture and maintains momentum throughout

## Purpose
Gates the structural integrity and pacing of a narrative. Use after a full draft exists and basic
clarity has been confirmed. Ensures the story has identifiable acts or beats, each beat earns
its place, there are no dead spots where momentum dies, the causal chain holds, and the pacing
matches the channel and audience attention window. Structure is the skeleton; without it, even
great content collapses.

## When to Use
- After a full draft exists and basic clarity has been confirmed
- When a narrative "feels long" or "drags" but the content seems necessary
- Before presenting a narrative to stakeholders for structural approval
- When converting between formats (article to presentation, video to email)
- During structural revision after initial feedback

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists
- Narrative clarity checklist has been passed or is being run concurrently
- The target format, channel, and length are established
- The story's central conflict or tension has been identified
- The chosen narrative framework (if any) is documented

## Checklist

### Act and Beat Structure
- [ ] **Structure is identifiable** — The narrative follows a recognizable framework (3-act, 5-act, Harmon Circle, Snyder beats, Sparkline, or custom) and the chosen structure can be named and mapped
- [ ] **Beginning, middle, and end are distinct** — Each major section serves a different function: setup/context, confrontation/development, and resolution/payoff
- [ ] **Each beat changes value** — Every scene, section, or segment shifts something: emotion, information level, stakes, status, or trajectory. No beat leaves the audience in the same state as before it
- [ ] **Beats are sequenced by causality** — Sections connect via "but" or "therefore," not "and then." Each beat causes or responds to the adjacent beats in a logical chain
- [ ] **Turning points are present** — At least one major reversal, pivot, or surprise exists that changes the direction of the narrative and prevents predictability
- [ ] **Midpoint shift exists** — The halfway point of the narrative contains a meaningful escalation, revelation, or change in approach that re-engages the audience
- [ ] **Inciting incident is early** — The event that launches the main tension occurs within the first 15-20% of the narrative, not after prolonged setup

### Pacing and Rhythm
- [ ] **No dead spots** — No section longer than 15% of total length passes without new information, increased tension, or emotional shift; every section moves forward
- [ ] **Pacing varies intentionally** — The narrative alternates between tension and release, fast and slow, dense and sparse; it does not maintain a single tempo throughout
- [ ] **Longest section justified** — The single longest section has a clear structural reason for its length (e.g., climax sequence, key proof point, central emotional peak)
- [ ] **Opening pace is fast** — The first 10% of the narrative moves quickly, establishing situation and stakes without extensive backstory or context-setting
- [ ] **Closing pace is deliberate** — The final 10% does not rush the resolution; the CTA, moral, or emotional payoff lands with appropriate weight and breathing room
- [ ] **Micro-pacing considered** — Sentence and paragraph length varies to create rhythm: short sentences for urgency, longer sentences for reflection, white space for impact

### Length and Fit
- [ ] **Total length appropriate for channel** — The narrative fits the expected consumption window: email (<500 words), social (<300 words or <60s), presentation (18 min max), video (platform-specific norms)
- [ ] **No section overstays** — Individual sections are proportional; no single section exceeds 30% of total length unless it is the climactic sequence
- [ ] **Audience attention curve respected** — For longer formats, re-engagement hooks (stories, questions, reveals, format changes) appear at natural drop-off points (every 2-3 min for video, every 3-4 paragraphs for text)
- [ ] **Lean test passed** — The narrative cannot be shortened by more than 10% without losing essential content; if it can, it has not been sufficiently compressed

### Structural Coherence
- [ ] **Setup pays off** — Every element introduced in the first third (character, detail, question, object) is used or resolved later; no Chekhov's guns are left unfired
- [ ] **No orphan sections** — Every section connects to what comes before and after; removing any section would break the logical or emotional flow
- [ ] **Climax is the highest-tension moment** — The most intense or important moment occurs in the final third, not the middle, unless the structure deliberately and effectively subverts this
- [ ] **Resolution is satisfying** — The ending resolves the central tension and does not introduce new unresolved threads that leave the audience frustrated

### Structural Testing
- [ ] **Beat sheet documented** — The narrative's beats can be written as a single-page outline where each beat is one sentence; if a beat cannot be summarized, it is unfocused
- [ ] **Structure survives the shuffle test** — If sections were accidentally reordered, the audience would notice immediately because the causal and emotional logic would break
- [ ] **Framework is invisible** — The chosen structural framework (3-act, Sparkline, Hero's Journey) serves the content without being obvious to the audience; the story feels natural, not formulaic
- [ ] **Parallel structures used where appropriate** — Repeated patterns (three examples, alternating perspectives, recurring motifs) create rhythm and reinforce the narrative's architecture

## Scoring
Must pass **18/25 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Structure is identifiable
- Each beat changes value
- No dead spots
- Setup pays off
- Inciting incident is early

Any CRITICAL failure requires structural revision before the narrative advances.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to emotional arc review (emotional-arc-quality.md) and conflict/stakes
  review (conflict-stakes-quality.md). The structure is sound enough to evaluate emotional
  and tension layers.
- **On Fail:** Return to the narrative architect for restructuring. Outline the beats on index
  cards or a beat sheet before rewriting prose. Reference Snyder's Beat Sheet
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/snyder/) for screenplay-style beats, Harmon's Story Circle
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/harmon/) for circular structures, Duarte's Sparkline
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) for presentation pacing, or Coyne's Story Grid
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/coyne/) for genre-based structural requirements.
