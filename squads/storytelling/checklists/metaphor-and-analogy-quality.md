# Metaphor and Analogy Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that metaphors and analogies clarify, resonate, and do not mislead

## Purpose
Gates the quality and effectiveness of figurative language in a narrative. Metaphors and analogies are the most powerful tools for making the unfamiliar familiar, but bad metaphors confuse, mislead, or bore. Use this checklist whenever a narrative relies on metaphors, analogies, or extended comparisons to explain concepts, especially for technical products, abstract ideas, or complex transformations.

## Prerequisites
- A narrative draft exists containing at least one metaphor or analogy
- The concepts being explained through metaphor are identified
- The target audience's frame of reference is understood
- The core message is established

## Checklist

### Clarity and Accuracy
- [ ] **Metaphor clarifies, not decorates** — Each metaphor makes a concept easier to understand, not just more literary; removing the metaphor would lose clarity
- [ ] **Mapping is accurate** — The comparison holds up under scrutiny: the relationship between the compared elements is logically sound, not just superficially similar
- [ ] **No false implications** — The metaphor does not suggest properties that are untrue or misleading about the concept it explains
- [ ] **Metaphor is self-explanatory** — The audience does not need specialized knowledge to understand the comparison; the vehicle (what you are comparing to) is more familiar than the tenor (what you are explaining)
- [ ] **Complexity reduced** — The metaphor makes the concept simpler, not more complicated; it does not require its own explanation

### Originality and Freshness
- [ ] **No dead metaphors** — Overused metaphors ("think outside the box," "move the needle," "low-hanging fruit," "deep dive," "at the end of the day") are replaced with original alternatives
- [ ] **No mixed metaphors** — No single passage combines two unrelated metaphors that clash (e.g., "We need to hit the ground running and keep our eye on the ball while navigating uncharted waters")
- [ ] **Freshness tested** — Each metaphor has been evaluated: would the audience feel they have heard this comparison before? If yes, it is revised or replaced
- [ ] **Specificity over generality** — Metaphors use specific, concrete images rather than broad categories ("like a surgeon removing a splinter with forceps" rather than "like a professional doing their job")

### Audience Fit
- [ ] **Metaphor draws from the audience's world** — The comparison references experiences, objects, or situations the specific target audience encounters in their daily life or work
- [ ] **Cultural resonance verified** — Metaphors work across the audience's cultural contexts; they do not rely on region-specific, age-specific, or subculture-specific references unless the audience is homogeneous
- [ ] **Technical level appropriate** — The metaphor neither oversimplifies for expert audiences nor assumes expertise for general audiences
- [ ] **Emotional register matches** — The metaphor's emotional tone (playful, serious, urgent, warm) aligns with the narrative's overall tone and the moment in which it appears

### Consistency and Extension
- [ ] **Extended metaphors are sustained** — If a metaphor is used as a throughline, it is maintained consistently across the narrative without breaking or shifting
- [ ] **No metaphor overload** — The narrative does not introduce more than 3 distinct metaphors; excessive metaphors compete for attention and muddy the message
- [ ] **Metaphor supports the central message** — Each figurative comparison reinforces the narrative's core point rather than introducing tangential associations
- [ ] **Metaphor is memorable** — At least one metaphor in the narrative is vivid and distinctive enough that the audience could recall and repeat it after the story ends

## Scoring
Must pass **12/16 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Metaphor clarifies, not decorates
- No mixed metaphors
- Metaphor draws from the audience's world
- Extended metaphors are sustained

Any CRITICAL failure requires metaphor revision.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to final editing review (editing-and-trimming-quality.md) and format-specific checklist.
- **On Fail:** Strip all metaphors and rewrite the concepts in plain language first. Then introduce one metaphor at a time, testing each with the question: "Does this make the concept clearer for THIS audience?" Reference the narrative framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for message-first storytelling. Workshop metaphors with someone from the target audience to verify resonance before finalizing.
