# Video Script Narrative Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a video script is structured for retention, emotional impact, and visual storytelling

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of video scripts before production begins, when changes are cheap
and fast. Use for any video format: YouTube long-form, social media short-form, brand films,
explainer videos, customer testimonials, course content, product demos, or paid ads. Ensures the
script is designed around the platform's retention curve, hooks within the first moments, uses
visual storytelling rather than illustrated radio, and delivers its core value before asking
anything of the viewer. Production budgets are wasted on poorly structured scripts.

## When to Use
- Before any video production begins, when script changes are still cheap and fast
- When a previous video had poor retention curves or low completion rates
- Before investing production budget in filming, animation, or post-production
- When converting written content (blog, case study, presentation) into video format
- During script revision after table reads or stakeholder feedback

## Prerequisites
- A complete video script or detailed scene-by-scene outline exists
- The target platform and its conventions are known (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram Reels, LinkedIn, website embed, paid ad)
- The target video length and aspect ratio are defined
- The audience segment and content objective are clear
- Visual references, storyboard concepts, or location plans are available or in development
- The production approach (talking head, B-roll heavy, animation, screen capture, live action) is decided

## Checklist

### Retention Curve Design
- [ ] **Hook in first 3 seconds** — The video opens with a visually or verbally arresting moment: a bold claim, a surprising image, a direct question, action in progress, or a pattern interrupt; no logos, bumpers, intros, or "Hey guys, welcome back"
- [ ] **Re-hook at 30-second mark** — A second engagement spike is designed near the 30-second point to catch viewers who survived the opening but are deciding whether to invest further time
- [ ] **Pattern interrupts every 60-90 seconds** — For videos over 2 minutes, visual or tonal shifts (new location, graphic overlay, pace change, humor beat, story escalation, format change) are scripted at regular intervals
- [ ] **No front-loading of context** — Background information, disclaimers, and "before we begin" context are either eliminated or woven in at the moment they become relevant, not dumped upfront
- [ ] **Retention dips anticipated** — Specific points where attention is likely to flag (technical explanations, slower expository passages, transitions) are identified and reinforced with visual hooks, story beats, or tonal shifts
- [ ] **Promise of payoff stated early** — The audience knows what they will get by staying (the answer, the result, the technique, the revelation) within the first 10% of the video

### Visual Storytelling
- [ ] **Show, don't tell enforced** — At least 50% of key information is communicated through visuals (demonstrations, graphics, actions, environments, reactions) rather than narration alone
- [ ] **Script includes visual direction** — The script specifies what the viewer sees alongside what they hear; visual cues, shot descriptions, and graphic callouts are written into the script document
- [ ] **Talking-head time limited** — If the video includes an on-camera speaker, unbroken talking-head segments do not exceed 20-30 seconds before a visual change (cutaway, graphic, B-roll, screen recording)
- [ ] **Visual metaphors used** — At least one abstract concept is illustrated visually rather than explained verbally: a physical demonstration, an animated comparison, a before/after shot, or a visual analogy
- [ ] **B-roll is purposeful** — Every B-roll or cutaway shot specified in the script serves the narrative: illustrating the point, providing evidence, creating emotion, or maintaining pacing; no filler footage

### Narrative Structure
- [ ] **Story arc present** — The video follows a narrative structure with setup (problem/promise), development (journey/teaching/tension), and resolution (payoff/answer/transformation)
- [ ] **Payoff delivered before CTA** — The primary value (answer, revelation, technique, transformation, entertainment) is delivered before the audience is asked to subscribe, click, purchase, or take any action
- [ ] **Single through-line maintained** — The video pursues one topic, one story, or one argument; tangents that do not serve the main thread are cut regardless of how interesting they are
- [ ] **Emotional beats scripted** — Specific moments designed to produce specific emotions (surprise, empathy, humor, inspiration, tension, relief) are identified and annotated in the script

### Audio and Pacing
- [ ] **Narration pacing varies** — The script indicates deliberate pace changes: faster delivery for excitement or lists, slower delivery for key points or emotional moments, pauses for impact
- [ ] **Script reads aloud naturally** — The narration has been read at delivery pace and sounds conversational and natural, not written-for-the-page, stilted, or overly formal
- [ ] **Music and sound design noted** — The script includes cues for music changes, sound effects, ambient audio, or strategic silence that support the emotional arc and pacing
- [ ] **Runtime verified by read-through** — The script has been timed by reading aloud with pauses for visual beats, and it fits the target duration within a 10% margin

### Platform Optimization
- [ ] **Format matches platform norms** — Aspect ratio (16:9, 9:16, 1:1), length expectations, caption placement, and style conventions for the target platform are accounted for in the script
- [ ] **Thumb-stop moment in first frame** — The opening visual, if frozen as a thumbnail, would compel a viewer scrolling a feed to stop, click, or watch; the first frame is as designed as the first sentence
- [ ] **Captions and text overlays scripted** — Key text overlays, lower thirds, or burned-in captions are written into the script, especially for platforms where 80%+ of viewers watch without sound initially

## Scoring
Must pass **17/22 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Hook in first 3 seconds
- Show, don't tell enforced
- Payoff delivered before CTA
- Script reads aloud naturally
- Story arc present

Any CRITICAL failure requires script revision before any production begins.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to storyboarding, shot listing, and production scheduling. Brief the
  production team with the annotated script. Schedule a table read with all key participants.
  Finalize thumbnail concept alongside the script.
- **On Fail:** Restructure the script before producing anything. Reference the Hook and Open
  Loop checklist (hook-and-open-loop-quality.md) for opening fixes, the Structure and Pacing
  checklist (structure-and-pacing-quality.md) for arc and pacing issues, and the Emotional Arc
  checklist (emotional-arc-quality.md) for engagement design. Watch 5 high-performing videos in
  the same niche on the same platform and reverse-engineer their retention structures, hook
  strategies, and visual pacing before rewriting.
