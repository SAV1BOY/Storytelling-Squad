# Business Story Cycle Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the 10 steps of the Story Cycle are present or a justified subset is used

## Purpose
Ensure the business narrative follows Howell's Story Cycle framework with all 10 steps present or a deliberate, documented subset that serves the specific business context. The Story Cycle adapts classical narrative structure for organizational and brand storytelling.

## Prerequisites
- Complete business narrative draft or brand story outline
- Business objectives and audience defined
- Familiarity with Howell's 10-step Story Cycle

## Checklist

### Story Cycle Steps
- [ ] **Step 1: Backstory Established** — The history, context, or origin that shapes the current situation is provided, giving the audience grounding
- [ ] **Step 2: Hero Identified** — The central character (customer, founder, brand, or team) is clearly defined with relatable qualities
- [ ] **Step 3: Stakes Articulated** — What is at risk is named explicitly; the audience understands what happens if the hero fails
- [ ] **Step 4: Disruption Occurs** — A change event (market shift, crisis, opportunity, realization) breaks the status quo and demands response
- [ ] **Step 5: Antagonist/Obstacle Named** — The force opposing the hero (competition, internal resistance, market conditions, legacy systems) is specific
- [ ] **Step 6: Mentor or Tipping Point Present** — A guide, insight, or catalytic event enables the hero to see a path forward
- [ ] **Step 7: Journey/Struggle Shown** — The effort, iteration, or transformation process is depicted with real detail, not glossed over
- [ ] **Step 8: Victory/Resolution Achieved** — A concrete outcome or milestone is reached that resolves the central tension
- [ ] **Step 9: Moral/Lesson Extracted** — The takeaway is articulated in a way that applies beyond this specific story to the audience's own situation
- [ ] **Step 10: Call to Action or New Beginning** — The story propels the audience toward a next step, new chapter, or changed behavior

### Justified Subset
- [ ] **If Fewer Than 10 Steps Used, Omissions Are Documented** — Any omitted step has a written rationale explaining why it is unnecessary for this context
- [ ] **Remaining Steps Still Form a Complete Arc** — The subset used still creates a beginning, middle, and end with emotional movement
- [ ] **No Critical Steps Skipped** — Steps 2 (Hero), 4 (Disruption), and 8 (Resolution) are never omitted; they are the minimum viable story

### Business Integration
- [ ] **Story Serves a Business Objective** — The narrative connects to a measurable business goal (brand awareness, conversion, alignment, fundraising)
- [ ] **Audience Relevance Verified** — The story's hero, stakes, and resolution are relevant to the specific audience receiving it
- [ ] **Authenticity Maintained** — The story is truthful, verifiable, and does not exaggerate outcomes or misrepresent challenges

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All applicable items checked (16 for full cycle, 13+ for justified subset) |
| Conditional | 10-12 items checked; cycle is mostly present but gaps weaken the arc |
| Fail | Fewer than 10 items, or critical steps (Hero, Disruption, Resolution) missing |

## Next Steps
- Map each step to a specific section of the narrative and verify it is dramatized, not summarized
- If the story feels flat, check whether the Struggle (Step 7) is given enough space and specificity
- Test the narrative with an audience proxy and ask them to retell the story; missing steps will be evident in their retelling
