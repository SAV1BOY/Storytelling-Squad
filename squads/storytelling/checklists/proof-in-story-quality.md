# Proof-in-Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that evidence is embedded naturally within the narrative flow

## Purpose
Gates how evidence, data, and credibility markers are integrated into the story. Proof that is dumped in an appendix or stat-block is ignored; proof woven into the narrative is believed. Use this checklist for any persuasive narrative: case studies, pitch decks, brand stories, sales pages, or presentations where claims need substantiation.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists
- The story brief lists available proof assets (data, testimonials, credentials, demonstrations)
- The core claims of the narrative are identified
- The target audience's skepticism level is understood

## Checklist

### Evidence Presence
- [ ] **At least 3 proof points embedded** — The narrative contains a minimum of three distinct pieces of evidence (data, quote, example, credential, demonstration)
- [ ] **Every major claim has support** — No significant assertion in the narrative relies solely on the narrator's word; each is backed by at least one proof element
- [ ] **Multiple proof types used** — The narrative uses at least two different categories of proof: quantitative data, testimonial/quote, case example, expert citation, demonstration, or social proof
- [ ] **Proof is current** — All data, statistics, and references are from the last 2 years or are explicitly contextualized if older

### Natural Integration
- [ ] **Proof appears inside the story, not after it** — Evidence is woven into narrative scenes, dialogue, or progression rather than listed in a separate "results" or "evidence" section
- [ ] **Data is contextualized** — Numbers are given meaning through comparison, contrast, or metaphor ("That's enough to fill 3 Olympic swimming pools" rather than just "7.5 million liters")
- [ ] **Testimonials are in character voice** — Quotes sound like real people speaking, not marketing copy; they include specific details about the speaker's situation
- [ ] **Proof advances the story** — Each evidence point moves the narrative forward (raises stakes, resolves tension, enables transformation) rather than pausing it
- [ ] **Proof is introduced at moments of doubt** — Evidence appears at points where the audience is most likely to be skeptical, not randomly distributed

### Credibility and Trust
- [ ] **Sources are identified** — Data points cite their origin (study name, company, publication) even if briefly
- [ ] **Proof is verifiable** — The audience could, in principle, check the claims; nothing relies on "trust me" alone
- [ ] **Negative proof included** — The narrative acknowledges at least one limitation, caveat, or "what didn't work" to build trust through honesty
- [ ] **Authority markers present** — The narrator or protagonist's credentials, experience, or relevant authority is established early, not assumed

### Proof Sufficiency
- [ ] **Proof matches audience sophistication** — The type and depth of evidence matches what this specific audience finds persuasive (executives want ROI, engineers want specs, consumers want social proof)
- [ ] **Proof-to-claim ratio balanced** — The narrative does not bury the story under excessive data, nor does it make bold claims with thin evidence
- [ ] **Strongest proof supports the CTA** — The most compelling evidence point is positioned near the call to action, reinforcing the desired next step

## Scoring
Must pass **12/16 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Every major claim has support
- Proof appears inside the story, not after it
- Sources are identified
- Proof matches audience sophistication

Any CRITICAL failure requires an evidence integration revision.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to format-specific checklist (deck, email, video, etc.) and final editing review (editing-and-trimming-quality.md).
- **On Fail:** Return to the narrative architect with a proof gap analysis. Reference the Hall framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/hall/) for evidence-driven storytelling, or Miller's StoryBrand (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for positioning proof as "the guide's authority." Source additional proof assets from the stakeholder before revising.
