# Proof-in-Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that evidence is embedded naturally within the narrative flow

## Purpose
Gates how evidence, data, and credibility markers are integrated into the story. Proof that is
dumped in an appendix, footnote, or stat-block is ignored by audiences; proof woven into the
narrative is believed and remembered. Use this checklist for any persuasive narrative: case
studies, pitch decks, brand stories, sales pages, landing pages, or presentations where claims
need substantiation. The goal is evidence that advances the story rather than pausing it. Well-integrated proof
makes a narrative more compelling; poorly integrated proof makes it feel like an interrupted
story with footnotes.

## When to Use
- For any persuasive narrative making claims that require substantiation
- After the narrative structure is solid and before final editing
- When a draft reads as "all story, no substance" or "all claims, no proof"
- For case studies, pitch decks, sales narratives, landing pages, and brand stories
- When adapting a narrative for a more skeptical or sophisticated audience

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists
- The story brief lists available proof assets (data, testimonials, credentials, demonstrations)
- The core claims of the narrative have been identified and listed
- The target audience's skepticism level and sophistication are understood
- Sources for all data points are documented and accessible

## Checklist

### Evidence Presence
- [ ] **At least 3 proof points embedded** — The narrative contains a minimum of three distinct pieces of evidence: data points, direct quotes, concrete examples, credentials, or demonstrations
- [ ] **Every major claim has support** — No significant assertion in the narrative relies solely on the narrator's word; each is backed by at least one proof element
- [ ] **Multiple proof types used** — The narrative uses at least two different categories: quantitative data, testimonial/quote, case example, expert citation, demonstration, social proof, or visual evidence
- [ ] **Proof is current** — All data, statistics, and references are from the last 2 years, or are explicitly contextualized if older ("This 2019 study remains the definitive analysis because...")
- [ ] **Proof density matches claim boldness** — Bolder claims are supported by stronger or more numerous evidence points; routine claims need less proof

### Natural Integration
- [ ] **Proof appears inside the story, not after it** — Evidence is woven into narrative scenes, dialogue, or progression rather than listed in a separate "results," "evidence," or "why us" section
- [ ] **Data is contextualized** — Numbers are given meaning through comparison, contrast, or metaphor ("That's the population of Iceland" rather than just "380,000 people")
- [ ] **Testimonials are in character voice** — Quotes sound like real people speaking, with specific details about their situation; they do not read like marketing copy or PR statements
- [ ] **Proof advances the story** — Each evidence point moves the narrative forward: it raises stakes, resolves tension, enables transformation, or builds credibility at a moment of doubt
- [ ] **Proof is introduced at moments of doubt** — Evidence appears at points where the audience is most likely to be skeptical or resist, not distributed randomly throughout the narrative
- [ ] **Transitions around proof are smooth** — The narrative does not lurch into "data mode" and then lurch back; evidence flows within the same voice and rhythm as the surrounding story

### Credibility and Trust
- [ ] **Sources are identified** — Data points cite their origin (study name, company, publication, date) even if briefly and conversationally
- [ ] **Proof is verifiable** — The audience could, in principle, check the claims independently; nothing relies on "trust me" or unsourced assertions
- [ ] **Negative proof included** — The narrative acknowledges at least one limitation, caveat, "what didn't work," or counterpoint to build trust through intellectual honesty
- [ ] **Authority markers present** — The narrator or protagonist's credentials, experience, track record, or relevant authority is established early, not assumed or left implicit
- [ ] **No cherry-picking evident** — The evidence presented is representative of the broader reality, not selectively chosen to create a misleading picture

### Proof Sufficiency
- [ ] **Proof matches audience sophistication** — The type and depth of evidence matches what this specific audience finds persuasive (executives want ROI, engineers want specs, consumers want social proof, academics want methodology)
- [ ] **Proof-to-claim ratio balanced** — The narrative does not bury the story under excessive data, nor does it make bold claims with thin evidence; the ratio feels calibrated
- [ ] **Strongest proof supports the CTA** — The most compelling evidence point is positioned near the call to action, reinforcing the desired next step with maximum credibility

### Proof Presentation
- [ ] **Data visualized where possible** — Numbers that are central to the argument are presented visually (chart, comparison, infographic) or through vivid analogy, not just stated as raw figures
- [ ] **Proof is attributed to real people** — Testimonials and quotes include the speaker's name, title, and company (with permission), not anonymous "one customer said..." attributions
- [ ] **Proof is recent and relevant** — The most prominent evidence comes from situations similar to the audience's own context: same industry, same scale, same challenge type
- [ ] **Proof progression builds confidence** — Evidence is sequenced from smaller, easy-to-accept claims to larger, bolder claims; credibility compounds across the narrative

## Scoring
Must pass **17/23 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Every major claim has support
- Proof appears inside the story, not after it
- Sources are identified
- Proof matches audience sophistication
- No cherry-picking evident

Any CRITICAL failure requires an evidence integration revision pass.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to format-specific checklist (deck, email, video, etc.) and final editing
  review (editing-and-trimming-quality.md). The evidence layer is solid.
- **On Fail:** Return to the narrative architect with a proof gap analysis: list every claim,
  its current evidence, and the gap. Reference the Hall framework
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/hall/) for evidence-driven storytelling, Miller's StoryBrand
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for positioning proof as "the guide's authority," or
  the proof-in-story integration technique from the narrative framework
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/). Source additional proof assets from the
  stakeholder, customer interviews, or third-party research before revising.
