# Email Sequence Narrative Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that an email sequence tells a cohesive story across multiple messages

## Purpose
Gates the narrative integrity of multi-email sequences: welcome sequences, launch campaigns,
nurture flows, onboarding series, re-engagement sequences, or any series where multiple emails
work together over time to move the audience toward an action or transformation. Use after the
full sequence is drafted and before automation setup. Ensures each email advances a larger story
arc while still delivering value as a standalone message, and that the sequence builds momentum rather than merely repeating the same ask in different words.
An email sequence is a serialized story; each installment must justify its existence while
serving the whole.

## When to Use
- After a full email sequence is drafted and before automation platform setup
- When an existing sequence has declining open rates across the series
- Before launching any welcome, nurture, launch, or onboarding email sequence
- When converting a single long-form narrative into a multi-email journey
- During quarterly email performance reviews to diagnose weak sequences

## Prerequisites
- All emails in the sequence are drafted (at minimum, outlines with key content per email)
- The sequence goal (conversion, education, relationship building, activation, retention) is defined
- The target audience segment and their awareness stage are documented
- The sending cadence (daily, every 2 days, weekly) and total number of emails are set
- Subject lines for all emails are drafted
- The primary CTA and any secondary CTAs are defined

## Checklist

### Story Arc Across the Sequence
- [ ] **Overarching narrative exists** — The full sequence tells one recognizable story with a beginning (problem/context), middle (journey/teaching/proof), and end (resolution/offer/transformation)
- [ ] **Sequence has a protagonist** — A consistent character (the founder, a customer, the reader as hero, or a recurring persona) appears across emails, creating continuity and emotional investment
- [ ] **Emotional progression is deliberate** — The sequence moves through planned emotional phases across emails: curiosity > understanding > desire > conviction > action (or a similar intentional arc)
- [ ] **Stakes escalate across emails** — Later emails raise the consequences of inaction or the appeal of action higher than earlier emails; the sequence intensifies, not flatlines
- [ ] **Final email delivers the climax** — The last email (or second-to-last, leaving the final for urgency) contains the strongest emotional appeal, the best proof, and the most compelling CTA

### Individual Email Quality
- [ ] **Each email has one job** — Every email focuses on a single purpose (introduce, teach, prove, empathize, overcome objection, offer, create urgency) and does not try to accomplish everything at once
- [ ] **Each email stands alone** — A reader encountering any single email in isolation can understand its value and message without needing to have read the previous emails in the sequence
- [ ] **Each email advances the arc** — Removing any single email from the sequence would create a noticeable gap in the narrative progression, emotional build, or logical argument
- [ ] **Subject lines create curiosity** — Each subject line generates enough interest to justify opening, using specificity, tension, benefit, or story hooks rather than clickbait or generic labels
- [ ] **Email length is appropriate** — Each email can be read in under 3 minutes; longer content links out to blog posts, videos, or landing pages rather than expanding the email body endlessly
- [ ] **Preview text is optimized** — The first line of each email (visible in inbox preview) reinforces the subject line's promise and motivates the open

### Forward Hooks and Continuity
- [ ] **Each email hooks forward** — Every email except the last contains a reason to anticipate and open the next email: a teased continuation, an unanswered question, a promised resource, or a story cliffhanger
- [ ] **Callbacks to previous emails present** — At least 2 emails in the sequence reference something from an earlier email ("Remember when I told you about..." or "Last time we covered..."), rewarding engaged readers
- [ ] **Consistent voice throughout** — The writing voice, tone, personality, and register are recognizably the same person or brand across all emails in the sequence
- [ ] **Visual and structural consistency** — Email formatting, greeting style, sign-off, layout, and design elements are consistent enough across the sequence that each email is instantly recognized as part of the series

### Payoff and Conversion
- [ ] **Value precedes ask** — The sequence delivers substantive value (insight, tool, story, framework, encouragement) before making its primary commercial or action request; the audience feels they have received before being asked to give
- [ ] **CTA appears in every email** — Each email includes at least a soft or secondary CTA (reply, read more, try this), even before the primary offer is introduced, building the habit of engagement
- [ ] **Primary CTA is introduced with narrative context** — The main offer, when it arrives, is framed within the story the sequence has been telling (solving the problem established in email 1) rather than appearing cold
- [ ] **Urgency is earned** — Any deadline, scarcity, or limited-time element in the final emails has been set up by earlier emails that established the value and opportunity; urgency without preceding desire is just pressure
- [ ] **Sequence ends, not fades** — The final email provides clear closure: a last chance, a summary of the journey, or a definitive next step; the sequence does not simply stop sending

## Scoring
Must pass **16/21 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Overarching narrative exists
- Each email has one job
- Each email hooks forward
- Value precedes ask
- Sequence ends, not fades

Any CRITICAL failure requires sequence restructuring before automation setup.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Set up the automation platform, configure triggers and timing, implement tracking
  (open rates, click rates, conversion per email), and monitor performance after launch to
  identify weak links in the sequence for optimization.
- **On Fail:** Create a sequence map on a single page: one row per email with columns for
  Purpose, Emotion, Story Beat, Key Content, CTA, and Forward Hook. Verify the map tells a
  complete story before rewriting any individual email. Reference the narrative framework
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for story arc guidance, Miller's StoryBrand
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for message clarity and customer-as-hero positioning,
  or the Hook and Open Loop checklist (hook-and-open-loop-quality.md) for subject line and
  opening strategies. The most common failure mode is writing each email independently without
  a sequence-level narrative plan; the cure is always to map the full arc before writing any
  individual email.
