# Email Sequence Narrative Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that an email sequence tells a cohesive story across multiple messages

## Purpose
Gates the narrative integrity of multi-email sequences: welcome sequences, launch sequences, nurture campaigns, onboarding flows, or any series where multiple emails work together to move the audience toward an action. Use after the sequence is drafted and before automation setup. Ensures each email advances a larger story while standing on its own.

## Prerequisites
- All emails in the sequence are drafted
- The sequence goal (conversion, education, relationship, activation) is defined
- The target audience and their awareness stage are documented
- The sending cadence and total number of emails are set
- Subject lines are drafted

## Checklist

### Story Arc Across the Sequence
- [ ] **Overarching narrative exists** — The full sequence tells one recognizable story with a beginning (problem/context), middle (journey/teaching), and end (resolution/offer)
- [ ] **Sequence has a protagonist** — A consistent character (founder, customer, the reader themselves) appears across emails, creating continuity
- [ ] **Emotional progression is deliberate** — The sequence moves through planned emotional phases: curiosity > understanding > desire > conviction > action (or similar arc)
- [ ] **Stakes escalate across emails** — Later emails raise the consequences of inaction or the appeal of action higher than earlier emails
- [ ] **Final email delivers the climax** — The last email (or second-to-last) contains the strongest emotional appeal, the best proof, and the most urgent CTA

### Individual Email Quality
- [ ] **Each email has one job** — Every email focuses on a single purpose (introduce, teach, prove, empathize, offer) and does not try to do everything
- [ ] **Each email stands alone** — A reader encountering any single email in isolation can understand its value without having read the previous emails
- [ ] **Each email advances the arc** — Removing any email from the sequence would create a noticeable gap in the overall narrative progression
- [ ] **Subject lines create curiosity** — Each subject line generates enough interest to open, using specificity, tension, or benefit (not clickbait)
- [ ] **Email length is appropriate** — Each email can be read in under 3 minutes; longer content links out rather than expanding the email body

### Forward Hooks and Continuity
- [ ] **Each email hooks forward** — Every email except the last contains a reason to anticipate and open the next email (a teased story, an unanswered question, a promised resource)
- [ ] **Callbacks to previous emails present** — At least 2 emails in the sequence reference something from an earlier email, rewarding consistent readers
- [ ] **Consistent voice throughout** — The writing voice, tone, and personality are recognizably the same person or brand across all emails
- [ ] **Visual and structural consistency** — Email formatting, greeting style, sign-off, and layout are consistent enough to be instantly recognized in the inbox

### Payoff and Conversion
- [ ] **Value precedes ask** — The sequence delivers substantive value (insight, tool, story, framework) before making its primary commercial or action request
- [ ] **CTA appears in every email** — Each email includes at least a secondary CTA, even if the primary offer is not yet introduced
- [ ] **Primary CTA is introduced with context** — The main offer, when it appears, is framed within the narrative (solving the problem the sequence has been building) not dropped in cold
- [ ] **Urgency is earned** — Any deadline or scarcity in the final emails has been set up by earlier emails establishing the opportunity's value and time-sensitivity

## Scoring
Must pass **14/18 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Overarching narrative exists
- Each email has one job
- Each email hooks forward
- Value precedes ask

Any CRITICAL failure requires sequence restructuring.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Set up automation, configure tracking, and monitor open rates and click rates per email to identify weak links post-launch.
- **On Fail:** Outline the full sequence on a single page: one row per email with columns for Purpose, Emotion, Story Beat, CTA, and Forward Hook. Reference the narrative framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for story arc guidance and Miller's StoryBrand (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for message clarity. Ensure the sequence map tells a complete story before rewriting individual emails.
