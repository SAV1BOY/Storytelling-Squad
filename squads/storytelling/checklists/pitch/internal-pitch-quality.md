# Internal Pitch Quality
> Quality gate for verifying the business case is supported by narrative, ROI is shown, risk is addressed, and approval path is clear

## Purpose
Ensure internal pitches (budget requests, project proposals, strategic initiatives, headcount requests) combine rigorous business logic with compelling narrative. Internal audiences are often harder to convince than external ones because they know the organization's constraints, politics, and history.

## Prerequisites
- Business case documented with financial projections
- Stakeholder map with decision-makers and influencers identified
- Organizational priorities and constraints understood
- Approval process and criteria known

## Checklist

### Business Case With Narrative
- [ ] **Problem Framed as Story** — The business problem is introduced through a specific example, incident, or customer story, not just a data point
- [ ] **Status Quo Cost Quantified** — The cost of doing nothing is expressed in terms the organization cares about: revenue loss, efficiency drag, competitive risk, talent attrition
- [ ] **Solution Connected to Strategy** — The proposal is explicitly linked to organizational priorities, OKRs, or strategic pillars; it is not a standalone idea
- [ ] **Vision of Success Painted** — The narrative describes what the organization looks like after the proposal is implemented, making success tangible and desirable

### ROI Shown
- [ ] **Investment Required Is Clear** — The total cost (budget, headcount, time, opportunity cost) is stated transparently with no hidden costs
- [ ] **Return Is Quantified** — The expected return is expressed in the organization's preferred metrics: revenue, margin, efficiency, NPS, retention, risk reduction
- [ ] **Timeline to ROI Stated** — The time horizon for realizing the return is defined: when will the investment begin to pay off
- [ ] **Assumptions Documented** — Key assumptions underlying the ROI calculation are stated so decision-makers can evaluate their reasonableness

### Risk Addressed
- [ ] **Top Risks Named** — The three most likely or most severe risks of the proposal are identified explicitly, not glossed over
- [ ] **Mitigation Plans Present** — Each identified risk has a specific mitigation strategy; the pitch does not just acknowledge risk, it manages it
- [ ] **Reversibility Addressed** — The pitch explains what happens if the initiative does not work: can it be wound down, and at what cost
- [ ] **Risk of Inaction Compared** — The risk of proceeding is compared to the risk of doing nothing; decision-makers can weigh both sides

### Approval Path Clear
- [ ] **Decision Maker Identified** — The person who can say "yes" is named and the pitch is tailored to their priorities and communication style
- [ ] **Approval Criteria Known** — The pitch addresses the specific criteria the decision-maker will use to evaluate the proposal (financial threshold, strategic fit, resource availability)
- [ ] **Next Step Is Specific** — The pitch ends with a clear ask: approve the budget, schedule a deeper review, approve a pilot, assign a team
- [ ] **Stakeholder Buy-In Secured** — Key influencers and potential blockers have been consulted before the formal pitch; surprises are minimized

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the internal pitch is both a compelling story and a rigorous business case |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; business case is sound but narrative or risk section needs strengthening |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or the pitch lacks financial justification or ignores organizational politics |

## Next Steps
- If the pitch is all data and no narrative, add an opening story that illustrates the problem through a real internal or customer example
- If ROI is unclear, build a simple model with best/base/worst scenarios and present the base case prominently
- If stakeholder resistance is anticipated, pre-meet with potential blockers to understand and address their concerns before the formal pitch
