# Sales Pitch Quality
> Quality gate for verifying pain is identified, solution is shown, proof is present, objections are preempted, and CTA is clear

## Purpose
Ensure the sales pitch is structured to move the prospect from awareness of their pain to confidence in the solution to action. Every element must earn its place by advancing the prospect toward a decision.

## Prerequisites
- Prospect profile and pain points researched
- Product or service capabilities documented
- Common objections catalogued
- Pricing and next steps defined

## Checklist

### Pain Identified
- [ ] **Pain Is Named by the Prospect** — The pitch elicits or acknowledges the prospect's specific pain in their own words, not just the seller's assumptions
- [ ] **Pain Is Quantified** — The cost of the pain is expressed in measurable terms: dollars lost, hours wasted, opportunities missed, risk exposure
- [ ] **Pain Is Urgent** — The narrative communicates why solving this pain now matters more than waiting; the cost of delay is clear
- [ ] **Emotional Impact Acknowledged** — Beyond the business metrics, the frustration, stress, or risk the prospect personally feels is acknowledged

### Solution Shown
- [ ] **Solution Maps to Pain** — Each feature or capability presented directly addresses a specific pain point; no features are mentioned without a pain connection
- [ ] **Solution Is Demonstrated** — The prospect sees the solution in action (demo, walkthrough, example, case study), not just described in theory
- [ ] **Differentiation Is Specific** — What makes this solution different from alternatives is concrete: speed, cost, capability, integration, support, not generic claims
- [ ] **Value Is Quantified** — The ROI, time savings, or risk reduction is expressed in numbers the prospect can validate against their own situation

### Proof Present
- [ ] **Case Study or Testimonial Included** — At least one real customer example demonstrates the solution working for a similar prospect
- [ ] **Proof Is Relevant** — The social proof comes from a customer similar to the prospect in industry, size, or challenge, not a mismatched reference
- [ ] **Results Are Specific** — Proof includes specific outcomes (percentages, dollar amounts, timeframes), not vague endorsements

### Objections Preempted
- [ ] **Top 3 Objections Addressed** — The most common objections (price, timing, switching cost, risk, internal resistance) are addressed before the prospect raises them
- [ ] **Objections Handled With Evidence** — Each objection is countered with data, customer examples, or guarantees, not just reassurance
- [ ] **Risk Reduction Offered** — A risk-reduction mechanism is present: trial, guarantee, phased rollout, or pilot program

### CTA
- [ ] **Next Step Is Clear** — The pitch ends with one specific, immediate next step the prospect can take
- [ ] **CTA Is Low-Friction** — The requested action is easy to take: schedule a follow-up, start a trial, sign a letter of intent
- [ ] **Urgency Connected to CTA** — A reason to act now (limited offer, implementation timeline, competitive window) is connected to the next step

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 17 items checked; the pitch moves the prospect from pain to action with proof at every step |
| Conditional | 13-16 items checked; structure is sound but proof or objection handling needs strengthening |
| Fail | Fewer than 13 items, or pain is assumed rather than confirmed, or no CTA exists |

## Next Steps
- If pain is assumed, restructure the opening to include discovery questions that surface the prospect's own language
- If proof is weak, invest in collecting specific, quantified customer success stories
- Role-play the pitch with someone playing a skeptical prospect and test objection handling
