# Partnership Pitch Quality
> Quality gate for verifying mutual value is clear, alignment is demonstrated, and next step is defined

## Purpose
Ensure the partnership pitch communicates mutual benefit, demonstrates strategic alignment, and proposes a clear path forward. Unlike sales pitches (where one party pays), partnership pitches must convince both sides that the relationship creates more value together than apart.

## Prerequisites
- Potential partner's strategy, goals, and challenges researched
- Own organization's strategic goals documented
- Specific partnership model proposed (co-marketing, integration, distribution, co-creation)

## Checklist

### Mutual Value Clear
- [ ] **Value to Partner Articulated First** — The pitch leads with what the partner gains, not what you need from them; their benefit is front and center
- [ ] **Value to Your Organization Stated** — Your own strategic interest in the partnership is transparently communicated; hidden agendas destroy trust
- [ ] **Combined Value Greater Than Sum** — The pitch explains what the partnership creates together that neither party could achieve alone (1+1=3)
- [ ] **Value Is Specific and Measurable** — The benefit to each side is expressed in concrete terms: reach, revenue, capability, credibility, access

### Alignment Demonstrated
- [ ] **Strategic Alignment Shown** — The pitch connects the partnership to both organizations' strategic priorities, not just a tactical opportunity
- [ ] **Audience/Market Overlap Identified** — The pitch demonstrates where audiences, customers, or markets overlap, making the partnership logical to both sides
- [ ] **Cultural Compatibility Addressed** — The pitch acknowledges that the organizations are compatible in values, working style, or brand positioning
- [ ] **Complementary Strengths Mapped** — Each partner's unique strengths are identified and the pitch shows how they complement each other

### Proposal and Structure
- [ ] **Partnership Model Defined** — The type of partnership (revenue share, co-marketing, integration, distribution, co-development) is named and explained
- [ ] **Roles and Responsibilities Outlined** — What each party contributes (resources, assets, effort, expertise) is clearly proposed, even if subject to negotiation
- [ ] **Success Metrics Proposed** — How both parties will measure whether the partnership is working is defined upfront
- [ ] **Risk Addressed** — Potential risks, failure modes, or exit conditions are acknowledged honestly, not hidden

### Next Step Defined
- [ ] **Specific Next Step Proposed** — The pitch ends with a concrete next action: joint working session, pilot project, term sheet review, introduction to counterpart
- [ ] **Timeline Suggested** — A proposed timeline for moving from pitch to pilot to full partnership is included
- [ ] **Decision Maker Engaged** — The pitch is directed at or will reach the person with authority to approve the partnership on the partner's side
- [ ] **Low-Commitment Entry Point** — The initial ask is small enough to get a "yes" without requiring full organizational commitment (pilot, test, exploratory meeting)

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the partnership pitch demonstrates clear mutual value with a defined path forward |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; value and alignment are present but the proposal or next step needs sharpening |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or the pitch is one-sided, vague on mutual benefit, or has no clear next step |

## Next Steps
- If the pitch feels one-sided, rewrite the opening paragraph entirely from the partner's perspective
- If alignment is assumed, gather specific data on audience overlap, strategic fit, or market adjacency
- If the next step is too big, propose a smaller pilot that lets both sides test the relationship with low risk
