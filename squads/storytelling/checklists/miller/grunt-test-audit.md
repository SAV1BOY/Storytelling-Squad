# Grunt Test Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the website or messaging answers three questions in 5 seconds: What do you offer? How does it improve my life? How do I buy?

## Purpose
Ensure the primary communication (website, landing page, pitch deck opening) is so clear that even a distracted, uninterested visitor ("a caveman") could grunt the answers to three fundamental questions within five seconds of exposure. If any question goes unanswered, potential customers leave.

## Prerequisites
- Website homepage or primary landing page live or in mockup
- Above-the-fold content finalized
- Target customer persona defined

## Checklist

### Question 1: What Do You Offer?
- [ ] **Offering Named Clearly** — The product or service category is stated in plain language in the header or first visible text
- [ ] **No Clever Taglines Replacing Clarity** — If a tagline exists, it supplements the clear offer statement, not replaces it
- [ ] **Category Recognized Instantly** — A visitor knows within 2 seconds whether this is a software product, consulting service, physical good, or other category
- [ ] **Specificity Over Generality** — The offer is specific ("project management for remote teams") not vague ("we help businesses succeed")

### Question 2: How Does It Improve My Life?
- [ ] **Benefit Stated in Customer Terms** — The improvement is described from the customer's perspective: what they gain, save, avoid, or become
- [ ] **Improvement Is Concrete** — The benefit is measurable or visualizable ("save 10 hours a week," "never miss a deadline") not abstract ("optimize your workflow")
- [ ] **Emotional and Practical Benefit Both Present** — The customer understands both how they will feel and what they will practically gain
- [ ] **Visible Above the Fold** — The benefit statement does not require scrolling to find; it is in the first screen of content

### Question 3: How Do I Buy/Start?
- [ ] **CTA Is Visible** — A button, link, or instruction for the next step is visible without scrolling
- [ ] **CTA Uses Action Language** — The button text is a verb phrase ("Get Started," "Book a Demo," "Buy Now") not a label ("Learn More," "Submit")
- [ ] **Path Is Frictionless** — Clicking the CTA leads directly to the next step (checkout, calendar, sign-up) not to another informational page
- [ ] **CTA Stands Out Visually** — The call-to-action button or link contrasts with surrounding elements; it is the most obvious interactive element

### Overall Five-Second Test
- [ ] **All Three Questions Answered Simultaneously** — A visitor can identify what you offer, how it helps, and how to start in a single five-second glance
- [ ] **No Competing Messages** — The above-the-fold area does not contain multiple offers, rotating carousels, or conflicting value propositions
- [ ] **Works on Mobile** — The three answers are accessible on a mobile screen without zooming, scrolling, or rotating

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; a stranger passes the grunt test on first visit |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; two of three questions are immediately clear, one needs work |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or any of the three core questions is unanswered in 5 seconds |

## Next Steps
- Record five people visiting the site for the first time and ask them to answer the three questions after 5 seconds
- If the tagline is clever but unclear, replace it with a clear statement and test conversion
- If the CTA is below the fold, move it up and duplicate it at natural scroll-stop points
