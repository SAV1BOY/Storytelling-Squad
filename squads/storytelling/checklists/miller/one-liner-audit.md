# One-Liner Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the one-liner follows Problem-Solution-Result in approximately 30 words and passes the cocktail party test

## Purpose
Ensure the brand's one-liner is a concise, memorable statement that anyone in the organization can deliver naturally in conversation. It must communicate the problem, the solution, and the result clearly enough that a stranger immediately understands the value.

## Prerequisites
- StoryBrand BrandScript completed
- Target customer persona defined
- One-liner drafted

## Checklist

### Structure: Problem-Solution-Result
- [ ] **Problem Stated First** — The one-liner opens with the customer's pain point or challenge, hooking attention through relevance
- [ ] **Solution Follows Naturally** — The brand's offering is positioned as the bridge from problem to result, not as the hero
- [ ] **Result Closes the Statement** — The one-liner ends with a concrete, desirable outcome the listener can picture
- [ ] **Three Parts in Order** — The sequence is Problem then Solution then Result, not rearranged or combined in confusing ways

### Brevity and Clarity
- [ ] **Under 35 Words** — The entire one-liner is 30 words or fewer, ideally; absolute maximum is 35
- [ ] **No Jargon** — Industry-specific terms, acronyms, or insider language are absent; a 12-year-old could understand it
- [ ] **No Filler Words** — Every word earns its place; adjectives like "innovative," "cutting-edge," or "world-class" are removed
- [ ] **Single Sentence or Two Short Sentences** — The one-liner does not require a paragraph to deliver

### Cocktail Party Test
- [ ] **Natural to Say Aloud** — The one-liner sounds like something a human would actually say in conversation, not marketing copy
- [ ] **Provokes Curiosity** — After hearing it, the listener's natural response is "How do you do that?" or "Tell me more"
- [ ] **Memorable After One Hearing** — A listener could roughly repeat the one-liner after hearing it once, even if not word-for-word
- [ ] **Works Without Context** — The one-liner makes sense to someone with zero prior knowledge of the brand

### Emotional and Practical Impact
- [ ] **Problem Resonates** — The problem named is one the target audience actually cares about and recognizes in their own life
- [ ] **Result Is Aspirational** — The outcome described is something the listener genuinely wants, not just a feature restatement
- [ ] **Distinct From Competitors** — The one-liner would not work equally well for a direct competitor; it has brand specificity

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the one-liner is clear, concise, and conversation-ready |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; structure is right but phrasing needs polish |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or the one-liner cannot be delivered naturally in conversation |

## Next Steps
- Have five employees deliver the one-liner to strangers and record the responses
- If the one-liner exceeds 35 words, cut the least essential phrase and test again
- If it does not provoke curiosity, sharpen the problem statement to be more specific and urgent
