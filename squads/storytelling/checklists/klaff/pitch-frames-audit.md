# Pitch Frames Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the pitch frame is identified, controlled, and not lost during delivery

## Purpose
Ensure the pitcher operates from a strong frame (power, time, intrigue, or prize) as defined by Oren Klaff in "Pitch Anything." Frame control determines who holds psychological authority in any interaction. Losing frame means losing the pitch.

## Prerequisites
- Pitch drafted and rehearsed
- Target audience and their likely frame identified
- Understanding of Klaff's four frame types: Power Frame, Time Frame, Intrigue Frame, Prize Frame

## Checklist

### Frame Identification
- [ ] **Pitcher's Frame Named** — The primary frame the pitcher intends to hold is explicitly identified before the pitch begins
- [ ] **Audience's Frame Anticipated** — The likely frame the audience will bring (analyst frame, power frame, time constraint) is predicted and prepared for
- [ ] **Frame Collision Planned** — The moment when pitcher's frame and audience's frame collide is anticipated, with a strategy for winning the collision
- [ ] **Frame Type Appropriate** — The chosen frame matches the context: power frame for status situations, intrigue for skeptics, prize for competitive situations, time for urgency

### Frame Control
- [ ] **Frame Established Early** — The pitcher's frame is set within the first 60 seconds through behavior, language, or staging, not left to emerge organically
- [ ] **Frame Maintained Under Pressure** — When the audience challenges the frame (interruptions, objections, power plays), the pitcher responds by reinforcing rather than abandoning the frame
- [ ] **No Beta Submission** — The pitcher does not defer unnecessarily, apologize for taking time, or signal that the audience is doing them a favor by listening
- [ ] **Frame Stacking Used** — Multiple frames are layered when needed (intrigue + prize, time + power) to strengthen frame control

### Frame Not Lost
- [ ] **Audience Power Plays Deflected** — If the audience attempts to seize frame (checking phones, cutting time, challenging authority), the pitcher has a pre-planned response
- [ ] **No Frame Surrender Signals** — The pitcher does not use language that surrenders frame: "If you have time," "I know you're busy," "Just one more thing"
- [ ] **Reframe Capability Demonstrated** — If the frame is momentarily lost, the pitcher recovers within seconds by reframing the interaction
- [ ] **Frame Control Is Subtle** — Frame mastery does not look aggressive or combative; it looks like quiet confidence and social intelligence

### Strategic Application
- [ ] **Frame Serves the Pitch Goal** — Frame control is used to advance the pitch's objective, not as an ego exercise or dominance display
- [ ] **Audience Feels Elevated** — Despite frame control, the audience feels respected and important, not diminished; prize frame especially should make them feel they are gaining access
- [ ] **Frame Transitions Are Smooth** — If the frame shifts during the pitch (opening with intrigue, moving to prize), transitions feel natural
- [ ] **Post-Pitch Frame Maintained** — Frame control extends beyond the pitch itself into follow-up, negotiation, and next-step conversations

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the pitcher holds frame throughout with skill and subtlety |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; frame is mostly controlled but slips at key moments |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or frame is lost and not recovered during the pitch |

## Next Steps
- Role-play the pitch with someone playing an aggressive audience member to test frame resilience
- If beta submission is present, rewrite opening lines to eliminate permission-seeking language
- Study Klaff's examples of frame collision and practice the specific language of reframing
