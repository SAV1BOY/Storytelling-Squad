# Five-Second Moment Audit
> Quality gate for verifying a single moment of change is identified and the story is built around it

## Purpose
Ensure the story is anchored to a specific, identifiable five-second moment when everything changed for the protagonist. Matthew Dicks teaches that every great story is about a single moment of transformation, and everything else in the story exists to make that moment land.

## Prerequisites
- Story draft or outline completed
- Protagonist's transformation identified
- Understanding of Dicks' five-second moment concept from "Storyworthy"

## Checklist

### Moment Identification
- [ ] **Single Moment Identified** — One specific moment in time (not a period, not a theme, not a feeling) is identified as the story's core; it could be described in 5 seconds
- [ ] **Moment Is a Change** — The moment represents a shift: a realization, a decision, a discovery, or an irreversible event where the protagonist's understanding of the world changed
- [ ] **Moment Is Specific** — The moment has a place, a time, sensory details, and a concrete trigger; it is not abstract ("I gradually learned to love myself")
- [ ] **Moment Is Personal** — The moment happened to the protagonist (or the storyteller), not to someone else observed from the outside

### Story Built Around the Moment
- [ ] **Everything Before Serves as Setup** — Every element preceding the moment creates the conditions necessary for it to land with maximum impact
- [ ] **Setup Creates Contrast** — The audience understands who the protagonist was before the moment so that the change is felt as a genuine shift
- [ ] **No Unnecessary Backstory** — Only the information needed to make the five-second moment land is included; extraneous context is cut
- [ ] **Pacing Builds Toward the Moment** — The narrative accelerates or intensifies as it approaches the moment, not meanders

### Moment Execution
- [ ] **Moment Is Dramatized** — The five-second moment is shown in real time with sensory detail, not summarized or told in retrospect
- [ ] **Audience Feels the Change** — The moment produces an emotional response in the audience: surprise, recognition, tears, laughter, goosebumps
- [ ] **Moment Is Not Announced** — The storyteller does not say "and that's when I realized..."; the realization emerges from the dramatized moment itself
- [ ] **Moment Is Not Vague** — The change is clear enough that the audience could state what shifted, even if they phrase it differently than the storyteller would

### After the Moment
- [ ] **Brief Resolution** — The story ends shortly after the moment; it does not belabor the aftermath or explain what the moment meant
- [ ] **No Moral Tacked On** — The lesson is embedded in the moment, not appended as a conclusion or thesis statement
- [ ] **Story Lands, Not Crashes** — The ending gives the audience a beat to absorb the moment before it is over

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the story is built like an arrow pointed at one transformative moment |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; moment exists but the story meanders or the moment is not dramatized fully |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or no single moment of change can be identified |

## Next Steps
- If the moment is vague, ask: "What is the exact second when things changed? Where was I standing? What did I hear?"
- If the story has multiple moments, choose the most powerful one and restructure everything around it
- Practice telling only the five-second moment to see if it stands alone; if it does not, the moment is not specific enough
