# Story Beginning-End Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the first and last lines are opposites with maximum contrast

## Purpose
Ensure the story's opening and closing create maximum contrast, demonstrating transformation through juxtaposition. Matthew Dicks teaches that the first and last sentences of a story should be as opposite as possible, proving that change occurred.

## Prerequisites
- Complete story draft with opening and closing lines written
- Five-second moment of transformation identified
- Story arc understood from beginning state to ending state

## Checklist

### Opposite Construction
- [ ] **First and Last Lines Identified** — The opening and closing sentences (or images) are explicitly documented side by side for comparison
- [ ] **Lines Are Opposites** — The first and last lines express opposing states, emotions, situations, or beliefs (e.g., "I was alone" / "I was finally home")
- [ ] **Contrast Is Maximum** — The distance between the two lines is as large as the story can credibly support; they are not merely different, they are opposite
- [ ] **Opposition Is Thematic** — The contrast reflects the story's core transformation, not a superficial change in circumstance

### Opening Line Quality
- [ ] **Opens with Action or Location** — The first line places the audience in a specific moment, not a philosophical reflection or background information
- [ ] **Establishes the "Before" State** — The opening line communicates (or implies) who the protagonist is before the transformation
- [ ] **Creates Curiosity** — The first line makes the audience want to hear the next sentence; it hooks without gimmickry
- [ ] **No Throat-Clearing** — The story starts at the latest possible point; there is no preamble, context-setting, or "Let me tell you a story about..."

### Closing Line Quality
- [ ] **Lands the Transformation** — The last line embodies the "after" state, showing the audience the change without explaining it
- [ ] **Brief and Final** — The closing is concise; it does not trail off, add caveats, or append a lesson
- [ ] **Resonates Emotionally** — The last line produces an emotional response: the audience feels the weight of the transformation
- [ ] **Does Not Explain** — The closing line does not interpret the story for the audience; it trusts them to feel the meaning

### Structural Integrity
- [ ] **Arc Connects the Two Lines** — Every element between the opening and closing serves the journey from the first state to the opposite state
- [ ] **No Content After the Ending** — The story stops at the closing line; there is no epilogue, no "and that's why..." no housekeeping
- [ ] **Beginning and End Are the Bookends** — If the first and last lines were placed side by side, the audience could intuit the entire story's emotional journey

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the first and last lines create a powerful frame of transformation |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; contrast is present but not maximized, or one line is weaker |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or first and last lines are not in opposition |

## Next Steps
- Write the first and last lines on a single index card; if the contrast is not immediately visible, revise
- If the opening has throat-clearing, delete the first paragraph and start where the action begins
- If the closing explains, cut everything after the last moment of dramatized action and test with an audience
