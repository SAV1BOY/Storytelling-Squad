# Opening and Close Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative begins with impact and ends with resonance

## Purpose
Gates the first and last impressions of a narrative. The opening determines whether the audience stays; the close determines what they remember and do. Use this checklist as a final quality pass on the bookends of any narrative: articles, presentations, videos, emails, pitches, or speeches. Ensures the opening hooks and the closing resolves, calls back, or propels action.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists
- The hook and open loop checklist has been reviewed (or is being run concurrently)
- The central message and CTA are established
- The emotional arc has been mapped

## Checklist

### Opening Quality
- [ ] **First sentence is active** — The opening sentence contains a subject doing something, creating immediate momentum; no passive constructions or state-of-being verbs
- [ ] **Opening earns the next sentence** — The first sentence creates enough curiosity or interest that the audience is compelled to continue to the second sentence
- [ ] **No preamble or warm-up** — The narrative begins at the latest possible moment; there is no "Before I begin..." or "In order to understand..." wind-up
- [ ] **Tone is established immediately** — The audience knows within the first two sentences what kind of experience this will be (serious, playful, urgent, intimate, bold)
- [ ] **Protagonist or situation introduced early** — The audience meets a character or enters a situation within the first 10% of the narrative; abstract setup is minimal
- [ ] **Opening is specific, not general** — The narrative starts with a concrete detail, moment, or statement, not a broad generalization about the world

### Close Quality
- [ ] **Close is deliberate, not exhausted** — The ending feels intentionally crafted, not like the writer ran out of material
- [ ] **Callback to opening present** — The close references, echoes, or transforms an element from the opening, creating structural symmetry
- [ ] **Central message reinforced** — The ending restates or embodies the core message in a fresh way, not by repeating the opening verbatim
- [ ] **Emotional landing is satisfying** — The final emotional beat resolves the emotional arc; the audience feels a sense of completion, not abrupt abandonment
- [ ] **CTA is positioned at or near the close** — If the narrative has a call to action, it appears in the final 10% when motivation is highest

### Symmetry and Architecture
- [ ] **Opening question is answered** — If the opening poses a question or tension, the close provides the answer or resolution
- [ ] **Transformation is visible between open and close** — Comparing the opening state to the closing state reveals clear change: in the character, the audience's understanding, or the situation
- [ ] **Close introduces no new threads** — The final section does not raise questions, topics, or tensions that are left unresolved
- [ ] **Length of close is proportional** — The resolution occupies 10-15% of the total narrative; it is neither rushed nor overextended

### Impact and Memorability
- [ ] **Last sentence is crafted** — The final sentence has been deliberately written for impact: rhythm, word choice, and finality are considered
- [ ] **Close is quotable or shareable** — The final thought is strong enough that the audience could repeat it to someone else
- [ ] **Audience knows what to do next** — Whether explicit (CTA) or implicit (reflect, share, change behavior), the audience leaves with a clear next action or takeaway
- [ ] **Silence after the close** — The narrative does not undercut its ending with disclaimers, afterthoughts, or "one more thing" additions that dilute the impact

## Scoring
Must pass **14/18 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Opening earns the next sentence
- No preamble or warm-up
- Callback to opening present
- Last sentence is crafted

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision of the opening, close, or both.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** The narrative bookends are strong. Proceed to final production and format-specific review.
- **On Fail:** Rewrite the opening and close independently, then connect them. For openings, reference the Hook and Open Loop checklist (hook-and-open-loop-quality.md) and Klaff's frame-setting techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/klaff/). For closes, reference Duarte's "new bliss" ending (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) or Dicks' "five-second moment" technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/). Write 3 alternative openings and 3 alternative closes, then select the strongest combination.
