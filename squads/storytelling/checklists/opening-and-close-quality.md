# Opening and Close Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative begins with impact and ends with resonance

## Purpose
Gates the first and last impressions of a narrative. The opening determines whether the audience
stays; the close determines what they remember and what they do next. These two bookends carry
disproportionate weight in audience experience and recall (the peak-end rule). Use this checklist
as a final quality pass on the bookends of any narrative: articles, presentations, videos, emails,
pitches, speeches, or landing pages. Ensures the opening hooks without misleading and the close
resolves, calls back, and propels action.

## When to Use
- As a final quality pass on the bookends of any completed narrative
- When a narrative's body is strong but it "starts slow" or "ends weakly"
- Before publishing, presenting, or sending any high-stakes content
- When revising content that has poor engagement metrics (low read-through, early drop-off)
- During A/B testing to evaluate alternative openings or closes

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists with beginning and ending written
- The hook and open loop checklist has been reviewed (or is being run concurrently)
- The central message and CTA are established and documented
- The emotional arc has been mapped and the intended closing emotion is defined
- The opening and closing have been read as a pair to check for symmetry

## Checklist

### Opening Quality
- [ ] **First sentence is active and specific** — The opening sentence contains a subject doing something concrete, creating immediate momentum; no passive constructions, state-of-being verbs, or abstract generalizations
- [ ] **Opening earns the next sentence** — The first sentence creates enough curiosity, tension, or interest that the audience is compelled to continue to the second sentence; it does not merely exist
- [ ] **No preamble or warm-up** — The narrative begins at the latest possible moment; there is no "Before I begin..." or "In order to understand..." or "Let me provide some context..." wind-up
- [ ] **Tone is established immediately** — The audience knows within the first two sentences what kind of experience this will be: serious, playful, urgent, intimate, bold, technical, or conversational
- [ ] **Protagonist or situation introduced early** — The audience meets a character, enters a situation, or encounters a tension within the first 10% of the narrative; abstract setup is minimal
- [ ] **Opening is specific, not general** — The narrative starts with a concrete detail, specific moment, named person, or precise statement, not a broad generalization about the world or industry

### Close Quality
- [ ] **Close is deliberate, not exhausted** — The ending feels intentionally crafted as the capstone of the narrative, not like the writer ran out of material, energy, or time
- [ ] **Callback to opening present** — The close references, echoes, mirrors, or transforms an element from the opening, creating structural symmetry and a sense of completeness
- [ ] **Central message reinforced** — The ending restates or embodies the core message in a fresh way: from a new angle, with accumulated weight, or through the lens of the journey traveled
- [ ] **Emotional landing is satisfying** — The final emotional beat resolves the emotional arc; the audience feels a sense of completion, resolution, or purposeful forward momentum, not abrupt abandonment
- [ ] **CTA is positioned at or near the close** — If the narrative has a call to action, it appears in the final 10-15% when motivation, trust, and clarity are at their highest

### Symmetry and Architecture
- [ ] **Opening question is answered** — If the opening poses a question, mystery, or tension, the close provides the answer, resolution, or transformed perspective
- [ ] **Transformation is visible between open and close** — Comparing the opening state to the closing state reveals clear change: in the character, the audience's understanding, the situation, or the emotional register
- [ ] **Close introduces no new threads** — The final section does not raise new questions, topics, characters, or tensions that are left unresolved; it only resolves existing ones
- [ ] **Length of close is proportional** — The resolution occupies 10-15% of the total narrative; it is neither rushed through in a single sentence nor overextended into a second story
- [ ] **Opening and close are the strongest writing** — The first and last paragraphs (or scenes, or slides) represent the highest-quality writing in the entire piece; they have received the most revision attention

### Impact and Memorability
- [ ] **Last sentence is crafted for impact** — The final sentence has been deliberately written and revised for rhythm, word choice, and emotional finality; it lands with a full stop, not a trailing off
- [ ] **Close is quotable or shareable** — The final thought, image, or call to action is strong enough that the audience could repeat it to someone else or would screenshot/bookmark it
- [ ] **Audience knows what to do next** — Whether explicit (CTA) or implicit (reflect, share, change behavior, feel differently), the audience leaves with a clear next action or transformed understanding
- [ ] **Silence after the close** — The narrative does not undercut its ending with disclaimers, afterthoughts, P.S. additions, or "one more thing" tangents that dilute the impact of the final beat

### Revision and Testing
- [ ] **Opening revised at least 3 times** — The opening has gone through a minimum of 3 distinct revision passes; it is not a first draft
- [ ] **Close revised at least 3 times** — The close has received the same revision attention as the opening; endings deserve as much craft as beginnings
- [ ] **Opening and close read as a pair** — Reading only the first paragraph and the last paragraph back-to-back reveals a coherent, satisfying mini-arc
- [ ] **Alternative opens and closes were considered** — At least 2 alternative openings and 2 alternative closes were drafted before the final versions were selected

## Scoring
Must pass **19/26 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Opening earns the next sentence
- No preamble or warm-up
- Callback to opening present
- Last sentence is crafted for impact
- Opening and close are the strongest writing

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision of the opening, close, or both.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** The narrative bookends are strong. Proceed to final production, design, or
  format-specific review. The opening and close will carry the piece.
- **On Fail:** Rewrite the opening and close independently, then reconnect them. For openings,
  reference the Hook and Open Loop checklist (hook-and-open-loop-quality.md) and Klaff's
  frame-setting techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/klaff/). For closes, reference
  Duarte's "new bliss" ending (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) or Dicks' "five-second
  moment" technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/). Write 3-5 alternative openings and
  3-5 alternative closes using different strategies, then select the strongest combination and
  verify the callback between them works.
