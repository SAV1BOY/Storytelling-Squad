# Hook and Open Loop Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative opening captures attention and creates forward momentum

## Purpose
Gates the effectiveness of the opening hook and any open loops used to sustain attention.
Use this after drafting but before finalizing any narrative where the first 3 seconds (video/audio)
or first sentence (text) determines whether the audience stays. Critical for content in
competitive-attention environments: social media, email subject lines, presentation openings,
pitch meetings, and any context where the audience has an easy exit.

## When to Use
- After drafting any content where the audience can easily disengage (social, email, video, web)
- When open rates, click rates, or retention metrics indicate weak openings
- Before finalizing subject lines, video intros, presentation openings, or social post first lines
- When A/B testing opening strategies across variants
- During revision of underperforming content

## Prerequisites
- A complete or near-complete narrative draft exists
- The target audience and channel are defined
- The core message and CTA are established
- Competitive context is understood (what else is competing for the audience's attention?)
- The emotional entry point for the audience is identified

## Checklist

### Hook Presence and Speed
- [ ] **Hook appears in first sentence or first 3 seconds** — The very first unit of content (sentence, frame, slide) contains a hook; no preamble, logo, or introduction precedes it
- [ ] **Hook is not a generic question** — The opening avoids "Have you ever wondered...?" "Did you know...?" or any overused rhetorical question that audiences have learned to ignore
- [ ] **Hook creates immediate stakes** — The audience understands within the first moment what they might gain or lose by paying attention to this content
- [ ] **Hook is audience-specific** — The opening references something the target audience specifically cares about, fears, or desires, not a universal truism applicable to anyone
- [ ] **Pattern interrupt present** — The opening breaks expectations: a surprising fact, a counterintuitive claim, an unexpected format, a bold declaration, or an in-media-res story start
- [ ] **Hook is honest** — The attention-grabbing element is truthful and representative of the content that follows; it does not overpromise or misrepresent

### Curiosity Gap and Open Loops
- [ ] **Curiosity gap established** — The opening introduces a question, tension, or incomplete idea that can only be resolved by continuing to read, watch, or listen
- [ ] **Promise made explicitly or implicitly** — The audience can sense what payoff awaits them if they keep engaging: a solution, a revelation, a transformation, or an answer
- [ ] **Open loop is specific, not vague** — The unanswered question is concrete ("How did a 3-person team outperform a Fortune 500 company?") not generic ("You won't believe what happens next")
- [ ] **Loop resolution planned and mapped** — Every open loop created in the first 25% of content has a specific resolution point documented later in the narrative
- [ ] **No more than 3 concurrent open loops** — The narrative does not stack so many unresolved threads that the audience feels confused, anxious, or manipulated rather than curious
- [ ] **Loops resolve in order of creation** — Earlier loops close before or alongside later loops; no first-opened loop is the last to close unless deliberately nested

### "Why Now" and Urgency
- [ ] **Timeliness established** — The opening communicates why this story matters right now: a recent event, a seasonal moment, a market shift, a deadline, or a trend
- [ ] **Relevance to audience's current situation** — The hook connects to something the audience is actively experiencing, deciding, or feeling at this moment in their life or work
- [ ] **Cost of inaction implied** — The audience senses a consequence of ignoring or delaying engagement with this content: a missed opportunity, a continuing pain, or a competitive disadvantage
- [ ] **Freshness signal present** — The content signals that it contains something new: new data, a new perspective, a recent story, or a fresh framework, not recycled common knowledge

### Hook-to-Body Transition
- [ ] **Hook connects to the body** — The attention-grabbing opening flows naturally into the narrative; there is no jarring shift in topic, tone, or register between the hook and the main content
- [ ] **Promise aligns with delivery** — What the hook promises is actually delivered in the body of the content; no bait-and-switch where the opening suggests one topic and the body covers another
- [ ] **Second hook within first 20%** — A reinforcing hook or new tension point appears before the audience reaches the first quarter of the content, providing a second reason to stay
- [ ] **Escalation follows the hook** — The story does not plateau after the opening; stakes, information, or tension continue to build immediately following the initial hook

### Hook Testing and Validation
- [ ] **Multiple hook variants considered** — At least 3 different hook strategies were drafted and the strongest was selected based on audience fit and strategic alignment
- [ ] **Hook tested on a naive reader** — Someone unfamiliar with the content has read or viewed only the opening and reported whether they would continue; their response informed revision
- [ ] **Hook is channel-optimized** — The hook format accounts for how the audience encounters it: subject line + preview text for email, first frame for video, first line before "see more" for social
- [ ] **Hook does not require prior knowledge** — A first-time visitor encountering this content with no previous relationship to the brand can still understand and be engaged by the opening

## Scoring
Must pass **17/23 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Hook appears in first sentence or first 3 seconds
- Curiosity gap established
- Promise aligns with delivery
- Hook connects to the body
- Hook is honest

Any CRITICAL failure means the opening must be rewritten before the narrative advances.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Proceed to structure and pacing review (structure-and-pacing-quality.md). For
  video content, also run video-script-narrative-quality.md. For social content, run
  social-media-story-quality.md.
- **On Fail:** Rewrite the opening using a different hook strategy. Reference Klaff's frame
  control techniques (squads/storytelling/checklists/klaff/) for pitch hooks, the Harmon Story
  Circle (squads/storytelling/checklists/harmon/) for narrative hooks, or Dicks' "first, last,
  best, worst" technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for finding a stronger entry
  point. Write 5 hook variants using different strategies (question, bold claim, story start,
  statistic, contrast) and test the strongest with a sample audience.
