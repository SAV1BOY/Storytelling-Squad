# Deck Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a presentation deck tells a story, not just displays information

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of slide decks and visual presentations. Use after a deck is drafted and before design finalization or delivery. Applies to pitch decks, keynote presentations, sales decks, board updates, and any slide-based storytelling. Ensures the deck follows a narrative arc, uses contrast effectively, and builds toward a decisive moment.

## Prerequisites
- A complete deck draft exists (slides with content, even if not designed)
- The presentation context is defined (audience, setting, time limit, stakes)
- The core message and CTA are established
- The pitch or presentation narrative has been reviewed for content quality

## Checklist

### Sparkline and Narrative Arc
- [ ] **Sparkline structure present** — The deck alternates between "what is" (current reality) and "what could be" (vision/possibility), creating a rhythmic contrast throughout
- [ ] **Opening establishes the world** — The first 2-3 slides set context: who is the audience, what is the current situation, and why are we here
- [ ] **Tension introduced early** — By slide 5 or the first 15% of the deck, a problem, gap, or opportunity that demands attention is introduced
- [ ] **Rising action through the middle** — The middle section builds evidence, deepens the problem, or escalates the opportunity progressively
- [ ] **Climactic moment exists** — There is a single slide or sequence that represents the peak: the big reveal, the key insight, or the transformative offer
- [ ] **Resolution and CTA close the deck** — The final slides resolve the tension and present a clear, specific call to action

### Contrast and Persuasion
- [ ] **Before/after contrast used** — At least one slide pair or sequence shows the world without the solution vs. the world with it
- [ ] **Enemy slide present** — The deck names or visualizes the antagonist (broken status quo, competitor weakness, industry failure) that the audience should oppose
- [ ] **"New bliss" slide present** — The deck paints a vivid picture of the desired future state that the audience can aspire to

### Audience Journey
- [ ] **Audience journey mapped** — Each slide answers the question "what does the audience think/feel at this point?" and the answer changes across the deck
- [ ] **Cognitive load managed** — No single slide contains more than one core idea; complex ideas are broken across multiple slides
- [ ] **Transitions are logical** — Each slide flows naturally from the previous; a viewer can predict the general direction of the next slide
- [ ] **Questions anticipated** — Slides that are likely to prompt audience questions are followed by slides that address those questions

### S.T.A.R. Moment and Memorability
- [ ] **S.T.A.R. moment planned** — At least one "Something They'll Always Remember" moment is designed: a dramatic visual, a live demonstration, a shocking statistic, or an emotional story
- [ ] **S.T.A.R. moment is near the climax** — The most memorable moment occurs in the second half of the deck, near the peak of the narrative arc
- [ ] **Deck has a repeatable headline** — There is one sentence or phrase from the deck that the audience could use to describe the presentation to someone who was not there

### Technical and Pacing
- [ ] **Slide count appropriate** — The deck has roughly 1 slide per minute of presentation time, plus or minus 20%
- [ ] **No text-wall slides** — No slide contains more than 30 words of body text; data-heavy slides use visuals, charts, or progressive reveal
- [ ] **Consistent visual language** — Typography, color, imagery style, and layout are consistent throughout, reinforcing rather than distracting from the narrative
- [ ] **Deck works without presenter** — Key narrative beats are understandable from the slides alone (for leave-behind scenarios), or a separate leave-behind version exists

## Scoring
Must pass **15/20 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Sparkline structure present
- Climactic moment exists
- Resolution and CTA close the deck
- S.T.A.R. moment planned

Any CRITICAL failure requires deck restructuring before design finalization.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to visual design and rehearsal. Time the presentation and iterate on pacing.
- **On Fail:** Restructure the deck narrative. Reference Duarte's Sparkline framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) for contrast structure, Snyder's Beat Sheet (squads/storytelling/checklists/snyder/) for dramatic pacing, or the presentation framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/presentation/) for overall deck architecture. Storyboard the deck on paper before rebuilding slides.
