# Social Media Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that social media content tells a complete micro-story native to the platform

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of social media posts, threads, carousels, reels, stories, and
short-form video. Use before publishing any social content that aims to build brand, drive
engagement, generate leads, or spark conversation rather than merely broadcast information.
Ensures the content is a complete micro-narrative with a thumb-stopping opening, platform-native
structure, emotional resonance, and shareability. Social content is not a truncated version of
longer content; it is its own narrative format with its own rules.

## When to Use
- Before publishing any social media content that aims to build brand or drive engagement
- When social content consistently underperforms engagement benchmarks
- Before adapting longer-form content into social-native formats
- During content calendar reviews to evaluate planned posts before they go live
- When launching on a new social platform and establishing content standards

## Prerequisites
- A draft social media post, thread, carousel, reel script, or story sequence exists
- The target platform is specified (LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts, Threads)
- The audience segment active on this specific platform is understood
- The content objective is defined (engagement, traffic, conversion, brand awareness, community building)
- Platform-specific constraints are known (character limits, aspect ratios, video duration, link behavior, algorithm preferences)
- The brand's existing social voice and content patterns are documented or understood

## Checklist

### Micro-Story Completeness
- [ ] **Complete narrative in miniature** — The post contains a recognizable beginning (setup/context), middle (tension/insight/turn), and end (resolution/takeaway), however compressed the format demands
- [ ] **Single point delivered** — The post communicates exactly one idea, one lesson, one insight, or one emotional moment; it does not attempt to compress an essay, listicle, or full argument into a single post
- [ ] **Transformation or change present** — Something shifts between the start and end of the post: a belief, a situation, an understanding, or an emotional state; the audience ends in a different place than they started
- [ ] **Takeaway is clear** — The reader or viewer finishes knowing exactly what they were supposed to learn, feel, or do; no "what was the point of that?" reaction
- [ ] **Story is self-contained** — The post does not require clicking a link, reading a previous post, watching another video, or having insider context to make complete sense

### Thumb-Stopping Opening
- [ ] **First line hooks immediately** — The opening line (or first frame/second of video) stops the scroll with a bold claim, surprising fact, emotional statement, direct address, provocative question, or visual pattern interrupt
- [ ] **First line is not formulaic** — The opening avoids overused formulas: "I've been thinking about..." "Hot take:" "Unpopular opinion:" "Thread:" "Most people don't know..." unless executed with genuine freshness
- [ ] **Visual hook present (if applicable)** — For visual platforms (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts), the first image or frame is arresting, unusual, or emotionally charged enough to pause a fast-scrolling thumb
- [ ] **Opening creates an information gap** — The first 1-2 lines or first 2-3 seconds introduce enough tension, curiosity, or incomplete information that the reader expands the post, keeps watching, or swipes to the next carousel slide
- [ ] **Opening is earned, not clickbait** — The hook is truthful and representative of the content that follows; no false promises or sensationalized claims that the post does not deliver on

### Platform-Native Design
- [ ] **Format matches platform conventions** — The content uses the platform's native and currently favored formats: threads for X, carousels or text posts for LinkedIn, Reels for Instagram, duets/stitches for TikTok, etc.
- [ ] **Length is platform-appropriate** — Content respects platform norms and algorithm preferences: LinkedIn posts optimized for feed visibility, tweets within character limits, videos at platform-optimal duration
- [ ] **Tone matches platform culture** — The voice feels natural on this specific platform: professional and insight-driven on LinkedIn, conversational and opinionated on X, visual-first on Instagram, raw and authentic on TikTok
- [ ] **Engagement mechanic included** — The post includes a natural invitation for interaction: a genuine question, a "tag someone" prompt, a poll, a "save this" worthy framework, a debate-sparking take, or a "what would you add?" close
- [ ] **Platform-specific features leveraged** — The content takes advantage of platform tools: alt text, hashtags, location tags, collaboration features, stickers, sounds, or trending formats where appropriate

### Shareability and Virality
- [ ] **Post passes the "screenshot test"** — The content is valuable, resonant, or provocative enough that someone would screenshot it and share it in a DM, group chat, or Slack channel
- [ ] **Identity signal present** — Sharing or liking the post says something positive about the sharer: their expertise, values, taste, humor, or insider knowledge; people share content that makes them look good
- [ ] **Emotional trigger present** — The post evokes a specific emotion that drives sharing behavior: inspiration, righteous outrage, deep recognition ("this is SO me"), humor, awe, nostalgia, or belonging
- [ ] **Post works without the caption (visual platforms)** — For Instagram, TikTok, and visual-first platforms, the visual content carries the story independently; the caption adds depth but is not required for comprehension

### Brand and Strategy Alignment
- [ ] **Voice is consistent with brand** — The post sounds like the same person or brand as other recent content in the feed; no jarring tone shifts that confuse the audience about who is speaking
- [ ] **Post serves the content strategy** — The post fits within a larger content plan, pillar, or narrative arc; it is not random, purely reactive, or disconnected from the brand's ongoing conversation
- [ ] **CTA is natural, not forced** — If a call to action exists, it flows organically from the content's value ("If this framework helped, the full guide is linked in bio" vs. "SMASH THAT LIKE BUTTON AND SUBSCRIBE")

## Scoring
Must pass **17/22 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Complete narrative in miniature
- First line hooks immediately
- Format matches platform conventions
- Post passes the "screenshot test"
- Opening is earned, not clickbait

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision before publishing.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Schedule the post for optimal timing based on platform analytics. Prepare a
  response strategy for comments, DMs, and engagement within the first 60 minutes. Monitor
  performance and note which elements drove engagement for future content.
- **On Fail:** Rewrite from the audience's perspective: what would make THEM stop scrolling,
  read/watch to the end, feel something, and share it with someone? Reference the Hook and Open
  Loop checklist (hook-and-open-loop-quality.md) for opening strategies. Study the top 10
  performing posts (by engagement rate, not just likes) in the same niche on the same platform
  and reverse-engineer their structural patterns, hook strategies, and emotional mechanics.
  If the content was adapted from longer-form content, start over as a platform-native piece
  rather than compressing or excerpting the original.
