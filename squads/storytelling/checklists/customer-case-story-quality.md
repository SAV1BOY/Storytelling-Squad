# Customer Case Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a customer case study reads as a compelling story, not a data sheet

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of customer case studies, success stories, and testimonial-driven
content. Use when a case study has been drafted and before it goes to design, publication,
or sales enablement. Ensures the case study follows a story arc with a relatable protagonist,
includes specific and verifiable proof, is retellable by sales teams and customers in their
own conversations, and does not read like a product spec sheet with a customer's logo on it.

## When to Use
- After a customer case study draft is written and before it enters design or publication
- When a case study reads like a product feature list with a customer logo
- Before adding case studies to sales enablement libraries or pitch decks
- When converting customer interviews into published narrative content
- During quarterly content audits to evaluate existing case study quality

## Prerequisites
- Customer interview or raw material has been collected (transcript, recording, or detailed notes)
- Customer has approved participation and disclosure boundaries are set (what can be named, what is NDA)
- Quantitative results or outcomes are available and verified
- The target audience for the case study (prospects, investors, partners, media) is defined
- The customer's industry and company context are understood

## Checklist

### Context and Before State
- [ ] **Customer's world is established** — The case study opens with the customer's situation, industry, company scale, and relevant context so the reader understands who they are in 2-3 sentences
- [ ] **Before-state pain is specific** — The problem the customer faced is described with concrete details: numbers, symptoms, failed previous attempts, or direct quotes about the frustration
- [ ] **Emotional weight of the problem conveyed** — The reader feels why this problem mattered beyond the metric: impact on team morale, customer relationships, personal stress, career risk, or company survival
- [ ] **Reader can see themselves** — The before-state description maps to problems the target reader likely faces; it is specific enough to be vivid but universal enough to be relatable
- [ ] **Urgency of the problem established** — The case study explains why the customer needed to solve this problem when they did: a deadline, a crisis, a market shift, or a breaking point

### Friction and Decision
- [ ] **Decision-making journey included** — The case study describes what the customer tried before, why those approaches failed or fell short, and what led them to explore a new solution
- [ ] **Objections or hesitations acknowledged** — The narrative mentions at least one doubt, concern, or internal barrier the customer had before committing to the solution
- [ ] **Why this solution chosen** — A specific, differentiated reason is given for why the customer selected this product/service over alternatives, in the customer's own words if possible
- [ ] **Champion identified** — A named person or role-identified individual within the customer organization serves as the protagonist who recognized the problem and drove the decision

### Action and Implementation
- [ ] **Implementation is described, not skipped** — The narrative includes what actually happened during adoption: timeline, key milestones, team involvement, and any integration or change management
- [ ] **Effort is honest** — The case study does not pretend everything was instant or effortless; reasonable implementation friction, learning curves, or adaptation periods are acknowledged
- [ ] **Turning point is identifiable** — There is a specific moment or milestone where the customer saw the solution working and knew the decision was right
- [ ] **Support or partnership highlighted** — The narrative acknowledges how the provider helped during implementation, demonstrating responsiveness or partnership quality

### Results and Proof
- [ ] **Results are specific and quantified** — At least two concrete metrics are cited with actual numbers: revenue increase, time saved, error reduction, NPS change, cost decrease, speed improvement
- [ ] **Results are time-bound** — Metrics include a timeframe ("within 6 months," "after the Q3 rollout," "in the first 90 days") not just abstract percentage improvements
- [ ] **Direct customer quote included** — At least one verbatim quote from the customer describes the impact in their own authentic words, not cleaned-up marketing language
- [ ] **Unexpected or secondary benefits mentioned** — The case study includes at least one outcome the customer did not initially anticipate, adding credibility and depth

### Retellability and Structure
- [ ] **Story arc is complete** — The case study follows a clear narrative arc: situation > problem > decision > action > result, with each phase getting appropriate weight
- [ ] **Headline tells the outcome** — The title or subtitle communicates the key result or transformation, not just "Company X Case Study" or the customer's name
- [ ] **Skimmable and deep** — The case study works at two levels: a quick scan of headlines, pull quotes, and bold stats gives the gist; a full read provides the rich narrative
- [ ] **Shareable takeaway exists** — There is one sentence, stat, or insight compelling enough that a salesperson or reader would repeat it in conversation without needing to look it up

### Sales Enablement
- [ ] **Objection addressed** — The case study preemptively addresses at least one common sales objection through the customer's experience (cost, implementation time, learning curve, risk)
- [ ] **Industry relevance clear** — The case study is tagged or framed for the specific industry, company size, or use case so prospects can quickly find relevant examples
- [ ] **Customer approved final version** — The customer has reviewed and approved the published version, including all quotes, metrics, and characterizations of their experience
- [ ] **Derivative formats planned** — The case study can be extracted into a one-slide summary, a pull-quote graphic, a 60-second video synopsis, or a social media post

## Scoring
Must pass **19/26 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Before-state pain is specific
- Results are specific and quantified
- Direct customer quote included
- Story arc is complete
- Reader can see themselves

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision before publication or sales enablement.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to design, formatting, and publication. Create derivative assets: a one-page
  summary, a pull quote graphic, a sales deck slide, and a social media version. If the case
  study will also be a video or presentation, run the relevant format checklist.
- **On Fail:** Return to the case study writer. Re-interview the customer for missing specifics
  using targeted questions: "Tell me about a specific moment when you realized this was working"
  and "What was the worst day before you solved this?" Reference the narrative framework
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for story arc guidance and the Proof-in-Story
  checklist (proof-in-story-quality.md) for evidence integration. Do not publish a case study
  that reads like a product feature list with a customer logo.
