# Customer Case Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a customer case study reads as a compelling story, not a data sheet

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of customer case studies, success stories, and testimonial-driven content. Use when a case study has been drafted and before it goes to design or publication. Ensures the case study follows a story arc, features a relatable protagonist, includes specific proof, and is retellable by sales teams and customers alike.

## Prerequisites
- Customer interview or raw material has been collected
- Customer has approved participation and disclosure boundaries are set
- Quantitative results or outcomes are available
- The target audience for the case study (prospects, investors, partners) is defined

## Checklist

### Context and Before State
- [ ] **Customer's world is established** — The case study opens with the customer's situation, industry, and scale so the reader understands the context in 2-3 sentences
- [ ] **Before-state pain is specific** — The problem the customer faced is described with concrete details: numbers, symptoms, failed attempts, or direct quotes about frustration
- [ ] **Emotional weight of the problem conveyed** — The reader feels why this problem mattered, not just what it was (impact on team morale, revenue, customers, personal stress)
- [ ] **Reader can see themselves** — The before-state description maps to problems the target reader likely faces; it is not so niche that only one company would relate

### Friction and Decision
- [ ] **Decision-making journey included** — The case study describes what the customer tried before, why those approaches failed, and what led them to the solution
- [ ] **Objections or hesitations acknowledged** — The narrative mentions at least one doubt, concern, or barrier the customer had before committing
- [ ] **Why this solution chosen** — A specific reason (not generic) is given for why the customer selected this product/service over alternatives
- [ ] **Champion identified** — A named or role-identified person within the customer organization is the protagonist who drove the decision

### Action and Implementation
- [ ] **Implementation is described, not skipped** — The narrative includes what actually happened during adoption: timeline, challenges, team involvement
- [ ] **Effort is honest** — The case study does not pretend everything was instant or effortless; reasonable implementation friction is acknowledged
- [ ] **Turning point is identifiable** — There is a specific moment or milestone where the customer saw the solution working

### Results and Proof
- [ ] **Results are specific and quantified** — At least two concrete metrics are cited (revenue increase, time saved, error reduction, NPS change) with actual numbers
- [ ] **Results are time-bound** — Metrics include a timeframe ("within 6 months," "after Q3 rollout") not just abstract improvements
- [ ] **Direct customer quote included** — At least one verbatim quote from the customer describes the impact in their own words
- [ ] **Unexpected or secondary benefits mentioned** — The case study includes at least one outcome the customer did not initially expect

### Retellability and Structure
- [ ] **Story arc is complete** — The case study follows situation > problem > action > result (SPAR) or a similar narrative arc from beginning to resolution
- [ ] **Headline tells the outcome** — The title or subtitle communicates the key result, not just "Customer X Case Study"
- [ ] **Skimmable and deep** — The case study works at two levels: a quick scan of headlines and pull quotes gives the gist; a full read gives the rich story
- [ ] **Shareable takeaway exists** — There is one sentence or stat compelling enough that a salesperson or reader would repeat it in conversation

## Scoring
Must pass **14/18 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Before-state pain is specific
- Results are specific and quantified
- Direct customer quote included
- Story arc is complete

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision before publication.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to design and publication. If the case study will be presented as a video or deck, run the relevant format checklist.
- **On Fail:** Return to the case study writer. Re-interview the customer for missing specifics using the "tell me about a specific moment when..." technique. Reference the narrative framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for story arc guidance. Consult the Proof-in-Story checklist (proof-in-story-quality.md) for evidence integration.
