# Character Transformation Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that the narrative contains a meaningful, visible character arc

## Purpose
Gates the transformation arc within a narrative. Characters who do not change are forgettable. Use this checklist for any narrative featuring a protagonist: founder stories, customer case studies, brand origin stories, keynote narratives, or campaign heroes. Ensures the transformation is specific, earned, and emotionally resonant.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists with an identifiable protagonist
- The protagonist's role is defined (founder, customer, user, audience surrogate, brand)
- The conflict and stakes have been established
- The desired audience takeaway is documented

## Checklist

### State A and State B
- [ ] **State A is concrete** — The protagonist's starting condition is described with specific, observable details (behavior, belief, situation), not abstractions like "struggling"
- [ ] **State B is concrete** — The protagonist's ending condition is equally specific and observable; the change is visible, not just claimed
- [ ] **Gap between A and B is significant** — The distance between starting and ending states is large enough to be meaningful but believable
- [ ] **State A is relatable** — The audience can see themselves in the protagonist's starting condition or recognize someone they know
- [ ] **State B is aspirational** — The ending state represents something the audience wants for themselves or believes is worth achieving

### The Lie and the Truth
- [ ] **False belief identified** — The protagonist holds a specific, stated or demonstrated belief at the start that is limiting or incorrect
- [ ] **Truth discovered** — By the end, the protagonist has replaced the false belief with a new understanding, and this new belief is articulated
- [ ] **Belief change drives behavior change** — The internal shift in belief causes an observable external change in the protagonist's actions or decisions
- [ ] **Moment of realization exists** — There is a specific scene or beat where the protagonist confronts the gap between their old belief and reality

### Transformation Mechanics
- [ ] **Transformation is earned** — The change results from struggle, effort, or meaningful experience, not from luck or sudden epiphany without groundwork
- [ ] **Catalyst is identifiable** — A specific event, person, or piece of information triggers the beginning of the transformation
- [ ] **Resistance is shown** — The protagonist resists change at some point, demonstrating that the transformation is difficult
- [ ] **Cost of change acknowledged** — The narrative recognizes what the protagonist gave up, risked, or endured to transform

### Showing vs. Telling
- [ ] **Transformation shown through action** — At least one scene demonstrates the new behavior rather than just narrating it ("she started speaking up in meetings" vs. a scene of her speaking up)
- [ ] **Before/after contrast is dramatized** — The narrative includes at least one parallel moment showing the protagonist handling a similar situation differently before and after the change
- [ ] **Supporting characters or evidence reflect the change** — Other characters, metrics, or environmental details confirm the transformation is real
- [ ] **Emotional resonance present** — The transformation moment produces an emotional response (relief, pride, hope, recognition) in the audience

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- State A is concrete
- State B is concrete
- False belief identified
- Transformation shown through action

Any CRITICAL failure requires revision of the character arc.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to emotional arc review (emotional-arc-quality.md) and proof-in-story review (proof-in-story-quality.md).
- **On Fail:** Return to the narrative architect. Reference Campbell's Hero's Journey (squads/storytelling/checklists/campbell/) for transformation structure, Dicks' Homework for Life technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for finding authentic transformation moments, or Harmon's Story Circle (squads/storytelling/checklists/harmon/) for the "changed" return. Interview the real person if this is a true story to surface the genuine internal shift.
