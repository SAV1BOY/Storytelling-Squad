# Editing and Trimming Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a narrative has been ruthlessly edited to its strongest, most essential form

## Purpose
Gates the editing discipline of a narrative draft. Use after the story content is complete but before final production. This is the "kill your darlings" pass. Ensures every word, scene, and section earns its place, the narrative is compressed to its optimal length, and clarity has increased through subtraction.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists that has passed content-level checklists (clarity, structure, conflict)
- The target length or duration is defined
- The editor is ideally a different person from the original writer
- At least 24 hours have passed since the draft was written (fresh eyes)

## Checklist

### Darling Detection
- [ ] **Favorite passages tested** — Every passage the writer is most proud of has been evaluated for whether it serves the narrative or just the writer's ego; at least one has been cut or shortened
- [ ] **Clever lines justified** — Any particularly witty, poetic, or elaborate line has been verified to serve the story; cleverness that slows comprehension is removed
- [ ] **Tangent audit complete** — Every anecdote, aside, or digression has been evaluated; those that do not directly support the central message are removed
- [ ] **Redundancy eliminated** — No idea is stated more than twice in the entire piece; the second occurrence is cut if a third exists

### Scene and Section Justification
- [ ] **Every scene earns its place** — Each section, paragraph, or scene can answer "what would be lost if I removed this?" with a specific, substantive answer
- [ ] **Opening trimmed to the bone** — The first paragraph or opening sequence begins at the latest possible moment; all throat-clearing and preamble is cut
- [ ] **Ending trimmed to the bone** — The narrative ends at the earliest satisfying moment; no denouement that restates what the audience already knows
- [ ] **Transitions are lean** — Transitional phrases and sentences between sections are minimal; the juxtaposition of sections does the transitional work where possible

### Word-Level Compression
- [ ] **Adverbs audited** — Every adverb (words ending in -ly) has been evaluated; those modifying strong verbs are removed (not "ran quickly" but "sprinted")
- [ ] **Qualifier words removed** — "Very," "really," "quite," "somewhat," "just," "actually," and "basically" appear fewer than 3 times total in the final draft
- [ ] **Passive voice converted** — All passive constructions are rewritten in active voice unless passive is deliberately chosen for emphasis or rhythm
- [ ] **Nominalizations unpacked** — Abstract nouns derived from verbs ("utilization" > "use," "implementation" > "built," "facilitation" > "helped") are replaced with their verb forms
- [ ] **Word count reduced by at least 15%** — The final draft is measurably shorter than the first complete draft by a minimum of 15%

### Clarity Through Subtraction
- [ ] **Every sentence re-read for clarity** — A line-by-line pass confirms that each sentence communicates its meaning on the first read, without re-reading
- [ ] **Complex sentences split** — Sentences with more than two clauses are broken into shorter sentences unless the rhythm demands it
- [ ] **Jargon re-checked** — Any technical terms that survived earlier drafts are re-evaluated for necessity; those that remain are defined or replaced
- [ ] **Reading flow verified** — The entire piece has been read aloud in one sitting; any point where the reader stumbles, loses breath, or loses the thread is revised

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Every scene earns its place
- Opening trimmed to the bone
- Word count reduced by at least 15%
- Reading flow verified

Any CRITICAL failure requires another editing pass.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** The narrative is ready for final production, design, or delivery. Run the format-specific checklist if not already completed.
- **On Fail:** Assign a second editor for a fresh cut. Reference Dicks' "first, last, best, worst" technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for identifying the essential core. Use the "half it" exercise: cut the piece to 50% length and see what survives. The parts that survive are the real story. Restore only what is genuinely needed.
