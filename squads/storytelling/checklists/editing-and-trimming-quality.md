# Editing and Trimming Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a narrative has been ruthlessly edited to its strongest, most essential form

## Purpose
Gates the editing discipline of a narrative draft. Use after the story content is complete and
has passed content-level checklists (clarity, structure, conflict, proof) but before final
production, design, or delivery. This is the "kill your darlings" pass. Ensures every word,
scene, and section earns its place, the narrative is compressed to its optimal length, clarity
has increased through subtraction, and the writing is muscular rather than flabby. Good writing
is rewriting; this checklist validates that the rewriting actually happened.

## When to Use
- After the narrative has passed all content-level checklists (clarity, structure, conflict, proof)
- Before final production, design, recording, or publication of any narrative
- When a draft exceeds its target length by more than 20%
- When feedback says "it's good but it's too long" or "it drags in the middle"
- During final revision passes before stakeholder sign-off

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists that has passed content-level checklists
- The target length or duration is defined and documented
- The editor is ideally a different person from the original writer
- At least 24 hours have passed since the draft was written (fresh eyes principle)
- The original first-draft word count or duration is recorded for comparison

## Checklist

### Darling Detection
- [ ] **Favorite passages tested** — Every passage the writer is most proud of has been evaluated for whether it serves the narrative or just the writer's ego; at least one beloved passage has been cut or shortened
- [ ] **Clever lines justified** — Any particularly witty, poetic, or elaborate line has been verified to serve the audience's understanding; cleverness that slows comprehension or draws attention to the writing itself is removed
- [ ] **Tangent audit complete** — Every anecdote, aside, background detail, or digression has been evaluated against the central message; those that do not directly support it are removed regardless of how interesting they are
- [ ] **Redundancy eliminated** — No idea, argument, or point is stated more than twice in the entire piece; the second restatement is cut if a third exists anywhere in the draft

### Scene and Section Justification
- [ ] **Every scene earns its place** — Each section, paragraph, scene, or slide can answer the question "what would be lost if I removed this?" with a specific, substantive answer
- [ ] **Opening trimmed to the bone** — The first paragraph, slide, or opening sequence begins at the latest possible moment; all throat-clearing, background-setting, and "let me set the stage" preamble is cut
- [ ] **Ending trimmed to the bone** — The narrative ends at the earliest satisfying moment; no extended denouement that restates what the audience already knows or feels
- [ ] **Transitions are lean** — Transitional phrases and sentences between sections are minimal; juxtaposition and logical flow do the transitional work where possible, without explicit "Now let's move on to..."
- [ ] **No orphan content** — Every piece of information introduced is used; no details, characters, or data points appear and then are never referenced again

### Word-Level Compression
- [ ] **Adverbs audited** — Every adverb (especially -ly words) has been evaluated; those modifying already-strong verbs are removed ("ran quickly" becomes "sprinted," "said loudly" becomes "shouted")
- [ ] **Qualifier words removed** — "Very," "really," "quite," "somewhat," "just," "actually," "basically," "literally," and "honestly" appear fewer than 3 times total in the final draft
- [ ] **Passive voice converted** — All passive constructions are rewritten in active voice unless passive is deliberately chosen for emphasis, rhythm, or strategic de-emphasis
- [ ] **Nominalizations unpacked** — Abstract nouns derived from verbs are replaced with their verb forms ("utilization" > "use," "implementation" > "built," "facilitation" > "led," "optimization" > "improved")
- [ ] **Word count reduced by at least 15%** — The final draft is measurably shorter than the first complete draft by a minimum of 15%, documented with before and after word counts
- [ ] **Prepositional phrase chains shortened** — Strings of prepositional phrases ("the optimization of the process of the delivery of the product") are rewritten as direct statements

### Clarity Through Subtraction
- [ ] **Every sentence re-read for clarity** — A line-by-line pass confirms that each sentence communicates its meaning on the first read, without requiring re-reading or mental parsing
- [ ] **Complex sentences split** — Sentences with more than two subordinate clauses are broken into shorter sentences unless the rhythm or meaning demands the longer construction
- [ ] **Jargon re-checked** — Any technical terms, buzzwords, or insider language that survived earlier drafts are re-evaluated for genuine necessity; those that remain are defined or replaced
- [ ] **Reading flow verified** — The entire piece has been read aloud in one sitting; any point where the reader stumbles, loses breath, loses the thread, or zones out is revised or cut

### Final Tightening
- [ ] **Opening sentence is the strongest sentence** — The very first sentence has been rewritten at least 3 times and represents the best writing in the piece
- [ ] **Closing sentence is the second-strongest** — The final sentence has been crafted for rhythm, impact, and memorability with the same care as the opening
- [ ] **Formatting supports scanning** — Bold text, headers, bullet points, or paragraph breaks are used to make the structure visible and enable quick scanning without losing depth
- [ ] **Fresh-eyes review completed** — Someone other than the writer has read the final draft and confirmed it is clear, compelling, and free of passages that stall momentum

## Scoring
Must pass **17/23 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Every scene earns its place
- Opening trimmed to the bone
- Word count reduced by at least 15%
- Reading flow verified
- Redundancy eliminated

Any CRITICAL failure requires another complete editing pass, ideally by a different editor.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** The narrative is ready for final production, design, formatting, or delivery
  preparation. Run the appropriate format-specific checklist if not already completed.
- **On Fail:** Assign a second editor for a fresh cut. Use the "half it" exercise: cut the piece
  to 50% of its current length and see what survives; the parts that survive are the real story,
  and you restore only what is genuinely needed from the cut material. Reference Dicks' "first,
  last, best, worst" technique (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) for identifying the
  essential core. Use the "so what?" test on every paragraph: if the answer is not immediately
  obvious, the paragraph needs revision or removal. Remember: the goal is not shorter for its
  own sake, but stronger through the removal of everything that is not load-bearing.
