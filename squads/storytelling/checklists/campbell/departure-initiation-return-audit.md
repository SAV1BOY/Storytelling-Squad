# Departure-Initiation-Return Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the three macro-phases of the monomyth are present with clear beats and smooth transitions

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative's large-scale structure follows Campbell's three-act monomyth framework: Departure (separation from the known), Initiation (trials in the unknown), and Return (integration of the gained wisdom). Each phase must contain identifiable beats and transition smoothly into the next.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft or detailed outline
- Hero Journey Beats Audit completed or in progress
- Clear identification of the hero and the ordinary vs. special world

## Checklist

### Departure Phase
- [ ] **Ordinary World Grounded** — The departure phase begins with a vivid, specific depiction of the hero's status quo that the audience can feel
- [ ] **Disruption Is Unmistakable** — The call to adventure creates a rupture that the audience recognizes as the point of no return approaching
- [ ] **Internal Resistance Shown** — The hero's reluctance or fear is dramatized, not summarized, giving emotional weight to the departure
- [ ] **Departure Has a Definite Moment** — There is a single identifiable scene or sentence where the hero leaves the ordinary world; it is not gradual or ambiguous

### Initiation Phase
- [ ] **Escalating Difficulty** — Trials in the initiation phase increase in stakes, complexity, or emotional intensity; they do not plateau
- [ ] **Transformation Occurs During Initiation** — The hero visibly changes (skills, beliefs, relationships) as a direct result of trials faced, not off-screen
- [ ] **Central Ordeal Is the Peak** — The most intense, dangerous, or emotionally charged moment occurs within the initiation phase, not before or after
- [ ] **Allies and Enemies Tested** — Relationships formed during initiation are tested under pressure, revealing who is trustworthy
- [ ] **New Rules of the Special World** — The initiation phase establishes that the ordinary world's rules do not apply; the hero must adapt

### Return Phase
- [ ] **Return Is Motivated** — The hero has a reason to go back (duty, love, knowledge to share), not just narrative convenience
- [ ] **Return Carries Cost or Resistance** — The journey home involves obstacles, reluctance, or sacrifice; it is not frictionless
- [ ] **Integration Shown** — The hero demonstrates the ability to live in the ordinary world with the wisdom or power gained; the two worlds merge
- [ ] **Audience Feels Completion** — The return delivers emotional closure; the narrative does not end abruptly or trail off

### Transitions Between Phases
- [ ] **Departure-to-Initiation Transition Clear** — The audience knows exactly when the hero has left the ordinary world and entered the unknown
- [ ] **Initiation-to-Return Transition Clear** — The shift from trials to homecoming is marked by a recognizable turning point (reward seized, decision made)
- [ ] **Pacing Across Phases Balanced** — No phase is so compressed or expanded that it undermines the others; rough ratio is 25/50/25 or justified deviation
- [ ] **Thematic Thread Unbroken** — The controlling idea or theme is traceable through all three phases without interruption

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 17 items checked; the three phases feel like a complete, satisfying arc |
| Conditional | 13-16 items checked; one phase is weak but structurally present |
| Fail | Fewer than 13 items, or an entire macro-phase is missing or unrecognizable |

## Next Steps
- If a phase is weak, expand it with additional beats from the Hero Journey Beats Audit
- If transitions are unclear, add a bridging scene or moment that signals the phase shift to the audience
- Validate pacing by timing or word-counting each phase and comparing to the 25/50/25 benchmark
