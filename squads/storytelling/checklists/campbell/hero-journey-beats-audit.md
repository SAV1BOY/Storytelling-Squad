# Hero Journey Beats Audit
> Quality gate for verifying all essential Hero's Journey beats are present and functional

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative follows the classical monomyth structure identified by Joseph Campbell, with each beat serving its dramatic purpose rather than existing as a checkbox exercise.

## Prerequisites
- Complete draft of the narrative or story outline
- Familiarity with the 12-stage Hero's Journey framework
- Clear identification of who the hero is in this narrative

## Checklist

### Ordinary World
- [ ] **Ordinary World Established** — The hero's normal life is shown with enough detail that the audience understands what is at stake if it changes
- [ ] **Contrast Potential Set** — The ordinary world is depicted in a way that creates clear contrast with the special world to come

### Call to Adventure
- [ ] **Call Present and Specific** — A definite inciting event disrupts the ordinary world; it is not vague or ambiguous
- [ ] **Stakes Implied by Call** — The call communicates (directly or indirectly) what happens if the hero ignores it

### Refusal of the Call
- [ ] **Refusal Shown** — The hero hesitates, resists, or outright declines the call at least once before accepting
- [ ] **Refusal Motivated** — The refusal stems from a believable fear, obligation, or flaw, not arbitrary delay

### Mentor Appearance
- [ ] **Mentor Identified** — A character, experience, or insight serves the mentor function of preparing the hero
- [ ] **Gift or Wisdom Provided** — The mentor gives the hero something (tool, knowledge, confidence) needed for the journey

### Crossing the Threshold
- [ ] **Threshold Crossed** — There is a clear, identifiable moment when the hero commits and enters the special world
- [ ] **No Easy Return** — Once crossed, the hero cannot simply walk back; the commitment feels irreversible

### Tests, Allies, and Enemies
- [ ] **Tests Present** — The hero faces at least two challenges that build skills or reveal character before the ordeal
- [ ] **Allies and Enemies Distinguished** — Supporting and opposing forces are introduced and their roles are clear

### Ordeal
- [ ] **Ordeal Faced** — The hero confronts the central crisis, the greatest danger or deepest fear in the narrative
- [ ] **Death and Rebirth Echo** — The ordeal contains a symbolic or literal death-rebirth moment where the old self is shed

### Reward
- [ ] **Reward Earned** — The hero gains something tangible or intangible as a direct result of surviving the ordeal

### The Road Back
- [ ] **Return Journey Initiated** — The hero begins the transition back to the ordinary world with consequences in pursuit

### Return Completed
- [ ] **Return Completed** — The hero arrives back in the ordinary world, changed, carrying the elixir or lesson
- [ ] **Elixir Shared** — The hero's transformation or reward benefits the community, not just the individual
- [ ] **Transformation Visible** — The audience can point to specific evidence of how the hero has changed from beginning to end

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 18 items checked, or omissions are deliberately justified in writing |
| Conditional | 14-17 items checked, missing items have partial justification |
| Fail | Fewer than 14 items checked, or core beats (Call, Ordeal, Return) missing |

## Next Steps
- If any beat is missing, determine whether it is structurally absent or merely implicit; make it explicit
- Cross-reference with the Departure-Initiation-Return audit for macro-phase alignment
- Feed findings into the Archetype Alignment Audit to ensure the hero's arc matches the brand archetype
