# Five Commandments Audit
> Quality gate for verifying inciting incident, progressive complications, crisis, climax, and resolution are all present

## Purpose
Ensure every unit of story (scene, sequence, act, global) contains Shawn Coyne's Five Commandments of Storytelling. These are the irreducible components that make a story work at every level of magnification.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft or detailed outline
- Story units identified (scenes, sequences, acts, global story)
- Genre and controlling idea documented

## Checklist

### Inciting Incident
- [ ] **Inciting Incident Present** — Each story unit begins with an event that upsets the life balance of the character, either causal (caused by a character) or coincidental (caused by chance)
- [ ] **Incident Creates Imbalance** — The inciting incident shifts the value charge of the scene; the character's situation is measurably different after it occurs
- [ ] **Incident Is Specific** — The inciting incident is a concrete event, not a vague feeling or gradual shift; you can point to the exact moment

### Progressive Complications
- [ ] **Complications Escalate** — Each complication raises the stakes or narrows the character's options; the difficulty increases, it does not plateau
- [ ] **Turning Point Identified** — The most extreme complication (the turning point) is identifiable and creates the conditions for the crisis
- [ ] **Turning Point Is Active or Revelatory** — The turning point is either an action that changes everything or a revelation that reframes the situation
- [ ] **No Flat Complications** — No complication merely repeats the difficulty level of a previous one; each adds new pressure

### Crisis
- [ ] **Crisis Is a Dilemma** — The crisis presents a genuine choice between two options, either "best bad choice" (both options are negative) or "irreconcilable goods" (both options are positive but mutually exclusive)
- [ ] **Crisis Is Not Obvious** — The right answer is not immediately clear; the audience can argue for either side of the dilemma
- [ ] **Crisis Forces Character Revelation** — The choice the character faces reveals who they truly are under pressure

### Climax
- [ ] **Climax Is an Action** — The character makes a decision and acts on it; the climax is not a thought, a realization, or a conversation alone
- [ ] **Climax Resolves the Crisis** — The action directly addresses the dilemma posed by the crisis; it is the answer to the question
- [ ] **Climax Is Irreversible** — Once the climactic action is taken, the character cannot undo it; the world has changed

### Resolution
- [ ] **Resolution Shows New State** — The aftermath of the climax is shown; the audience sees the new value charge of the character's situation
- [ ] **Resolution Sets Up Next Unit** — The new state of affairs creates conditions for the next scene, sequence, or act's inciting incident
- [ ] **Value Shift Confirmed** — The value at the beginning of the unit is demonstrably different from the value at the end (e.g., life to death, ignorance to knowledge, injustice to justice)

### Multi-Level Application
- [ ] **Scene Level Verified** — At least three representative scenes have been audited for all five commandments
- [ ] **Global Level Verified** — The overall story contains all five commandments at the macro level
- [ ] **Commandments Are Proportional** — No commandment consumes a disproportionate amount of story space without justification

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 19 items checked; every story unit has all five commandments functioning |
| Conditional | 15-18 items checked; commandments present but some lack dramatic force |
| Fail | Fewer than 15 items, or any commandment entirely missing at the global level |

## Next Steps
- Create a Five Commandments spreadsheet for every scene, listing each commandment with a one-sentence description
- If the crisis is not a true dilemma, rewrite it until both options are genuinely difficult
- If complications do not escalate, reorder or replace them to create increasing pressure
