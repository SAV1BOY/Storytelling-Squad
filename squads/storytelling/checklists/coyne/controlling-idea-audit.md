# Controlling Idea Audit
> Quality gate for verifying a single controlling idea is stated, structured as value + cause, and consistent throughout

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative is organized around one controlling idea (theme) expressed as a value-laden statement with a clear cause. The controlling idea is the story's argument about how the world works, and every element should serve it.

## Prerequisites
- Controlling idea drafted in the format: "[Value] is achieved/lost when/because [cause]"
- Complete narrative draft
- Genre and global value spectrum identified

## Checklist

### Controlling Idea Stated
- [ ] **Single Controlling Idea Documented** — One sentence captures the story's meaning in the format: value + cause (e.g., "Justice prevails when a protagonist is willing to sacrifice personal gain for the community")
- [ ] **Value Component Clear** — The controlling idea names a specific universal value (justice, love, survival, truth, freedom), not a vague concept
- [ ] **Cause Component Specific** — The "when" or "because" clause identifies the specific human action or condition that produces the value outcome
- [ ] **Positive or Negative Charge Identified** — The controlling idea is either prescriptive (value achieved because of X) or cautionary (value lost because of Y)

### Consistency Throughout
- [ ] **Opening Aligns** — The story's beginning establishes the world in a state that sets up the controlling idea's argument
- [ ] **Midpoint Tests the Idea** — The midpoint complication directly challenges or pressures the controlling idea
- [ ] **Climax Proves the Idea** — The climactic action demonstrates the controlling idea in its most concentrated form; the audience feels the truth of the argument
- [ ] **Resolution Confirms the Idea** — The story's final state is a living example of the controlling idea in action
- [ ] **No Contradictory Scenes** — No scene argues against the controlling idea without that contradiction being addressed or resolved later
- [ ] **Subplots Reinforce or Counterpoint** — Secondary storylines either support the controlling idea from a different angle or provide a deliberate counterargument that the main plot answers

### Depth and Nuance
- [ ] **Not Cliche or Platitude** — The controlling idea is not a bumper sticker ("love conquers all"); it has specificity and earned complexity
- [ ] **Applicable Beyond This Story** — The controlling idea expresses a truth about human experience that the audience can recognize in their own life
- [ ] **Irony or Complexity Present** — The controlling idea acknowledges the cost, paradox, or limitation of the value it champions

### Audience Experience
- [ ] **Audience Could Articulate the Idea** — After experiencing the story, the audience could paraphrase the controlling idea even if they do not use that terminology
- [ ] **Idea Felt Not Preached** — The controlling idea emerges from the dramatic action, not from a character speech, voice-over, or title card
- [ ] **Emotional Resonance** — The controlling idea produces an emotional response in the audience, not just intellectual agreement

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the controlling idea is the invisible spine of the story |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; idea is present but inconsistently expressed or too on-the-nose |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or no identifiable controlling idea, or multiple conflicting ideas |

## Next Steps
- Write the controlling idea on an index card and evaluate every scene against it
- If the idea feels like a platitude, add the specific cost or condition that makes it earned
- If scenes contradict the idea, decide whether to revise the scenes or revise the controlling idea
