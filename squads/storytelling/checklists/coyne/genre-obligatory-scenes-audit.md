# Genre Obligatory Scenes Audit
> Quality gate for verifying genre is identified, obligatory scenes are present, and conventions are met

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative identifies its content genre according to Coyne's Story Grid framework, includes every obligatory scene the genre demands, and honors the conventions that audiences expect. Missing obligatory scenes or conventions will leave the audience feeling cheated.

## Prerequisites
- Genre identified using Story Grid categories (Action, Horror, Thriller, Love, Performance, Society, War, Worldview, Morality, Status)
- Obligatory scenes and conventions for the genre listed from Story Grid reference materials
- Complete narrative draft

## Checklist

### Genre Identification
- [ ] **Content Genre Named** — The primary content genre is explicitly identified using Story Grid taxonomy, not vague labels like "drama" or "literary"
- [ ] **Genre Backed by Value Spectrum** — The genre's core value spectrum is defined (e.g., Life/Death for Action, Love/Hate for Love Story, Accomplishment/Failure for Performance)
- [ ] **Secondary Genre Acknowledged** — If a secondary genre exists, it is identified and its obligations are tracked separately

### Obligatory Scenes Present
- [ ] **All Obligatory Scenes Listed** — Every obligatory scene for the identified genre is documented in a reference checklist specific to this story
- [ ] **Each Obligatory Scene Located in Draft** — Every listed obligatory scene can be pointed to in the manuscript with page/section reference
- [ ] **Obligatory Scenes Deliver Emotionally** — Each obligatory scene achieves its intended emotional impact, not just structural presence
- [ ] **No Missing Obligatory Scene Without Documentation** — If an obligatory scene is absent, the omission is deliberate, documented, and the narrative still satisfies
- [ ] **Hero at the Mercy of the Villain Scene** — For genres that require it (Action, Thriller, Horror), this specific scene exists and the hero's survival is genuinely in doubt

### Conventions Met
- [ ] **All Conventions Listed** — Every convention for the genre is documented (e.g., Love Story requires a rival, a confidant, proof of love)
- [ ] **Conventions Present in Draft** — Each listed convention is identifiable in the narrative
- [ ] **Conventions Serve the Story** — Conventions are integrated organically, not inserted as afterthoughts; they feel like natural parts of the world
- [ ] **Conventions Are Not Cliches** — Required conventions are executed with freshness; meeting audience expectations does not mean being predictable

### Value Spectrum Integrity
- [ ] **Global Value Shift Documented** — The story's overall movement along the genre's value spectrum is mapped (e.g., from injustice to justice in a Society story)
- [ ] **Polarity Shift Present** — The story moves from one pole of the value spectrum to the other, or to a nuanced position between them
- [ ] **Value Shift Earned** — The shift is produced by the protagonist's actions and choices through the obligatory scenes, not by external rescue

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; genre obligations fully met with craft |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; most obligations met but 1-2 scenes need strengthening |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or obligatory scenes missing without justification |

## Next Steps
- Build a genre-specific checklist from Story Grid resources and tape it to the wall during revision
- If obligatory scenes are present but emotionally flat, rewrite with higher stakes or deeper character investment
- Study three masterworks in the same genre to see how they handle the same obligatory scenes
