# Status Transaction Audit
> Quality gate for verifying status dynamics are intentional, appropriate, and avoid accidental low-status positioning

## Purpose
Ensure that every interaction in the narrative or presentation involves deliberate status choices. Keith Johnstone's work shows that status is transacted in every human interaction; unconscious status choices lead to unintended power dynamics that undermine the storyteller's message.

## Prerequisites
- Narrative or presentation draft completed
- Key interactions between characters (or presenter and audience) identified
- Understanding of Johnstone's status concepts from "Impro"

## Checklist

### Status Awareness
- [ ] **Status Dynamics Identified** — Every major interaction in the narrative has its status dynamics mapped: who is playing high, who is playing low, and why
- [ ] **Status Is Intentional** — The status choices in each interaction serve the narrative purpose; they are not accidental or default behaviors
- [ ] **Status Is Dynamic** — Status shifts during interactions; it is not static throughout the narrative; characters raise and lower status in response to events
- [ ] **Both High and Low Status Demonstrated** — The protagonist (or presenter) demonstrates both high-status and low-status behaviors at appropriate moments

### Appropriate Status Choices
- [ ] **High Status When Needed** — Characters or presenters play high status (confidence, authority, calm) when the situation requires leadership, expertise, or gravitas
- [ ] **Low Status When Needed** — Characters or presenters play low status (vulnerability, humility, openness) when the situation requires empathy, approachability, or confession
- [ ] **Status Matches Context** — The status level is calibrated to the audience and situation: not arrogant with peers, not servile with subordinates, not submissive with decision-makers
- [ ] **Status Transitions Are Smooth** — Shifts between high and low status feel natural and purposeful, not jarring or manipulative

### No Accidental Low Status
- [ ] **No Unnecessary Apologies** — The narrative or presentation does not include reflexive apologies, self-deprecation, or permission-seeking that accidentally lowers status
- [ ] **No Fidgeting Language** — Filler phrases ("I think maybe," "I'm not sure but," "Sorry to bother you") that signal low status unintentionally are removed
- [ ] **Body Language Aligned** — If performed, physical behavior (eye contact, posture, gestures) supports the intended status; no contradictions between words and body
- [ ] **Expertise Owned** — When the character or presenter has authority on a subject, they claim it cleanly without hedging or false modesty

### Strategic Status Play
- [ ] **Status Serves the Story** — Status transactions advance the plot, reveal character, or shift relationships; they are not neutral interactions
- [ ] **Status Creates Comedy or Tension** — Status mismatches (high-status person brought low, low-status person rising) are used deliberately for dramatic or comedic effect
- [ ] **Audience Status Respected** — The narrative or presenter never accidentally condescends (too high) or grovels (too low) relative to the audience
- [ ] **Status Resolution Present** — By the end of the narrative, status relationships are resolved in a way that supports the controlling idea

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; status is a conscious, strategic tool throughout |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; most status is intentional but some accidental low-status moments remain |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or pervasive accidental low-status undermines the narrative |

## Next Steps
- Mark each interaction with H (high), L (low), or S (shift) and verify the pattern is intentional
- If accidental low status is present, remove hedging language and replace with direct statements
- Practice key scenes with deliberate status choices and note how the dynamic changes
