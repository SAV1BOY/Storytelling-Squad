# Spontaneity Blocks Audit
> Quality gate for verifying creative blocks are identified, deblocking techniques applied, and flow achieved

## Purpose
Ensure that spontaneity and creative flow are actively cultivated, not left to chance. Johnstone identified that most people are not uncreative but blocked by fear, judgment, and social conditioning. This audit checks whether those blocks are diagnosed and addressed.

## Prerequisites
- Creative team or individual engaged in story development
- Awareness of common blocks: fear of failure, self-censorship, perfectionism, originality pressure
- Deblocking exercises or techniques available

## Checklist

### Block Identification
- [ ] **Common Blocks Assessed** — The team or individual has identified which specific blocks are present: fear of judgment, perfectionism, trying to be original, self-censorship, overthinking
- [ ] **Block Sources Named** — The origin of the block is traced: audience expectations, past criticism, organizational culture, personal habits, or imposter syndrome
- [ ] **Blocks Are Specific** — The identified blocks are concrete ("I delete my first idea because it seems too obvious") not vague ("I just can't think of anything")
- [ ] **Patterns Recognized** — Recurring block patterns are documented; the same blocks appearing across sessions are flagged for deeper intervention

### Deblocking Techniques Applied
- [ ] **"Be Obvious" Practiced** — Contributors are encouraged to voice the first, most obvious idea rather than holding out for a brilliant one; Johnstone's key deblocking technique
- [ ] **Judgment Suspended** — During generative phases, evaluation and criticism are explicitly deferred; ideas are captured before they are assessed
- [ ] **Quantity Over Quality Exercised** — Brainstorming sessions prioritize volume of ideas first, selection second; the inner critic is turned off during generation
- [ ] **Physical Warm-Ups Used** — If applicable, physical or vocal warm-up exercises are used to bypass intellectual blocks and access embodied creativity

### Flow Achieved
- [ ] **Ideas Flowing Freely** — During creative sessions, ideas emerge without excessive pauses, second-guessing, or withdrawal; contributors are producing, not freezing
- [ ] **Mistakes Welcomed** — When an idea does not work, it is treated as material to build on, not as a failure to punish; the atmosphere is safe for error
- [ ] **Surprise Present** — The creative output includes ideas that surprise even their originators; the process is generating novelty, not recycling safe options
- [ ] **Energy Is Positive** — The creative environment feels energized, playful, and forward-moving, not tense, cautious, or depleted

### Sustainability
- [ ] **Deblocking Is Regular** — Deblocking exercises are part of the ongoing creative practice, not a one-time intervention
- [ ] **Progress Is Visible** — Over time, creative output is becoming more fluent, bold, and varied; the same blocks are appearing less frequently
- [ ] **Individual Needs Addressed** — Different contributors may have different blocks; the approach is personalized, not one-size-fits-all

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; creative flow is active and blocks are managed |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; some blocks identified and addressed but flow is inconsistent |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or blocks are unaddressed and creative output is stalled |

## Next Steps
- If "be obvious" is not practiced, run a session where every contributor must say the first thing that comes to mind with zero filtering
- If judgment is not suspended, implement a formal "no criticism" rule for the first 15 minutes of any creative session
- If blocks persist, explore Johnstone's mask work or free-association exercises as deeper interventions
