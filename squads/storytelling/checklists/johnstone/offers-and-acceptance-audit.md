# Offers and Acceptance Audit
> Quality gate for verifying quality offers are made, accepted and built upon, and the narrative advances

## Purpose
Ensure that narrative offers (every element introduced into a scene or story: a character detail, an object, an emotion, a line of dialogue, a change in status) are high quality, properly accepted by the narrative, and developed rather than abandoned. An offer ignored is energy wasted; an offer developed is story created.

## Prerequisites
- Narrative draft or collaborative scene output available
- Key narrative elements catalogued
- Understanding of Johnstone's concept of "offers" from "Impro"

## Checklist

### Quality of Offers
- [ ] **Offers Are Specific** — Elements introduced into the narrative are concrete and detailed, not vague placeholders ("a problem" vs. "a letter from the IRS")
- [ ] **Offers Have Emotional Charge** — Each significant offer carries an emotional implication: threat, desire, humor, sadness, curiosity, or tension
- [ ] **Offers Create Possibilities** — Each offer opens up multiple directions for the narrative to follow; it enriches rather than constrains
- [ ] **Offers Are Bold** — At least some offers involve creative risk, subverting expectations or introducing unexpected elements

### Acceptance and Development
- [ ] **Offers Are Accepted** — When an element is introduced, the narrative engages with it rather than ignoring it or contradicting it in the next beat
- [ ] **Offers Are Built Upon** — Accepted offers are developed: they reappear, evolve, combine with other offers, or produce consequences later in the narrative
- [ ] **No Orphan Offers** — Elements introduced early in the narrative are not abandoned; if a gun appears in act one, it is addressed by act three
- [ ] **Offers Are Transformed** — The best offers change meaning as the narrative progresses; an object, phrase, or detail gains new significance through context

### Narrative Advancement
- [ ] **Each Offer Moves the Story Forward** — Every significant element introduced contributes to plot progression, character development, or thematic deepening
- [ ] **Offers Chain Together** — One offer leads to the next in a causal or thematic chain; the narrative does not feel like a series of disconnected introductions
- [ ] **Conflict Emerges From Offers** — Tension arises naturally from the interaction of different offers rather than being imposed externally
- [ ] **Resolution Uses Established Offers** — The story's resolution draws on elements (offers) that were introduced earlier, creating a sense of completeness and earned payoff

### Craft Assessment
- [ ] **Economy of Offers** — The narrative does not introduce more elements than it can develop; restraint in offering produces richer development of fewer elements
- [ ] **Offers Match Tone** — The elements introduced are consistent with the story's tone and genre; no tonal whiplash from mismatched offers
- [ ] **Audience Tracks the Offers** — The audience can follow the thread of each significant offer through the narrative without confusion

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; offers are rich, developed, and drive the narrative forward |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; offers are present but some are undeveloped or orphaned |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or significant offers are routinely ignored or abandoned |

## Next Steps
- Catalogue every offer in the narrative and track which ones are developed, which are orphaned, and which are overloaded
- For orphan offers, either develop them or remove them; every introduced element should earn its place
- If offers are bland, practice the "make your partner look good" principle: each new offer should give the narrative rich material to work with
