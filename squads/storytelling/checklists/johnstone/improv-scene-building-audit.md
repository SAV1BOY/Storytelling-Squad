# Improv Scene Building Audit
> Quality gate for verifying "Yes, and" is present, offers are made and accepted, and building replaces blocking

## Purpose
Ensure collaborative narrative creation follows Johnstone's improvisation principles: offers are made generously, accepted with "Yes, and," and built upon to advance the scene. Blocking (negating, ignoring, or deflecting offers) kills narrative momentum and collaborative energy.

## Prerequisites
- Collaborative narrative or brainstorm output documented
- Participants familiar with basic improv principles
- Scene or narrative segment to evaluate

## Checklist

### "Yes, And" Presence
- [ ] **"Yes, And" Is the Default** — When one contributor introduces an idea, element, or direction, others accept and build on it rather than replacing or ignoring it
- [ ] **Additions Are Constructive** — Each "and" adds new information, raises stakes, or deepens the scene rather than merely agreeing without contributing
- [ ] **"Yes, And" Operates Across Contributions** — The pattern is visible across multiple exchanges, not just a single instance; it is the mode of collaboration
- [ ] **Disagreement Is Channeled Productively** — When a contributor disagrees with a direction, they reframe it as a "Yes, and what if..." rather than a "No, but..."

### Offers Made
- [ ] **Offers Are Generous** — Contributors make bold, specific offers that give other collaborators material to work with
- [ ] **Offers Include Detail** — Each offer has enough specificity (character, emotion, action, setting) to be built upon, not just vague suggestions
- [ ] **Offers Advance the Narrative** — Each offer moves the story forward or deepens it; offers are not tangential or self-serving
- [ ] **Risk-Taking Present** — At least some offers involve creative risk: unusual ideas, emotional vulnerability, or unexpected directions

### Offers Accepted
- [ ] **No Blocking** — No contributor negates, cancels, or ignores another's offer ("That wouldn't happen," "No, instead...," "Let's do something different")
- [ ] **No Wimping** — Contributors do not accept offers passively without adding; accepting without building is wimping, not "Yes, and"
- [ ] **Offers Are Named and Honored** — When someone introduces an element, it is acknowledged and incorporated, not silently dropped in the next exchange
- [ ] **Building Is Visible** — Each successive contribution can be traced back to a previous offer; the chain of building is identifiable

### Scene Advancement
- [ ] **Narrative Advances** — The collaborative process produces forward movement: events happen, characters develop, tensions escalate
- [ ] **No Circular Conversations** — The scene does not return to the same point repeatedly because offers are being ignored or deflected
- [ ] **Surprise Emerges** — The "Yes, and" process produces outcomes no single contributor would have created alone; the collaboration generates novelty
- [ ] **Scene Has Shape** — The collaborative output has a beginning, development, and a sense of arrival; it is not just a series of disconnected additions

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the collaborative process is generative, respectful, and productive |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; mostly "Yes, and" but some blocking or wimping present |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or blocking is the dominant pattern |

## Next Steps
- If blocking is prevalent, run a 10-minute "Yes, and only" exercise where "no" is literally forbidden
- If offers are weak, practice "gift-giving": each contributor's job is to make the other contributors look good
- Record a collaborative session and transcribe it; mark each exchange as "Yes, and," "Block," or "Wimp"
