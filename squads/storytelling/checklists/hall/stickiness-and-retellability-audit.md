# Stickiness and Retellability Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the story is memorable, retellable in under 2 minutes, and details stick

## Purpose
Ensure the story is crafted so well that the audience remembers it, can retell it to others, and specific details persist in memory. A story that cannot be retold dies with the presentation; a retellable story multiplies its impact exponentially.

## Prerequisites
- Complete story draft or final version
- Story tested with at least one audience (live, recorded, or read)
- Key details and emotional beats identified

## Checklist

### Memorability
- [ ] **Core Plot Memorable** — After one hearing/reading, the audience can recall the basic setup, conflict, and resolution without prompting
- [ ] **Specific Details Stick** — At least 2-3 concrete details (a name, a number, an image, a quote) are recalled by listeners unprompted
- [ ] **Emotional Peak Remembered** — The most emotionally intense moment is the part people remember most vividly; it is not buried or rushed
- [ ] **Story Has a "Handle"** — The story can be referenced with a short phrase or image that immediately brings back the full narrative for anyone who heard it

### Retellability
- [ ] **Retellable in Under 2 Minutes** — A listener can relay the essential story (not just the lesson) to someone else in under 2 minutes
- [ ] **Structure Is Simple** — The story follows a clear, linear structure that does not require the reteller to explain complex timelines, characters, or subplots
- [ ] **One Protagonist** — The story centers on one person's experience; multiple characters are supporting, not co-equal, keeping retelling simple
- [ ] **Surprise or Twist Is Retellable** — If the story contains a surprise, it is the kind of surprise people want to share ("You won't believe what happened...")

### Specific Details That Stick
- [ ] **Sensory Details Anchor Memory** — The story includes at least one vivid sensory detail (a sound, a smell, a visual) that lodges in the listener's mind
- [ ] **Numbers Are Memorable** — Any data or statistics used are rounded, contextualized, and sticky (e.g., "the size of a football field" not "approximately 57,600 square feet")
- [ ] **Dialogue Quoted** — At least one line of actual or reconstructed dialogue is included; direct quotes are more memorable than paraphrases
- [ ] **Unnecessary Details Cut** — Details that do not contribute to memorability, emotion, or plot are removed; lean stories stick better than bloated ones

### Virality Potential
- [ ] **Story Provokes Retelling Urge** — After hearing the story, listeners feel compelled to share it, not just appreciate it
- [ ] **Story Works Without the Original Teller** — The story does not depend on the original presenter's charisma; it retains power when a third party tells it
- [ ] **Story Fits Social Contexts** — The story is appropriate and effective when retold at dinner, in a meeting, on social media, or in a hallway conversation

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the story is a self-replicating narrative that spreads through retelling |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; story is memorable but retelling is difficult or details are too many |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or no test listener can retell the story after one hearing |

## Next Steps
- Tell the story to three people and 24 hours later ask them to retell it; note what they remember and what they change
- If retelling takes more than 2 minutes, simplify the plot and cut characters
- If no details stick, add one vivid sensory image and one quotable line of dialogue
