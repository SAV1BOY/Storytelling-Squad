# Story Selection Strategy Audit
> Quality gate for verifying the right story is chosen for the intersection of audience, objective, channel, and timing

## Purpose
Ensure story selection is strategic, not habitual. The right story at the wrong time, on the wrong channel, or for the wrong audience wastes its power. This audit verifies that the story chosen is the optimal match for the specific communication situation.

## Prerequisites
- Story portfolio or reservoir of available stories
- Communication brief with audience, objective, channel, and timing defined
- Kindra Hall's story selection framework or equivalent decision matrix

## Checklist

### Audience Match
- [ ] **Audience Profile Known** — The specific audience for this communication is documented: demographics, psychographics, current knowledge level, and emotional state
- [ ] **Story Resonates With This Audience** — The protagonist, conflict, or situation in the story mirrors or connects to this specific audience's experience
- [ ] **Cultural and Contextual Fit** — The story is appropriate for the audience's cultural context, industry norms, and sensitivities
- [ ] **Knowledge Gap Addressed** — The story does not assume knowledge the audience lacks or explain things the audience already knows

### Objective Match
- [ ] **Objective Is Defined** — The communication goal is specific: persuade, inspire, inform, build trust, overcome objection, create urgency, align, or entertain
- [ ] **Story Serves the Objective** — The story's emotional and logical payload directly advances the stated objective; it is not just a good story told in the wrong context
- [ ] **Story Does Not Undermine Other Objectives** — Telling this story does not accidentally damage trust, credibility, or other concurrent goals
- [ ] **Story Type Matches Objective** — The story type (Value, Founder, Customer, Purpose) aligns with the objective per Hall's framework

### Channel Match
- [ ] **Channel Identified** — The delivery channel is specified: keynote, one-on-one, email, social media, video, blog, sales call, meeting
- [ ] **Story Length Fits Channel** — The story's duration or word count is appropriate for the channel (30 seconds for social, 3 minutes for keynote, 2 paragraphs for email)
- [ ] **Story Format Fits Channel** — The story's structure works for the medium: visual stories for video, dialogue-rich for podcasts, punchy for social media
- [ ] **Story Tone Matches Channel** — The emotional register is appropriate for the platform (informal for social, polished for keynote, conversational for sales)

### Timing Match
- [ ] **Timing Is Deliberate** — The story is deployed at a moment when the audience is receptive: after a problem is felt, during a decision window, at a cultural moment
- [ ] **Not Too Early or Late** — The story is not told before the audience has context to appreciate it or after the decision window has closed
- [ ] **Story Has Not Been Overused** — This specific story has not been told to this audience recently enough to feel stale

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 15 items checked; the story is the right story for the right audience at the right time in the right place |
| Conditional | 11-14 items checked; story is well-chosen but one dimension (audience, channel, timing) could be better optimized |
| Fail | Fewer than 11 items, or a fundamental mismatch exists between the story and the situation |

## Next Steps
- If no story in the portfolio fits, identify the gap and develop a new story using the Homework for Life reservoir
- Create a story selection matrix: rows are available stories, columns are audience, objective, channel, timing; score each combination
- After deployment, evaluate: did the audience respond as expected? If not, which dimension was misjudged?
