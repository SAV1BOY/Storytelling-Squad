# Webinar Narrative Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a webinar integrates teaching, tension, and offer into a cohesive narrative experience

## Purpose
Gates the narrative quality of webinars, masterclasses, live workshops, and online presentations
that combine education with a commercial or action-oriented objective. Use after the webinar
script or outline is drafted and before rehearsal or technical setup. Ensures the webinar
delivers genuine, standalone value while building naturally toward its offer or CTA, without
the teaching and selling feeling like two separate events stitched together with an awkward
transition. The best webinars feel like a single continuous experience where the offer is the
natural climax of the learning journey.

## When to Use
- After a webinar script or outline is drafted and before rehearsal or technical setup
- When a previous webinar had low attendance retention or poor conversion rates
- Before any live educational event that includes a commercial or action-oriented CTA
- When converting in-person workshop content into a webinar format
- During webinar optimization to improve the teaching-to-offer transition

## Prerequisites
- A webinar script, detailed outline, or annotated slide deck exists
- The topic, target audience, and their awareness level are defined
- The offer, product, service, or desired action (CTA) is clearly specified
- The webinar duration is set (typically 45-90 minutes, including Q&A)
- Teaching content has been developed and validated for accuracy
- Registration and promotion messaging are drafted or in progress

## Checklist

### Teaching Arc
- [ ] **Teaching delivers genuine value** — The educational content is useful on its own, even if the audience never takes the offered action; it is not a thinly veiled sales pitch disguised as education
- [ ] **Content organized around 3-5 key insights** — The teaching portion is structured into distinct, memorable takeaways or frameworks, not a firehose of disconnected information
- [ ] **Each insight builds on the previous** — Teaching points are sequenced so each one requires or enriches the one before; the audience feels a sense of progressive learning, not random topic jumping
- [ ] **Quick wins included** — At least one teaching point gives the audience something they can apply immediately during the webinar or right after it ends, creating goodwill and demonstrating competence
- [ ] **Knowledge gap created intentionally** — The teaching reveals enough to be genuinely valuable but also makes the audience aware of what they still do not know or cannot do alone, naturally positioning the offer as the next step
- [ ] **Teaching is audience-level appropriate** — The content matches the audience's starting knowledge; it does not bore experts with basics or lose beginners with advanced concepts

### Tension Arc
- [ ] **Problem established in first 10 minutes** — The webinar opens by framing the audience's pain, challenge, missed opportunity, or aspiration with specificity, urgency, and emotional resonance
- [ ] **Stakes raised throughout** — As teaching progresses, the consequences of not solving the problem (or not solving it well) become clearer, more personal, and more costly
- [ ] **Story woven through teaching** — At least one running narrative thread (founder story, customer transformation, audience-surrogate journey) connects the teaching points into a story, not just a lecture
- [ ] **Emotional variation present** — The webinar alternates between intellectual beats (learning, frameworks, data) and emotional beats (stories, humor, vulnerability, urgency) to maintain full-brain engagement
- [ ] **Engagement hooks at drop-off points** — Interactive elements (polls, chat prompts, rhetorical questions, surprising reveals, story beats) are placed at 10-15 minute intervals where attention naturally dips

### Offer Arc
- [ ] **Transition to offer is natural** — The shift from teaching to presenting the offer feels like a logical conclusion or next step, not an abrupt pivot marked by "Now let me tell you about..."
- [ ] **Offer solves the gap teaching created** — The product, service, program, or action directly addresses the knowledge gap, capability gap, or implementation gap that the teaching intentionally revealed
- [ ] **Offer is presented as a story** — The offer section includes at least one transformation story (customer result, before/after case study) rather than just a feature list and pricing slide
- [ ] **Objections addressed within narrative** — The 3-5 most common reasons for not taking action are woven into the offer section through stories, FAQ-style beats, or preemptive framing
- [ ] **Scarcity or urgency is real** — Any limited-time pricing, enrollment caps, or deadline claims are genuine, specific, and verifiable; no fake countdown timers
- [ ] **CTA is repeated and clear** — The specific action (register, purchase, book a call, download) is stated at least 3 times in the final third with a clear, simple mechanism (URL, button, instructions)

### Production and Flow
- [ ] **Total runtime respects the audience** — The webinar fits within its advertised time window; teaching, offer, and Q&A are proportioned appropriately (typically 55-65% teaching, 20-25% offer, 15-20% Q&A)
- [ ] **Opening hook in first 2 minutes** — The webinar begins with a compelling reason to stay: a bold promise, a surprising statistic, a provocative question, or a "by the end of this session you will..." commitment
- [ ] **Slide design supports narrative** — Slides reinforce the spoken narrative rather than duplicating it word-for-word; no text-heavy slides that the presenter reads aloud to the audience
- [ ] **Q&A is prepared** — Common questions are anticipated and answers are rehearsed; the presenter is ready for hostile, off-topic, or sales-objection questions during the live Q&A

## Scoring
Must pass **17/22 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Teaching delivers genuine value
- Transition to offer is natural
- Offer solves the gap teaching created
- Opening hook in first 2 minutes
- CTA is repeated and clear

Any CRITICAL failure requires webinar restructuring before rehearsal.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to full rehearsal with timing. Time each section independently and verify
  proportions. Test technical setup (screen share, slides, polls, chat). Prepare for live Q&A
  using improv-readiness-quality.md. Do at least one full run-through at speed.
- **On Fail:** Restructure using a three-act framework: Act 1 (problem + promise + credibility),
  Act 2 (teaching insights woven with stories), Act 3 (gap reveal + offer + proof + CTA).
  Reference Duarte's Sparkline (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) for presentation
  narrative structure and the pitch framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/pitch/) for offer
  positioning. If the teaching and offer feel disconnected, the teaching topic likely needs to
  be reframed so that the offer is the natural next chapter of the learning journey, not a
  separate commercial break inserted into the educational experience. Ask: "What would the
  audience naturally want to do next after learning this?" The answer should be your offer.
