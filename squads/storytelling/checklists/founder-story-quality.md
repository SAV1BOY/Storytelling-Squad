# Founder Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a founder narrative is authentic, compelling, and strategically purposeful

## Purpose
Gates the quality of a founder's personal origin story. Use when crafting or revising an
About the Founder page, pitch deck origin slide, keynote personal narrative, media interview
preparation, podcast guest appearance, or any content where the founder's personal journey
carries strategic weight. The founder story is not a resume or LinkedIn summary; it is a
narrative that builds trust, authority, and emotional connection by showing who this person
is through what they have lived.

## When to Use
- When creating or revising the founder's About page, pitch deck origin slide, or keynote narrative
- Before media interviews, podcast appearances, or PR campaigns featuring the founder
- When preparing investor pitch materials that include the team and origin section
- During recruiting campaigns where the founder story is used to attract talent
- When the company hits a milestone that adds a new chapter to the founder's journey

## Prerequisites
- A draft founder narrative exists (written or recorded)
- The founder has been interviewed or has provided raw material with specific stories
- The business context and target audience are defined
- The strategic purpose of the founder story is clear (fundraising, brand building, recruiting, media, sales)
- The founder has approved the level of personal disclosure they are comfortable with

## Checklist

### Origin Moment
- [ ] **Specific origin moment identified** — The story pinpoints a single, concrete moment or event that sparked the journey, not a vague "I always wanted to..." or "I've been passionate about..."
- [ ] **Origin is sensory and vivid** — The founding moment includes at least two sensory details (what was seen, heard, felt, where they were standing) that place the audience in the scene
- [ ] **Origin connects to a real problem** — The founding moment arose from encountering a genuine pain, gap, or injustice in the real world, not from reading a market analysis or spotting a trend
- [ ] **Timeline is clear** — The audience can follow when key events happened relative to each other without confusion about the chronological sequence
- [ ] **Origin is not the company launch** — The story goes back further than "we incorporated on..."; it captures the human moment before the business decision

### Personal Motivation and Vulnerability
- [ ] **Personal "why" is explicit** — The story explains why this specific person cares about this specific problem in a way that goes beyond financial opportunity or career progression
- [ ] **Vulnerability is present** — The founder reveals at least one moment of doubt, fear, failure, personal cost, or emotional struggle that makes the story human and relatable
- [ ] **Vulnerability is strategic** — The vulnerable moment is relevant to the narrative arc and builds trust and respect, not pity or concern; it demonstrates resilience and conviction
- [ ] **Sacrifices named** — The story mentions what the founder gave up, risked, or endured to pursue this path: money, stability, relationships, reputation, comfort
- [ ] **Emotional honesty present** — The narrative includes genuine feelings (frustration, excitement, fear, pride, exhaustion, exhilaration) rather than corporate-neutral, sanitized language
- [ ] **Imperfection acknowledged** — The founder story does not present a flawless hero; at least one mistake, wrong turn, or lesson learned is included

### Mission Connection
- [ ] **Personal story connects to company mission** — The founder's personal experience logically and emotionally leads to the company's reason for existing; the connection is not forced
- [ ] **Bridge from personal to universal** — The story transitions from "my problem" to "our problem" to "your problem," making the audience feel they are part of the mission, not spectators
- [ ] **Mission is forward-looking** — The story does not only look backward at the origin; it connects the founding moment to a future vision the audience can participate in and help build
- [ ] **Values are demonstrated, not declared** — The founder's core values (perseverance, fairness, curiosity, service) are shown through actions in the story, not listed as adjectives

### Authority and Credibility
- [ ] **Relevant expertise established** — The narrative demonstrates why this founder is uniquely qualified to solve this problem: domain experience, hard-won insight, unusual access, or sustained obsession
- [ ] **Results or traction mentioned** — At least one proof point demonstrates that the founder's vision is working and producing results, not just aspirational thinking
- [ ] **Authority shown through action, not claimed** — Credibility comes from what the founder did, built, discovered, or endured, not from self-applied labels ("I'm a visionary" or "I'm a thought leader")
- [ ] **Humility balanced with confidence** — The founder acknowledges what they learned from others, what remains unsolved, or what surprised them, while showing deep conviction in the direction

### Narrative Craft
- [ ] **Story is the right length for the format** — The founder story is compressed or expanded to fit the target format: 60 seconds for a pitch, 300 words for an about page, 5 minutes for a keynote segment
- [ ] **Third-person validation present** — At least one external voice (co-founder, early customer, mentor, journalist) corroborates or enriches the founder's own account
- [ ] **Story is retellable** — A listener who heard the founder story once could retell its essence to someone else; it has a memorable core moment and a clear arc
- [ ] **Story evolves with the company** — The founder story can grow new chapters as the company scales without requiring a complete rewrite; the origin remains relevant at any stage

## Scoring
Must pass **17/24 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Specific origin moment identified
- Personal "why" is explicit
- Vulnerability is present
- Personal story connects to company mission
- Authority shown through action, not claimed

Any CRITICAL failure requires the founder story to be revised or the founder to be re-interviewed.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Integrate the founder story into the target format (pitch deck origin slide, about
  page, keynote opening, media kit). Run the relevant format-specific checklist for the
  destination format.
- **On Fail:** Conduct a deeper founder interview using Dicks' "Homework for Life" technique
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/dicks/) to surface the real origin moment beneath the
  polished version. Reference Campbell's Hero's Journey (squads/storytelling/checklists/campbell/)
  for structuring the personal quest, or Ganz's Story of Self
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/ganz/) for connecting personal narrative to collective purpose.
  Avoid the instinct to make the story "more impressive"; instead focus on making it more true,
  more specific, and more human. The best founder stories are the ones the founder would tell
  a close friend over dinner, not the ones crafted for a press release.
