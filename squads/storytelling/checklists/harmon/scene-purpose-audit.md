# Scene Purpose Audit
> Quality gate for verifying every scene changes a value, decision, or state with no decorative scenes

## Purpose
Ensure that every scene in the narrative has a reason to exist beyond atmosphere or information delivery. Each scene must change something: a character's emotional state, a relationship's dynamic, a power balance, or a decision. Scenes that change nothing are candidates for cutting.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft with scenes or segments identified
- Each scene summarized with its starting and ending state
- Character arcs documented

## Checklist

### Value Change
- [ ] **Every Scene Shifts a Value** — Each scene begins at one value state (positive, negative, or neutral) and ends at a different one; no scene ends where it started
- [ ] **Value Shift Is Identifiable** — For each scene, you can name the value that changed (trust, power, hope, safety, knowledge, love, status)
- [ ] **Shift Is Meaningful** — The value change matters to the character or the audience; it is not trivial or cosmetic

### Decision and Action
- [ ] **Character Makes a Decision** — At least one character in each scene faces a choice and acts on it, even if the choice is to do nothing (which must have consequences)
- [ ] **Decisions Have Stakes** — The choices characters make carry risk; wrong decisions produce fallout in subsequent scenes
- [ ] **No Passive Scenes** — Characters are not mere observers or recipients of information; they respond, react, decide, or resist

### State Change
- [ ] **Relationship Dynamics Shift** — Scenes involving multiple characters change the power balance, trust level, or emotional connection between them
- [ ] **Information Changes the Situation** — If a scene primarily delivers information, the information materially changes what a character can or will do next
- [ ] **Emotional State Progresses** — The character's emotional journey advances; they do not feel the same way at scene's end as at scene's start

### No Decorative Scenes
- [ ] **Every Scene Passes the Cut Test** — If the scene were removed, something in the narrative would break, be confusing, or lose emotional impact
- [ ] **Atmospheric Scenes Earn Their Place** — Any scene that primarily creates mood also advances character, relationship, or plot; mood alone is insufficient
- [ ] **Exposition Is Dramatic** — Scenes that deliver backstory or context do so through conflict, discovery, or tension, not through static dialogue or narration
- [ ] **No Redundant Scenes** — No two scenes accomplish the same narrative purpose; each scene does unique work

### Craft Verification
- [ ] **Scene Opening Hooks** — Each scene begins at a point of tension or interest, not with throat-clearing or setup
- [ ] **Scene Ending Propels** — Each scene ends on a moment that pushes the audience into the next scene (question, decision, reversal, cliffhanger)
- [ ] **Scene Length Justified** — Long scenes contain enough value shifts to warrant their length; short scenes deliver their shift efficiently

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; every scene earns its place through change |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; most scenes change values but 1-2 are soft |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or more than 2 decorative scenes remain |

## Next Steps
- Create a scene map listing: scene number, starting value, ending value, decision made, what breaks if cut
- Merge or cut scenes that fail the cut test
- Strengthen weak scenes by adding a decision point or complication
