# But/Therefore Causality Audit
> Quality gate for verifying every narrative transition is causal ("but" or "therefore"), never episodic ("and then")

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative maintains causal momentum where every scene or beat connects to the next through conflict ("but") or consequence ("therefore"). The presence of "and then" transitions signals episodic, non-causal storytelling that loses audience engagement.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft or detailed scene-by-scene outline
- Each scene or beat summarized in one sentence
- Understanding of the South Park "but/therefore" rule popularized by Trey Parker and Matt Stone, adopted by Harmon

## Checklist

### Causal Chain Verification
- [ ] **Every Transition Tested** — Each scene-to-scene transition has been examined and labeled as "but," "therefore," or "and then"
- [ ] **Zero "And Then" Transitions** — No transition relies on mere sequence ("and then this happened"); every connection is causal or conflictual
- [ ] **"But" Transitions Create Conflict** — Each "but" introduces an obstacle, reversal, complication, or surprise that changes the character's trajectory
- [ ] **"Therefore" Transitions Show Consequence** — Each "therefore" demonstrates a logical result of the previous action, even if the result is unexpected

### Momentum and Engagement
- [ ] **Forward Momentum Maintained** — The causal chain pulls the audience forward; no point in the narrative feels like treading water
- [ ] **No Decorative Detours** — Every scene that exists contributes to the causal chain; scenic or atmospheric passages are integrated into cause-and-effect
- [ ] **Escalation Through Causality** — The "but/therefore" chain produces escalating stakes, not lateral movement at the same intensity level
- [ ] **Audience Can Predict Pattern** — A reader can intuit that something will go wrong ("but") or that action will have consequences ("therefore") without knowing the specifics

### Structural Application
- [ ] **Act-Level Causality** — The three-act or multi-act structure follows "but/therefore" logic at the macro level, not just scene-to-scene
- [ ] **Subplot Causality** — Secondary storylines also follow "but/therefore" chains rather than running as parallel "and then" sequences
- [ ] **Character Decision Causality** — Character choices lead to consequences ("therefore") or face obstacles ("but"); decisions are not inconsequential

### Problem Detection
- [ ] **Saggy Middle Diagnosed** — Any flat or boring section in the middle of the narrative has been traced to "and then" causality and repaired
- [ ] **Random Events Justified** — If an external event (coincidence, accident) occurs, it creates a "but" that the character must respond to with a "therefore"
- [ ] **Ending Earned by Chain** — The resolution is the final link in a causal chain, not a deus ex machina or disconnected event

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 14 items checked; the narrative is a relentless causal chain |
| Conditional | 10-13 items checked; most transitions are causal but 1-2 "and then" moments remain |
| Fail | Fewer than 10 items, or more than 2 "and then" transitions present |

## Next Steps
- List every scene transition and label it "but," "therefore," or "and then" in a spreadsheet
- Convert each "and then" to either "but" (add conflict) or "therefore" (add consequence)
- Read the one-sentence summaries aloud connected by "but" and "therefore" to test the chain's strength
