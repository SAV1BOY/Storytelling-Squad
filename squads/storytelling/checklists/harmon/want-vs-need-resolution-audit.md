# Want vs Need Resolution Audit
> Quality gate for verifying external want and internal need are both addressed with tension between them

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative creates dramatic depth by distinguishing the protagonist's conscious desire (want) from their unconscious requirement for growth (need), building tension between the two, and resolving both by the story's end.

## Prerequisites
- Protagonist's want and need explicitly documented
- Complete narrative draft
- Character arc outline showing how want and need evolve

## Checklist

### Want Defined
- [ ] **External Want Is Concrete** — The protagonist's want is specific, visible, and something the audience can track (get the job, win the case, find the treasure, close the deal)
- [ ] **Want Is Established Early** — The audience knows what the protagonist wants within the first 15-20% of the narrative
- [ ] **Want Drives Action** — The want is what propels the protagonist to act, make decisions, and face obstacles throughout the story

### Need Defined
- [ ] **Internal Need Is Identified** — The protagonist's deeper need is documented: a flaw to overcome, a truth to accept, a wound to heal
- [ ] **Need Is Shown Through Behavior** — The need is revealed through the protagonist's patterns, mistakes, and blind spots, not through exposition
- [ ] **Need Is Not Obvious to the Hero** — The protagonist is unaware of or resistant to their need for most of the story; it is unconscious

### Tension Between Want and Need
- [ ] **Want and Need Conflict** — Pursuing the want makes the need harder to fulfill, or the need requires sacrificing or redefining the want
- [ ] **Tension Escalates** — The conflict between want and need intensifies as the story progresses; it does not remain static
- [ ] **Key Choice Forces the Tension** — At the climax or a critical moment, the protagonist must choose between want and need, or find a way to reconcile them
- [ ] **Audience Feels the Dilemma** — The tension is not just structural but emotionally felt; the audience understands why both want and need matter

### Resolution
- [ ] **Both Want and Need Addressed** — By the story's end, the narrative has resolved or meaningfully addressed both the want and the need, not just one
- [ ] **Need Resolution Feels Earned** — The protagonist's growth toward fulfilling the need is gradual and tested, not sudden or easy
- [ ] **Want Outcome Reflects Growth** — Whether the protagonist gets the want or not, the outcome is shaped by their relationship to the need
- [ ] **Resolution Creates Thematic Clarity** — The way want and need resolve communicates the story's controlling idea or moral

### Craft Verification
- [ ] **No Confusion Between Want and Need** — The narrative is clear enough that the audience could articulate both, even if not in those terms
- [ ] **Supporting Characters Illuminate the Tension** — At least one character challenges the protagonist's want, and at least one embodies or reflects the need
- [ ] **Resolution Is Not Preachy** — The need is fulfilled through dramatic action, not through a character monologue or lesson statement

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 17 items checked; want and need create a rich, resolved character arc |
| Conditional | 13-16 items checked; want and need present but tension or resolution could deepen |
| Fail | Fewer than 13 items, or want and need are identical or one is entirely missing |

## Next Steps
- If want and need are identical, redefine the need as a deeper truth the want is masking
- If tension is weak, add a scene where pursuing the want directly damages the need
- Map the want and need trajectories side by side across the story to verify their interplay
