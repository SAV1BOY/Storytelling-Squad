# Story Circle 8 Steps Audit
> Quality gate for verifying all 8 steps of Dan Harmon's Story Circle are present and functional

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative follows Harmon's simplified monomyth circle: You, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take, Return, Change. Each step must be identifiable and serve the story's emotional engine.

## Prerequisites
- Complete narrative draft or detailed outline
- Protagonist clearly identified
- Understanding of Harmon's Story Circle framework

## Checklist

### Step 1: You (A character is in a zone of comfort)
- [ ] **Comfort Zone Established** — The protagonist's ordinary state is shown with enough specificity that the audience understands their baseline
- [ ] **Character Is Sympathetic or Interesting** — The audience has a reason to follow this character before anything happens

### Step 2: Need (But they want something)
- [ ] **Want Is Explicit** — The character's conscious desire is stated or shown clearly; the audience knows what they are pursuing
- [ ] **Need Is Implicit** — An underlying need (often unconscious) is hinted at through behavior, flaw, or gap in the character's life

### Step 3: Go (They enter an unfamiliar situation)
- [ ] **Threshold Crossing Is Clear** — There is a definite moment when the character leaves the comfort zone; it is not gradual or ambiguous
- [ ] **Unfamiliarity Is Felt** — The new situation has different rules, pressures, or dynamics than the comfort zone

### Step 4: Search (They adapt to it)
- [ ] **Adaptation Shown** — The character actively tries strategies, forms alliances, or learns skills to navigate the new situation
- [ ] **Struggle Is Present** — Adaptation is not easy; the character faces resistance, failure, or confusion

### Step 5: Find (They get what they wanted)
- [ ] **Want Achieved or Within Reach** — The character obtains (or appears to obtain) the thing they consciously desired
- [ ] **Midpoint Shift Occurs** — Getting what they wanted changes the dynamic; the story pivots from seeking to dealing with consequences

### Step 6: Take (But pay a heavy price)
- [ ] **Price Is Heavy** — The cost of getting what they wanted is significant: loss, sacrifice, revelation, or irreversible change
- [ ] **Price Is Connected to the Need** — The heavy price forces the character to confront their deeper, unconscious need

### Step 7: Return (They return to the familiar situation)
- [ ] **Return to Origin** — The character re-enters their original world, context, or situation, completing the circle geographically or metaphorically
- [ ] **Return Is Not Easy** — The journey back involves its own challenges, resistance, or emotional difficulty

### Step 8: Change (Having changed)
- [ ] **Change Is Visible** — The character is demonstrably different from Step 1; the audience can point to specific evidence of transformation
- [ ] **Change Addresses the Need** — The transformation resolves or acknowledges the deeper need identified in Step 2, not just the surface want
- [ ] **Circle Feels Complete** — The ending echoes the beginning in a way that highlights the distance traveled

### Structural Integrity
- [ ] **All 8 Steps Present** — No step is missing; each is identifiable in the narrative
- [ ] **Steps Flow in Order** — The sequence follows the circle without skipping or reordering steps
- [ ] **Top Half vs Bottom Half Contrast** — Steps 1-4 (order, comfort, known) contrast with Steps 5-8 (chaos, cost, unknown) creating dramatic tension

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 19 items checked; the circle is complete and emotionally satisfying |
| Conditional | 15-18 items checked; steps present but some lack dramatic weight |
| Fail | Fewer than 15 items, or any step entirely missing |

## Next Steps
- If a step is weak, write a one-sentence version of what that step accomplishes and build the scene around it
- If Want and Need are identical, deepen the character to create tension between surface desire and deeper truth
- Map the circle visually and verify the top/bottom contrast
