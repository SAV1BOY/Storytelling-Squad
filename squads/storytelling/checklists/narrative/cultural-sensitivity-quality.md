# Cultural Sensitivity Quality
> Quality gate for verifying metaphors are appropriate, references accessible, and tone calibrated for the audience

## Purpose
Ensure the narrative respects and accounts for cultural diversity in its audience. Metaphors, idioms, humor, imagery, and references must be accessible to the intended audience and free from unintentional bias, stereotyping, or exclusion.

## Prerequisites
- Target audience demographics and cultural context documented
- Markets or regions where the narrative will be deployed identified
- Draft narrative reviewed by at least one person from a different cultural background than the author

## Checklist

### Metaphor Appropriateness
- [ ] **Metaphors Are Culturally Neutral or Universally Understood** — Central metaphors (journey, building, growth, light/dark) are accessible across the audience's cultural contexts
- [ ] **No Culturally Specific Metaphors Without Context** — Metaphors drawn from specific cultures (sports analogies, regional expressions, religious references) include enough context for non-native audiences
- [ ] **No Appropriation** — Imagery, symbols, or language from specific cultural traditions is used with understanding and respect, not as exotic decoration
- [ ] **Metaphors Checked for Double Meaning** — Key metaphors have been reviewed for unintended meanings in the audience's cultural and linguistic contexts

### Reference Accessibility
- [ ] **References Are Broadly Recognizable** — Pop culture, historical, and literary references are either widely known or explained in context
- [ ] **No Insider References Without Bridge** — References that assume specific cultural knowledge (TV shows, sports events, political figures) include a brief context bridge for outsiders
- [ ] **Numbers and Measurements Contextualized** — Units, currencies, and scales are presented in formats the audience understands or are accompanied by conversions
- [ ] **Humor Travels** — If humor is used, it is based on universally relatable situations rather than culture-specific wordplay or stereotypes

### Tone Calibration
- [ ] **Formality Level Appropriate** — The narrative's level of formality matches the cultural expectations of the audience (some cultures expect formality in business; others expect warmth)
- [ ] **Directness Calibrated** — The degree of directness in calls to action, criticism, or persuasion is calibrated for cultural norms (high-context vs. low-context communication)
- [ ] **Emotional Expression Appropriate** — The level of emotional expression matches audience expectations; some cultures value restraint, others value expressiveness
- [ ] **Power Distance Respected** — The narrative's assumptions about hierarchy, authority, and egalitarianism match the audience's cultural framework

### Bias and Stereotype Review
- [ ] **No Stereotypes** — The narrative does not rely on cultural, gender, age, or professional stereotypes, even positive ones
- [ ] **Inclusive Representation** — If people are depicted (images, stories, examples), they reflect the diversity of the audience
- [ ] **Language Is Inclusive** — Gendered language, ableist terms, and exclusionary phrasing have been identified and revised
- [ ] **External Review Completed** — At least one person from outside the author's cultural background has reviewed the narrative for blind spots

## Scoring
| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| Pass | All 16 items checked; the narrative is culturally aware, inclusive, and accessible |
| Conditional | 12-15 items checked; mostly appropriate but some metaphors or references need adjustment |
| Fail | Fewer than 12 items, or stereotypes, appropriation, or exclusionary language is present |

## Next Steps
- If culturally specific metaphors are essential, add a brief contextual bridge for non-native audiences
- If the narrative will be translated, have the translator review metaphors for translatability before finalizing the source language version
- Commission a cultural sensitivity review from a professional if the narrative will reach diverse global audiences
