# Public Narrative Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a public narrative mobilizes action through the Self/Us/Now framework

## Purpose
Gates the effectiveness of public narratives designed to inspire collective action: campaign
speeches, movement messaging, organizational rallying cries, nonprofit fundraising appeals,
community calls to action, advocacy communications, or social change campaigns. Based on
Marshall Ganz's public narrative framework. Use when the goal is to move a group of people
to act together around shared values, not just to inform or entertain them.

## When to Use
- When preparing speeches, campaign messages, or calls to collective action
- For nonprofit fundraising appeals, advocacy campaigns, or community mobilization
- When a leader needs to rally a team, organization, or movement around shared purpose
- Before any public address where the goal is action, not just awareness
- When adapting organizational mission into an emotionally compelling call to arms

## Prerequisites
- A draft public narrative exists (speech, open letter, video script, campaign message, or rally address)
- The speaker/narrator's identity, role, and personal connection to the cause are established
- The audience community is identified with their shared values and concerns
- A specific collective action is defined (what the audience should do together)
- The values driving the narrative are articulated and genuine

## Checklist

### Story of Self
- [ ] **Personal origin moment present** — The narrator shares a specific moment from their own life that explains why they personally care about this cause; not a generic biography
- [ ] **Values revealed through experience** — The story of self demonstrates the narrator's core values through a lived experience, not through abstract declarations or lists of adjectives
- [ ] **Choice point highlighted** — The personal story includes a moment where the narrator made a significant choice that aligned with their values and set them on this path
- [ ] **Emotional authenticity present** — The personal narrative conveys genuine emotion (hope, anger, love, determination, grief, joy) without manipulation or performance
- [ ] **Self story is brief and purposeful** — The personal narrative takes no more than 20% of total content; it serves as a bridge to the collective story, not a memoir
- [ ] **Vulnerability calibrated** — The personal disclosure is honest enough to build trust but does not overwhelm the audience or shift focus from the cause to the speaker

### Story of Us
- [ ] **Shared identity established** — The narrative names and defines the "us": who this community is and what binds them together beyond geography or demographics
- [ ] **Shared values articulated** — At least one core value that the community holds is named and demonstrated through a collective experience, tradition, or defining moment from the group's history
- [ ] **Shared challenge identified** — The narrative describes a threat, injustice, or opportunity that affects the entire community, framed as a collective rather than individual problem
- [ ] **Community stories included** — The narrative references specific people, events, achievements, or traditions from the community's shared experience that the audience recognizes
- [ ] **Belonging is inclusive** — The "us" is defined in a way that invites the audience in and expands the circle rather than creating exclusive in-group dynamics or othering

### Story of Now
- [ ] **Urgent choice framed** — The narrative presents a specific, time-bound moment of decision: the audience must choose to act or accept the consequence of inaction
- [ ] **Action is specific and achievable** — The call to action names a concrete behavior the audience can take today, this week, or at this event, not a vague aspiration or lifestyle change
- [ ] **Cost of inaction is real** — The narrative articulates what will happen if the community does not act, with specific, credible, and emotionally resonant consequences
- [ ] **Hope is grounded** — The narrative demonstrates that success is possible by pointing to evidence, precedent, early wins, or existing momentum, not just optimism or wishful thinking
- [ ] **Moral present** — The narrative concludes with a clear moral imperative: this is what we must do because of who we are and what we believe; inaction contradicts our identity

### Integration and Delivery
- [ ] **Self-Us-Now flow is seamless** — The transitions between personal, collective, and urgent feel natural and progressive, not like three separate speeches stitched together with transitional phrases
- [ ] **Emotional arc builds** — The emotional intensity increases across the narrative: warmth and vulnerability in Self, solidarity and pride in Us, urgency and determination in Now
- [ ] **Audience is activated, not just informed** — The narrative is designed to produce immediate action (signing, showing up, donating, calling, marching) not just head-nodding agreement
- [ ] **Language is oral, not written** — The narrative is crafted for spoken delivery: short sentences, rhythmic repetition, call-and-response structures, and breath-friendly phrasing

### Authenticity and Ethics
- [ ] **Narrator has standing** — The person delivering this narrative has genuine connection to the cause and community; they are not parachuting into someone else's story
- [ ] **No manipulation of suffering** — The narrative does not exploit others' pain for emotional effect; stories of hardship are told with dignity and agency, not as spectacle
- [ ] **Power dynamics acknowledged** — If the narrator holds privilege relative to the affected community, this is acknowledged rather than ignored
- [ ] **Promise is honest** — The narrative does not promise outcomes that the proposed action cannot realistically deliver; hope is offered alongside honesty about the difficulty of change

## Scoring
Must pass **18/25 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Personal origin moment present
- Shared values articulated
- Action is specific and achievable
- Self-Us-Now flow is seamless
- Audience is activated, not just informed

Any CRITICAL failure requires narrative revision before delivery.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Move to delivery preparation and distribution planning. Rehearse for authenticity
  and emotional truth, not polish or performance. Practice with a small audience and observe
  whether they feel moved to act.
- **On Fail:** Revise using the Ganz public narrative structure (squads/storytelling/checklists/ganz/).
  If the Story of Self is weak, interview the narrator for a deeper, more specific personal
  connection to the cause. If the Story of Us is thin, research the community's shared history,
  language, and defining moments. If the Story of Now lacks urgency, identify the genuine
  deadline, tipping point, or window of opportunity. Consult the Public Narrative Agent for
  structural guidance. Test the narrative with community members before public delivery.
