# Brand Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a brand narrative is cohesive, differentiated, and strategically sound

## Purpose
Gates the integrity of a brand-level story: the overarching narrative that defines who the brand
is, what it stands for, and why it matters. Use this when developing or revising brand narratives,
about pages, brand manifestos, positioning documents, or brand messaging frameworks. Ensures the
brand story is not just a tagline or mission statement but a full, defensible narrative ecosystem
that works across every touchpoint.

## When to Use
- When developing or revising the core brand narrative or positioning
- Before launching a new brand, rebrand, or brand refresh
- When brand messaging feels inconsistent across teams or channels
- During annual brand strategy reviews or after significant company milestones
- When onboarding new marketing agencies, content teams, or partners

## Prerequisites
- Brand positioning or strategy document exists
- Target audience segments are defined with psychographic detail
- Competitive landscape is understood (direct and indirect competitors)
- Brand values and mission statement are documented
- Key proof points (traction, testimonials, milestones, awards) are available
- Visual identity and design system exist or are in development

## Checklist

### Brand Promise
- [ ] **Promise is explicit** — The brand makes a clear, specific promise to its audience that can be stated in one sentence without jargon or ambiguity
- [ ] **Promise is differentiated** — The promise is not interchangeable with a competitor's; substituting another brand name would make it false, weaker, or nonsensical
- [ ] **Promise is kept** — Evidence exists (product quality, service delivery, customer results) that the brand actually delivers on its stated promise consistently
- [ ] **Promise addresses a real desire or pain** — The promise connects to something the audience actively wants, fears, or struggles with, not something the brand thinks they should care about
- [ ] **Promise is memorable** — The promise can be recalled after a single exposure; it is not a committee-crafted paragraph but a sharp, sticky statement

### Brand Enemy
- [ ] **Enemy is identified** — The brand story names or implies what it stands against: a broken status quo, an outdated approach, a common frustration, an injustice, or a harmful norm
- [ ] **Enemy is the audience's enemy** — The antagonist is something the audience also opposes, creating shared cause and tribal belonging rather than brand-centric complaining
- [ ] **Enemy is specific, not generic** — "Bad customer service" is too vague; "being put on hold for 45 minutes by a robot that doesn't understand you" is specific and visceral
- [ ] **Brand is positioned as the weapon against the enemy** — The narrative clearly frames the brand as the tool, ally, or guide that helps the audience defeat the shared enemy
- [ ] **Enemy creates contrast** — The enemy's existence makes the brand's value proposition more vivid through opposition; without the enemy, the brand story would be less compelling

### Proof and Credibility
- [ ] **Origin story present** — The narrative includes how and why the brand was created, grounding it in a real moment, genuine motivation, or authentic frustration
- [ ] **Social proof integrated** — Customer numbers, testimonials, notable clients, community size, or user-generated content are woven into the brand narrative
- [ ] **Track record demonstrated** — Milestones, awards, press mentions, results, or longevity that prove the brand's promise is not aspirational but proven
- [ ] **Vulnerability included** — The brand story acknowledges at least one challenge, failure, early mistake, or limitation it overcame, building trust through honesty
- [ ] **Founder or team credibility established** — The people behind the brand have their relevant experience, passion, or authority established within the narrative

### Ritual and Consistency
- [ ] **Brand ritual defined** — There is a repeatable experience, phrase, process, or interaction uniquely associated with the brand that customers recognize and expect
- [ ] **Story is consistent across channels** — The core narrative elements (promise, enemy, proof, personality) are recognizable whether encountered on the website, social media, email, packaging, or in person
- [ ] **Visual and verbal identity aligned** — The tone, language, and imagery of the story match the brand's visual identity, design system, and sensory experience
- [ ] **Internal alignment confirmed** — Employees or team members can articulate the brand story in their own words with reasonable accuracy; it is not a marketing-only artifact
- [ ] **Story evolves without breaking** — The brand story has room to grow (new products, new markets, new chapters) without requiring a complete narrative rewrite

### Emotional and Cultural Resonance
- [ ] **Emotional territory claimed** — The brand owns a specific emotional space (empowerment, belonging, rebellion, safety, discovery) that is consistently evoked across all storytelling
- [ ] **Brand personality is distinct** — If the brand were a person at a dinner party, their conversation style, humor, and opinions would be recognizably different from competitors
- [ ] **Customer language reflected** — The brand story uses words, phrases, and framing that actual customers use when describing their own experience, not corporate-generated language
- [ ] **Cultural moment acknowledged** — The brand story connects to or responds to a current cultural tension, aspiration, or shift that makes it feel timely rather than timeless-but-irrelevant

## Scoring
Must pass **18/25 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Promise is explicit
- Promise is differentiated
- Enemy is identified
- Story is consistent across channels
- Internal alignment confirmed

Any CRITICAL failure requires brand story revision with senior stakeholder involvement.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Deploy brand story across channel-specific formats. Run the appropriate
  channel-specific checklists (email-sequence-narrative-quality.md, social-media-story-quality.md,
  video-script-narrative-quality.md, deck-story-quality.md) for each adaptation. Create a
  brand story one-pager for internal distribution.
- **On Fail:** Reconvene the brand strategy workshop. Reference Miller's StoryBrand framework
  (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for brand-as-guide positioning, the narrative
  architecture framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for overarching story
  structure, or the Duarte Sparkline (squads/storytelling/checklists/duarte/) for brand
  presentation narrative. Conduct customer interviews to validate that the promise and enemy
  resonate with real audience language and concerns. Do not finalize the brand story in
  isolation from customer input.
