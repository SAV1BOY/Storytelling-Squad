# Brand Story Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a brand narrative is cohesive, differentiated, and strategically sound

## Purpose
Gates the integrity of a brand-level story: the overarching narrative that defines who the brand is, what it stands for, and why it matters. Use this when developing or revising brand narratives, about pages, brand manifestos, or brand positioning documents. Ensures the brand story is not just a tagline but a full, defensible narrative ecosystem.

## Prerequisites
- Brand positioning or strategy document exists
- Target audience segments are defined
- Competitive landscape is understood
- Brand values and mission statement are documented
- Key proof points (traction, testimonials, milestones) are available

## Checklist

### Brand Promise
- [ ] **Promise is explicit** — The brand makes a clear, specific promise to its audience that can be stated in one sentence
- [ ] **Promise is differentiated** — The promise is not interchangeable with a competitor's; substituting another brand name would make it false or weaker
- [ ] **Promise is kept** — Evidence exists (product, service, results) that the brand actually delivers on its stated promise
- [ ] **Promise addresses a real desire or pain** — The promise connects to something the audience actively wants or a problem they actively suffer

### Brand Enemy
- [ ] **Enemy is identified** — The brand story names or implies what it stands against: a broken status quo, an outdated approach, a common frustration, an injustice
- [ ] **Enemy is the audience's enemy** — The antagonist is something the audience also opposes, creating shared cause rather than brand-centric complaining
- [ ] **Enemy is specific, not generic** — "Bad customer service" is too vague; "being put on hold for 45 minutes by a robot that doesn't understand you" is specific
- [ ] **Brand is positioned as the weapon against the enemy** — The narrative clearly frames the brand as the tool, ally, or guide that helps the audience defeat the shared enemy

### Proof and Credibility
- [ ] **Origin story present** — The narrative includes how and why the brand was created, grounding it in a real moment or genuine motivation
- [ ] **Social proof integrated** — Customer numbers, testimonials, notable clients, or community size are woven into the brand story
- [ ] **Track record demonstrated** — Milestones, awards, results, or longevity that prove the brand's promise is not just aspirational but proven
- [ ] **Vulnerability included** — The brand story acknowledges at least one challenge, failure, or limitation it overcame, building trust

### Ritual and Consistency
- [ ] **Brand ritual defined** — There is a repeatable experience, phrase, process, or interaction that is uniquely associated with the brand
- [ ] **Story is consistent across channels** — The core narrative elements (promise, enemy, proof) are recognizable whether encountered on the website, social media, email, or in person
- [ ] **Visual and verbal identity aligned** — The tone, language, and imagery of the story match the brand's visual identity and design system
- [ ] **Internal alignment confirmed** — Employees or team members can articulate the brand story in their own words with reasonable accuracy

## Scoring
Must pass **13/17 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- Promise is explicit
- Promise is differentiated
- Enemy is identified
- Story is consistent across channels

Any CRITICAL failure requires brand story revision with stakeholder involvement.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Deploy brand story across channel-specific formats. Run channel-specific checklists (email-sequence, social-media, video, deck) for each adaptation.
- **On Fail:** Reconvene brand strategy workshop. Reference Miller's StoryBrand framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/miller/) for brand-as-guide positioning, or the narrative architecture framework (squads/storytelling/checklists/narrative/) for overarching story structure. Conduct customer interviews to validate that the promise and enemy resonate.
