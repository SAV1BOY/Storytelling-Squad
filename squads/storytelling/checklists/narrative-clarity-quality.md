# Narrative Clarity Quality Checklist
> Quality gate for validating that a narrative communicates one clear message without confusion or noise

## Purpose
Gates the clarity and comprehensibility of a completed narrative draft. Use after the first full
draft is written and before any design, production, or delivery. This ensures the audience can
grasp the core message within seconds and follow the logic without re-reading. Clarity is not
the same as simplicity; a clear narrative can handle complex ideas by presenting them in a
structured, jargon-free, logically connected sequence.

## Prerequisites
- A complete narrative draft exists (not an outline or bullet points)
- The story brief has passed the Story Brief Quality checklist
- The intended audience and channel are known
- The primary message has been defined in the brief
- At least one person other than the writer is available for a fresh-eyes read

## Checklist

### Central Message (Miller Grunt Test)
- [ ] **One central message identifiable** — A reader can state the single main point in one sentence after a single read-through without referring back to the text
- [ ] **Grunt Test passed (5-second scan)** — Within 5 seconds of viewing the opening, a stranger can answer: (1) What is this about? (2) Why should I care? (3) What do I do next?
- [ ] **Message appears in first 20% of content** — The core thesis or promise is not buried; it surfaces early and explicitly, not hidden behind setup or context
- [ ] **Message restated at close** — The ending echoes or reinforces the central message without word-for-word repetition; it arrives from a new angle or with new weight
- [ ] **Message survives the elevator test** — If the audience stopped reading/watching at any random point after the first 25%, they would still know the core message

### Language and Accessibility
- [ ] **No unexplained jargon** — Every technical term, acronym, or insider phrase is either replaced with plain language or defined on first use within the text
- [ ] **Reading level appropriate** — The draft reads at or below the target audience's expected reading level (use Flesch-Kincaid or Hemingway Editor to verify; aim for Grade 8-10 for general audiences)
- [ ] **Sentences average under 20 words** — Spot-check 10 sentences from different sections; the average length does not exceed 20 words
- [ ] **No passive voice in key claims** — The protagonist, brand, or customer is the active subject in every critical statement; passive voice is reserved for deliberate de-emphasis only
- [ ] **Concrete over abstract** — Claims use specific numbers, names, timeframes, or examples rather than vague qualifiers ("many," "significant," "very," "a lot")
- [ ] **Pronouns have clear referents** — Every "it," "they," "this," and "that" clearly points to a specific noun; ambiguous pronouns are replaced with the noun they reference

### Logical Progression
- [ ] **Each section follows from the previous** — There are no logical leaps; every transition answers "why does this follow?" and the answer is self-evident
- [ ] **Causality chain intact** — The narrative uses "but" and "therefore" connectors (not just "and then") between major beats; events are linked by cause and effect
- [ ] **No contradictions** — No two statements in the draft conflict with each other; facts, claims, and characterizations are internally consistent
- [ ] **Supporting points serve the central message** — Every paragraph or scene can be traced back to supporting the one main point; any that cannot are flagged for removal
- [ ] **Hierarchy of information is clear** — The audience can distinguish primary points from supporting evidence from illustrative detail; formatting or language signals the hierarchy

### Noise Removal
- [ ] **No tangential anecdotes** — Every story or example directly illustrates or proves the central message; entertaining-but-irrelevant asides are cut
- [ ] **No redundant sections** — No idea is stated more than twice in the full draft (once to introduce, once to reinforce at the close); a third instance is always cut
- [ ] **Qualifier words minimized** — Words like "just," "really," "very," "quite," "somewhat," "actually," "basically" appear fewer than 3 times total
- [ ] **No throat-clearing opening** — The draft does not begin with preamble, background history, or "In today's rapidly changing world..." style filler
- [ ] **Every sentence earns its place** — Removing any single sentence would create a noticeable gap in meaning or flow; if removing it changes nothing, it must go

## Scoring
Must pass **16/21 minimum**. The following items are CRITICAL and must all pass:
- One central message identifiable
- Grunt Test passed
- No unexplained jargon
- Each section follows from the previous
- Every sentence earns its place

Any CRITICAL failure requires a revision pass focused specifically on clarity.

## Next Steps
- **On Pass:** Advance to structure/pacing review (structure-and-pacing-quality.md) and
  emotional arc review (emotional-arc-quality.md). The narrative is clear enough to evaluate
  at the structural and emotional levels.
- **On Fail:** Return to the narrative architect. Use the "one sentence" exercise: write the
  entire narrative's point in a single sentence, then rebuild every section to serve that
  sentence. Reference the Miller StoryBrand framework for message simplification. Use the
  Editing and Trimming checklist (editing-and-trimming-quality.md) to cut noise. Have a
  non-expert read the draft and report what they understood; their summary reveals the
  actual message vs. the intended message.
