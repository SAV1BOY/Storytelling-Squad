# Three-Act Outline
> Use this template for any narrative that needs a beginning, middle, and end. The most universal story structure — works for articles, presentations, videos, case studies, and pitches.

## Instructions
Fill in each act and its beats. Act I is 25% of the total, Act II is 50%, Act III is 25%. The midpoint in Act II is the fulcrum — design it first, then work outward. Every act must end with a turning point that propels the story into the next act.

## Template Body

### Story Metadata
- **Story title:** [TITLE]
- **Format:** [Article / Video / Deck / Speech / Case Study]
- **Hero:** [PROTAGONIST — customer, founder, team, user]
- **Total length:** [WORD COUNT or MINUTES]
- **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

---

### ACT I — SETUP (0–25%)
*Establish the world, the hero, and the problem. End with a commitment to act.*

**Opening Hook**
- **First line / image:** [WHAT GRABS ATTENTION]
- **Why it works:** [CURIOSITY, SURPRISE, EMOTION, RECOGNITION]

**The World Before**
- **Setting:** [WHERE and WHEN — the hero's current reality]
- **Hero's daily life:** [ROUTINES, BELIEFS, CAPABILITIES]
- **What's working:** [THE GOOD — what they don't want to lose]
- **What's not working:** [THE FLAW — what they can't see or won't face]

**The Inciting Incident**
- **What disrupts the status quo?** [EVENT]
- **Why can't the hero ignore it?** [STAKES]

**The Debate / Resistance**
- **Why does the hero hesitate?** [FEAR, COST, RISK]
- **What pushes them forward?** [PRESSURE, OPPORTUNITY, DEADLINE]

**Act I Turning Point — The Decision**
- **What does the hero decide to do?** [COMMITMENT — this ends Act I]
- **What are they walking into?** [THE UNKNOWN]

---

### ACT II — CONFRONTATION (25–75%)
*The hero faces escalating challenges. The middle is where most stories fail — keep raising stakes.*

**Early Wins / Fun and Games (25–40%)**
- **First actions taken:** [WHAT THE HERO TRIES]
- **Small victories:** [EARLY RESULTS — hope builds]
- **New allies or tools:** [WHO OR WHAT HELPS]

**Rising Complications (40–50%)**
- **Complication 1:** [OBSTACLE — something harder than expected]
- **Complication 2:** [ESCALATION — stakes increase]
- **Complication 3:** [THE WORST ONE — seems insurmountable]

**Midpoint — The Shift (50%)**
- **What changes everything?** [REVELATION, REVERSAL, or ESCALATION]
- **Type:** [False victory (things seem great, but...) / False defeat (things seem lost, but...)]
- **New understanding:** [WHAT THE HERO (and audience) NOW SEES DIFFERENTLY]

**Consequences and Fallout (50–65%)**
- **What goes wrong as a result of the midpoint?** [CONSEQUENCES]
- **Internal doubt:** [WHAT THE HERO QUESTIONS]
- **External pressure:** [WHAT CLOSES IN]

**The Low Point (65–75%)**
- **What is the darkest moment?** [ALL IS LOST — the hero faces the worst]
- **What is at risk of being permanently lost?** [STAKES AT MAXIMUM]
- **Key emotion:** [DESPAIR, DOUBT, FEAR]

**Act II Turning Point — The Insight**
- **What does the hero realize?** [THE LESSON — often connects to the flaw from Act I]
- **What new plan emerges?** [THE APPROACH THAT WILL WORK — because the hero has changed]

---

### ACT III — RESOLUTION (75–100%)
*The hero acts on the insight, faces a final test, and the world is transformed.*

**The Final Push (75–90%)**
- **What does the hero do differently this time?** [NEW ACTION — informed by the lesson]
- **What is the final obstacle?** [LAST CHALLENGE]
- **How do they overcome it?** [THE CLIMAX — the decisive moment]

**The Climax (90–95%)**
- **The peak moment:** [THE SCENE that resolves the central question]
- **Value shift:** [WHAT CHANGES — belief, status, capability]
- **Proof of transformation:** [HOW WE KNOW THE HERO HAS CHANGED]

**The New World (95–100%)**
- **Closing image:** [MIRROR OF OPENING — showing the contrast]
- **What's the hero's life like now?** [TRANSFORMED STATUS QUO]
- **What does the audience take away?** [KEY MESSAGE]
- **Call to action (if applicable):** [WHAT SHOULD THE AUDIENCE DO?]

## Example

**ACT I:** Sarah manages content at a 100-person startup. She publishes 4 blog posts a week, but traffic is flat. The CEO says, "If content doesn't drive pipeline by Q3, we're cutting the team." Sarah decides to stop writing about features and start telling customer stories.

**ACT II:** The first customer story gets 3x the engagement of a typical post. (Early win.) She tries to scale it but can't get customers to agree to interviews. (Complication.) She discovers a competitor's case study ranking above them for their own product name. (Midpoint — false defeat.) She nearly gives up, but then finds that a simple story template and a 15-minute interview format get an 80% customer yes rate. (Insight.)

**ACT III:** Sarah publishes 12 customer stories in Q3. Pipeline from content triples. The CEO presents her framework at the board meeting. Closing image: Sarah's team has doubled, and the homepage features a customer story, not a product description.
