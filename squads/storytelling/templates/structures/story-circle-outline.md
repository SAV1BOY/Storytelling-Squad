# Story Circle Outline
> Use this template for any narrative that needs a clean, cyclical structure. Based on Dan Harmon's 8-step Story Circle (adapted from Campbell). Works well for brand stories, product narratives, and customer journeys.

## Instructions
Fill in each of the 8 steps in order. The circle maps to a simple logic: the top half is the known world, the bottom half is the unknown. The hero descends, transforms, and returns changed. Use the prompts to guide each step. Every story should complete the circle.

### The Story Circle

```
        1. YOU (comfort zone)
       /                      \
  8. CHANGE                 2. NEED
     (return,               (something's
      having changed)        missing)
      |                        |
  7. RETURN                 3. GO
     (back to               (cross the
      familiar)              threshold)
      |                        |
  6. TAKE                   4. SEARCH
     (get what              (adapt to
      they needed)           new world)
       \                      /
        5. FIND (the hard way)
```

---

**Step 1 — YOU (A character is in a zone of comfort)**
- **Who is the hero?** [NAME / ROLE / ARCHETYPE]
- **What is their comfort zone?** [DAILY REALITY — routines, beliefs, environment]
- **What does the audience need to know about them?** [KEY CONTEXT]
- **What makes them relatable?** [UNIVERSAL TRAIT or STRUGGLE]

**Step 2 — NEED (But they want something)**
- **What do they want?** [CONSCIOUS DESIRE — the thing they think they need]
- **What do they actually need?** [UNCONSCIOUS NEED — the deeper truth]
- **What signals the need?** [EVENT, FEELING, or REALIZATION that surfaces the want]

**Step 3 — GO (They enter an unfamiliar situation)**
- **What do they do?** [ACTION — the step into the unknown]
- **What's unfamiliar about it?** [NEW TERRITORY — new tool, new approach, new environment]
- **What are they leaving behind?** [OLD WAY, OLD COMFORT]

**Step 4 — SEARCH (They adapt to the new situation)**
- **What challenges do they face?** [OBSTACLES, LEARNING CURVE, RESISTANCE]
- **What small wins do they get?** [EARLY PROGRESS — keeps them going]
- **Who or what helps them?** [ALLIES, TOOLS, MENTORS]

**Step 5 — FIND (They get what they wanted)**
- **What do they find?** [THE THING — solution, answer, result]
- **But at what cost?** [SACRIFICE, HARD LESSON, UNEXPECTED CONSEQUENCE]
- **Why is this the hard way?** [IT'S EARNED, NOT GIVEN]

**Step 6 — TAKE (But they pay a heavy price for it)**
- **What is the true price?** [WHAT THEY HAD TO GIVE UP — old belief, time, comfort, ego]
- **What do they now possess that they didn't before?** [SKILL, INSIGHT, PROOF]
- **How has the experience changed them internally?** [IDENTITY SHIFT]

**Step 7 — RETURN (They go back to where they started)**
- **How do they re-enter the familiar world?** [RETURN TO CONTEXT — same job, same role, same life]
- **What's the same?** [EXTERNAL CONSTANTS]
- **What's different?** [INTERNAL CHANGE — perception, capability, confidence]

**Step 8 — CHANGE (Having fundamentally changed)**
- **How is the hero different from Step 1?** [BEFORE vs. AFTER — specific contrast]
- **What can they do now that they couldn't before?** [NEW CAPABILITY]
- **What is the new normal?** [TRANSFORMED STATUS QUO]
- **Closing image or line:** [MIRROR OF OPENING — showing the change]

## Example

**1. YOU:** Raj is a marketing manager at a 50-person SaaS company. He writes blog posts, manages social, and reports on traffic. He's good at tactics but has never built a strategic narrative.

**2. NEED:** The CEO asks Raj to create a "brand story" for a new product launch. Raj realizes he doesn't know how to tell a story — he knows how to write copy.

**3. GO:** Raj enrolls in a storytelling workshop and starts interviewing customers instead of just writing about features.

**4. SEARCH:** The first two customer interviews are awkward. He asks the wrong questions. One customer gives one-word answers. He almost gives up.

**5. FIND:** On the third interview, a customer tells a story about how the product saved their team 10 hours a week. Raj records it and realizes: the best brand story isn't about the brand.

**6. TAKE:** Raj rewrites the entire launch narrative around the customer's transformation. It means scrapping 3 weeks of work and pushing back the timeline. His CEO is frustrated.

**7. RETURN:** The launch goes live. The customer story outperforms every previous launch email by 3x.

**8. CHANGE:** Raj is still a marketing manager, but now every piece of content starts with a customer story. He's no longer a copywriter — he's a storyteller.
