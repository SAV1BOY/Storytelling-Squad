# Sparkline Outline
> Use this template for presentations, talks, and long-form content that need to build momentum by alternating between current reality and future possibility. Based on Nancy Duarte's Sparkline structure.

## Instructions
The Sparkline alternates between "what is" (current reality) and "what could be" (envisioned future). Each swing increases in emotional intensity until the final "new bliss" — the call to action. Use at least 3 alternating pairs. The contrast between the two states creates tension that pulls the audience forward.

## Example

**Opening:** "Every company says they're customer-centric. Let me show you what that actually looks like — and doesn't."

**What Is #1:** Most support teams measure success by ticket resolution time. Faster = better. But 40% of "resolved" tickets are reopened within a week. (Frustration, 4/10)

**What Could Be #1:** Imagine measuring success by whether the customer's problem actually went away — not just whether the ticket was closed. One company tried this and saw reopen rates drop to 8%. (Hope, 5/10)

**What Is #2:** The reason we optimize for speed is that our tools are built around tickets, not customers. We literally can't see the full picture. (Concern, 6/10)

**What Could Be #2:** A customer-first support platform shows the full history, predicts the next issue, and routes to the right person. Demo: here's what that looks like. (Confidence, 7/10)

**What Is #3:** Companies that don't make this shift lose 15% of their customers annually to "death by a thousand tickets." (Urgency, 8/10)

**What Could Be #3:** Companies that did make the shift saw NPS jump 30 points and support costs drop 20%. Here are three of them. (Conviction, 9/10)

**New Bliss:** "Stop counting tickets. Start keeping customers. Start your pilot today." (Inspiration, 10/10)
