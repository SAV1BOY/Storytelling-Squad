# Story Grid Scene Template
> Use this template to analyze or plan individual scenes within a larger narrative. Based on Shawn Coyne's Story Grid methodology. Essential for ensuring every scene turns and earns its place.

## Instructions
Complete one template per scene. Every scene must have a value shift (something changes from positive to negative or vice versa), a turning point, and a polarity shift. If a scene doesn't turn, it's not a scene — cut it or redesign it. Use this for story editing, storyboarding, or scene-level planning.

## Example

### Scene Identification
- **Story title:** BrightPath Customer Case Study
- **Scene number:** 3
- **Scene name:** "The Dashboard Reveals the Truth"
- **POV character:** Maria Santos, VP Student Success

### Scene Event
- **What happens:** Maria opens the new analytics dashboard for the first time and discovers that their most popular course has the highest student churn.

### Value Shift
- **Core value:** Competence
- **Value at start:** Competence + (Maria feels confident about the new tool)
- **Value at end:** Competence − (Maria realizes her team's flagship course is the problem)
- **Polarity shift:** + to −

### Five Commandments
**1. Inciting Incident:** Maria runs her first real report on the dashboard during a team meeting.
**2. Progressive Complications:** The data shows churn is higher than expected. Then it shows the highest churn is in their best-rated course. The turning point: the data pinpoints the exact module where students drop off — Module 4, the one Maria personally designed.
**3. Crisis:** Best bad choice — present this data to the CEO now (and admit her module is the problem) or wait and try to fix it quietly (risking another bad quarter).
**4. Climax:** Maria presents the data to the CEO in the same meeting. She says, "Module 4 is the leak. I built it. I'll fix it."
**5. Resolution:** The CEO is surprised but impressed. Gives Maria two weeks and a designer to rebuild Module 4.
