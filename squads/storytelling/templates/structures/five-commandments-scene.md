# Five Commandments Scene Template
> Use this template to ensure any story unit — scene, chapter, case study, pitch section — contains all five essential elements of a working story. Based on Robert McKee and Shawn Coyne's Five Commandments.

## Instructions
Apply this to any narrative unit. If any of the five elements is missing, the story unit will feel flat. This template works at every level: a single scene, an entire article, a pitch, or a campaign. Fill in sequentially. If you can't identify the crisis, the story doesn't have real tension yet.

## Template Body

### Story Unit Identification
- **Parent story:** [TITLE of larger work]
- **Unit type:** [Scene / Chapter / Section / Full piece]
- **Unit name:** [DESCRIPTIVE NAME]
- **Characters involved:** [WHO]
- **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

---

### 1. Inciting Incident
*The event that upsets the balance and sets the unit in motion.*

- **What happens?** [THE EVENT — be specific]
- **Type:** [Causal (caused by a character's action) / Coincidental (external event)]
- **Balance disrupted:** [WHAT WAS STABLE that is now unstable]
- **Question raised:** [WHAT THE AUDIENCE NOW WANTS TO KNOW]

### 2. Progressive Complications
*Things get harder. Each complication raises the stakes.*

- **Complication 1:** [OBSTACLE or REVELATION]
- **Complication 2:** [ESCALATION — stakes increase]
- **Complication 3:** [FURTHER ESCALATION]
- **Turning Point:** [THE COMPLICATION that forces a crisis — the moment of no return]
  - **Turning point type:** [Action (something happens) / Revelation (something is learned)]

### 3. Crisis
*The character faces an impossible choice. This is the heart of the story.*

- **Crisis type:** [Best bad choice / Irreconcilable goods]
- **Option A:** [CHOICE] → [CONSEQUENCE if chosen]
- **Option B:** [CHOICE] → [CONSEQUENCE if chosen]
- **Why is this hard?** [WHAT MAKES NEITHER OPTION EASY]
- **What values are in conflict?** [e.g., safety vs. growth, honesty vs. loyalty, speed vs. quality]

### 4. Climax
*The character chooses. The choice is shown through action, not dialogue.*

- **What does the character do?** [THE ACTION TAKEN]
- **What does this reveal about them?** [CHARACTER TRUTH — what we learn about who they really are]
- **How does the audience feel?** [EMOTIONAL RESPONSE — relief, surprise, admiration, tension]

### 5. Resolution
*The new normal. The dust settles. The value has shifted.*

- **New state:** [WHAT'S DIFFERENT NOW — situation, relationship, belief]
- **Value shift:** [FROM → TO — e.g., "despair → cautious hope"]
- **Hook forward:** [WHAT QUESTION OR TENSION carries into the next unit]
- **Proof / evidence (if non-fiction):** [DATA, QUOTE, RESULT that validates the resolution]

---

### Quality Check
- [ ] The inciting incident is specific and disrupts a clear status quo
- [ ] Complications escalate (each is harder than the last)
- [ ] The crisis is a genuine dilemma (not an obvious choice)
- [ ] The climax is an action, not a statement
- [ ] The resolution shifts the value and sets up what comes next
- [ ] This unit would be missed if removed from the larger story

## Example

### Story Unit: "The Pitch That Almost Wasn't" (Section 2 of Founder Story)

**1. Inciting Incident:** Three days before the biggest pitch of her career, Lena's co-founder sends a text: "I'm out. I don't believe in this anymore." (Causal — a character's choice disrupts the balance.)

**2. Progressive Complications:**
- Complication 1: The pitch deck has both their names on every slide. Rewriting means starting from scratch.
- Complication 2: Two of their three reference customers were the co-founder's relationships. She can't use them.
- Turning Point (Revelation): While reviewing old customer emails, Lena finds an unsolicited testimonial from a customer she'd forgotten — a small nonprofit that credits the product with doubling their volunteer retention.

**3. Crisis:** Best bad choice — ask the investors to postpone (looks weak, may lose the slot) or pitch alone with half the team and an untested story (may fail publicly).

**4. Climax:** Lena rebuilds the deck overnight around the nonprofit's story. She walks into the pitch alone, opens with: "Three days ago, my co-founder left. Here's why I'm still standing here."

**5. Resolution:** She doesn't close the round that day, but two investors ask for follow-up meetings. One says, "That was the most honest pitch I've heard this year." Lena realizes the company's story was always about resilience — the product, the customers, and now the founder.
