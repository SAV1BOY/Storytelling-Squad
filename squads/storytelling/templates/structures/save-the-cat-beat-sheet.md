# Save the Cat Beat Sheet
> Use this template for tightly paced narratives — video scripts, keynotes, brand films, or any story that needs precise structural timing. Based on Blake Snyder's 15-beat structure.

## Instructions
Fill in each beat in order. The percentage markers indicate where each beat should fall relative to total runtime or word count. For a 10-minute video, "Catalyst at 10%" means it should happen around minute 1. Adapt percentages for your format. The beats are non-negotiable in order; their exact timing is flexible.

- **Total length:** [MINUTES or WORD COUNT]
- **Genre / type:** [e.g., customer transformation, origin story, product launch]
- **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

### Beat Sheet

**Beat 1 — Opening Image (0%)**
*The "before" snapshot. Show the world as it is.*
- **What we see/read:** [OPENING SCENE or IMAGE]
- **Tone:** [MOOD — establishes emotional baseline]
- **Purpose:** Set up the contrast with the Final Image.

**Beat 2 — Theme Stated (5%)**
*Someone states the lesson the hero will learn, though they don't know it yet.*
- **Theme line:** "[QUOTE or STATEMENT — the story's deeper truth]"
- **Who says it:** [CHARACTER or NARRATOR]

**Beat 3 — Set-Up (1–10%)**
*Introduce the hero, their world, and what needs fixing.*
- **Hero:** [WHO — and why we care about them]
- **World:** [STATUS QUO — daily life, environment]
- **Stakes planted:** [WHAT'S AT RISK — hinted, not stated]
- **"Save the Cat" moment:** [THE MOMENT that makes the audience root for the hero]

**Beat 4 — Catalyst (10%)**
*The event that changes everything.*
- **What happens:** [INCITING INCIDENT]
- **Impact:** [HOW IT DISRUPTS THE STATUS QUO]

**Beat 5 — Debate (10–25%)**
*The hero wrestles with the choice. Should I act or stay safe?*
- **Internal debate:** [WHAT HOLDS THEM BACK]
- **External pressure:** [WHAT PUSHES THEM FORWARD]
- **Question posed:** "Should I [ACTION]?"

**Beat 6 — Break into Two (25%)**
*The hero makes a choice and enters the new world. Act II begins.*
- **Decision:** [WHAT THEY CHOOSE TO DO]
- **New world:** [WHAT CHANGES — environment, mindset, stakes]

**Beat 7 — B Story (30%)**
*A secondary thread — often a relationship — that carries the theme.*
- **B Story character:** [WHO — mentor, partner, ally]
- **Relationship dynamic:** [HOW THEY INTERACT WITH THE HERO]
- **Thematic connection:** [HOW THIS THREAD MIRRORS THE MAIN LESSON]

**Beat 8 — Fun and Games (30–50%)**
*The promise of the premise. The audience gets what they came for.*
- **Key scenes / moments:**
  1. [SCENE — the hero doing the thing]
  2. [SCENE — early wins or discoveries]
  3. [SCENE — the "trailer moments"]
- **Tone:** [ENERGETIC, EXPLORATORY, ENGAGING]

**Beat 9 — Midpoint (50%)**
*A false victory or false defeat. Stakes escalate.*
- **What happens:** [EVENT — seems like a win or a loss, but it's not over]
- **Stakes raised:** [NEW INFORMATION that changes the game]
- **Type:** [False victory / False defeat]

**Beat 10 — Bad Guys Close In (50–75%)**
*External pressures mount. Internal doubts grow.*
- **External threat:** [WHAT GETS WORSE]
- **Internal doubt:** [WHAT THE HERO STARTS TO QUESTION]
- **Team fracture (if applicable):** [ALLIES WAVER or DISAGREE]

**Beat 11 — All Is Lost (75%)**
*The lowest point. The opposite of the Midpoint.*
- **What happens:** [THE WORST MOMENT]
- **"Whiff of death":** [WHAT IS LOST — metaphorical or literal]

**Beat 12 — Dark Night of the Soul (75–80%)**
*The hero processes the loss. Despair before insight.*
- **Hero's internal state:** [DEFEAT, DOUBT, GRIEF]
- **Key question:** "Was this all for nothing?"

**Beat 13 — Break into Three (80%)**
*Inspiration strikes. The hero finds the answer — often from the B Story.*
- **Insight:** [WHAT THE HERO REALIZES — connects A and B stories]
- **New plan:** [WHAT THEY DECIDE TO DO DIFFERENTLY]

**Beat 14 — Finale (80–99%)**
*The hero executes the new plan. The story climaxes.*
- **Action:** [WHAT THE HERO DOES]
- **Confrontation:** [THE FINAL CHALLENGE]
- **Resolution:** [HOW IT ENDS — the climax payoff]
- **Proof of change:** [HOW THE HERO DEMONSTRATES TRANSFORMATION]

**Beat 15 — Final Image (100%)**
*The "after" snapshot. Mirror of the Opening Image, showing change.*
- **What we see/read:** [CLOSING SCENE or IMAGE]
- **Contrast with opening:** [WHAT'S DIFFERENT NOW]

## Example

**Opening Image:** A founder sitting alone at a kitchen table at 2 AM, staring at a rejected loan application.
**Catalyst:** A mentor says, "Stop selling a product. Start telling a story."
**Midpoint (False Victory):** The startup's first pitch with a story-driven deck gets a standing ovation — but the investor passes.
**All Is Lost:** Co-founder quits. Runway is 6 weeks.
**Break into Three:** The founder rewrites the pitch around the customer's story, not the product.
**Final Image:** The same kitchen table, but now there's a signed term sheet on it and a team on a video call celebrating.
