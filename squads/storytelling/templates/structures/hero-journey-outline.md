# Hero's Journey Outline
> Use this template to structure any long-form narrative using Joseph Campbell's monomyth. Best for brand stories, founder journeys, keynote narratives, and customer transformation arcs.

## Instructions
Fill in each stage sequentially. Not every story needs all 12 stages — mark stages as [N/A] if they don't apply. The key structural beats are: Ordinary World, Call to Adventure, Ordeal, and Return with the Elixir. Reference Campbell's monomyth and Vogler's Writer's Journey adaptation.

## Template Body

### Story Metadata
- **Story title:** [TITLE]
- **Hero (protagonist):** [WHO — customer, founder, user, community]
- **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

### ACT I — DEPARTURE

**1. The Ordinary World**
- Where is the hero before the story begins?
- **Setting:** [WHERE and WHEN]
- **Hero's status quo:** [DAILY LIFE, ROUTINES, BELIEFS]
- **What's missing or flawed?** [THE UNSPOKEN NEED]
- **Emotional state:** [HOW THE HERO FEELS — comfortable but incomplete]

**2. The Call to Adventure**
- What disrupts the hero's world?
- **Inciting event:** [WHAT HAPPENS — problem, opportunity, discovery]
- **What is at stake?** [WHAT THE HERO STANDS TO GAIN OR LOSE]
- **Emotional state:** [EXCITEMENT, FEAR, CURIOSITY]

**3. Refusal of the Call**
- Why does the hero hesitate?
- **Objection / fear:** [WHAT HOLDS THEM BACK]
- **What would happen if they refused?** [COST OF INACTION]

**4. Meeting the Mentor**
- Who or what gives the hero what they need to proceed?
- **Mentor:** [PERSON, RESOURCE, INSIGHT, BRAND, PRODUCT]
- **Gift / tool / wisdom:** [WHAT THE MENTOR PROVIDES]
- **How does this change the hero's belief?** [SHIFT]

**5. Crossing the Threshold**
- The hero commits to the journey.
- **Decision moment:** [WHAT THEY DECIDE TO DO]
- **Point of no return:** [WHY THEY CAN'T GO BACK]
- **New world entered:** [WHAT'S DIFFERENT NOW]

### ACT II — INITIATION

**6. Tests, Allies, and Enemies**
- The hero faces challenges and meets helpers.
- **Test 1:** [CHALLENGE + HOW THEY RESPOND]
- **Test 2:** [CHALLENGE + HOW THEY RESPOND]
- **Allies gained:** [WHO HELPS ALONG THE WAY]
- **Enemies / obstacles:** [WHO OR WHAT OPPOSES THEM]

**7. Approach to the Inmost Cave**
- The hero prepares for the central ordeal.
- **What is the "cave"?** [THE BIGGEST CHALLENGE AHEAD]
- **Preparation:** [WHAT THE HERO DOES TO GET READY]
- **Rising tension:** [WHAT MAKES THIS FEEL HIGH-STAKES]

**8. The Ordeal**
- The hero faces the supreme challenge.
- **Central crisis:** [THE DARKEST MOMENT — what almost breaks them]
- **What is "dying" here?** [OLD BELIEF, OLD IDENTITY, OLD WAY]
- **Emotional low point:** [HOW IT FEELS]

**9. The Reward**
- The hero survives and gains something.
- **What was gained?** [KNOWLEDGE, TOOL, PROOF, TRANSFORMATION]
- **How does the hero feel?** [RELIEF, PRIDE, CLARITY]
- **But is it over?** [HINT AT REMAINING CHALLENGE]

---

### ACT III — RETURN

**10. The Road Back**
- The hero begins the return, but faces consequences.
- **New challenge:** [WHAT THREATENS THE REWARD]
- **Choice:** [WHAT DECISION MUST THEY MAKE?]

**11. The Resurrection**
- The hero is tested one final time and transformed.
- **Final test:** [THE LAST OBSTACLE]
- **Transformation proof:** [HOW THE HERO SHOWS THEY'VE CHANGED]
- **New identity:** [WHO THE HERO IS NOW]

**12. Return with the Elixir**
- The hero returns to the ordinary world, changed.
- **What they bring back:** [THE GIFT — insight, product, result, story]
- **How the world is different:** [IMPACT ON OTHERS]
- **Closing image:** [FINAL MOMENT — contrast with opening]

## Example

### ACT I
**1. Ordinary World:** Maria runs student success at BrightPath. She spends every Monday morning wrestling 3 spreadsheets to figure out which students are falling behind. By the time she identifies them, it's too late.

**2. Call to Adventure:** Student churn hits 22%. The CEO tells Maria: "Fix this or we cut the program."

**4. Meeting the Mentor:** Maria attends a webinar where she sees a platform that predicts at-risk students in real time. She thinks, "This could change everything."

**5. Crossing the Threshold:** Maria signs up for a trial, despite her team's skepticism about "another tool."

### ACT II
**8. The Ordeal:** Two weeks in, the data reveals that their most popular course has the highest churn. The team panics — their flagship is the problem.

**9. The Reward:** But the data also shows exactly which module causes drop-off. They redesign it in a week.

### ACT III
**12. Return with the Elixir:** Six months later, churn is at 11%. Maria presents at a conference: "We stopped guessing and started listening to the data."
