# StoryBrand BrandScript
> Use this template to create a complete BrandScript using Donald Miller's StoryBrand SB7 framework. The BrandScript is the source document for all brand messaging — website copy, emails, pitches, and campaigns flow from it.

## Instructions
Fill in all 7 elements in order. The customer is always the hero; the brand is always the guide. If you write the brand as the hero, start over. Test the completed BrandScript by reading it as a story: "A [hero] who wants [desire] has a [problem]. They meet a [guide] who gives them a [plan] and calls them to [action]. That action helps them [avoid failure] and ends in [success]."

### SB7 Element 1 — A CHARACTER (The Hero)
*The customer is the hero, not your brand.*

- **Who is the hero?** [CUSTOMER — role, identity, situation]
- **What do they want?** [DESIRE — one clear, specific thing related to your offering]
- **How would they say it?** "[DESIRE IN THEIR OWN WORDS]"
- **Can you state it simply?** [TEST: would a caveman understand what they want?]

### SB7 Element 2 — HAS A PROBLEM
*Define the villain and the three levels of problem it creates.*

- **The Villain:** [THE ROOT CAUSE of the hero's frustration — a thing, force, or condition. Not a person.]
  - Villain characteristics: [Is it relatable? Evil? Real? Singular?]

- **External Problem:** [THE TANGIBLE PROBLEM — what the villain causes that the hero can see/measure]
- **Internal Problem:** [THE FEELING — how the external problem makes the hero feel]
- **Philosophical Problem:** "It's wrong that [STATEMENT ABOUT HOW THINGS SHOULDN'T BE]."
  - This should be a statement of justice or fairness.

### SB7 Element 3 — AND MEETS A GUIDE
*Your brand is the guide. You show empathy and authority.*

- **Empathy statement:** "We understand [WHAT THE HERO IS GOING THROUGH]."
  - Show you get their pain: [SPECIFIC EMPATHETIC LANGUAGE]
- **Authority markers:**
  1. [PROOF POINT — testimonials, stats, logos, awards]
  2. [PROOF POINT]
  3. [PROOF POINT]

### SB7 Element 4 — WHO GIVES THEM A PLAN
*The plan removes confusion and risk. Give them simple steps.*

- **Process Plan (3 steps):**
  1. [STEP 1 — what they do first] — [SHORT LABEL]
  2. [STEP 2 — what happens next] — [SHORT LABEL]
  3. [STEP 3 — the result they get] — [SHORT LABEL]

- **Agreement Plan (optional):** [LIST OF PROMISES or GUARANTEES that reduce risk]
  - [PROMISE 1]
  - [PROMISE 2]
  - [PROMISE 3]

### SB7 Element 5 — AND CALLS THEM TO ACTION
*Be direct. Tell them what to do.*

- **Direct CTA:** [PRIMARY BUTTON / ACTION — e.g., "Start Free Trial," "Schedule a Call," "Buy Now"]
- **Transitional CTA:** [LOWER-COMMITMENT OPTION — e.g., "Download the Guide," "Watch the Demo," "Take the Quiz"]

### SB7 Element 6 — THAT HELPS THEM AVOID FAILURE
*What bad things happen if they don't act? Paint the stakes.*

- **Failure 1:** [NEGATIVE OUTCOME if they do nothing]
- **Failure 2:** [NEGATIVE OUTCOME]
- **Failure 3:** [NEGATIVE OUTCOME]
- **What does failure feel like?** [EMOTIONAL COST]

### SB7 Element 7 — AND ENDS IN SUCCESS
*Paint a vivid picture of life after engaging with your brand.*

- **Success 1:** [POSITIVE OUTCOME — what they achieve]
- **Success 2:** [POSITIVE OUTCOME — how they feel]
- **Success 3:** [POSITIVE OUTCOME — what others notice]
- **Transformation:** The hero goes from [BEFORE IDENTITY] to [AFTER IDENTITY].
- **One-sentence success vision:** "[VIVID DESCRIPTION of their new reality]"

---

### BrandScript Summary (Read as a Story)
> A [HERO] wants [DESIRE]. The problem is [VILLAIN/EXTERNAL PROBLEM], which makes them feel [INTERNAL PROBLEM]. It's wrong that [PHILOSOPHICAL PROBLEM]. They discover [BRAND/GUIDE], who understands [EMPATHY] and has [AUTHORITY]. The guide gives them a plan: [STEP 1], [STEP 2], [STEP 3], and calls them to [CTA]. By acting, they avoid [FAILURE] and achieve [SUCCESS], transforming from [BEFORE] to [AFTER].

## Example

**1. Character:** A VP of Product at a mid-market SaaS company who wants to understand which features drive retention.
**2. Problem:** Villain: fragmented data tools. External: can't get answers without a data team. Internal: feels like they're guessing. Philosophical: "It's wrong that product leaders have to fly blind."
**3. Guide:** "We've been product leaders too — we know the frustration of waiting for insights." Authority: Trusted by 200+ SaaS companies. G2 Leader. Average time-to-insight: 4 days.
**4. Plan:** 1) Connect your data (10 min). 2) Ask a question in plain English. 3) Get a visual answer with recommendations.
**5. CTA:** Direct: "Start Free Trial." Transitional: "See a 3-min demo."
**6. Failure:** Keeps guessing on feature priorities. Loses customers to churn they didn't see coming. Falls behind competitors who are data-driven.
**7. Success:** Makes confident feature decisions. Cuts churn by double digits. Becomes the data-informed leader their team respects.
