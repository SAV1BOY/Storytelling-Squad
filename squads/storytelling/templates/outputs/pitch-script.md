# Pitch Script
> Use this template to write a structured pitch script for investor, sales, partnership, or conference pitches. Based on Oren Klaff's Pitch Anything methodology — frame control, storytelling, intrigue, and prize positioning.

## Instructions
Fill in each section before rehearsing. The script should be spoken aloud, not read — write for the ear, not the eye. Time each section against your total pitch window. The presenter should own the frame from the first sentence to the last.

### Section 1 — Set the Frame (10% of time)
*Take control of the room. Establish how this pitch will be experienced.*

- **Opening line:** "[FIRST WORDS — confident, unexpected, human]"
- **Frame type:** [Power frame / Time frame / Analyst frame / Prize frame]
- **Frame-setting statement:** [WHAT ESTABLISHES YOUR AUTHORITY AND CONTEXT]
- **Transition to story:** [ONE SENTENCE that bridges from frame to narrative]

### Section 2 — Tell the Story (30% of time)
*Deliver the narrative that makes the audience feel the problem and see the opportunity.*

- **The world as it is:** [STATUS QUO — paint the current reality vividly]
- **The specific victim:** [WHO SUFFERS — a real person or archetype, not an abstraction]
- **The pain moment:** [SCENE — describe a specific moment of frustration or failure]
- **The insight:** [WHAT YOU REALIZED — the "aha" that started everything]
- **The solution (high level):** [WHAT YOU BUILT / DID — one sentence]
- **The result:** [PROOF — metric, outcome, transformation]
- **Story paragraph:** [WRITE 4-6 SENTENCES as you would speak them]

### Section 3 — Create Intrigue (20% of time)
*Reveal something the audience doesn't expect. Make them want to know more.*

- **Surprising data point:** [STAT THAT CHALLENGES ASSUMPTIONS]
- **Counterintuitive insight:** [SOMETHING THE AUDIENCE BELIEVES IS TRUE BUT ISN'T]
- **Competitive gap:** [WHAT EVERYONE ELSE MISSED — and why you see it]
- **Demo or proof moment:** [WHAT YOU SHOW — live demo, before/after, customer video]
- **Intrigue question:** "[QUESTION that makes the audience lean forward]"

### Section 4 — Offer the Prize (20% of time)
*Position the opportunity as a prize, not a plea. The audience should feel lucky to be in the room.*

- **What you're offering:** [THE OPPORTUNITY — deal, product, partnership]
- **Why it's scarce:** [LIMITED AVAILABILITY — time, seats, capacity, round closing]
- **Social proof:** [WHO ELSE IS IN — logos, names, investors, customers]
- **Prize statement:** "[WE'RE SELECTIVE ABOUT WHO WE WORK WITH BECAUSE...]"
- **What the audience gets:** [VALUE TO THEM — ROI, access, advantage, bragging rights]

### Section 5 — The Hookpoint and Decision (15% of time)
*Drive to a clear decision. Remove ambiguity about next steps.*

- **Summary (3 sentences max):** [RECAP — problem, solution, proof]
- **The ask:** "[EXACTLY WHAT YOU WANT — investment, meeting, pilot, approval]"
- **Urgency:** [WHY DECIDE NOW]
- **Easy next step:** [WHAT THEY DO IMMEDIATELY AFTER SAYING YES]
- **Closing line:** "[FINAL WORDS — confident, memorable, forward-looking]"

## Example

**Frame:** "I have 15 minutes. In that time, I'm going to show you why 70% of ML models never reach production — and what we built to fix it."

**Story:** "Last year, a VP of Engineering at a Fortune 500 company told me his team had spent 18 months and $2M building an ML model to predict customer churn. It was brilliant. It worked perfectly in a notebook. It never made it to production. His exact words: 'We built a sports car and left it in the garage.' That's not an anomaly. That's the industry."

**Intrigue:** "What if I told you the bottleneck isn't the model — it's the last mile? We've reduced deployment from months to hours. Here's a live demo."

**Prize:** "We're closing our Series A in three weeks. The round is 70% committed. We're being selective because our next investors become partners in a category-defining infrastructure company."

**Decision:** "I'm asking for a follow-up meeting this week to walk through the financials. Are you in?"
