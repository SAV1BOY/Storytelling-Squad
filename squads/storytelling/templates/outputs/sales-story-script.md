# Sales Story Script
> Use this template to build a narrative-driven sales conversation. Instead of leading with features, lead with the prospect's pain, bridge with empathy, deliver proof through story, and close with a clear CTA.

## Instructions
Customize for each prospect or prospect segment. The sales rep should internalize the structure, not read it verbatim. The goal is a conversation that feels like a story the prospect recognizes themselves in. Reference the StoryBrand framework (customer as hero, brand as guide).

### Part 1 — The Prospect's Pain (Open with Empathy)
*Start with their world, not yours. Show you understand the problem before offering the fix.*

- **Opening question:** "[QUESTION that surfaces the pain — e.g., 'How are you currently tracking X?']"
- **Expected pain response:** [WHAT THEY'RE LIKELY TO SAY]
- **Empathy bridge:** "I hear that a lot. Most [ROLE] we talk to are dealing with [SHARED PAIN]."
- **Validate the frustration:** "[SPECIFIC STATEMENT that shows you get it — not generic sympathy]"
- **Pain quantified:** [STAT or BENCHMARK that makes the pain tangible — "Companies like yours typically lose X hours/dollars on this"]

### Part 2 — The Story (Bridge from Pain to Possibility)
*Tell a customer story that mirrors the prospect's situation. Specific beats pain; generic loses trust.*

- **Setup:** "We worked with [CUSTOMER — name or description], who was in a similar situation..."
- **Their before state:** [1-2 SENTENCES — what their world looked like before]
- **Their trigger:** [WHAT MADE THEM ACT — the breaking point]
- **What they did:** [HOW THEY ENGAGED WITH YOUR SOLUTION — keep it simple]
- **The turning point:** [THE MOMENT THEY KNEW IT WAS WORKING]
- **The result:** [PRIMARY METRIC — specific, time-bound]
- **Customer quote (optional):** "[THEIR WORDS — makes it feel real, not scripted]"

### Part 3 — The Solution (Position as the Plan)
*Now — and only now — introduce your solution. Frame it as the plan the prospect needs.*

- **Transition:** "Based on what you've shared, here's what we'd recommend..."
- **Step 1:** [FIRST THING THEY DO — simple, low-friction]
- **Step 2:** [WHAT HAPPENS NEXT — what the product/service does]
- **Step 3:** [THE RESULT THEY GET — tied to their specific pain]
- **Why this works:** [ONE SENTENCE — the differentiator]

### Part 4 — The Proof (Stack the Evidence)
*Layer additional proof to build confidence.*

- **Metric 1:** [RESULT from another customer]
- **Metric 2:** [RESULT — different dimension, same credibility]
- **Social proof:** [LOGOS, NAME-DROPS, AWARDS — as appropriate]
- **Risk reducer:** [GUARANTEE, FREE TRIAL, PILOT OFFER — whatever lowers the barrier]

### Part 5 — The CTA (Close with Clarity)
*Be direct. Tell them what to do next.*

- **Direct ask:** "[SPECIFIC NEXT STEP — 'Let's set up a pilot' / 'I'll send the agreement' / 'Can we schedule a demo for your team?']"
- **Urgency (if genuine):** [WHY ACT NOW — limited offer, upcoming deadline, competitive pressure]
- **Fallback ask:** "[LOWER-COMMITMENT ALTERNATIVE — 'At minimum, let me send you the case study to share with your team']"
- **Objection preempt:** "The most common concern I hear is [OBJECTION]. Here's how we handle that: [ANSWER]."

### Objection Handling
| Objection | Response | Proof |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| "We're not ready yet" | [RESPONSE] | [EVIDENCE] |
| "It's too expensive" | [RESPONSE — reframe as cost of inaction] | [ROI DATA] |
| "We've tried something like this before" | [RESPONSE — differentiate] | [CUSTOMER EXAMPLE] |
| "I need to talk to my team" | [RESPONSE — offer to present to them] | [OFFER] |
| "How is this different from [COMPETITOR]?" | [RESPONSE] | [COMPARISON POINT] |

### Follow-Up Sequence
| Day | Action | Content |
|-----|--------|---------|
| Day 0 | Post-call email | [RECAP + NEXT STEP + CASE STUDY LINK] |
| Day 3 | Value add | [RELEVANT ARTICLE or RESOURCE] |
| Day 7 | Check-in | [BRIEF NOTE — reference their specific pain] |
| Day 14 | Last touch | [FINAL OUTREACH — new proof or urgency] |

## Example

**Pain:** "How are you currently identifying which features drive retention?" — "We wait for the data team to run a query. Takes about a week." — "That's really common. Most product leaders we talk to are making sprint decisions without the data they need. One told us she was 'building in the dark.'"

**Story:** "We worked with a VP of Product at a 200-person SaaS company — similar size to yours. She was spending 4 hours every Monday waiting for a report that was already outdated. She started a trial with us, and by day 3, she'd answered a question her data team had been working on for two weeks. Six months in, her team's time-to-insight went from 5 days to 4 minutes."

**CTA:** "Based on what you've described, I think a 14-day trial would show you the difference in the first week. Can I set that up for you today?"
