# Email Sequence Narrative
> Use this template to plan a 5-7 email sequence where each email advances a narrative arc. Instead of disconnected blasts, the sequence tells a story that builds toward a decision. Works for launches, nurture sequences, onboarding, and re-engagement.

## Instructions
Plan the full arc before writing any single email. Each email must stand alone (not everyone opens every email) but also advance the story for those who read them all. Use the Three-Act structure across the sequence: Emails 1-2 are Act I (setup + problem), Emails 3-5 are Act II (proof + tension), Emails 6-7 are Act III (resolution + CTA).

### Email 1 — The Hook
*Open the story loop. Make them curious enough to open Email 2.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 1]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — curiosity or pain-driven]
- **Preview text:** [PREVIEW — extends the subject line]
- **Opening line:** "[FIRST SENTENCE — story opening, question, or bold claim]"
- **Body outline:**
  - [INTRODUCE THE PROBLEM or TELL A SHORT STORY — 3-4 sentences]
  - [HINT AT THE SOLUTION without revealing it fully]
  - [PLANT A QUESTION the reader will want answered]
- **CTA:** [SOFT CTA — read more, reply, explore]
- **Closing line:** "[TEASER for next email — optional]"
- **Word count target:** [150-250 words]

### Email 2 — The Problem
*Deepen the pain. Make them feel understood.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 3]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — empathy or "me too" framing]
- **Opening line:** "[CONNECT to Email 1 — 'Remember when I mentioned...' or cold open]"
- **Body outline:**
  - [EXPAND THE PROBLEM — data, story, or scenario that makes it vivid]
  - [VALIDATE THEIR EXPERIENCE — "If you've ever felt X, you're not alone"]
  - [INTRODUCE THE COST of not solving this]
- **CTA:** [TRANSITIONAL CTA — download guide, take quiz, watch video]
- **Word count target:** [200-300 words]

### Email 3 — The Proof
*Show that the transformation is possible. Lead with a customer story.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 5]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — result-driven, uses a number]
- **Opening line:** "[CUSTOMER STORY HOOK — 'Last year, [NAME] was in the same spot you are...']"
- **Body outline:**
  - [TELL THE CUSTOMER STORY — before, trigger, after — 4-6 sentences]
  - [HIGHLIGHT THE KEY METRIC]
  - [CONNECT their story to the reader's situation]
- **CTA:** [DIRECT CTA — try it, sign up, book a call]
- **Word count target:** [200-300 words]

### Email 4 — The Deeper Truth
*Challenge an assumption. Provide an insight they haven't heard.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 8]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — counterintuitive or myth-busting]
- **Opening line:** "[CHALLENGE A BELIEF — 'Most people think X. Here's what we found...']"
- **Body outline:**
  - [PRESENT THE INSIGHT — what most people get wrong about this problem]
  - [SUPPORT WITH DATA or a second customer story]
  - [REFRAME the problem in a way that makes the solution obvious]
- **CTA:** [DIRECT CTA — stronger than before]
- **Word count target:** [200-300 words]

### Email 5 — The Turning Point
*Create urgency. This is the "now or never" narrative beat.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 10]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — urgency or scarcity, but honest]
- **Opening line:** "[STATE THE STAKES — what happens if they don't act]"
- **Body outline:**
  - [PAINT THE COST OF INACTION — specific scenario]
  - [CONTRAST WITH THE TRANSFORMATION — what acting looks like]
  - [INTRODUCE URGENCY — deadline, limited offer, closing window]
- **CTA:** [DIRECT CTA — clear, urgent]
- **Word count target:** [150-250 words]

### Email 6 — The Resolution
*Resolve the story. Show the full picture and make the ask.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 12]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — resolution framing, outcome-focused]
- **Opening line:** "[CALLBACK to the story from Email 1]"
- **Body outline:**
  - [RESOLVE THE OPENING STORY — what happened, how it ended]
  - [SUMMARIZE THE TRANSFORMATION — before vs. after]
  - [STACK ALL PROOF — metrics, quotes, logos]
  - [MAKE THE FINAL ASK — clearly, confidently]
- **CTA:** [STRONG DIRECT CTA — last primary push]
- **Word count target:** [200-300 words]

### Email 7 — The Last Chance (Optional)
*Final touch. Short, personal, direct.*

- **Send day:** [DAY 14]
- **Subject line:** [SUBJECT — personal, short — e.g., "quick question" or "closing the loop"]
- **Opening line:** "[PERSONAL TONE — feels like a 1:1 note, not a marketing email]"
- **Body outline:**
  - [ACKNOWLEDGE this is the last email on this topic]
  - [RESTATE THE SINGLE MOST COMPELLING PROOF POINT]
  - [OFFER HELP — "reply to this email if you have questions"]
- **CTA:** [FINAL CTA or GRACEFUL EXIT]
- **Word count target:** [75-150 words]

## Example

**Email 1 (Hook):** Subject: "She spent 4 hours every Monday on a report no one read." Body opens with Maria's spreadsheet story. Ends: "Tomorrow, I'll tell you what she did about it."

**Email 3 (Proof):** Subject: "22% to 11%: How BrightPath cut student churn in half." Full customer story with the key metric and a quote.

**Email 5 (Turning Point):** Subject: "What's your Monday morning costing you?" Quantifies the cost of manual reporting — $50K/year in lost productivity. CTA: "Start your free trial before Friday."

**Email 7 (Last Chance):** Subject: "One last thing." 3 sentences. "If any of these emails resonated, I'd love to help. Reply and tell me what you're working on."
