# First Last Best Worst Exercise

## Exercise Name
First, Last, Best, Worst

## Purpose
A rapid story discovery exercise developed by Matthew Dicks that unlocks personal stories by using simple prompts to access specific memories. The framework works because "first," "last," "best," and "worst" are emotional superlatives — they force the brain to retrieve vivid, specific moments rather than generic recollections. This exercise reliably produces 5-10 story candidates in under 20 minutes.

## Duration
- **Solo version:** 15-20 minutes
- **Group version:** 30-45 minutes (includes sharing and discussion)

## Materials Needed
- This worksheet (printed or digital — one per participant)
- Pen or pencil
- Timer
- A topic list (provided below, or create your own)

## Instructions

### Setup (2 minutes)
1. Choose a topic from the list below or generate one relevant to your team's work.
2. Draw a simple 2x2 grid on paper (or use the template below). Label the quadrants: First, Last, Best, Worst.

### Solo Writing (10-15 minutes)
3. Set a timer for 3 minutes per quadrant.
4. For each quadrant, write down the memory that comes to mind for that topic. Write the specific moment — not a summary, but the scene. Where were you? Who was there? What happened? What did you feel?
5. Do not edit. Do not judge. Capture the memory as it surfaces.

### Template Grid

**Topic:** [CHOSEN TOPIC]

| | First | Last |
|---|---|---|
| **Best** | [FIRST + BEST memory of this topic] | [LAST + BEST memory of this topic] |
| **Worst** | [FIRST + WORST memory of this topic] | [LAST + WORST memory of this topic] |

Additionally, answer each individually:

- **First:** [THE FIRST TIME you experienced this topic — what do you remember?]
- **Last:** [THE MOST RECENT TIME you experienced this topic — what happened?]
- **Best:** [THE BEST experience you have had with this topic — describe the moment]
- **Worst:** [THE WORST experience you have had with this topic — describe the moment]

### Suggested Topic List
- Car
- Pet
- Job
- Boss
- Teacher
- Trip
- Gift
- Meal
- Argument
- Compliment
- Failure
- Customer interaction
- Product launch
- Team meeting
- Presentation
- Feedback received
- Sale closed / sale lost
- Deadline
- Mentor
- Risk taken

### Evaluation (5 minutes)
6. Review your four entries. For each, ask: Is there a moment of change here? Did something shift — a belief, a relationship, an understanding?
7. Star the entry with the strongest moment of change. That is your most storyworthy candidate.
8. For the starred entry, write one sentence: "This is a story about [THEME]." Example: "This is a story about the moment I realized I was managing people the way my worst boss managed me."

### Group Sharing (15-20 minutes, if in a group)
9. Each participant shares their starred entry in 60-90 seconds. Not a polished story — just the moment and why it matters.
10. After each share, the group offers one observation: "What I heard in your story was..." This helps the storyteller see what resonates with an audience.

## Debrief Questions
- Which quadrant produced the strongest memory — first, last, best, or worst?
- Did any topic surprise you by surfacing a memory you had not thought about in years?
- Were your "worst" entries more vivid than your "best" entries? (This is common — struggle is memorable.)
- Could you use any of these stories in a professional context — a presentation, an email, a pitch, a team meeting?
- What would happen if you did this exercise with a different topic every week for a month?

## Variations
- **Speed round:** 60 seconds per quadrant instead of 3 minutes. Forces instinct over deliberation. First memories are often the most authentic.
- **Business-only topics:** Restrict topics to professional experiences: first client, worst pitch, best campaign, last product launch. Produces immediately usable business stories.
- **Cross-team version:** Pairs from different squads do the exercise together. Cross-pollination reveals stories that one squad can use but the other would never think to tell.
- **Audience-targeted version:** Choose topics your target audience would relate to. Write from their perspective. Builds empathy and produces customer-resonant narratives.
- **Series version:** Run the exercise weekly for 4 weeks with different topics. By week 4, participants have 16+ story candidates — enough to populate a personal story bank.
