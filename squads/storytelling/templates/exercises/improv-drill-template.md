# Improv Drill Template
> Use this template to run improvisational storytelling exercises for teams. Improv drills build narrative agility, quick thinking, active listening, and collaborative story construction. Works for team meetings, workshops, and storytelling skill development.

## Instructions
Choose 1-3 drills per session. Read the setup aloud, explain the rules, run the drill, then debrief. Each drill takes 10-20 minutes including debrief. The facilitator should participate to model vulnerability. Remind the group: there are no wrong answers in improv — only "yes, and."

### Drill 1 — "Yes, And" Story Chain
*Foundation drill. Builds collaborative narrative and active listening.*

**Setup:**
- Group sits in a circle (or goes in order on video call).
- One person starts a story with a single sentence.
- The next person continues with "Yes, and..." adding one sentence.
- Continue around the circle for 2-3 full rotations.

**Rules:**
- Every sentence must begin with "Yes, and..."
- No contradicting, correcting, or blocking what the previous person said.
- No planning ahead — listen and respond in the moment.
- The story must move forward (no repeating or stalling).

**Variations:**
- **Themed:** Start with "Once upon a time, a customer..." — keeps it business-relevant.
- **Speed round:** 5-second limit per sentence.
- **Constraint:** Every third sentence must include a number or data point.

**Debrief questions:**
1. When did the story feel most alive? Why?
2. When did someone take the story in a direction you didn't expect? How did it feel?
3. What's the connection between "yes, and" in improv and collaboration at work?

### Drill 2 — One-Word Story
*Extreme collaboration. Forces listening and surrender of control.*

**Setup:**
- Group goes in order, each person contributing exactly one word to build a story.
- Continue for 2-3 minutes or until the story reaches a natural end.

**Rules:**
- One word per person, no exceptions.
- No pausing longer than 2 seconds.
- Try to form coherent sentences and advance the plot.
- Anyone can say "Period." to end a sentence and start a new one.

**Variations:**
- **Two-word:** Each person gets two words (easier for beginners).
- **Directed:** Facilitator calls out "new character!" or "plot twist!" at random intervals.

**Debrief questions:**
1. What was the hardest part of this exercise?
2. When did you have to let go of where you wanted the story to go?
3. How does this relate to working on a shared narrative with a team?

---

### Drill 3 — Story in a Box
*Structured improvisation. Participants tell a story from random prompts.*

**Setup:**
- Prepare cards or slips with: Characters, Settings, Problems, Objects (10 of each).
- Each participant draws one from each category.
- They have 2 minutes to improvise a story using all four elements.

**Prompt categories:**
- **Characters:** [e.g., A skeptical CFO, A first-year teacher, A retired astronaut, A customer who won't stop emailing]
- **Settings:** [e.g., A board meeting, A coffee shop at 6 AM, A product demo, An elevator]
- **Problems:** [e.g., The budget got cut, The demo crashed, A competitor stole the idea, Nobody showed up]
- **Objects:** [e.g., A sticky note, A broken laptop, A handwritten letter, A prototype]

**Rules:**
- All four elements must appear in the story.
- The story must have a beginning, middle, and end.
- 2-minute time limit (facilitator keeps time).

**Debrief questions:**
1. How did you connect unrelated elements into a coherent story?
2. Which constraint was most generative (forced the best ideas)?
3. How is this similar to building a narrative from messy real-world inputs?

### Drill 4 — Emotion Shift
*Narrative range. Practice telling the same story with different emotional lenses.*

**Setup:**
- One participant tells a short story (1 minute) — can be real or made up.
- The facilitator then assigns a new emotion: "Now tell it as if it's hilarious / tragic / mysterious / inspiring."
- The participant retells the same story with the new emotional lens.
- Repeat with 2-3 different emotions.

**Emotion options:**
- [ ] Joyful / celebratory
- [ ] Suspenseful / mysterious
- [ ] Heartbreaking / bittersweet
- [ ] Absurd / comedic
- [ ] Urgent / alarming
- [ ] Calm / reflective

**Debrief questions:**
1. How did the emotion change which details you emphasized?
2. Which version felt most authentic?
3. How does emotional framing affect how an audience receives the same facts?

---

### Drill 5 — The Hot Seat (Advanced)
*Spontaneous storytelling under gentle pressure. Builds presentation confidence.*

**Setup:**
- One person sits in the "hot seat."
- The group gives them a topic (business or personal).
- They have 60 seconds to tell a story about it. No prep.

**Topic ideas:**
- "A time you failed publicly"
- "Your worst meeting ever"
- "A customer who changed how you think"
- "The moment you knew you were in the right job (or wrong one)"
- "A decision you almost didn't make"

**Rules:**
- No "I don't have a story for that" — find one, even if it's small.
- Audience listens silently — no interrupting.
- After each story, the group shares one thing they noticed or appreciated.

**Debrief questions:**
1. What was it like to be put on the spot?
2. Did a story come to you that you'd forgotten about?
3. What made certain stories more engaging than others?

## Example

**Session:** Monthly storytelling practice, Content team (6 people)
**Drills used:** "Yes, And" Story Chain (themed: customer stories), Story in a Box
**Key moment:** During Story in a Box, someone drew "skeptical CFO" + "elevator" + "the budget got cut" + "a sticky note" and told a 2-minute story about a CFO who found a sticky note on the elevator wall that said "Your best customers are leaving and here's why." The team laughed, but then realized it was essentially the opening hook of a case study they'd been struggling to write.
**Takeaway:** Random constraints often produce more creative openings than strategic brainstorming.
