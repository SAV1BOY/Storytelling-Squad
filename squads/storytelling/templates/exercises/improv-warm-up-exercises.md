# Improv Warm-Up Exercises

## Exercise Name
Improv Warm-Ups for Storytelling Teams

## Purpose
Improvisation exercises, drawn from Keith Johnstone's work and the broader improv tradition, train the spontaneity, listening, and "yes, and" mindset that underpin collaborative storytelling. These exercises are not about being funny — they are about removing the internal editor, building trust within the team, and practicing the real-time narrative thinking that makes storytellers adaptable and responsive. Use these as openers for workshops, training sessions, or regular team meetings.

## Duration
- **Single exercise:** 5-10 minutes
- **Full warm-up set (3-4 exercises):** 20-30 minutes

## Materials Needed
- Open space (chairs pushed back, room to stand and move)
- No props required for most exercises
- Timer
- Facilitator who models enthusiasm and low-stakes energy

## Instructions

### General Facilitation Notes
- The facilitator goes first to model willingness and vulnerability.
- Celebrate mistakes loudly. In improv, mistakes are gifts — they create unexpected stories.
- No one is forced to participate. Willing energy beats coerced compliance.
- Debrief each exercise briefly before moving to the next.

---

### Exercise 1: "Yes, And" Story Building
**Time:** 5-7 minutes
**Group size:** Pairs or full group (circle)
**Skill trained:** Acceptance, collaborative building, narrative momentum

**How to play:**
1. Stand or sit in a circle.
2. One person begins a story with a single sentence. Example: "Yesterday, I found a letter under my door."
3. The next person continues the story, starting their sentence with "Yes, and..." Example: "Yes, and the letter was written in a language I had never seen before."
4. Continue around the circle. Each person must accept what came before and add to it. No contradicting, no "but," no redirecting.
5. After 2-3 rounds, let the story find a natural ending.

**Debrief:** How did it feel to accept someone else's contribution without editing it? Where did the story go that you did not expect?

---

### Exercise 2: Word-at-a-Time Story
**Time:** 5-7 minutes
**Group size:** 3-8 people (circle)
**Skill trained:** Listening, letting go of control, trusting the group

**How to play:**
1. Stand in a circle.
2. The group tells a story one word at a time. Each person contributes exactly one word, moving clockwise.
3. The goal is to form coherent sentences and a story that makes sense. This requires listening to what came before and anticipating where the sentence might go.
4. If someone says a word that ends a sentence, the next person starts a new sentence.
5. Play for 2-3 minutes or until the story reaches a natural end.

**Debrief:** When did you try to control the story vs. letting it emerge? What happened when someone said something unexpected?

---

### Exercise 3: Status Swap (Johnstone)
**Time:** 7-10 minutes
**Group size:** Pairs
**Skill trained:** Status awareness, character dynamics, nonverbal storytelling

**How to play:**
1. Pair up. Assign one person as "high status" and one as "low status."
2. Give a scenario: two colleagues meeting in a hallway, a customer and a salesperson, a manager and a new hire.
3. Play the scene for 60 seconds. High-status person uses direct eye contact, still body, minimal words, takes up space. Low-status person fidgets, averts eyes, over-explains, takes up less space.
4. At the facilitator's clap, swap status. The high-status person becomes low-status and vice versa. Continue the same scene.
5. Play for another 60 seconds.

**Debrief:** How did swapping status change the story? How does status affect who the audience roots for? Where do you see status dynamics in your brand's storytelling?

---

### Exercise 4: Freeze and Justify
**Time:** 7-10 minutes
**Group size:** Full group (4+ people)
**Skill trained:** Quick thinking, narrative pivots, finding story in any situation

**How to play:**
1. Two people begin an improvised scene (any scenario).
2. At any point, anyone in the audience shouts "Freeze!" The two performers freeze in their exact physical positions.
3. The person who called freeze taps one performer on the shoulder and takes their place, assuming their exact physical position.
4. The new person must start a completely different scene that justifies the physical position they are in. Example: if the frozen position looks like someone reaching for a shelf, the new scene might start with "I can never reach the good cereal."
5. Continue for 5-7 minutes, with multiple freezes.

**Debrief:** How does physical position create story? How did you justify unexpected situations — and how is that skill relevant to adapting stories for unexpected business contexts?

---

### Exercise 5: One-Minute Expert
**Time:** 5-7 minutes
**Group size:** Full group
**Skill trained:** Confidence, narrative fluency, thinking on your feet

**How to play:**
1. Each person draws a random topic from a hat (or the facilitator assigns one). Topics should be absurd: "The history of the paper clip," "Why cats make better CEOs," "The emotional life of spreadsheets."
2. Each person must deliver a 60-second "expert lecture" on their topic — with complete confidence, as if they are the world's leading authority.
3. The audience responds with genuine questions after each lecture.
4. The "expert" must answer questions without breaking character.

**Debrief:** How did commitment to the bit make the nonsense feel credible? How does that confidence translate to telling real stories under pressure?

---

## Debrief Questions (General)
- Which exercise was the hardest for you? What does that reveal about your storytelling habits?
- Where did you feel the urge to edit, control, or plan? How did it feel to let go?
- How does the "yes, and" principle apply to your work — building on colleagues' ideas, client feedback, or unexpected audience reactions?
- What would change in your next presentation or piece of writing if you applied one thing from these exercises?

## Variations
- **Remote-friendly adaptations:** "Yes, And" and "Word-at-a-Time" work well on video calls. Use a talking order (alphabetical or screen position) instead of a circle. "One-Minute Expert" works perfectly on video.
- **Writing-based alternative:** For teams uncomfortable with performance, run "Yes, And" as a written exercise: each person writes a sentence and passes the document. Same principle, lower exposure.
- **Themed warm-ups:** Tie exercises to the session topic. If the workshop is about customer stories, use customer scenarios. If it is about pitch narratives, use investor scenarios.
- **Progressive intensity:** Start with low-exposure exercises (Word-at-a-Time) and build to higher-exposure ones (One-Minute Expert). Let the group warm up before asking for individual performance.
