# Public Narrative Practice

## Exercise Name
Public Narrative Crafting Exercise

## Purpose
Based on Marshall Ganz's Public Narrative framework (developed at Harvard Kennedy School), this exercise teaches participants to craft and deliver a narrative that links personal story, community identity, and a call to action. Public narrative has three components: Story of Self (why you are called to lead), Story of Us (the shared values of the community), and Story of Now (the urgent challenge that demands action). This structure is used by organizers, leaders, and brands to move audiences from passive agreement to active commitment.

## Duration
- **Full workshop:** 90-120 minutes
- **Condensed version:** 45-60 minutes (focus on Story of Self only)

## Materials Needed
- Printed worksheet (one per participant — see template below)
- Timer (strict timing is essential to this exercise)
- Space for pairs and small groups
- Optional: video recording for self-review

## Instructions

### Part 1: Framework Introduction (10-15 minutes)

Teach the three stories:

**Story of Self** — A personal story about a moment when you were called to the values that drive your work. Not your resume. Not your credentials. A specific moment when something happened that put you on this path.
- Answers: "Why me? Why do I care about this?"
- Key: A choice point — a moment when you could have gone one way but went another.

**Story of Us** — A story about the shared identity and values of the community you are addressing. What experiences, challenges, or aspirations do we hold in common?
- Answers: "Why us? What do we share?"
- Key: Shared experience, not demographic description.

**Story of Now** — A story about the urgent challenge facing the community right now and the specific action needed. Creates tension between the world as it is and the world as it should be.
- Answers: "Why now? What must we do?"
- Key: A choice the audience can make — specific, concrete, immediate.

### Part 2: Story of Self — Writing (10-15 minutes)

1. Think about your life. Identify a moment — a specific moment, not a period — when you experienced the values that drive your current work. This could be a childhood experience, a professional turning point, a conversation, a failure, a choice.
2. Write the moment using the template below. Stay in the scene. Describe what happened, what you felt, and what you chose.
3. Keep it to 2 minutes when spoken. Shorter is harder and more powerful.

### Story of Self Template

- **The moment:** [DESCRIBE THE SCENE — where were you, who was there, what was happening]
- **The challenge:** [WHAT TENSION OR CHOICE DID YOU FACE?]
- **The choice:** [WHAT DID YOU DO? WHAT DID YOU DECIDE?]
- **The outcome:** [WHAT HAPPENED AS A RESULT?]
- **The value:** [WHAT VALUE OR BELIEF DID THIS MOMENT CRYSTALLIZE FOR YOU?]
- **The connection to now:** [HOW DOES THIS MOMENT CONNECT TO THE WORK YOU DO TODAY?]

### Part 3: Story of Self — Practice in Pairs (10-15 minutes)

4. Pair up. Tell your Story of Self in 2 minutes. Your partner times you.
5. Partner feedback (2 minutes):
   - "The moment I felt connected to your story was when..."
   - "The value I heard was..."
   - "I wanted more detail about..."
6. Switch roles. Repeat.
7. Revise your story based on feedback (2 minutes).

### Part 4: Story of Us — Writing (10 minutes)

8. Think about the community you are addressing (your team, your customers, your audience). What shared experience connects you?
9. Write the Story of Us using the template below. The goal is for the audience to think "Yes, that is us."

### Story of Us Template

- **The shared experience:** [WHAT HAVE WE BEEN THROUGH TOGETHER OR WHAT DO WE ALL FACE?]
- **The shared value:** [WHAT DO WE BELIEVE IN COMMON?]
- **The evidence:** [A SPECIFIC STORY OR MOMENT THAT PROVES THIS SHARED IDENTITY]
- **The identity statement:** [WHO ARE WE, AND WHAT MAKES US "US"?]

### Part 5: Story of Now — Writing (10 minutes)

10. Identify the urgent challenge or opportunity facing the community right now.
11. Write the Story of Now using the template below. The goal is to create the tension between what is and what could be — and to offer a concrete action.

### Story of Now Template

- **The challenge:** [WHAT IS THE CURRENT SITUATION THAT DEMANDS ACTION?]
- **The stakes:** [WHAT HAPPENS IF WE DO NOTHING?]
- **The opportunity:** [WHAT BECOMES POSSIBLE IF WE ACT?]
- **The ask:** [WHAT SPECIFIC ACTION ARE YOU ASKING THE AUDIENCE TO TAKE? — must be concrete, achievable, and immediate]
- **The hope:** [WHY DO YOU BELIEVE THIS COMMUNITY CAN DO THIS?]

### Part 6: Full Narrative Assembly (10-15 minutes)

12. Combine your three stories into one narrative. The flow is: Self leads to Us leads to Now. "I was called to this work because [Self]. We share this calling because [Us]. And right now, we must act because [Now]."
13. Practice the full narrative. Target time: 4-5 minutes total.
14. Deliver to a small group (3-4 people). Group provides feedback:
    - "Where did you feel most authentic?"
    - "Where did the energy drop?"
    - "Did the call to action feel earned by the stories that preceded it?"

## Debrief Questions
- Which of the three stories was hardest to write — Self, Us, or Now? Why?
- Did telling your Story of Self feel vulnerable? How did the audience respond to that vulnerability?
- Is your Story of Us genuinely shared, or did you project your own values onto the group?
- Is your Story of Now actionable? Could someone in the audience do the thing you asked within 24 hours?
- How could you adapt this structure for a brand — where the brand's founding moment is the Story of Self, the customer community is the Story of Us, and the product launch or campaign is the Story of Now?

## Variations
- **Brand narrative version:** Replace personal Story of Self with the brand's origin story. Story of Us becomes the customer community. Story of Now becomes the current campaign or product launch. Produces a complete brand narrative arc.
- **Leadership version:** Focus exclusively on Story of Self. Leaders craft their leadership origin story — the moment that shaped how they lead. Use in onboarding, team introductions, or keynote openings.
- **Sales version:** Story of Self becomes "why I believe in this product." Story of Us becomes "what we share with our customers." Story of Now becomes "why this is the moment to buy/adopt/change."
- **Written version:** For teams uncomfortable with live delivery, write the full narrative as a 500-word essay. Achieves the same structural learning without the performance component.
- **Coaching pairs:** Assign ongoing pairs who practice and refine their public narratives over 3-4 weeks, meeting for 30 minutes each week. Iterative practice produces dramatically stronger narratives than a single session.
