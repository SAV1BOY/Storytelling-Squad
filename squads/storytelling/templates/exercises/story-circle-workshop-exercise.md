# Story Circle Workshop Exercise

## Exercise Name
Story Circle Workshop

## Purpose
Based on Dan Harmon's Story Circle (itself a simplified adaptation of Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey), this group exercise teaches participants to build complete narrative arcs using an eight-step circular structure. The story circle works because it maps the universal pattern of human change: comfort, desire, unfamiliar territory, struggle, discovery, cost, return, and transformation. It is practical for business storytelling because it applies equally to customer journeys, brand narratives, product launches, and personal leadership stories.

## Duration
- **Full workshop:** 60-90 minutes
- **Condensed version:** 30-40 minutes (skip group critique, shorten sharing)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or large paper (for drawing the circle)
- Printed story circle templates (one per participant — see template below)
- Markers or pens
- Timer
- Sticky notes (optional, for group version)
- Examples of completed story circles (provide 1-2 from the swipe file)

## Instructions

### Part 1: Teach the Circle (10-15 minutes)

Draw the story circle on the whiteboard. Eight steps, arranged clockwise around a circle. A horizontal line divides the circle: the top half is the ordinary world; the bottom half is the unfamiliar world.

**The Eight Steps:**
1. **You** (comfort zone) — A character is in their familiar situation.
2. **Need** (desire) — They want something.
3. **Go** (unfamiliar situation) — They enter an unfamiliar situation to get it.
4. **Search** (adaptation) — They adapt, struggle, and search.
5. **Find** (discovery) — They find what they were looking for.
6. **Take** (cost) — But they pay a price for it.
7. **Return** (changed) — They return to their familiar situation.
8. **Change** (transformed) — They have changed.

Walk through one example. Use a well-known movie (Finding Nemo, The Matrix) or a business case study. Map each beat to a step. Show how the circle always returns to the beginning — but the character is different.

### Part 2: Individual Story Circle (15-20 minutes)

**Solo Exercise:**
1. Choose a story to map. Options:
   - A customer's journey with your product/service
   - Your company's founding story
   - A personal professional transformation
   - A campaign narrative you need to build
2. Fill in each of the eight beats on the template below. Write 1-2 sentences per beat. Do not write a polished story — write the skeleton.
3. After filling in all eight beats, draw an arrow from step 8 back to step 1. Ask: How is the character in step 8 different from the character in step 1? That difference is the story's meaning.

### Story Circle Template

**Story title:** [WORKING TITLE]
**Protagonist:** [WHO IS THE MAIN CHARACTER — customer, founder, team, audience member]
**Core transformation:** [ONE SENTENCE — who they were before vs. who they are after]

| Step | Beat | Your Story |
|------|------|-----------|
| 1. You | Comfort zone / status quo | [DESCRIBE THE CHARACTER'S STARTING STATE] |
| 2. Need | Desire / problem recognized | [WHAT DO THEY WANT OR WHAT PROBLEM EMERGES?] |
| 3. Go | Cross the threshold | [HOW DO THEY ENTER THE UNFAMILIAR SITUATION?] |
| 4. Search | Struggle and adaptation | [WHAT OBSTACLES DO THEY FACE? HOW DO THEY ADAPT?] |
| 5. Find | Discovery / the thing they sought | [WHAT DO THEY DISCOVER OR ACHIEVE?] |
| 6. Take | The price paid | [WHAT DOES IT COST THEM — TIME, MONEY, BELIEFS, COMFORT?] |
| 7. Return | Back to the familiar world | [HOW DO THEY RETURN TO THEIR CONTEXT?] |
| 8. Change | Transformation complete | [HOW ARE THEY DIFFERENT NOW?] |

**The meaning of this story:** [COMPLETE THE SENTENCE: "This is a story about how _____."]

### Part 3: Pair Share and Feedback (10-15 minutes)

4. Pair up. Share your story circle with your partner. Tell it in 2-3 minutes using the beats as a guide — do not read the template, tell the story.
5. Partner gives feedback using these prompts:
   - "The moment that hooked me was..." (identifies the strongest beat)
   - "I wanted to know more about..." (identifies where the story needs more detail)
   - "The transformation felt [clear / unclear] because..." (tests whether step 8 lands)
6. Switch roles. Repeat.

### Part 4: Group Critique (15-20 minutes, optional)

7. 2-3 volunteers share their story circle with the full group. Tell the story in 3 minutes.
8. Group provides structured feedback:
   - Which step was the strongest?
   - Where did the story lose momentum?
   - Is the transformation earned — does the cost (step 6) feel proportional to the change (step 8)?
   - Could this story work for [specific channel or audience]?

### Part 5: Application Bridge (5-10 minutes)

9. Each participant answers: "Where will I use this story circle in the next two weeks?" Write it down. Be specific — name the project, the channel, the audience.

## Debrief Questions
- Which step was hardest to fill in? (Common answers: step 6, the cost. Stories without cost feel hollow.)
- Did your story change when you told it out loud vs. when you wrote it on paper?
- How does the story circle compare to how you currently structure stories at work?
- Where do most marketing stories fail in this framework? (Common answer: they skip from step 2 to step 5 — they show the need and the solution but skip the search and the cost.)
- How would your story change if you swapped the protagonist — from the brand to the customer, or vice versa?

## Variations
- **Customer journey mapping:** Use the story circle as a customer journey tool. Map an actual customer's experience through all eight beats. Reveals where the journey has narrative gaps.
- **Campaign planning:** Use one circle per campaign phase. The campaign itself becomes a story told over time.
- **Competitive analysis:** Map a competitor's brand story onto the circle. Identify which beats they own and which they neglect. Build your story to fill the gaps they leave.
- **Reverse engineering:** Take a successful piece of content (ad, speech, case study) and reverse-map it onto the circle. Learn the structure behind content that works.
- **Multiple protagonists:** Fill in the same story circle for different audiences (customer, investor, employee). The same company story has different protagonists depending on who is listening.
