# Homework for Life Worksheet

## Exercise Name
Homework for Life

## Purpose
Train yourself to notice storyworthy moments in everyday life. Developed by Matthew Dicks, this daily practice builds the most fundamental storytelling skill: the ability to recognize small moments of change, surprise, or realization that are the raw material of compelling stories. Over time, it rewires how you see the world — every day becomes a source of stories.

## Duration
- **Daily practice:** 5 minutes per day (the nightly entry)
- **Weekly review:** 15 minutes (pattern recognition)
- **Monthly review:** 30 minutes (story mining and selection)

## Materials Needed
- This worksheet (printed or digital)
- A consistent place to write (notebook, spreadsheet, or notes app)
- A daily reminder or trigger (e.g., set an alarm for 9 PM)

## Instructions
1. Every night before bed, reflect on your day.
2. Identify one moment that was different from every other moment. It does not need to be dramatic. The best stories come from small, specific, human moments.
3. Write one sentence describing the moment. Not a paragraph. Not a story. One sentence that captures the seed.
4. In a second column, write one phrase about why it is storyworthy — what changed, what surprised you, what you felt.
5. Add tags for easy retrieval later (e.g., work, family, insight, failure, connection, humor).
6. Do this every day for at least 30 consecutive days. The first week will feel forced. By week three, you will start noticing moments in real time.
7. At the end of each week, review your entries. Circle or highlight the one with the most story potential.
8. At the end of each month, review all entries. Select the top 3 that could become full stories for business or personal use.

### Daily Entry Format

| Date | The Moment | Why It Is Storyworthy | Tags |
|------|-----------|----------------------|------|
| [YYYY-MM-DD] | [ONE SENTENCE — what happened, specifically] | [ONE PHRASE — what changed, what surprised you] | [TAGS] |

### Weekly Reflection (complete every 7 days)

- **Most storyworthy moment this week:** [ENTRY DATE AND MOMENT]
- **Pattern emerging:** [WHAT KINDS OF MOMENTS KEEP SHOWING UP]
- **Moment I almost missed:** [SOMETHING YOU ONLY NOTICED BECAUSE YOU WERE LOOKING]
- **Story potential:** [COULD ANY OF THESE BECOME A FULL STORY? WHICH ONE AND FOR WHAT?]

### Monthly Review (complete every 30 days)

- **Total entries:** [NUMBER]
- **Top 3 story candidates:**
  1. [DATE] — [MOMENT] — Potential format: [SPEECH / BLOG / EMAIL / CASE STUDY / SOCIAL]
  2. [DATE] — [MOMENT] — Potential format: [FORMAT]
  3. [DATE] — [MOMENT] — Potential format: [FORMAT]
- **Theme of the month:** [WHAT PATTERN EMERGES ACROSS ALL ENTRIES]
- **Skill growth:** [WHAT ARE YOU GETTING BETTER AT — noticing, specificity, emotional awareness]

## Debrief Questions
- What kinds of moments do you tend to notice most — work, family, personal, funny, sad?
- Are you capturing moments of change, or just interesting observations? (Moments of change make stories; observations make anecdotes.)
- Has the practice changed how you experience your day? Are you noticing more?
- Which entry surprised you the most when you reviewed it?
- Could you tell a 2-minute story from any of your entries right now? Try it.

## Variations
- **Team version:** Each team member shares their best entry of the week in a Monday standup. Builds team storytelling culture and provides a bank of real stories.
- **Business-focused version:** Limit entries to moments from work — customer interactions, team dynamics, product insights, market observations. Produces directly usable business stories.
- **Gratitude hybrid:** Add a third column: "What I am grateful for in this moment." Combines story mining with gratitude practice.
- **Pair practice:** Partner with a colleague. Share entries weekly and give each other feedback on which moments have the most story potential. External perspective reveals blind spots.
