# First, Last, Best, Worst Template
> Use this exercise to generate a large volume of story seeds quickly. For each topic, recall your first, last, best, and worst experience. Based on Matthew Dicks' story-mining technique from "Storyworthy." Works for individuals and workshop groups.

## Instructions
Set a timer for 15 minutes. Go through each topic row and fill in one short phrase per cell. Don't overthink — write the first thing that comes to mind. After completing the grid, circle the 3-5 entries that give you the strongest emotional response. Those are your best story candidates. This exercise generates 40+ story seeds in under 20 minutes.

### Debrief Questions (For Workshops)
1. Which cell was hardest to fill? Why?
2. Which entry surprised you — something you hadn't thought about in years?
3. Do you notice any themes across your grid?
4. Which story would you most want to tell to this group? Why?
5. How could one of these stories connect to your professional work?

## Example

| Topic | First | Last | Best | Worst |
|-------|-------|------|------|-------|
| Job | Lifeguard, age 16 | Head of Content, current | Running a 3-person startup where I did everything | Corporate job where I wrote reports no one read |
| Presentation | College speech class, hands shaking | Webinar last week, 200 attendees | Keynote where someone cried (in a good way) | Product demo where the software crashed live |
| Failure | Didn't make the basketball team, 8th grade | Lost a key hire last month to a competitor | Launched a product that flopped, but the post-mortem changed how we build | Sent an email to 10K people with the wrong link |

**Top seed:** "Worst presentation — the live demo crash. The product froze on screen in front of 50 prospects. I had 10 seconds to decide: make a joke or panic. I told the story of why we built the product instead. Three people came up after and said it was the best demo they'd ever seen — because it was honest."
