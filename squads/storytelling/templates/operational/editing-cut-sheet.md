# Editing Cut Sheet
> Use this template during the editing process to document what was cut from a narrative and why. Keeps editing decisions transparent, defensible, and reversible. Use alongside the Story Review Checklist.

## Instructions
Fill in one row per cut. Every cut needs a reason and a suggested alternative (even if the alternative is "nothing — the story is tighter without it"). Share the completed cut sheet with the author alongside the edited draft so they understand the editorial logic. This prevents edit wars and builds storytelling skill over time.

### Editorial Principles Applied
*Check which principles guided this round of editing:*
- [ ] Every sentence must advance the story or deepen emotion
- [ ] Cut anything the audience doesn't need to understand the next beat
- [ ] When in doubt, cut — it can always be restored
- [ ] Replace abstractions with specifics
- [ ] If a passage is "fine," it's not good enough
- [ ] The story should start as late as possible and end as early as possible

## Example

| # | Element Cut | Location | Why Cut | Alternative | Before | After |
|---|-------------|----------|---------|-------------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Company background paragraph | Section 1, lines 3-8 | Audience already knows who BrightPath is — this is for existing customers | None — start with the challenge | "BrightPath Education was founded in 2018 and serves over 50,000 students across 12 campuses. Their mission is to..." | Removed. Story now opens with Maria's Monday morning. |
| 2 | "Innovative and cutting-edge" | Section 3, line 2 | Empty adjectives — show, don't tell | Replace with a specific capability | "...our innovative and cutting-edge platform..." | "...a platform that predicts at-risk students 3 weeks earlier..." |
| 3 | Paragraph about competitor comparison | Section 4 | Off-message — the story is about transformation, not competition | None — keep focus on the customer | "Unlike other tools on the market that require..." | Removed. |

**Summary for Author:** The story was well-structured with a clear transformation arc. The main edits were: (1) cutting the company background to get to the story faster, (2) replacing abstract adjectives with specific proof, and (3) removing the competitor comparison that distracted from the customer's journey. Word count went from 1,400 to 1,050 — a 25% reduction that makes the story tighter and faster. Recommended: revise, then ready for customer review.
