# Story Feedback Form
> Use this form to give structured feedback on any narrative draft — article, script, deck, case study, or presentation. Ensures feedback is specific, actionable, and covers all dimensions of effective storytelling. Use for peer reviews, stakeholder reviews, and editorial rounds.

## Instructions
Complete one form per review. Score each dimension, then write specific notes with references to exact sections or lines. The "Actionable Notes" section at the bottom is the most important — this is what the author will actually use. Be honest but constructive. "This doesn't work" is not feedback. "This doesn't work because [reason], and here's what might fix it" is.

## Example

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Structure | 4/5 | Strong three-act arc. The turning point at paragraph 6 is effective. |
| Clarity | 3/5 | Core message is clear by the end but takes too long to emerge. Consider stating it earlier. |
| Emotion | 4/5 | The "Monday morning spreadsheet" scene is vivid. I felt Maria's frustration. |
| Proof | 2/5 | The 22% → 11% churn metric is strong, but the "$600K saved" figure needs a source or methodology note. |
| Pacing | 3/5 | Paragraphs 3-4 (company background) slow the story down. The reader already knows who BrightPath is by context. |
| Hook | 5/5 | "She spent 4 hours every Monday on a report no one read." — Perfect opening. |

**Top Priority Change:** Cut paragraphs 3-4 (company background) and start with the challenge. The story loses momentum between the hook and the tension. Moving straight from "Monday morning spreadsheets" to "churn hit 22%" keeps the reader engaged.

**Recommendation:** Approved with minor edits.
