# Licensing and Attribution Notes

## Purpose
Usage guidelines, attribution requirements, and ethical considerations for the
swipe files and source materials referenced by the storytelling squad.

---

## General Principles

### 1. Swipe Files Are for Inspiration, Not Copying
The materials in the swipe/ directory are analyses of publicly available work.
They are intended for:
- Studying narrative technique and structure.
- Adapting patterns and principles to original work.
- Training and professional development.

They are NOT intended for:
- Reproducing copyrighted text verbatim in client deliverables.
- Presenting others' work as your own.
- Distributing copyrighted materials.

### 2. Fair Use and Fair Dealing
The analyses in this squad's swipe files fall under fair use (US) / fair dealing
(UK/Commonwealth) principles because they are:
- **Transformative** — the original works are analyzed and commented upon, not reproduced in full.
- **Educational** — the purpose is professional learning and skill development.
- **Limited** — only small excerpts are quoted, alongside substantial original analysis.

However, fair use is context-dependent. When in doubt, consult legal.

## Attribution Requirements by Source Type

### Published Books
| Source | Can We Quote? | Attribution Required | Notes |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|-------|
| Short quotes (< 100 words) | Yes (fair use) | Author, title, year | Always attribute |
| Frameworks (e.g., Hero's Journey) | Yes | Credit the creator | Frameworks are ideas, not copyrightable, but attribution is professional |
| Proprietary terms (e.g., "StoryBrand") | Use with care | Trademark owner | "StoryBrand" is trademarked by StoryBrand/Donald Miller |
| Extensive reproduction | No | N/A | Requires publisher permission |

### TED Talks
- TED talk transcripts are copyrighted by TED Conferences LLC.
- Short quotes for analysis: permitted under fair use with attribution.
- Full transcripts: not permitted without license.
- TED content is available under a Creative Commons license for non-commercial use when sourced from TED.com.

### Podcasts
- Podcast content is copyrighted by the producer.
- Summarizing key ideas: permitted with attribution.
- Quoting short excerpts for analysis: permitted under fair use.
- Recording or redistributing full episodes: not permitted.

### Pitch Decks
- Publicly shared pitch decks (e.g., on Slidebean, Pitch Deck Hunt) are shared by
  the companies themselves or with their permission.
- Analysis and commentary: permitted.
- Reproducing deck designs or slides: not permitted without permission.
- Using a deck's structure as a template for original work: permitted.

### Speeches
- Published speeches are generally copyrightable by the speaker.
- Historical speeches may be in the public domain (check date and jurisdiction).
- Short quotes for analysis: permitted under fair use.
- The speech transcripts themselves should not be reproduced in full.

## Image and Media Usage

### In Internal Squad Documents
- Screenshots and images used for analysis and education: generally permitted
  under fair use for internal use.
- Do not redistribute externally without verifying rights.

### In Client Deliverables
- Never use copyrighted images without a license.
- Stock photo services (Unsplash, Pexels) for free use.
- Client-provided images: obtain written confirmation of rights.
- User-generated content: obtain permission from the creator.

---

## When to Seek Legal Review

Seek legal review before:
1. Reproducing more than 300 words of any single copyrighted source.
2. Using trademarked terms in client-facing materials.
3. Using any content in paid advertising.
4. Reproducing visual designs, layouts, or brand elements.
5. Using real company names in case studies without their knowledge.
6. Attributing quotes or statements that were made in private contexts.

---

## Ethical Guidelines

### For Real Examples
- When analyzing real brands, campaigns, or individuals, be accurate and fair.
- Do not present speculation as fact.
- When writing fictional case study examples (as in our swipe files), make it
  clear that the examples are illustrative, not reports of real events.

### For Client Work
- Always create original work for clients. Swipe files inform technique, not text.
- If a client's story closely resembles a public case, disclose the similarity.
- Never present another company's case study results as your own client's results.

### For Attribution
- When in doubt, attribute. Over-attribution is professional. Under-attribution is risky.
- Credit the human, not just the company. "Nancy Duarte's sparkline model" > "the sparkline model."
- If a framework has multiple contributors, credit the primary originator.

---

## Summary
Use swipe files to learn technique. Create original work for every deliverable.
Attribute generously. When unsure, ask legal or err on the side of more attribution.
