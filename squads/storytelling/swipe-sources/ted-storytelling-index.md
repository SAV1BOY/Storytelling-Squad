## How to Watch TED Talks for Storytelling Craft

### The Analysis Framework
For each talk, note:
1. **Opening technique:** How do they earn the first 30 seconds?
2. **Structure:** Is it chronological? Thematic? Framework + stories?
3. **Story count:** How many distinct stories are told?
4. **Data integration:** How do they introduce evidence?
5. **Emotional arc:** Where is the peak? How do they build to it?
6. **Closing technique:** How do they end? Callback? Question? Declaration?
7. **Stealable line:** What's the one line you'd quote to someone else?

### Speed-Study Method
- Watch at 1x speed the first time (for emotional impact).
- Re-watch at 1.5x with a notebook (for structural analysis).
- Transcribe the opening and closing paragraphs (for language patterns).
- Map the emotional arc on paper (for pacing study).

## TED Talk Patterns That Apply to All Storytelling

| Pattern | How It Works | Application |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Open with story, not thesis** | Personal anecdote earns permission to teach | Blog intros, email openers, keynote openings |
| **One big idea** | The best talks are about ONE thing | Landing pages, pitch decks, brand messaging |
| **Show then tell** | Demonstrate the principle before naming it | Product demos, case studies, training content |
| **Humor as permission** | A laugh in the first 2 minutes gives the audience permission to feel | Any content that deals with serious topics |
| **Callback close** | Return to the opening image, now transformed | Email sequences, keynotes, brand films |
