# Story Grid Podcast — Source Guide for Editorial Narrative Analysis

## Source Overview

The Story Grid podcast is built around the Story Grid methodology developed
by Shawn Coyne, a veteran book editor with decades of experience in
commercial publishing. The methodology provides a systematic, analytical
framework for understanding why stories work or fail at a structural level.
The podcast features discussions between Coyne and various co-hosts,
analyzing published works, exploring narrative theory, and applying
editorial principles to storytelling craft.

While the Story Grid was originally designed for fiction editing, its
principles translate powerfully to marketing and business storytelling.
The framework breaks stories down into measurable, identifiable components —
genre conventions, obligatory scenes, value shifts, turning points — that
can be studied, replicated, and applied to any narrative context.

Episodes are available on all major podcast platforms and through the
Story Grid website, which also offers extensive written resources, courses,
and analytical tools. The back catalog is substantial, covering everything
from foundational theory to detailed analyses of specific works.

What makes Story Grid uniquely valuable for marketers is its insistence that
storytelling is not mystical talent but learnable craft. The methodology
gives you a vocabulary and a diagnostic framework for understanding why
a piece of narrative content succeeds or fails — and that understanding
is directly applicable to marketing copy, brand narratives, presentations,
and customer communications.

## What to Study

- **The five commandments of storytelling**: Inciting incident, progressive complication, crisis, climax, resolution — the building blocks of every functional scene and story
- **Value shifts**: How every scene or story unit must move a character (or audience) from one emotional state to another
- **Genre conventions**: The expected elements that audiences require based on the type of story being told
- **Obligatory scenes**: The moments that must appear in a story for it to satisfy the audience's expectations
- **The global story arc**: How individual scenes build into sequences, sequences into acts, and acts into a complete narrative
- **Turning points**: The specific moments where stories change direction — and why they matter for holding attention
- **Beginning hook, middle build, ending payoff**: The three-part macro structure that applies to everything from a novel to a landing page
- **Point of view and narrative distance**: How the relationship between narrator and audience shapes the story's effect
- **Editorial diagnosis**: How to identify why a story is not working and what structural element is missing

## Key Episodes/Examples

1. **Foundational episodes on the five commandments** — Start here to learn the core vocabulary; these concepts underpin everything else in the methodology
2. **Episodes analyzing published novels** — Listen to how Coyne breaks down well-known books scene by scene; this teaches you to see structure in any narrative
3. **Episodes on genre and audience expectations** — Understanding genre is understanding your audience's unspoken contract with your content
4. **Episodes about the "foolscap method"** — A one-page story analysis tool that can be adapted for planning any marketing narrative
5. **Episodes on beginning hooks** — Study how the methodology defines what makes an opening effective; apply these principles to email subject lines, ad copy, and landing pages
6. **Episodes discussing value shifts** — These episodes explain why content that does not change the audience's emotional state fails to engage
7. **Episodes on the middle build** — The most common place stories (and marketing content) fail; learn why middles collapse and how to prevent it
8. **Episodes featuring guest writers discussing their process** — These show the methodology in practice and reveal how different creators adapt it
9. **Episodes on nonfiction storytelling** — These directly address how Story Grid principles apply beyond fiction, including business and marketing contexts
10. **Episodes on the ending payoff** — Study what makes an ending satisfying; apply these principles to calls to action, closing arguments, and campaign conclusions

## Narrative Techniques to Watch For

- **The inciting incident as attention mechanism**: Every Moth story, TED talk, and pitch deck that works has a clear inciting incident — Story Grid teaches you to identify and craft it
- **Progressive complication as engagement tool**: Stories that escalate in complexity and stakes hold attention; stories that flatline lose it
- **The crisis question**: At the heart of every effective story is a dilemma — a choice between two paths. This concept applies to customer journey narratives and brand positioning
- **Climax as the moment of truth**: The single moment where the story delivers on its promise. In marketing, this is the conversion moment, the testimonial reveal, the product demonstration
- **Resolution as new beginning**: Effective endings do not just close — they open a new chapter. In marketing, the resolution should point toward continued relationship
- **Polarity shifts**: Tracking whether scenes move from positive to negative or negative to positive — and why alternating polarities maintains interest
- **Conventions as audience trust**: Meeting genre expectations builds trust; violating them without purpose creates confusion. In marketing, this means understanding what your audience expects from your content type
- **The global value at stake**: Every story has one core value being tested (justice, love, survival). Every brand narrative should have one core value being demonstrated

## How to Use in Marketing

- **Content diagnosis**: When a blog post, email, or video is not performing, use the five commandments to identify which structural element is missing or weak
- **Landing page structure**: Map landing pages to the beginning hook (headline and hero), middle build (features, evidence, social proof), and ending payoff (call to action and vision)
- **Email sequence design**: Structure nurture sequences so each email contains its own inciting incident, complication, and resolution while advancing the global arc
- **Customer journey mapping**: Use Story Grid's macro structure to ensure the customer journey moves through clear phases with rising stakes and a satisfying resolution
- **Case study writing**: Structure case studies using the five commandments — the client's inciting incident, complications they faced, the crisis decision, the climactic solution, and the resolution
- **Brand narrative auditing**: Evaluate your entire brand narrative using Story Grid's genre and convention analysis to ensure you are meeting audience expectations
- **Video script development**: Write video scripts that include clear value shifts — the audience should feel differently at the end than they did at the beginning
- **Ad copy testing**: Use the concept of the inciting incident to evaluate whether your ad copy creates enough disruption to stop the scroll
- **Presentation structure**: Apply the three-part macro structure to any presentation for natural narrative flow
