## About The Moth
Founded in 1997 by George Dawes Green. True stories, told live, without notes.
The Moth is the gold standard for personal narrative. Every story follows an arc:
beginning, middle, end, with a transformation.

**Website:** themoth.org
**Podcast:** Available on all major platforms
**Format:** 5–15 minute stories told on stage to a live audience

## Moth GrandSLAM Winners to Study
GrandSLAM is The Moth's championship storytelling competition. Winners represent
the highest level of live narrative craft.

**What to listen for:**
1. **The opening line** — How do they earn the second sentence?
2. **The turn** — Where does the story pivot? Is it gradual or sudden?
3. **Physical detail** — What sensory details anchor the story?
4. **The ending** — How do they land? Do they explain or let it sit?
5. **Pacing** — When do they speed up? When do they slow down?

## Moth Storytelling Principles (from their guidelines)

1. **Tell a story, not a monologue.** A story has a beginning, middle, and end.
2. **Have stakes.** Something must be at risk — a relationship, an identity, a belief.
3. **Be a character in your story.** Don't narrate from the outside.
4. **Have a transformation.** You must be different at the end than at the beginning.
5. **Be specific.** "My mother" is better than "parents." "A Tuesday" is better than "one day."
6. **Show, don't tell.** "I was scared" is telling. "My hands wouldn't stop shaking" is showing.
7. **Keep it under time.** Constraint creates craft.
8. **Practice, but don't memorize.** Know the beats; find the words fresh each time.

## Listening Assignment
For storytelling squad members, listen to at least two Moth stories per week. After
each story, answer:
1. What was the opening line?
2. Where was the turn?
3. What was the single most vivid detail?
4. How did the story end?
5. What technique could I apply to my next project?
